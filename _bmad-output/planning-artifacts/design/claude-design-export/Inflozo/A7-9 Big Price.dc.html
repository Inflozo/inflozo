Big Price

The price set large enough to be the section: 88 or 112 px, flush left across the content width, a rule under it and the benefits beneath. A4·6 and A6·9 made the headline the display moment; here it is the number. It is the only design in A7 above the price ladder, and the only one that refuses three tiers — two 112 px numbers on one page is a poster, three is a chart.

The number is the section's one display moment — 88 px in the heading font at line height 1 and tracking −0.035em, flush to the page's left margin, with the tier name 20 px directly above it and the cadence still 17 px muted beside it, because a big number that swallowed its unit would be a number a reader has to guess at. The description and the action sit at the right end of the same line, baseline-aligned to the price's foot rather than centred against it, so the action stands where the number ends.
The rule under them is the section's own — 32 px below the price row, the benefits 28 px under it in two columns filled across rather than down, because at 1,000 px wide a column read downwards puts the fourth benefit level with the first. The free tier is one muted sentence with a text link, 28 px under the benefits: this design has room for exactly one alternative and no room for a second card. Three tiers is not offered — the sidebar names 1 Cards — and two tiers means one paid tier plus free.

The price's width is the design's real constraint, not its height. “€7” is 130 px at Display 88; “€1,200” is 560, and at Huge it is 720 — past which the description and action cannot share the line and drop to a row of their own under the price, which is a stated arrangement rather than an overflow. The theme switches on measured width, not on digit count, so a currency written as a code rather than a symbol behaves the same way. The editor advises Display 88 above four figures and says why; it does not disable Huge, because a site with a €1,200 patron tier may well want it shouted.

Two paid tiers stack rather than sit side by side — two 88 px numbers on one line at 632 px each would need the price at 56, which is 1 Cards' ladder and 6 Single Tier's arrangement. Stacked, both keep the display scale and the reader compares them by scrolling one screen rather than across a row, which is the trade this design makes and the reason it refuses a third. Settlement 2 is unchanged: the recommended tier keeps the accent fill, the other takes the outline, and there is no plane to step, so the badge does that work — drawn beside the tier name, where the name sits above the price.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub and note edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Tier names, prices, descriptions and benefits are Ghost’s: clicking one says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL opens the Link Picker — a subscribe ask is its Portal · Sign up action — and every action takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label. The cadence words and the computed saving line are catalog strings, not fields. “Or read free —” is now the freeLine field, its drawn text the default; the sentence's other half stays the tier's own description, and freeLabel still ends it.
DATA · from Ghost · outside the design's listTiers come from {{#get "tiers"}} filtered type:paid+visibility:public in Ghost's own price order; the free tier is its own type:free object. A Ghost list: no Add, no Remove, no reorder. Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript, elsewhere in the section: both of a tier's prices render side by side, each labelled in words, with no switch — and no saving line, which is the switch's own arithmetic.
