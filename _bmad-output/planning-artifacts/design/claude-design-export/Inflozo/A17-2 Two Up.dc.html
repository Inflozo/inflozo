Two Up

Two 636 cells — the largest ordinary image in A17, at 636 × 424 — with four lines of excerpt available and the card's type ladder one step up throughout. Four posts, two rows. The design for a publication with few pieces and good photography, and the one 12 Bento may advise when its own five cells cannot be filled — advice in the panel, never a switch ⚑ 29 August 2026.

Its one structural control is Columns: Aligned or Staggered — staggered drops the right column 88 px so the two columns read as two independent runs rather than two rows. That is the only place in A17 where a control moves a cell out of its row, and it is a control rather than a nineteenth design because nothing about the cell itself changes. ⚑ Mine, and the most arguable control in the category.

636 · 24 · 636 = 1,296, four posts in two rows 56 px apart — eight more than 1 Three Up's row gap, because a 424 px image needs it to stop the next row reading as a continuation. Every measure on the card steps up one: title 26 over 22, excerpt 16 over 15, image-to-text 16 over 14. The meta row does not step up: a 24 px avatar and 13 px text is the same at every cell width in A17, because it is A1·6's row and a bigger cell does not make an author more important.
Post 3's plate is a 636 × 424 field of hover surface, which is the largest the plate ever gets and the case that most tests A17-0's settlement 2. It holds because the plate is the image's box exactly — same ratio, same radius, same position — so the row still reads as two photographs even when one of them is not there. Post 4 draws as a title and a meta row under an image; at this scale the missing excerpt is the most visible it gets in the category, and the cell is 74 px shorter than its neighbour. ⚑ Mine: the 56 px row gap, the one-step type ladder, and Count 4 as this design's default.

STAGGERED · THE RIGHT COLUMN DROPS 88 · TWO RUNS RATHER THAN TWO ROWS · NO CELL CHANGES
Staggered drops the right column by 88 px — one row-gap-and-a-half — and nothing else. Same cell width, same ratio, same type, same gaps. The two columns stop being rows and become two runs, which is what a magazine does when two pieces of equal weight should not be compared. ⚑ Mine, including the 88. Staggered changes the reading order it looks like, and not the order it is. DOM order stays 1, 2, 3, 4 — down the left, then down the right is not what happens: post 2 is still the top of the right column, so the eye's path and the tab order agree. This is why the stagger is 88 and not a full cell height: a deeper drop would put post 2's title below post 3's, and then the two would disagree. Staggered is dropped at 834 and below, where the grid is one column and there is nothing to stagger against. It is also dropped in print. With an odd number of posts the last cell sits in the left column and the right column simply ends higher — which at Staggered looks intentional and at Aligned looks like a short row. That is the one place this control earns its keep on content rather than taste.
