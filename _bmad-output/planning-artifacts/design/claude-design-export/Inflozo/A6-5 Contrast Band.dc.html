Contrast Band

The whole banner on the inverted ground. Ground is not a control here: it is the design. It is the loudest thing A6 draws, it spends no accent at all, and it is the one design whose light and dark modes are not variations of each other — because in the Paper pack the band in dark mode is light.

The padding runs one step tighter than the rest of A6 — 44 · 64 · 88 — which is A4·9's scale carried unchanged. An inverted block is read as bigger than it is, and 96 px of padding inside one is the section that swallows a page. Comfortable 64 here occupies about the same optical space as Comfortable 96 on background.
The accent is spent nothing. An accent fill carrying the band's colour is 3.2:1 in Paper, so the action is a solid text-coloured fill carrying the band's own colour — the highest-contrast button in the library at 12.8:1 — and it hovers 3% darker rather than 6%, because a near-white fill darkening by 6% looks like a pressed state. The secondary is the carried colour at 72%, the same value as the sub. Above the footer the band's own bottom edge is the boundary, and the footer's hairline sits below 28 px of the page's ground; the band never carries a hairline of its own.

A6·5's two values are A5·10's two values, and the 56 px inner padding comes with them. At Page margin the band stops at the content width, takes the pack radius and adds 56 px of horizontal padding inside its own edge — so the text is 56 px from the band's edge and the band is 72 px from the window's, which is the arrangement 4 Card makes with a hairline instead of an inversion. Full bleed is always the value at ≤ 767, whatever the control says: 20 px of page ground either side of an inverted block reads as a mistake. The band is never the only inverted thing on a page — one band per page is the editor's advice, and where A3·5's footer is also a band the advice names it, since a banner and a footer both inverted make one 600 px dark region with a hairline in it.

The accent value is disabled with its ratio shown, and it stays disabled in packs whose accent would pass — a control whose availability varied by pack would make one design twelve. A4·9 and A5·10 reached this answer before it; this design carries it rather than arguing it again. An outlined button is not the answer either: A6 has no outlined action at any value on any design, because an outline on a band is a button that looks disabled and an outline on a page ground is a secondary pretending to be a primary. The focus ring changes colour here — the carried colour rather than the accent — which is the one place in A6 it does, and it is the same exception A5·10 made.

Paper's dark contrast is #EDE7DA carrying #171511, so in dark mode the band is the light thing on the page. That is the token doing what it says — contrast means inverted, not dark — and it is the one design in A6 whose two modes are not variations of each other. Muted on the light band is #57524A at 6.4:1, a re-tune rather than the light mode's 72% mix, since a 72% mix of near-black on a warm off-white is grey mud. 1 · The band is not a landmark, a region or a theme boundary — it is a background colour on a section that already has a name. Nothing announces the inversion, nothing about it is a state, and a reader who cannot see it loses nothing. A5·10 settled this and it carries. 2 · The action is A6's button with two colours substituted, not a different component: same 15 px/600, same 13×22, same 46 px, same radius. It is a <a> whose visible label is its whole name, as everywhere. 3 · The secondary at 72% of the carried colour is 7.4:1 — higher than its 5.4:1 on the page's own ground, which is worth knowing: the band is the most legible place in the library for muted text and the least legible for the accent. On a phone it goes to the full carried colour, and the reason is confusion with disabled text rather than contrast. Contrast · title 14.6:1 light band / 14.9:1 dark band · sub, eyebrow and note 7.4:1 / 6.4:1 · action label on its fill 14.6:1 / 14.9:1 · focus ring 12.8:1, the carried colour rather than the accent · the refused accent fill 3.2:1, shown on the control. Forced colours drop the band's colour entirely and the section renders on the user's own background with a bordered link; nothing in the design depends on the inversion, which is what makes that acceptable rather than a failure.

MEMBER ASK AND NO JAVASCRIPT · PATCH PASS · 29 AUG 2026 The ask is conditional. Where the primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting rather than shipping a button that fails. An ordinary link is unaffected. Ruled by the owner, 29 August 2026: the button keeps its place and its scale, and the site chooses what it says and where it goes — a fallback label and link (fallbackLabel ≤ 24 · fallbackUrl, in the panel beside the ask), so a reader meets a working button rather than a gap. Left empty, this design’s own rule applies: no primary label or URL, no section. Where the ask opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real href to a Portal address, so the page stays where it is and no window opens. No JavaScript: this design draws no form, so there is nothing to replace — every word and both actions are rendered by the server, and the banner is the banner.
