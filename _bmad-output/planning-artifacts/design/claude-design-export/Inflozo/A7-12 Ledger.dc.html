No planes anywhere: hairline rows, the tier name at the left, the price at the right, the benefits as one muted line beneath. It asks whether a tier needs a card at all, and answers that a price at the end of a rule is as legible as a price in a box and takes a third of the height. The quietest thing A7 can put on a page, and the design for a site whose whole layout is rules and type.

1 · A <ul> of rows with each tier name an <h3> at 24 px — and no <h2> above them, since the design draws no title. The section takes no aria-labelledby rather than inventing one: A7's second unlabelled section, with 8 Slim Row. A page should not run both, and the editor says so — two unlabelled sections of prices is a page with two anonymous regions. 2 · The middot line is a real <ul> of inline <li>, the separators drawn with ::before and aria-hidden. A reader hears “list of 4 items”, not a sentence with middots in it, and the list survives Benefits as a list without changing its markup — only its layout changes between the values. 3 · The price is one readable string on a fixed column, right-aligned by CSS and not by padding or tabular figures. Right alignment is the only visual difference from every other design's price; the markup is A7's. The rules are border-top on each row and a border-bottom on the last, never <hr>: they separate rows visually and nothing semantically. Rows are never links; one tab stop per action, and none at Actions None. Contrast · names and prices 12.1:1 · benefit line 5.4:1, which is the design's weakest point and is stated: the whole benefit list is muted here where every other design keeps it at full strength, and the argument is that a 15 px line of four phrases at full strength competes with the price. Benefits as a list returns it to full strength, and the editor names that value for a site whose benefits are the argument. Hairlines 1.4:1, decorative.

A Ghost list: no Add, no Remove, no reorder. Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript, elsewhere in the section: both of a tier's prices render side by side, each labelled in words, with no switch — and no saving line, which is the switch's own arithmetic.
