Contrast Band

The whole footer on one contrast ground — brand, columns and legal line together, no second tier. Ground is not a control here: it is the design. This is the footer for a page that needs a hard bottom edge, and it is the design that settles how carried text, hairlines and focus rings behave on an inverted band across the category.

One band, three carried values and nothing else: headings and wordmark at full strength, links, tagline and legal at 72%, hairlines at 15%. No hairline above the band — the ground change is the edge, and the page's own white stops dead. Padding is bigger than the light-ground designs by one step at every setting, because an inverted band needs air or it reads as a bar; 64 px top at Comfortable against A3·2's 56.

Hover: 72% → the pack's lightest surface with a 50% underline. Focus-visible: the ring is that same lightest surface, not accent — the A2·1 rule, carried forward verbatim, because Paper's accent on near-ink is 4.85:1 and a darker pack's accent fails outright. Glyph hover is a 10% wash of the carried colour; pressed is the same wash at 16%. Accent appears nowhere on this band, and that is deliberate: the band already is the emphasis.

The second value of the Legal control: the line moves to a deeper strip at the bottom edge, carried text at 60%, no hairline. The strip is the contrast token darkened one step — the only derived colour in the design, and flagged as mine: packs whose contrast is already near-black get a 6% lift instead of a darkening, so the strip is always distinguishable from the band.

In dark, contrast is a pale warm neutral carrying the deep background as its text, so the footer is the brightest element on the page and still the quietest — the same flip A2·1 Rule documents for its band. The mark inverts with it: deep square, pale letter. Carried values move from 72% / 15% to 70% / 15%, because dark text on a pale ground reads heavier at the same opacity. Hover and focus switch to the deep background colour. Structure identical.

Every value above the 4.5:1 floor with room to spare — an inverted band is the easiest ground in the pack to pass and the easiest to get wrong by reaching for accent.
