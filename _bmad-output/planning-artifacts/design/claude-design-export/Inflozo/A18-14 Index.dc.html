Index

Titles only, in three columns, read down one column and then down the next. The densest design in the library: twenty-five posts in 560 px of page, where 1 Rows spends the same height on four.

It is a list rather than a grid because it is one <ul> flowed into columns, not a set of cells — so the reading order and the DOM order are the same order, which is the thing multi-column layout gets right and a three-column grid of separate lists does not.

Three 416 px columns on a 24 px gutter — A10's cell divisions, unchanged. The whole library uses the same widths whether it is drawing a card, a statistic or a title, which is why a page mixing sections from four categories lines up. Fifteen posts in 468 px of list.
The entry is a title and a date and nothing else. No tag, no author, no excerpt, no picture — and the design is defined by that subtraction rather than by its columns. A 416 px column could hold a tag; it could not hold a tag on fifteen entries without the page becoming a table of tags.
The title is 17 px, the smallest in A18, and the date 13. That pairing is the floor: the brief's rule is that body text never goes below 15, and a title is not body text — 17 in the heading font on a 416 measure holds about 46 characters a line, which is a comfortable two lines for most post titles. ⚑ Mine, and the reason Title size has no value below it.
Columns are balanced by count, not by height. Fifteen posts make three columns of five whatever the titles do, so the last column is ragged and the entry counts are equal — the opposite of what CSS multi-column does by default, and the right choice here because a reader counting down a column should find the same number in each.

Detail is the design's one content control and it has three values: Title only, Title and date, Title and tag. Never two of them at once — a second line under a 17 px title is affordable and a third is a card. Title and date is the default because a date is the fact that makes an index navigable. ⚑ The reading order is down and then across, and nothing on the page says so. That is a real risk with three columns: a reader may scan left to right across the top and read the 1st, 6th and 11th posts as a row. The dates are what disambiguate it — they descend down each column and jump back up at the next — which is a second reason Title and date is the default and Title only carries the risk unmitigated. Cut: a reading-order control. Across-then-down is expressible and would put post 2 at the top of the second column; it is the arrangement a reader who scans horizontally expects, and it makes the DOM order and the visual order disagree, which is the one thing multi-column layout is chosen to avoid. Down-then-across is the only order offered.

Two 636 · Three 416 · Four 306, from A10's divisions. Three is the default and four is the ceiling: a 306 px column holds about 34 characters a line at 17 px, so an average post title is two lines and a long one is three. That is legible and it is where the design's density stops paying, because ragged entries of one to three lines lose the rhythm the index depends on. Two columns is the setting for long titles and for a page that has another section beside it. A 636 column holds most titles on one line, which makes the entries uniform and the index scannable in a way three columns never quite are. ⚑ Titles are never clipped here, unlike 3 Slim's. An index entry wraps to as many lines as it needs and the rule below it moves down. That is the one place this design spends space rather than saving it, and it is deliberate: a clipped title in a two-line entry would be unreadable where a clipped title on a single-line row is merely short.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESEvery visible authored text edits inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored: the eyebrow, the title, the sub, the note and the link label. Post content is Ghost’s and is never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking a post title, tag, author name or excerpt says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL field opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the head link’s target defaults to Matches the query so it cannot go stale when the query does. This design draws no image at any setting, so rule 10’s Image focus has nothing to attach to ⚑. No button, so rule 11’s optional icon has nothing to attach to ⚑ — the head link is text with an accent underline, and an icon on it would make it look like a button. No fixed English visitor-facing string ships: the head strings are authored with no defaults and this design draws no furniture text of its own; the main feed’s empty pair is authored, with defaults. No authored list exists in A18, so the P0·3 item controls have nothing to govern: the rows are Ghost’s and there is no Add ⚑. There is no Preview control and there never was one to remove.

THE ONE DISABLED VALUE IN A18, AND THE COUNT LINE THAT REPORTS A SHAPE Title size Medium is unavailable at Columns Four, with the reason shown — “19 px in a 306 column is three lines for most titles.” This is the only disabled value in A18, and it follows the library's rule that a value which fails is disabled with its reason rather than silently allowed. A17 disabled a value on contrast grounds; this one is disabled on measure. “15 drawn in 3 columns of 5.” The third count line in A18 to report a structure, after 5 Grouped's groups and 10 Lead and List's split. Here it earns its place because Show and Columns interact: 15 across three is even, 15 across four is 4·4·4·3, and the last column is short. The line reports the division and does not prevent an uneven one. Row density is 12 · 16 · 24 — the tightest ladder in A18, and at Compact an entry with a date is 62 px. The sixth design to keep a control's name and change its quantity, which A18-0 now records as a category-wide pattern rather than six separate flags. Cut: an alphabetical order. The Data group offers Newest first and Oldest first; an index is the one design where A–Z would genuinely help, and adding it to this design alone would give the shared query block a value that eleven other designs would have to ignore. ⚑ A finding for the architect rather than a control: if order ever gains a third value, this is the design that asked for it.

THE WRITTEN SPEC · ALL TEN FIELDS
1 · Descriptor. Titles at the category's smallest size flowed down two, three or four columns with one optional fact each; the densest design in the library and the only one that draws a whole archive at once. 2 · Structural descriptor. feed · none · page · many · none · titles-only columns
Shares its closed slots with 1 Rows, 3 Slim, 4 Dated, 11 Numbered and 13 Timeline. Not grid-of-N: the columns are a flow, not cells, and the reading order runs down them. 3 · Archetype. feed. One departure: the archetype has one column and this has up to four, so the ladder is applied to the column count rather than to the row — three at 1440, two at 834, one at 767. 4 · Responsive rule. 1440 Two 636 · Three 416 · Four 306 on a 24 gutter; entry padding 12 at Compact; title 17, date 13. 1080 Four becomes three; two and three narrow. 834 three and four both become two at 365. ≤ 767 one column at the full measure, padding 10. The entry — its type sizes, its rule and its two lines — is identical at all four widths; only the number of columns changes. 5 · Content fields. Section: the six. Query: all four. Post: title, url, and one of published_at or primary_tag at Detail's second and third values. Reads no image, no author, no excerpt, no reading time — tied with 3 Slim for the shortest field list in the category. Writes columns, detail and titleSize; shares titleSize's Small · Medium with 3 Slim at the same 17 and 19. 6 · Controls. Columns Two · Three · Four. Detail Title only · Title and date · Title and tag. Title size Small · Medium (Medium unavailable at Columns Four, reason shown). Rule Off · Between rows · Boxed. Row density Compact · Comfortable · Spacious (12 · 16 · 24). “View all” link Matches the query · Custom. Six, plus the universal trio (Background role · Vertical spacing · Top divider) and the Data group, both outside this list; Padding is retired into Vertical spacing. No Tag row — Detail Title only · Title and date · Title and tag already governs it. Order gains Title A–Z in the Data group. No Excerpt, no Meta, no reading-order control.
7 · Data. #get "posts" per the Data group, Count a stepper with this design's bounds. Designed for 15 and 25; correct at 3 to 30 — the category's widest upper range, with 3 Slim. Columns are balanced by count, not by height: 15 across three is 5·5·5; 15 across four is 4·4·4·3 and the last column is short, which the count line reports. 0 published: no render. 0 in the editor: three outlined columns of three outlined entries, plus the query line. 1: one title in the left column and two empty columns beside it — the worst single-post case in A18, worse than 3 Slim's because the empty columns are visible as absence. The panel suggests Columns Two at counts below four and does not switch. 2 or 3: the design draws one entry per column, which is a row of titles rather than an index; the count line's advice covers it. More than Show: not drawn. 8 · Empty state. At Detail Title and tag an untagged post draws its title alone and its entry is 21 px shorter; the column's other entries do not adjust, so an untagged post makes one short entry among tall ones — ordinary, and visible, and correct. At Title and date nothing can be missing. No image, author or excerpt case exists. Section fields absent → not drawn. 9 · Behaviour module. None; core assumed. No-JS degradation: pixel-identical with JavaScript off. Edit-safe. Considered and refused: filter-strip, which the registry says works perfectly without JavaScript and which an archive index genuinely invites — refused because A17·15 owns the filtered list and a site can place it above this section. Also refused: shuffle, as everywhere. 10 · Accessibility. <section aria-labelledby> → <h2>. ⚑ One <ul> for the whole index, flowed into columns by CSS — not one list per column — so a screen reader announces “list, fifteen items” once and reads them in the same order a sighted reader follows down the columns. That is the design's main accessibility argument and the reason it is built as a flow rather than as three lists. Each entry an <li> wrapping one <a>, title an <h3>. Date a <time datetime>. A6's ring on the entry; at Compact the entry is 62 px with a date and 44 px without, so the touch minimum is met at every setting — the second design after 3 Slim to sit exactly on that floor. Contrast 15.46:1 and 5.56:1 light, 15.64:1 and 6.89:1 dark.
