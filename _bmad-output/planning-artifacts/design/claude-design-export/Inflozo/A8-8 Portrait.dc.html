A8 TESTIMONIALS · DESIGN 8 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONE PHOTOGRAPH IN THE CATEGORY

Portrait

One quotation beside one photograph of the person who said it. Along with 15 Overlap it is the only design in A8 that reads image, and it is the only one where the photograph is a person rather than a scene. The avatar is not drawn here — a 36 px circle of the same face beside a 632 px photograph of it is the same fact twice.

Three things settled. The crop is a named control and never automatic, because a face is the one subject a theme must not decide the framing of. A missing image hands off to 1 Single rather than drawing a striped box where a person should be. And the quotation stays at Feature 27 even beside a 760 px photograph: the picture is the company the words keep, not the headline.

632 · 32 · 632, 3 Two Up's division and gutter — the same numbers because this is the same problem: two things of equal claim side by side. The two halves are vertically centred against each other, which is the one place in A8 something is centred rather than top-aligned: a 4:5 photograph is 790 px tall and a five-line quotation is 260, and top-aligning them would hang the words off the top corner of a picture.
The eyebrow does the head's whole job here. There is no title, no sub and no title-size control: a Large 40 title above a 27 px quotation beside a 790 px photograph is a third thing competing, and the section has room for two. The note and the link sit under the attribution in the text half — never under the photograph, where they would read as its caption. ⚑ The vertical centring, dropping the title entirely and the foot's placement are mine.

MATCH THE TEXT · THE IMAGE TAKES THE QUOTE'S HEIGHT
The crop is named, not computed. A theme that decides where to cut a photograph of a person will sooner or later cut through a chin, and no amount of face detection makes that a decision a template should be taking. The three values are ratios the site chooses; the image is object-fit: cover and centred on its own centre, with no focal-point control in A8 — a site that needs a different focal point crops the file, and the editor says so rather than offering a nine-point grid this category cannot honour anywhere else. Match the text is the honest third value and the only one that can leave a wide face in a narrow box: at three lines of quotation the image is 170 px tall and 632 wide, which is a landscape band rather than a portrait. It is offered because a site with a short quote and a group photograph wants exactly that, and the editor's help line names the risk. The scrim rule does not apply here at any value — no text is ever laid over this photograph, which is 15 Overlap's problem, not this design's. A vertical image next to centred text leaves white space above and below the words. That is the arrangement, not a fault: the quotation is 260 px of type in a 790 px column and the air is what makes it read as a pull quote rather than a caption. ⚑ All three values, the no-focal-point rule and Match the text's warning are mine.

NO IMAGE · hands off to 1 Single, and the avatar comes back with it

A missing photograph is the state this design has to answer well, because the photograph is half of it. There is no striped placeholder and no initials block at any width: the design hands off to 1 Single, centred at Feature 27, and the avatar returns — the reason it was suppressed was the big picture, and without it a 44 px circle is the only face there is. The sidebar reads “No photograph — drawing 1 Single” and links to the field.
At Image-led the quote steps down one, which is a width decision rather than a length one, and it is disclosed at the size control the same way a long quote is. ⚑ The hand-off, the returning avatar and the Image-led step are mine.

Knowing whose face you are looking at before you read the sentence is a real reading order, and it is the one arrangement where the name and the photograph are adjacent.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. The photograph carries alt text, requested by the editor, and its Image Picker popover shows Image focus disabled with the reason: this design names its crop instead. The help line ships in the panel: “A wide face in a narrow box — crop the file, or switch Crop to Match the text.”
DATA · nothing from Ghost · outside the design’s listNothing here comes from Ghost. There is no testimonial, review or endorsement resource, so every word is authored in this section: no Populate from… panel is offered on any A8 design and there is no missing-data branch. The only failure available is an empty repeater, and then the section does not render. Comments are refused as a source (a reader who replied under a post agreed to be read there — A28 owns it) and members are refused (a name and an email in Ghost’s members table are private data). Refused on record, at every value: no rating, score, star or “verified” mark (Ghost stores none of it), no per-item link, no decorative quote glyph, no autoplay. The photograph is a section field, not an item field: reordering the quotes changes the words and not the picture.
