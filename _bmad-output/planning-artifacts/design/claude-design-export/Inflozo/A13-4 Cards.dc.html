Cards

One step per card on a plane with a hairline: a 44 px icon inline with the title, the numeral at the card's top right, the body under both. The only design in A13 where a step gets an edge of its own — 10 Panel puts a plane around the whole section instead, which is a different thing.

It is one of three designs that read icon, and the reason the icon is inline rather than above is that a card already has a top edge: an icon sitting on its own line under that edge gives the card two headers. Because the card has a visible edge, a short card is stretched to its row's height rather than left short — the one place A13 departs from A13-0's top-aligned rule, and A12·2 made the same departure for the same reason.

ACCESSIBILITY · FOUR CARDS, ONE TAB STOP, NO CARD IS A CONTROL The card is the <li> itself — no role, no label, no tabindex, never <article> and never <figure>. A step is not an independent document and a card is not a control. Contrast is measured against the card's own fill rather than the page ground, which matters at Cards Surface in light: muted body on #FFFFFF is 5.9:1 rather than 5.6:1. The icon is aria-hidden whether it is a glyph or an upload, because the title beside it says what the step is — the same rule A12 applied to a photograph with a name beside it. There is no iconAlt field in A13 and none is generated. When the box carries the numeral plate instead, it is aria-hidden too. Reading order is icon, title, numeral, body in the DOM, announced as title then body — both silent elements are skipped, so a reader hears “Send the pitch. Two paragraphs on…”, item 1 of 4. Forced colours keep the card's border and the icon box's border and lose both fills, at which point Cards Surface and Cards Ground are the same drawing. Tab stops: one, the section link. Contrast: title and step title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 on the card's fill, body and numeral 5.9:1 / 6.0:1, icon glyph 5.9:1 on the hover surface, hairline 1.3:1 disclosed. At 400% zoom the cards are one across and the equal-height rule stops applying.
