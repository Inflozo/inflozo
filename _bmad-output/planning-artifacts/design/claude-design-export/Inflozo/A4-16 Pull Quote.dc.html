The headline takes the eyebrow's treatment — 13 px uppercase tracked, muted, no display moment — and the quotation takes the display position at 46 px in the heading font, regular weight rather than bold, on a 900 px measure. That reversal is the design: the section has one display moment as every section does, and here it is spent on the quotation. The quotation marks are typed into the field and part of the authored content, because a theme that adds its own leaves the site owner unable to quote a fragment mid-sentence. The name is required and the control cannot remove it. Every other text element in A4 can be turned off; this one cannot, because an unattributed quotation in a hero is the site putting words in an anonymous reader's mouth. Where a site has permission to quote but not to name, the honest field is a role — “A reader in Lisbon” — typed into the name. No stars, no five-dot rating, no company logo, no verified tick. Those are the furniture of a testimonial widget, and a publication's hero is not a pricing page. Flagged as mine, along with the three values and the missing fourth.

240 characters, hard. The editor counts down from 60 remaining and refuses the 241st. It is the only hard cap in the category — every other field advises — and it is hard because a paragraph in the display position is not a pull quote, it is an excerpt, and the design has no arrangement that survives one. The automatic step-down above 180 characters is the only place in A4 where content changes a control's effect rather than the layout. The control still reads Large; the rendered size is one value below it, and the editor says so under the size control rather than silently disagreeing with itself. Flagged as mine: the cap, the 180 threshold and the disclosure.

1 · The headline is still the page's <h1> even though it is the smallest text in the section — visual size is not heading level, and the page's outline must say what the page is rather than what a reader said. Where no headline is authored the quotation becomes the h1 instead, and the section never renders without one of the two. 2 · <blockquote> wrapping a <p>, with the attribution in a <figcaption> inside a <figure> — the HTML pattern for a quotation and its source, so the attribution is programmatically the quote's rather than a paragraph that happens to sit under it. The quotation marks are typed characters inside the paragraph, not CSS ::before, so they are read as part of the text. 3 · The portrait is decorative — alt="" — because the name is beside it; the initials fallback is aria-hidden for the same reason. Name and role are plain text in the figcaption, never links. No motion, no rotation, no carousel. One quotation, rendered. A hero that cycles testimonials moves the page's focal point without being asked and gives a screen-reader user a section whose content changes under them; a set of more than one quotation is A8 Testimonials', and A2·9 Rotator is where the library rotates anything at all — announcing itself as it does. There is nothing here for reduced motion to remove. Contrast · quote 12.1:1 light / 12.9:1 on the dark Contrast band · headline 5.4:1 / 5.9:1 · name 12.1:1 / 12.9:1 · role 5.4:1 / 5.9:1. At Display 60 the quote is large text and clears AAA comfortably; the headline at 13 px is the section's tightest measurement and is checked at every pack.
