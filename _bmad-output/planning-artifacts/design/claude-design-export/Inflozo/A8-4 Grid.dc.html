A8 TESTIMONIALS · DESIGN 4 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ROW-STRETCH RULE AND ITS COST

Grid

Six quote cards, three columns and two rows, on 2 Three Up's 416 / 24 grid. Everything about the card is Three Up's; the only new problem is the second row, and it is a real one: six quotations of honestly different lengths in a grid produce white space, and the question is where the design puts it.

The answer this design settles for the category: rows are equal within themselves and never across the grid. Each row is as tall as its own tallest quote, the two rows are different heights, and the slack falls at the foot of the short cards inside a row. The alternatives — one height for all six, or letting the cards pack — are the wrong shape for a card with an attribution pinned to its foot, and the packed version already exists as 5 Wall.

Row one is 232 px tall and row two is 208 px, because their tallest quotes are different lengths. That is the rule working, not failing: a grid whose rows are all as tall as its longest quotation puts 60 px of white under five cards to be tidy about one. Reading order is left to right, then down, which is the authored order, and no card is promoted for having a photograph or demoted for a two-line organisation line: cards 3 and 6 have no avatar and keep their place. Six is the ceiling here — past six a grid of equal-width cards is a wall, and 5 Wall is drawn for it with a different mechanism.

THE SAME 41-CHARACTER QUOTE UNDER THREE RULES · the argument, drawn

Equal across the grid is refused because the cost is paid by every short quote at once, and a card that is twice as tall as its content reads as a card missing something. Packed is refused because the attribution has to stay at the card's foot: a packed grid of cards with pinned feet is a wall, which is 5 Wall's job and 5 Wall's mechanism — CSS columns at natural heights, and no equal heights anywhere. Here the slack is 24 px on average and lands under two cards out of six. ⚑ Both refusals, and the row-not-grid rule, are mine.

1 · One <ul> of six <li> — not two lists of three, because the rows are a CSS grid and not a structure a reader should hear. A screen reader announces “list, 6 items” at every width and every column value, and the order never changes. 2 · The equal-height mechanism is invisible to assistive technology, which is the point: the stretch is on the blockquote, so nothing is padded, spaced or filled with anything a reader could hear. A card with white space at its foot and a card without sound identical. 3 · Six cards, one focus stop. The foot link is the only focusable element in the section — no card is a link, no avatar is a button, nothing is tabbable inside a quote — so tabbing past this section costs one press however many quotations it holds. A6's ring, 3.3:1 light and 6.3:1 dark. Contrast · quote and name 16.6:1 light / 15.1:1 dark on surface; role 5.9:1 / 6.4:1; eyebrow, sub and note on the ground 5.6:1 / 6.8:1. At cards Ground the card and page share a colour and every ratio is the ground's. Forced colours: six bordered boxes on one ground; the uneven rows remain visible as border geometry. Reduced motion changes nothing — there is no motion in this design at any value.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Every avatar carries Image focus — Centre · Top · Bottom in its Image Picker popover, never as a hidden field.
