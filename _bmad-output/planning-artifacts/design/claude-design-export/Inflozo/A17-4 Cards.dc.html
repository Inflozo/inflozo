A17 POST GRIDS · DESIGN 4 OF 18 · PAPER PACK · EIGHT CONTROLS

Cards

Each post on its own surface plane with a hairline and the pack's radius, equalised to the tallest card in its row, the meta row pinned to the card's foot. The only design in A17 that gives a post a plane — 17 Panel planes the whole section instead, and those two are the category's only planes of any kind.

It is also the only design in the category with a hover lift, and the reason is narrow: A17-0 refuses lifts because a bare cell has nothing to lift, but a plane can rise off the ground it sits on. The card takes the md shadow on hover and keeps the title underline; nothing scales and nothing moves.

The plane costs it two things the bare designs get free: the image must go edge to edge inside the card, so the plane's radius clips it, and a card with no excerpt cannot simply be shorter — equal-height rows are what a plane implies. Both are drawn below.

Edge to edge is the default and Inset is the honest alternative. Edge to edge gives the card one silhouette and makes the image part of the object; Inset nests two radii, which is correct in a pack with a small radius and fussy in a pack with a large one. At Tangerine's 14 px radius Inset is the better setting, and A17 has no way to say so per pack — a finding for the architect rather than a defect here. ⚑ Mine. Image None is the value that makes this design something else — a grid of text cards on planes, which no other A17 design provides and which is the right answer for a publication with no photography. It is a value rather than a switch to another design because the plane, not the image, is this design's identity. Compare 9 Big Type, which drops images and has no plane. Cards Equal height is the default; Natural height is available and disclosed. Natural lets a card be as short as its content, reinstating the bare grid's behaviour inside a plane — legitimate when every post has an excerpt, visibly ragged when one does not. The panel says which: “Natural height leaves short cards short.” The plane is surface, never background, and the hairline is not optional. In a pack whose surface and background are one step apart the hairline is the only thing carrying the card's edge — this is the tokenisation risk this design carries, and the reason it cannot offer a borderless variant. ⚑ Mine.

The lift is a shadow swap and nothing else — sm to md, 160 ms ease-out. No translateY, no scale, no border-colour change, no ground change. WHAT THE PLANE ADDS AND WHAT IT TAKES AWAYCard plane is the real addition: the plane’s fill was the one thing this design could not say. Outline reads as 1 Three Up with a border, which is why Surface stays the default.Tag and “View all” link added; Padding retired into Vertical spacing. Image and Cards are the two controls only this design can have, and they cost it the two 1 Three Up spends on Image ratio and the first cell’s span. Image ratio is cut and the ratio is fixed at Landscape 3:2 — with equal-height rows the ratio no longer changes the row's height, only the split between picture and text inside a fixed box, which is the least interesting thing a control can do. The plane's two controls are worth more here than a ratio the equal-height rule already flattens. ⚑ Mine, and the tightest trade in A17's control lists. No First cell control. A spanned plane is a wide card, and a wide card in a row of planes reads as an unequal box rather than an editorial claim — the eye sees a mistake, not a promotion. featured is read by no design in A17 after 29 August 2026 — the span belongs to the first post by position, and this design does not span at all. A17-0's settlement 3 has been corrected to five designs offering the span, not six; both files now say five. Cut: Row rule — planes and rules are two separating devices doing one job. Cut: Card padding — 20 px is the plane's measure, and a control over it is a designer's decision, not a user's. Cut: Shadow depth — two warm shadows exist and the resting one is sm; a “Flat / Raised” control would let a user set the hover state as the resting state and leave the lift with nowhere to go. Per row Four is available and Per row Five is not. A 240 px plane with 20 px padding leaves 200 px of text, which is a 30-character measure — the plane's padding is what sets this design's floor, and it is one step narrower than the bare designs' because a bare cell spends nothing on padding. 14 Dense at six across is the design for that density.

Dark asks this design one question no other A17 design faces: what is a raised plane on a deep ground? The answer is a lift and not a shadow — surface goes to #1F1C17, one step above the #171511 ground, and the resting sm shadow is dropped entirely, because a warm shadow on a near-black ground is invisible and only muddies the edge.
