Inline

Two to four marks set inside a sentence, on the line. The one design in A11 where a logo has a grammar — it is the object of a verb rather than an item in a list — and the one that reads lede. A11·11 Named draws a caption under a mark; this draws the mark inside the caption.

It is A10·11's mechanism carried forward verbatim: each mark is a chip inserted from the editor's toolbar, and a logo cannot be typed into the sentence by hand. It is also the only design in A11 that does not draw url, and the only one with no arrangement to collapse.

One paragraph on a 780 measure, leading set to the logo height plus 12 — 36 px at Inline 24 — and the same at every width. That is A10·11's rule for the same problem: the line must clear the tallest thing on it, and the tallest thing is a mark rather than a capital. Each mark is an unbreakable inline run and the sentence breaks around it, never through it. The aspect classes still govern: Meridian Press and longform are Wide and draw at the full 24, The Ledger is Regular and draws at 21.
The mark is centred on the line's x-height, not set on the baseline — a wordmark on the baseline sits high against 20 px text and a square mark sits higher still, and the x-height is the only line feature both can share. The mark takes no accent, no underline, no weight change and no baseline shift, and it is never a link. ⚑ Mine: the leading rule at this box size, the x-height centring, and the three vertical offsets it produces.

A mark is inserted from the toolbar as a chip, and it cannot be typed. The chip carries which logo it is, not a copy of the file, so replacing the upload changes the sentence and deleting the logo takes its chip with it. A chip is one atomic character to the cursor: one arrow key steps over it, one backspace removes it. A10·11's mechanism, unchanged. The two sizes are a pair and the panel says so: Body 20 with Inline 24, Large 24 with Large 32. The other two combinations are legal — Body 20 with Large 32 makes the mark taller than the line's ascender, which is a deliberate effect and not a mistake — and neither is disabled. The leading always follows the logo, never the type. Nothing collapses at any width, because a paragraph reflows. A mark is never a link here, so url is kept and not drawn — the only field in A11 any design ignores outright. A 24 px underlined mark inside a 20 px sentence reads as a typographic accident, and the target would be a 90 × 24 sliver in the middle of a paragraph. The lede's own prose can carry the link instead, which is what the sentence is for. ⚑ Mine, and A10·11's position for its inline values. A logo with no chip in the sentence is not drawn and nothing is appended to the paragraph. The panel reports the count — “2 of 3 placed” — and the two unplaced marks stay in the repeater for the design the site switches to. No lede → the section does not render, because a mark here sits inside the sentence and there is nothing else for it to sit in — the same outcome as no marks at all. Five or more logos → the chips are the count: a fifth mark is held unplaced and the panel advises 1 Row, because a sentence with five marks in it is a list with punctuation. Four marks is the ceiling and this is what it looks like: a 620 measure at 24/44, centred, with four marks in four clauses. The sentence is doing the work a list would do and it is close to the point where it stops being a sentence — which is why a fifth mark stays unplaced and the panel advises 1 Row rather than the sentence taking a fifth clause. Centred text with inline marks holds because the marks are inline runs and centre with the words around them.
In dark the marks are drawn from the dark files, and a mark with no dark file takes the plate — inside a sentence. That is the one place in A11 where the plate appears in running text, and its 12 px of padding pushes the line's leading by 24: so the leading rule becomes the logo height plus 12, or the plated height plus 12, whichever is taller, and the panel warns that a plated mark in a sentence costs a visibly taller line. ⚑ Mine, and the design's one real cost in dark.

ACCESSIBILITY · A SENTENCE THAT READS ITSELF One <h2> and one <p>. No <ul> anywhere — this is the only design in A11 with no list, because the marks are not items and turning three words of a sentence into a list would break the sentence in two. A10·11 took the same position for its inline values. Each mark is an <img> with its required alt inside the paragraph, so the sentence reads straight through: “Quoted this year in Meridian Press and longform, and reprinted in full by The Ledger in October.” That is the strongest argument in the category for alt being the brand's plain name — “Meridian Press logo” would put the word logo in the middle of a sentence. The marks are not <strong>, not <em>, not <cite> and not links, so nothing is emphasised that the site did not emphasise and nothing is focusable except an inline link the site wrote in the prose. Tab stops: the section link, plus any link inside the lede. The chip's dashed outline and 6 px of padding exist in the editor and are not published, so what a reader gets and what an author sees differ by exactly that — stated, because it is the one place in A11 where the editing view is not the published view. Contrast: the lede is text at 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark — not muted, because it is the section's reading matter rather than its meta — title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1. A logo takes no ratio, and at Greyscale a mark inside text-coloured prose will read lighter than the words around it, which the field mentions. Reflows to 400% without overlap and without a horizontal scrollbar, because the only thing that has to fit is a line of text. Reduced motion changes nothing. Print draws the sentence as it stands.
