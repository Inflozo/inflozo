Contrast Band

1 Grid inverted onto the contrast token, at the band's own vertical scale. The band is the section, so it replaces the section's Padding control with its own, and everything the band needs beyond the seven roles is derived from one token pair.

A11·7 found that a logo wall on a band wants the opposite file from the mode it is in. A12's band has no such problem, and that is worth stating: a photograph of a person needs no second file, no plate and no inversion on any ground. What the band does change is everything around the photograph — the initials block, the muted line, the focus ring, and whether the accent can appear at all.

The band is a background-color on the section, not a wrapper, so switching between this design and 1 Grid changes nothing in the DOM and nothing announced. Everything the band needs beyond the seven roles is derived from one pair — contrast #232019 carrying #FBF9F5: muted is the carried colour at 72% (#BEBCB7), the initials block is the carried colour at 10% (#3A362F), a hairline would be 18% and is never drawn here. ⚑ All three derivations are mine, and A11·7 set the 72% and 10%.
The photographs are the one thing on this frame the band does not touch. They are the same uploads at the same 72 px as on 1 Grid's ground — no plate, no second file, no filter — which is the difference between a photograph and a logo, and the reason A11·7 needed a whole settlement amended and this design needs none.

ACCESSIBILITY · A BAND THAT ANNOUNCES NOTHING NEW The band is a background-color on the section, so the DOM is 1 Grid's exactly: <section aria-labelledby>, one <h2>, a <ul> of <li>, two <p>s a person, photographs alt="". Switching a section between 1 Grid and this design changes nothing a screen reader hears — the inversion is entirely visual, which is what makes it a safe design to switch into. §7.4 is applied literally here and it is the only place in A12 it bites. The accent's 2.3:1 light and 2.1:1 dark are shown at the disabled value in the panel — “Accent 2.3:1 on this band. Links and focus rings use the band's own colour.” Nothing is silently substituted without saying so. Contrast on the band: title and name 15.5:1 light / 14.8:1 dark; sub, role and note at 72% 8.6:1 light / 6.1:1 dark; link and focus ring in the carried colour 14.1:1 / 13.4:1 — the highest-contrast ring in the category, because it is the carried colour rather than an accent. Initials on the 10% fill 6.2:1 / 5.9:1. Forced colours drop the band entirely and the section draws on the system's own ground with the system's text colour; the initials block keeps a system border so it is still a block. Print drops the band too, and the section prints as 1 Grid in the light palette. Nothing moves at any setting, and no photograph is treated in any way — the band changes the ground, not the content. At 400% zoom the grid is two across on the band and the band keeps its padding.
