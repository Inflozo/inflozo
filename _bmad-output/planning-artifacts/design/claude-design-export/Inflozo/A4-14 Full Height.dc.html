Drawn at half scale. Two rules fire together on a short window. The section never clips: where the set plus its padding needs more room than the window has, the section grows past 100svh and the reader scrolls — the height is a target, not a cap, and a hero that hid its own actions to stay one screen tall would have failed at the one job the category has. THE CUE · states and behaviour · drawn on the contrast ground for legibility
Read onrest · 44 px hit area around a 13 px label and a 20 px rule
Read onhover · label to full strength and the rule grows 20 → 28 px · 160 ms · the only length change in the category
Read onfocus · the ring wraps label and rule together
What it does. A <button>, not a link, because it moves the page rather than going somewhere — the same reasoning A3·16 gives its Back to top. It scrolls to the top of the next section and then moves focus there, so a keyboard user ends up where the page went. It does not bob, pulse or fade in. A hero that animates the one thing it wants you to ignore is a hero arguing with itself, and the category's motion budget is spent on hover states. Under reduced motion the scroll is instant instead of smooth; the cue itself is unchanged, because there was nothing to remove. It leaves once used. Past 40 px of scroll the cue is removed rather than faded, and it does not come back on scrolling up — it answers a question the reader has already answered.

At 200% text zoom on a laptop the set is about 1.6 times its authored height and the section is simply taller than the window, which is the correct outcome and the reason the cue's rule is written the way it is. 2 · Headline is the page's h1, plain text over the scrimmed picture, contrast measured against the scrim as 4 Full Bleed and carrying the same honest limit. Reading order is eyebrow, headline, sub, primary, secondary, cue — the cue is last in the DOM as well as at the foot of the frame. 3 · The cue is a <button> named by its visible label — “Read on” — with a 44 px hit area around a 13 px word and a 20 px rule, and the rule is aria-hidden CSS. It scrolls and then moves focus to the next section's heading, so the keyboard and the viewport agree; instant under reduced motion. It is not a link to an anchor, because a URL fragment for “the next thing on the page” is not a place. Removed rather than hidden · past 40 px of scroll, and whenever the section is not the height of the window, the cue leaves the DOM. Nothing is visually hidden and left in the accessibility tree, and no focusable element sits under the fold waiting to be tabbed into. Contrast · cue label at 82% carried on the scrimmed picture 8.9:1; rule 4.1:1, decorative; everything else as 4 Full Bleed.
