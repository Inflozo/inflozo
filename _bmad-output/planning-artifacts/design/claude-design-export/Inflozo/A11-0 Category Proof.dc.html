A11 LOGO WALLS · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

Twelve files the packs cannot touch

A11 is the first category whose principal content is not type. A logo wall draws artwork the site uploaded — files made by other people, in aspect ratios nobody agreed on, at whatever colour their owners chose. Re-skin the section into another pack and the ground, the head, the hairlines and the radius all change, while every logo on the frame stays exactly as it was. That inverts the usual tokenisation problem: the wall's job is not to be re-coloured, it is to make foreign artwork sit level on a ground it was not made for, in twelve packs and two modes.

1 · COUNTS, WRAPPING, AND THE LAST ROW
Count is a per-row count, never a total. Three 416 · Four 306 · Five 240 · Six 196, all four on a 24 px gutter across the 1,296 content width — A10's divisions exactly, extended by two. The total is what the site authored: three to twenty-four. Rows wrap in authored order, 40 px apart, and the short last row keeps the cell width and centres as a group. Stretching it is refused, because a wider cell is a bigger logo box and one logo drawn larger than its neighbours is the defect the whole category exists to avoid. Left-aligning it is refused except where a design's own Alignment control says Left. A last row of one is drawn, warned about, and not prevented. Seven logos at six across is six and one. The editor's counter names the counts that divide — “7 logos: five across leaves 2, four across leaves 3” — and the site decides. ⚑ The centring, the 40 px row gap and the orphan advice are all mine.

2 · MIXED ASPECT RATIOS, NORMALISED WITHOUT DISTORTION
Every logo sits in a box: a fixed height from the ladder, a max width of the cell minus 24, object-fit: contain, centred on both axes. Nothing is cropped, nothing is stretched, no aspect ratio is ever altered, and no logo is upscaled past its intrinsic size. Normalising by box height alone is the defect, not the fix. A square mark and a 5:1 wordmark drawn at one height are not the same optical weight — the square carries five times the ink. Three aspect classes, computed by the theme from the file's intrinsic ratio, with no control over them: Wide ≥ 3:1 takes 100% of the box · Regular 3:1 to 1.4:1 takes 88% · Square under 1.4:1 takes 72%. Logos in a row share a horizontal centre line, not a baseline — a wordmark's baseline and a mark's centre cannot both be honoured, and the centre line is the one that survives a square. SVG is preferred; PNG is accepted at 2× the box height and the editor warns below it. ⚑ The three classes, all three percentages, the centre line and the 2× floor are mine.

3 · GREYSCALE, MUTED, FULL COLOUR — AND WHAT DARK MODE DOES TO THEM
Treatment, three named values, Greyscale the default. Greyscale is grayscale(1) at full opacity. Muted is grayscale(1) at 64% in light and 74% in dark. Full colour is the file untouched. Refused: invert(), blend modes, duotone, an accent tint, and a per-logo override. A dark ground is where a logo wall breaks, and the answer is a second file, not a filter. ⚑ Amended by 7 Contrast Band: the test is the ground behind the logo, not the mode — that band reads logoDark in light mode and logo in dark. logoDark is optional per logo and drawn by every design. With no dark file the theme does not invert and does not lighten: it draws the logo on a plate carrying the pack's light-mode surface — #FFFFFF in Paper — at the pack radius, 12 px of padding, no hairline and no shadow. The dark-mode surface token cannot be used here: Paper's is #211D17, and dark artwork on it is the problem the plate exists to solve. This is the one place in the library where a dark section draws a light-mode token, and it is deliberate — legible, honest, and visibly a fallback, because the editor is asking for a file. The plate takes the treatment's opacity with the rest of the wall, so at Muted it sits at 74% in dark rather than standing at full strength beside artwork that does not. §7.4 cannot be applied to this control. A value that would fail contrast is meant to be disabled with its ratio shown, and the theme cannot measure an uploaded file — so Muted is never disabled, and the editor carries the advice instead: aim for 3:1 against the ground, non-text. The one enabled value in the library that the theme cannot prove. ⚑ Both percentages, the plate and its padding, and this exemption.

4 · WHETHER LOGOS ARE LINKS, AND WHAT A CAPTION IS
A link is per logo and optional. A logo with a url is an <a> wrapping the <img>; without one it is a bare <img>. The wall is never one link, and a wall where some logos link and some do not is the ordinary case — the panel says how many of the twelve are links. One hover rule at all three treatments: the cell takes the pack's hover surface at the pack radius and the treatment resolves to full colour, 160 ms. Reconciled: the resolve is a section control — Hover: Resolve to colour · None — and it applies to every mark, linked or not, while the surface stays the link's alone. That gives Greyscale and Muted something to reveal and Full colour something to sit on. Focus is A6's ring on the cell box. Refused: an external-link glyph, a tooltip, a “visit” label, a lift, a scale and a shadow. alt is required and is the brand's name. alt="" is refused on every logo in every design — a wall nobody can read is a wall of decoration, and the names are the content. “Caption” is two different fields. The section's note sits under the wall at 13 px muted on a 620 measure and carries the permission line or the “and forty others”. The per-logo caption is 40 characters and is drawn by 11 Named only; the other fourteen keep it and say how many they are not drawing. ⚑ The hover rule, the required alt and both caption positions are mine.

THE COMPONENT · ONE IMAGE IN A BOX, AND NOTHING ELSE
A logo is an <img> in a fixed-height box, centred, contained, with its aspect class setting the drawn height. No border, no plate, no shadow, no radius on the artwork itself, no caption, no hover and no motion — with three stated exceptions: the light-mode-surface plate in dark when no logoDark exists, the hover surface under a link, and 8 Marquee's transport. The box has no visible edge in any design. 5 Cards' card, 4 Boxed' panel and 3 Grid's hairlines are those designs' containers, drawn around the box and never on it. The radius token therefore lands on those three, on the dark plate and on a link's hover surface, and nowhere else in the category. THE RULES ALL FIFTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE
A11's finding, stated first: the category has one behaviour and one interaction, and thirteen designs have neither. 8 Marquee transports the wall and never stops for a pointer; 9 Rail moves only when the reader moves it. Everywhere else the resting state is the only state, and a link's hover is the only thing that responds to anything. A10 had no behaviour at all; A11 has exactly two designs' worth, and both are named on every frame that is not one of them. Space. Reconciled: this is the universal Vertical spacing control, outside every design's list — Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132, 80 at 834, 64 at 390; page margin 72 / 40 / 20; content width 1,296. Two designs carry their own vertical scale: 7 Contrast Band's band at 44 · 64 · 88 (A4·9's) and 10 Slim's at 32 · 44 · 56 (A7·8's, and the one scale in A11 that does not step with width). Head. Eyebrow 13 px uppercase tracked .08em muted, 10 px above the title; title on a 780 measure; sub 17 px muted on 620, 12 px below the title; head to wall 48; note 32 px under the wall on 620; link 20 px under the note. Title ladder: Small 28 · Medium 34 in twelve designs, Large 40 in 6 Split Head and 15 Big Type, Display 48 in 15 Big Type only — unlike A10, the display moment is available here, because the logos are artwork and do not compete for it. Counts and the responsive floor. Per row: Three 416 · Four 306 · Five 240 · Six 196 on a 24 px gutter. Six steps to four at 1080, everything steps to three at 834, and everything steps to two at ≤ 767 — never to one, because a single logo per row on a phone is a column of stamps eight screens long. ⚑ One stated exception: 12 Tiers’ lead tier goes to one across at ≤ 767, since two 40 px marks on a 350 px column is 175 px each and the lead stops leading. At 834 three cells of 234 on 26; at 390 two cells of 163 on 24. Structure. <section aria-labelledby> named by its title, an <h2>; the wall a <ul> of <li>; each logo an <img> with its alt, wrapped in an <a> when a URL is authored. <picture> with a prefers-color-scheme source is used, and only when logoDark exists. Refused: <figure>, a table, a carousel role, an aria-label on the list repeating the title, and a CSS background image for any logo. A section takes no accessible name wherever no title is drawn: 10 Slim always, 2 Caption Row with no title authored, and any design at Head None. The accent is spent in two places and nowhere else: the optional section link's underline and the focus ring on a link or a logo. No logo is ever accent-tinted, in any design, at any treatment. 7 Contrast Band substitutes the band's carried colour for both. With no section link and no logo URLs authored, fourteen of the fifteen contain no accent pixel at all — 9 Rail is the exception, because its two arrows carry the accent as their focus and hover colour whatever else is authored. Empty. No eyebrow, sub, note or link → absent, and the block closes up. No logos → the section does not render; the editor shows the empty repeater and its Add logo control. A logo with no alt cannot be saved — the one field in A11 required beyond the file itself. Fewer logos than the per-row count → one short row, centred. Under three → 1 Row at whatever count fits, and eleven designs name it. Print. Greyscale and Muted print as drawn — paper is where a greyscale wall was always going. 8 Marquee prints as a static wrapped wall of everything it holds; 9 Rail prints the same way, because a printed page has no overflow to scroll. 7 Contrast Band drops its band; the dark plate never prints, since print is the light mode. Content. Orbit Weekly throughout, with twelve invented publications — and twelve more for 13 Dense: Quire, Marginalia, Waypoint, The Gantry, Lantern, Oxbow, Third Estate, Halyard, Cornice, Tally, Weathervane and Ferry & Sons. THE SEVEN AMENDMENTS THE FIFTEEN DRAWN DESIGNS FORCED ON THIS DOCUMENT · ADDED AFTER 15 BIG TYPE
1 · Settlement 3 was restated from “dark mode” to “dark ground”, and 7 Contrast Band is why. It was drafted as a dark-mode fallback; that band reads logoDark in light mode and logo in dark, so the theme's test is whether the ground behind the logo is dark. A site that never turns dark mode on still needs the second file for that band, and the editor's wording differs there alone. 2 · The plate is not always inside the box. It is in nine designs. 5 Cards makes the card itself the plate; 13 Dense drops its padding to 8 at Tiny 22; 12 Tiers and 15 Big Type let it exceed the box at Large 48 and Huge 64, the row growing to fit while every mark keeps the centre line; 14 Inline lets it set the paragraph's leading. 3 · The never-one-across floor has one exception and one counterweight. 12 Tiers' lead tier stacks one mark per row at ≤ 767, because two 40 px marks on a 350 px column is 175 px each. 13 Dense goes the other way and keeps four across at 78 on a 12 px gutter. 4 · The 24 px gutter has two exceptions. 6 Split Head opens it to 25 at Count Three to keep the cells equal inside its 824 block — a whole pixel rather than a fraction across three cells — and 13 Dense tightens it to 12 at ≤ 767. Every other gutter in the category is 24. 5 · Four designs have no cell grid, not one. 8 Marquee, 9 Rail, 10 Slim and 12 Tiers' lead tier all draw logos at natural width on a fixed gap. The arrangement rules above apply to the other eleven. 6 · The short-last-row default is not universal. 1 Row, 3 Grid, 7 Contrast Band and 15 Big Type centre it; 4 Boxed centres at 1440 and left-aligns below, 5 Cards left-aligns at every width because cards have visible edges, 6 Split Head left-aligns inside its block, and 13 Dense defaults to Left because a nearly full last row continues the grid. Each states why on its own frames. 7 · A11 has one forced control value and it is 3 Grid's: at Cells Hairlines the last row is Left whatever Last row says, because a centred cell in a hairline matrix puts a cell wall where no column edge is. Everywhere else in the category a control holds at every width, though six controls stop having an effect below a breakpoint and stay enabled.

Three things to notice, and the first is the whole category. The logos are the only element on these frames that the pack cannot reach — and because they are drawn at Greyscale, the six frames look more alike than any other proof in this library. That is the treatment earning its default: Greyscale is the value that makes twelve packs and two modes one problem instead of twenty-four.
The radius token has almost nowhere to land. A row of contained images on a ground has no edges: Tangerine's 14 and Ink's 6 appear in this category only on 4 Boxed' panel, 5 Cards' cards, 3 Grid's block, the dark plate and a link's hover surface. And no frame here contains an accent pixel — no section link and no logo URLs are authored, so the wall spends the accent nowhere in any pack or mode.
The cells are fixed and the artwork floats inside them, which is why the two Square placeholders sit at 72% in all six frames and the row still reads level. A design that sized a cell to its logo, or set the logos on a baseline, would come apart on the first re-skin — and would come apart faster on the first upload.
STRESS FRAME · THE CATEGORY'S WORST REALISTIC CONTENT · A11·3 GRID AT SIX ACROSS · desktop 1440 · light

Seven logos at six across, and every cap at once

A 26-character eyebrow, a 104-character title at Medium 34, a 178-character sub, and seven logos in 196 px cells — the narrowest cell the category has and the orphan row the settlement warns about. The set includes a 5.2:1 wordmark that has to shrink to its max width, a 1:1 mark at 72%, and one logo whose 60-character alt is longer than the section's own title. A 120-character note and a 20-character link under all of it. Every string is inside its limit, which is what makes this the worst case rather than an impossible one.
