A18 POST LISTS · DESIGN 5 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SEVEN CONTROLS · THE CATEGORY'S GROUPING SETTLEMENT

Grouped

Rows collected under a heading — by month, by year or by tag — with the heading above its group or hung beside it. This is the design that answers §8's third question, and the answer has three parts: what a group can be, where its heading sits, and what happens to a group with one post in it.

The grouping is derived, never authored. Ghost returns posts in an order and the section cuts them where the month changes; the site does not name the groups, does not order them and cannot add one. That is the same principle as the rest of the category — the user owns the query and the design draws whatever comes back.

The heading is the eyebrow, at 600 weight instead of 500. 13 px, uppercase, .08em tracked, text-muted — A1's eyebrow with one weight step, and nothing else. Cut: the heading in the heading font, at 20 px, with a rule under it, and on the accent. All four were drawn; all four made the group heading compete with the post titles, which are the things a reader is looking for.
40 px between groups, 14 px between a heading and its first row. The 2.9:1 ratio is what makes the grouping legible without a rule — a heading belongs to what follows it, and the space has to say so before the words do. ⚑ Mine. Row rules run inside a group and stop at its end, so each group closes itself.
The date drops its month inside a month group. “12 Mar” under a heading reading March 2026 — repeating the month in every row is the commonest fault in a grouped list, and the rule is stated as: a row never repeats what its heading already says. At Group by Tag the date is drawn in full and the row's tag is dropped instead, for the same reason.

A group with one post keeps its heading, and this is the case §8 asks about by name. The alternatives were drawn and refused: merging a one-post group into its neighbour is a lie about the dates, and hiding the heading makes one row in the list unexplained. A month with one post in it is a fact about the site, and a grouped list is the design whose job is to show exactly that. Heading Left is a 200 px column, the same 200 as 1 Rows' meta column — reused rather than re-chosen, so a site switching between the two designs sees the same edge in the same place. It costs the text measure 240 px and buys a page where every group announces itself without interrupting the rows. At Heading Left with one-post groups the page becomes mostly heading column, and the panel says so. Group by Tag orders groups by their newest post, not alphabetically. ⚑ Mine. Alphabetical ordering puts “Data” above “Reporting” for reasons no reader can see; newest-first keeps the list's overall sense of recency, which is the order the query asked for. A post with no tag falls into a final group headed “Everything else” ⚑ — never “Untagged”, which names a state of the CMS rather than the posts.

WHAT THE GROUPING CANNOT DO · STATED AS A LIMIT, NOT DRAWN AS A FEATURE Groups cannot be collapsed — that is accordion, and an accordion of months hides posts behind a click on a page whose job is to list them. Groups cannot be reordered, filtered or renamed. A group heading is not a link, even at Group by Tag where a tag archive exists — A17 made the whole row one destination and a second link inside the list would be the only exception in either category.

THE COUNT LINE DOES MORE WORK HERE THAN IN ANY OTHER DESIGN IN THE LIBRARY “9 posts match. 9 drawn in 2 groups.” Every other section in A17 and A18 reports two numbers; this one reports three, because the group count is the thing the site cannot predict and cannot control. A site sets Show to 25 and gets four groups or fourteen depending on when it published, and the only place that is knowable before publishing is the panel. Show and grouping interact in a way worth stating: the count cuts posts, not groups. Show 10 on a set spanning three months draws ten posts and whatever groups they fall into, and the last group is very often partial. There is no “complete groups only” option — it would make Show mean something different here than everywhere else in the library, for a gain a reader cannot see. Excerpt is not offered on this design at any setting, and that is the one control the panel is missing against 1 Rows. ⚑ Mine, and the reasoning is spatial: an excerpt adds 26 px to every row, which at nine rows is 234 px of extra page, and it pushes the group headings so far apart that the grouping stops being visible at a glance — which is the whole reason to choose this design. A site that wants excerpts under headings has a longer scroll and a weaker structure; it is better served by 1 Rows. Cut: sticky group headings. A heading that pins to the top of the viewport as its group scrolls past is a real pattern and it needs no module — position: sticky is CSS. It is refused because a section is a band on a page, not a scrolling pane, and a heading that leaves its section and floats over the one below it is a page-level behaviour this library does not own. Named here so the architect sees it was considered.
