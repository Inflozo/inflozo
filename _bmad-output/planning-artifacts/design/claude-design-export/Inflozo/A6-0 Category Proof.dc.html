A6 CTA BANNERS · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

What the fifteen inherit, and what they had to settle.

The four things §8 asks A6 to settle, the ladders all fifteen draw, the rules none of them restate, the roster, the shared field list, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame and the consistency pass. Read this before any design file.

A6's frames draw the banner with the footer's top edge beneath it. A5 drew its sections alone on the page ground because a feature set has an ordinary section above and below it. A CTA banner is different: most of them are the last thing before the footer, §8 asks what that looks like, and a frame that left the footer out would be answering the question by omission. Where a design's rule concerns its neighbour more deeply — 13 Overlap's join, 10 Slim's dropped rule — the whole footer is drawn and the caption says so.

1 · THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · ALL FOUR ANSWERS ARE INVENTED AND ALL FOUR ARE FLAGGED

The brief asks four questions. Here are the answers, and the arguments.

SETTLEMENT 1 · BAND, CARD OR PICTURE · AND THE ACCENT BUDGET IN EACH

All three exist, as four grounds, and the accent budget falls as the ground gets louder. The page's own ground and a surface plane spend the accent once, on the button. A photograph also spends it once — the button is the only opaque object over the picture, so its label's contrast is measured against its own fill rather than an unknown photograph, which is the argument for a fill there and against a link. An inverted band spends nothing: an accent fill carrying the band's colour is 3.2:1 in Paper, so the action is a solid text fill carrying the band's own colour at 12.8:1.

Which designs own which. Ground is a control on 1, 2, 3, 9, 10, 11 and 15; it is the design on 4 Card, 5 Contrast Band, 7 Full Bleed Image and 8 Image Split. Where a ground needs its own edges, padding or refusals it gets a design rather than a value — which is why 1's Contrast value and 5 Contrast Band both exist, and the editor names the other at each. 12 Pair is the one exception and spends the accent twice, at Actions Button, because its two asks are alternatives and quieting one would choose for the reader. Its other value spends none. There is no outlined action anywhere in A6, at any value, on any design: an outline on a band is a button that looks disabled, and an outline on a page ground is a secondary pretending to be a primary.
SETTLEMENT 2 · ONE ACTION OR TWO · AND WHAT THE SECOND MAY BE

One toggle, on every design that has actions: Secondary action: On · Off — and the primary action is a constant the group states rather than a switch, canonical definition P0·4a, ruled 31 August 2026. Actions None was never offered anywhere in A6, because a banner with no action is a section head and A5 owns those, so the primary is always drawn and takes no switch. The old two-value list Both · Primary is quoted only to show the mapping: Both is the toggle on, Primary is the toggle off. The secondary is allowed to be two things: A1·1's ghost action at the primary's size, or a text link with a typed arrow. Never a second filled button, never an outline, never a twin.

SETTLEMENT 3 · HOW A BANNER READS IMMEDIATELY ABOVE THE FOOTER

Most banners will sit here, so the rule is the category's rather than each design's. The banner keeps its own bottom padding. The footer's top hairline is the edge between them. No banner in A6 draws a hairline of its own at its foot. And the part that is not the banner's to decide: the theme drops the footer's top padding by one named step when the section above it is an A6 banner — so 96 above and 96 below does not become 192 px of nothing.

SETTLEMENT 4 · INLINE FORM OR LINK-OUT · AND THE HAND-OFF TO GHOST PORTAL

A banner draws a field only where the address is the whole ask: 6 Inline Form and 14 Members, and no others. Everywhere else a signup is a link to #/portal/signup, which opens Ghost's own modal over the page — and the banner draws none of Portal: not its fields, not its states, not its confirmation, and it does not manage, trap or return focus into its dialog.

Why so few. Two forms posting to the same members endpoint gives a site two places to write its subscribe copy and two ways to fail. A banner whose ask is “read the archive” or “join the thread” has somewhere to send a reader, and sending them is cheaper than asking them to type. The form itself is A3·4's, cited and not re-decided — the five shared email* fields, four states at one height, a 1.5 px accent focus border with the library ring suppressed, no red anywhere, a reserved button width, a session-lived done state — at A4·11's 46 px row rather than A3·4's 44, because a form standing in the actions' slot is the height of the actions it replaced. Members off in Ghost: the field is replaced by the button alone, linking to the site's subscribe page; placeholder and note kept; the sidebar states the substitution and links to the setting. A3·4's answer, carried. 14 Members is the only design in A6 that reads Ghost at all, and it reads two facts: signed in or not, paid or not. A paid member sees no section.

THE RULES ALL FIFTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE Structure. <section aria-labelledby> named by its title, an <h2>. The eyebrow is a plain paragraph above it — not part of the heading, not a heading, not aria-hidden. Actions are <a> elements, never buttons: they navigate. The only <button> in the category is 6 Inline Form's submit. Labels. The visible label is the whole accessible name — no aria-label lengthening it — so “Start reading” has to stand alone, and the editor says so where a label reads “Click here” or “Learn more”. DOM order is eyebrow, title, sub, primary, secondary, note, on every design and at every width. Three stated exceptions: 10 Slim puts the note second, 11 Reasons puts its list after the actions even where it sits beside them, and 15 Signature puts the signature before the actions so a reader knows who is asking before they are asked. The note is a paragraph and never aria-describedby the primary: “No card needed” is a fact about the offer, not a description of a link. In 6's form the note's slot is the aria-describedby target and the live region, which is the one place it is both. Motion. 160 ms ease-out, one transition per state change. Hover darkens a fill 6% (3% on a band, where a near-white fill darkening by 6% looks pressed); pressed is 10%. Nothing scales, lifts, slides or gains a shadow anywhere in A6 — A5's rule, carried — and no arrow ever moves. Reduced motion removes transitions and keeps every state. Focus. A 2 px accent ring 2 px outside the button, its inner gap taking the colour of whatever the button sits on. On a band or a picture the ring is the carried colour rather than the accent — 12.8:1 against 3.2:1. Data. Nothing in A6 comes from Ghost except 14 Members' two facts. Every design is offered on every route. Empty: no eyebrow, sub or note → each simply absent; no primary label or URL → the section does not render and the editor names the field, because a CTA banner with no action is not a banner. Behaviours. There are none. No countdown (A2·6's), no rotation (A2·9's), no dismissal (A2's), no sticky (A2·11 Toast's), no accordion (A9's), no comparison table (A7's). A6 is fifteen resting states, which is why every frame in the category is one.

7 · THE CONSISTENCY PASS · WRITTEN AFTER ALL FIFTEEN WERE DRAWN

What was checked, and what it changed

Checked across all fifteen: the title ladder and every refusal of Display 48 · the action's metrics in every frame · the padding scale each design uses · the phone action pair · the focus ring's colour on every ground · the accent count per design · the DOM order and its three exceptions · the footer's treatment in the primary frame · which designs draw a form · every “kept and not drawn” claim against the shared field list · every reference to another category, by number. Six things were wrong and are now right. The action's padding read 13×22 on eleven designs and 12×22 on two, which is now 13×22 everywhere. The note's cap read 90 in the field list and 80 on one spec card. Two designs described the phone secondary as “muted” where the rule is full strength. The eyebrow's tracking was .08em on thirteen frames and .06em on two. 12 Pair's gutter was described as fixed at 32 before the plane-dependent rule was written, and the caption still said so. And the accent budget was stated as “once per banner” in three places before 12 Pair existed — it now reads “once, and twice only on 12 Pair, which says why”. The pattern in them: five of the six are numbers that were right in one place and stale in another, and all five were introduced by a design written before the rule it depends on. The order designs are drawn in is itself a source of defects, which is the argument for a pass like this one rather than for drawing more carefully. Every control list in the written spec was rewritten from its drawn panel as part of this pass. Where the two ever disagree again, the drawn panel is the authority. Not checked, and worth saying: no frame in A6 has been built, so every contrast ratio quoted is computed from the pack's hex values rather than measured in a browser, and the 320 px reflow behaviour of 9 Big Type's title is reasoned rather than observed.

8 · THE DESIGN PATCH PASS · 29 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED ACROSS ALL FIFTEEN

Four rulings, and the promise that was wrong

Every card below names the rule that required it. The rules are named and never numbered. No design was renumbered — this category is 1 to 15, no gap created, none closed, no number reused.

MEMBER BUTTONS ARE CONDITIONAL, AND GHOST’S SIGN-UP POP-UP NEEDS JAVASCRIPT Every one of the fifteen panels gains a Member ask block. Where a banner’s primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask, it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting instead of shipping a button that fails. Where the ask opens Ghost’s own sign-up pop-up, the same block carries the line: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real address to a Portal fragment, so the page stays where it is. Ruled by the owner: an ask the site cannot support does not vanish — the site chooses a fallback label and link and the button keeps its place, its scale and its accent.
THE NO-JAVASCRIPT NOTICE Tested against both live Ghost servers: Ghost’s sign-up address will not accept a plain form submission, so a subscribe form needs JavaScript. The claim that the row “posts natively” is withdrawn from 6 Inline Form and from 14 Members at Signup: Inline form, and a designed notice is drawn on both frames in the form’s place — one line saying what happened, and a link that works. The empty, focus, invalid, submitting and done states are untouched: Ghost’s own script applies those. The other thirteen designs draw no form and lose nothing.
NO DESIGN EVER TURNS INTO ANOTHER DESIGN Twenty-five sentences and frame labels deleted from the seven design frames — 3 Split, 7 Full Bleed Image, 8 Image Split, 11 Reasons, 12 Pair, 13 Overlap and 15 Signature — with nineteen more in the specification’s own design sections, four in its shared sections, and one in this document’s roster above. A missing picture, an emptied list, an unnamed signer, a second ask with no title, a footer the card cannot reach and a row too narrow to hold now all resolve the same way: the section hides what does not apply and stays itself, and the panel advises the design that suits better. Advice, never a switch.
AVATARS WITH NO PHOTOGRAPH 15 Signature only. The initials circle now follows its source: two initials where the site typed the name (“MO”), and one letter where the signer comes from Ghost (“M”), because Ghost cannot produce two initials from a name.
THE SIGNER PICKER, AND THE FEATURE-IMAGE DEFAULT 15 Signature: the Signer source picker offers only staff users who have published a post — Ghost gives a theme nothing for a user with none. If a bound signer’s posts are later unpublished, the section falls back to the authored name and portrait, which are kept, so the signature is never lost silently. 8 Image Split: the feature-image default is offered only on post, page and custom-entry templates; on a list route — home, tag, author — there is no feature image, so the field starts empty and the panel says so.
THE TWO FREE DESIGNS ARE THE OWNER’S CHOICE Shortlisted the five plainest banners, none of which needs the customer to have good photography: 1 Centred, 10 Slim, 2 Flush Left, 9 Big Type and 11 Reasons. Recommended 1 Centred and 10 Slim — the full ask and the one-line ask, which is the whole range a free site needs. Confirmed by the owner on 29 August 2026: 1 Centred · 10 Slim. Badged on the roster above, and carried by the spec’s “[Free] designs:” line.
THE SIX RULES WITH NO SUBJECT IN THIS CATEGORY Slider labels: A6 draws no slider — every control is a named-value row. Gap names: no design in A6 has a gap control; the gutters are fixed or follow the plane.
