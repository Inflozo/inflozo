Timeline

A hairline running vertically down the list with a marker at every post, dates on its left and titles on its right. 4 Dated puts the date first; this connects the dates to each other — one continuous line rather than a column of separate facts, which is the difference between a record and a chronology.

The rail does the job the hairline does everywhere else in A18. There is no rule between rows and no Rule control — the second design in the category with neither, after 8 Row Cards, and for the same reason: one separator per design.

The rail starts at the first marker and ends at the last. A line that ran past either end would imply posts outside the drawn set — which is exactly what a section that draws Show of a larger query must not imply, since the posts above and below are not on this page. It is the one place in A18 where a decorative element carries a factual claim, and it is why the ends are cut.
120 for the date, 20, a 12 px rail, 8, then the text. The marker is an 11 px filled circle in text-muted, centred on a 1 px hairline in border — two different tokens on purpose: a marker at the hairline's value disappears, and a hairline at the marker's value is a stripe.
The marker aligns to the title's cap-height, not to the row's top. Post 4 has no tag, so its title starts 21 px higher and its marker moves with it — the marker labels the post, and the post begins where its title begins. The date's baseline follows the same rule.
Dates are right-aligned into the rail. This is the one place A18 right-aligns a date column, and the reason is the rail: a ragged right edge beside a straight line reads as sloppiness, where in 4 Dated, with nothing to align against, ragged is correct. ⚑ Mine, and it is the sort of decision the two designs make differently on purpose.

At Marker None the design is closest to 4 Dated and the panel does not stop a site getting there — the two designs stay distinct because the rail is still drawn and 4 Dated has none. ⚑ Cut: an accent marker. It is the most obvious use of colour in the design and it is decoration — six accent dots down a page, spent on an element that carries no action and no state. The library spends accent on a hover underline and a focus ring, and this design spends it on neither more. Mine, and the same refusal 11 Numbered made about its numerals. The marker does not change size with Row density. 11 px at every setting: a marker that grew with the spacing would make Spacious look like a different design rather than a roomier one, and the rail's proportions are what a reader recognises.

Date In the meta drops the left column entirely and puts the rail on the page's margin. The text gains 140 px and the design becomes a marked list rather than a dated one — closer to a table of contents than to a chronology, and the better setting inside a narrow column or a two-column page. On the rail is the default and it is what makes the design worth choosing. A date at the left of a marker, at 15 px, aligned right into the line, is a chronology in the shape a reader already knows. It costs 140 px of the row and buys the only thing this design has that 1 Rows does not. Below 767 the two values converge: the column cannot survive a 350 px row, so the date moves into the meta whatever the control says, and the rail moves to the margin. The design's identity on a phone is the rail and the markers alone — which is why Marker None is disclosed in the panel as “not recommended on narrow screens”.

Post content is Ghost’s and is never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking a post title, tag, author name or excerpt says “Edit in Ghost”. This design draws no image at any setting, so rule 10’s Image focus has nothing to attach to ⚑. No button, so rule 11’s optional icon has nothing to attach to ⚑ — the head link is text with an accent underline, and an icon on it would make it look like a button. Member visibility is not offered anywhere in A18 ⚑ — every action here is navigation, and a list that hid itself from readers would hide the site’s writing rather than an offer.
WHAT THE RAIL COSTS THE PANEL, AND WHAT IT SAVES Rule is gone, and Marker takes its slot. That is the same substitution 8 Row Cards makes with Card: one control per design for “what separates one post from the next”, whether the answer is a line, a plane or a rail. Three designs in A18 answer it three ways and none of them offers two answers at once. Author takes the place of Meta, as in 4 Dated, and for the same reason: the date has left the meta line for a column of its own. The two designs share the field — author with values Off · Name · Name and reading time — so a site switching between 4 Dated and 13 Timeline keeps its setting, and one switching from 1 Rows gets the derived default A18-0 specifies. ⚑ Row density here is 20 · 28 · 40, measured between posts rather than around them. A ruled list needs symmetric padding above and below the rule; a railed list has no rule, so the space is a single gap. The fifth design in A18 to keep a control's name and change its quantity — and at this point the pattern is the category's, not the design's. Cut: a “today” or “latest” marker in accent. The newest post is already at the top at Newest first, and marking it would spend accent on restating the order. Cut: connecting the rail to the section's edges — the line stops at the first and last markers, which is a factual decision rather than a stylistic one.
