NAV ITEM · resting → hover → active → focus-visible
Interviews Interviews Essays Archive
Hover moves colour only — text-muted → text. No underline on hover: the underline means “you are here”, and hover must not impersonate it. Focus is a 2 px accent ring at 2 px offset with a 6 px radius, drawn inside the pill so it is never clipped by the capsule edge.
ACTIONS · resting → hover → focus-visible → pressed
Subscribe Subscribe Subscribe Subscribe ⌕
The button darkens the accent one step on hover and two when pressed; the pack supplies both steps, so no design picks a hover colour. On the accent fill the focus ring needs a background-coloured gap first, or the ring disappears into the button. The icon button fills to the hover surface and its glyph goes to text.
DROPDOWN ROW · resting → hover → focus
Field Notes Interviews Reviews
Hover and keyboard focus land on the same row shape, so arrow-keying through the panel looks identical to moving the mouse. Under reduced-motion every one of these still changes — instantly, with no transition.

1Skip link, then the landmark. A visually hidden “Skip to content” is the first focus stop on the page and becomes visible on focus, positioned over the page ground above the pill — not inside it, where the capsule would clip it. The pill is a <header>; its rounded shape carries no meaning and needs no announcement.
2Home link. Mark plus wordmark are one link with one accessible name, the site title — not two adjacent links to the same place. The mark is decorative and hidden from the accessibility tree. Not an h1 except on the home page.
3Nav and dropdown. One nav aria-label="Primary". The active item carries aria-current="page" — the accent underline is not the only signal. “Sections” is a button aria-expanded; Escape closes it and returns focus to the trigger; arrow keys move within the panel and grandchildren are a nested list, not a second flyout.
4Icon-only and contrast. The search button is 38 px visually but 44 px in its hit area, and it carries the label “Search” — an icon alone is not a name. Both the muted nav colour and the accent’s label are checked against the pill’s surface, not the page background, because the pill is what sits behind them.
5Stuck state. Narrowing on scroll does not reorder the DOM, so focus order is identical in both states. Nothing is removed from the tree when the ghost action and the search button drop — they move to the drawer, which stays reachable.
SPEC · A1·5 FLOATING PILL · full text in “A1 Headers — Spec.md”
Content fields Shared A1 list, no additions. Uses logo, siteTitle, navItems (4 shown, 5+ fold to More), children (two levels), signInLabel, ctaLabel/ctaUrl + ctaLabelShort, searchEnabled, darkModeToggle.
Data Ghost primary navigation. Empty nav → the pill shrinks to fit logo + actions and stays centred at Page-gutter width rather than stretching empty. Members off → both actions hidden; with no actions and no search the pill narrows to its content, which is the one case where it stops tracking the gutter.
Responsive 1440–1024 as drawn, inset 20/72. 1023–768 narrows only: inset 16/32, gaps 18; height, search, both actions and the 4-item cap hold. ≤ 767 inset 12/12, height floor 60, nav and Sign in to the drawer, short CTA label in the pill, drawer inherits the pill’s surface and inset.
Empty state No logo → wordmark alone. No CTA → nav gains the room, pill height unchanged. Nothing collapses the pill to less than 240 px wide; below that it takes the wordmark only.
Flagged The full-round outer corner overrides the pack radius token — the only such override in A1. Inner elements still take the token. If you would rather the pill take the token squared off, it becomes a Corner control and the design loses its name.
