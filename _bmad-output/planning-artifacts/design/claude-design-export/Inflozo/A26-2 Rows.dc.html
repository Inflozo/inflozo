A26 POST FOOTERS · DESIGN 2 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SIX CONTROLS + THE UNIVERSAL TRIO

Rows

Every block a full-width row across the content measure, each opened by a 120 px label column in 11 px uppercase and closed by a hairline, so the footer reads as four labelled bands rather than a stack of unrelated furniture.

The label column is what makes this design: 1 Author Bio's blocks are recognised by their shape, and these are recognised by their name. It is the design for a site whose posts carry different amounts of footer — a row with nothing in it does not render, and the rows that remain still line up.

This is the one design that takes the content width by default ⚑ — 1,296 rather than the article's 720 — because a label column beside a 720 measure would push the content off the article's left edge. The rows align to the article's left margin and run past its right, which is the visual signal that the piece has ended.
Tags are drawn as words, not pills, at Labels On ⚑: the row already says "Filed under", so a pill would be a second frame around a label that is already there.
The hairline belongs to the row above it — every row draws its own top border and the last row draws a bottom one, so a footer with two rows has three hairlines and a footer with four has five, and no rule is ever orphaned by a missing block.

Labels Off is the same design without its argument, kept because a site with one-word tags and a one-line bio does not need four labels to explain four rows ⚑. At Labels Off the tag style flips to pills ⚑ — with no label beside them the words need their own edge.
Two authors is one row with two people in it ⚑, not two rows: the label says "Written by" once and would have to say it twice. At three or more the row carries the avatars and the names line, as 1 Author Bio does.
A row with no content is not drawn and its hairline goes with it ⚑ — the failure this design has to avoid is a labelled empty row, which is worse than no row.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESInline on canvas with P0·1: the four row labels — tagsLabel “Filed under”, authorRowLabel “Written by”, shareRowLabel “Share”, readNextLabel “Read next” — plus shareLabel, copiedLabel and authorLinkLabel. Ghost-owned and never inline-editable: post titles, tag names, author names, bios and social handles — clicking one selects the section and the sidebar says “Edit in Ghost” ⚑. P0·3’s item controls land nowhere: A26 authors no array — the tags, the authors and the two neighbours are Ghost’s, and a Ghost-sourced list never shows an Add ⚑. P0·4 and Member Visibility land on 12 Subscribe alone in this category: a tag is navigation, a byline is attribution and a share link is not an offer. P0·5’s “Populate from…” panel lands nowhere — the footer reads the post’s own relations, not a query ⚑. No Preview control was removed because A26 never had one: previewing belongs to the editor, and P0·6’s state switcher is how the absent blocks, the copied confirmation and the member states are seen while editing. No fixed English visitor-facing string ships ⚑: every authored label carries a default, and the strings nobody authors — the destination names, “and others”, the ledger’s long dates — are theme translation-catalog strings. This design is where the category’s label rule lands hardest: the labels are the design.
DATA · THE POST’S OWN RELATIONS, AND NOTHING QUERIEDtags[], authors[], prev_post/next_post, post.url, post.title. {{#prev_post}}/{{#next_post}} are post-context only ⚑ — on a page the pair is simply absent, which is the category’s absent-block rule and not a new one. A row is drawn only when it has content and the hairline count follows. The share destinations are the site setting’s.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLESThe labels are not authored ⚑. "Filed under", "Written by", "Share" and "Read next" are the design's own strings, translated with the theme — four text fields would be four ways to write "Tags" wrong, and the row order is fixed so the labels cannot drift out of sequence.Label column width is not a control: 120 px holds the longest of the four at 11 px uppercase with room for a German translation ⚑, and a narrower column would wrap "Written by" onto two lines.Cut: label alignment, a label case value, per-row padding, a right-aligned value ⚑ — a right-aligned label column beside a left-aligned bio reads as a table, which is 14 Ledger.
RECONCILED IN THIS PASS · WHAT CHANGED IN THIS PANELThe four row labels stop being theme strings and become authored fields with defaults ⚑, editable inline. The hidden <dt> at Labels Off now carries the authored text and falls back to the default — stated because the old accessibility argument rested on the labels being catalog strings.The destinations become one site-wide ordered setting; the glyphs, where the row draws them, are fixed brand icons.The universal trio is outside the list; Row padding keeps its own name because it is the ladder inside a row rather than the section’s outer spacing. Six controls, unchanged.No Author socials row here: the patch named 1, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11 and 15, and this design draws an author row too — not added uninstructed, and recorded in the Reconciliation notes.

The design is five hairlines, and in dark they are the only thing carrying the structure — no shadow, no fill, no second weight. Measured dark: labels 6.8:1, tag words 12.4:1, name 15.1:1, bio 6.8:1, hairline 1.4:1 and decorative.
THE WRITTEN SPEC · ALL TEN FIELDS
1 · Descriptor. Every footer block a full-width row across the content measure, opened by a 120 px uppercase label column and closed by a hairline. The design that names its blocks instead of relying on their shape.2 · Structural descriptor. stack · none · page · few · none · hairline between every block
Count few: the row is the repeating unit and there are two to four of them — the difference from 1 Author Bio, whose unit is the author. Media none: the 48 px avatar sits inside a row and is not the section's media placement.3 · Archetype. stack. One departure: at ≤ 767 the label column becomes a label line above its row ⚑ rather than the columns simply narrowing.4 · Responsive rule. 1440 rows on the 1,296 content width at the 72 margin, label column 120 on a 24 gutter, padding 20/28/40, hairline above each row and below the last, avatar 48, bio clamped at two lines. 834 rows 754, label column held at 120 ⚑, padding unchanged. ≤ 767 rows 350, label above the row at a 12 gap, padding 20, the pair stacked.5 · Content fields. Read: tags[], authors[], prev_post, next_post, post.url, post.title. Authored: shareLabel, copiedLabel, authorLinkLabel. The four row labels are theme strings, not fields ⚑. Unread: subscribeHeading, subscribeLine, tagsLabel, archiveLabel, post.updated_at.6 · Controls, in sidebar order. Blocks (five values) · Width On the measure · Content width — defaulting to Content width ⚑ · Row padding Compact 20 · Comfortable 28 · Spacious 40 · Labels On · Off · Rules Hairlines · None · Share links a number picker, 2 to 4, default 4 ⚑. Six. Cut: label text, label width, label alignment, per-row padding.
7 · Data. Tags: 0 → the row is absent with its hairline ⚑; many → they wrap inside the row and the row grows. Authors: 1 or 2 → one row, two people ⚑; 3+ → avatars and a names line. Prev/next: 1 → one destination on the left of the row, the right half empty rather than stretched ⚑; 0 → no row. A row is drawn only when it has content, and the count of hairlines follows the count of rows.8 · Empty state. An empty row is never drawn ⚑ — the label is not a promise. With no tags, no bio and no next post the footer is one share row and two hairlines; with nothing at all it renders nothing. Editor: a dashed row per absent block, labelled, greyed, not present on the published page.9 · Behaviour module. share. No-JS, quoted: "Share links are real <a href="https://…"> URLs and work normally; only the copy-link button is hidden." Edit-safe. The rows and their labels are static markup and are identical with JavaScript off.10 · Accessibility. <footer> inside the <article>. The rows are a <dl> of <dt>/<dd> pairs ⚑, so a screen reader hears "Filed under, Reporting, The web, Archives" — at Labels Off the <dt> remains and is visually hidden ⚑, which is the whole reason the labels are theme strings. The pair is <nav aria-label="Post navigation">. Measured light: labels 5.4:1, tag words 13.1:1, bio 5.4:1. Print: the rows print with their labels, share and the pair omitted ⚑.Repeating items. Three, all Ghost's — tags, authors, destinations — plus the rows themselves, which are the design's own and are decided by Blocks. No Add, no Remove, no reorder. Selecting the section gives three text fields.Reconciled. The four row labels become authored fields with defaults — “Filed under”, “Written by”, “Share”, “Read next” — editable inline, and the visually hidden <dt> at Labels Off carries the authored text. The destinations move to one site-wide ordered setting with fixed brand glyphs. Six controls plus the universal trio; Row padding keeps its own name as a row-internal ladder. No socials row was added here — the patch named seven designs and this was not one; recorded rather than invented.Flagged ⚑ — the content width as this design's default; labels as theme strings rather than fields; the 120 px column held at 834; the label line at 390; Labels Off flipping tags to pills; two authors in one row; the empty half of a single destination; the hidden <dt> at Labels Off; no row without content; the rows printing.
POST FOOTERS PATCH · 30 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED ON THIS FRAME, AND THE RULE THAT REQUIRED IT
Bylines carry no number“Rosa Menendez and three others” is now “Rosa Menendez and others”. A template cannot subtract the drawn names from the author count, so the number could only ever have been hard-coded or wrong. The row, the avatars and the type are unchanged.
Avatars with no photographThe avatar fallback draws one letter — “Rosa Menendez” shows R. Every person here comes from Ghost, which cannot produce two initials from a display name, so two initials were never available. The circle, its sizes and its colours are unchanged.
Item counts are a number pickerShare links is a stepper, 2 to 4, default 4 — new in this pass, where the row drew Two · Three · Four — drawing the first N of the site’s enabled destinations in the site’s own order. The + is greyed at four with the reason visible: a fifth link takes the row onto a second line.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject in this design — it contains no subscribe and no sign-in form, so there is nothing for a designed notice to replace. The share links are real <a href> URLs and work unscripted; only the copy-link button is hidden.
Member buttons are conditionalNo subject at rest — this design draws no member action. At Blocks Everything the inherited subscribe bar carries the note, and the note is 12 Subscribe’s.
