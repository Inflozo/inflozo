Split Head

The section's head in a 416 px column at the left, the list in 856 px at the right. A8·6's split, carried forward with its geometry unchanged and its content swapped from quotes to posts — the same grid-column move, the same stacking rule at 834, the same refusal to make the head sticky.

It is the only design in A18 whose archetype is not feed, and the difference is where the head goes rather than what the rows do. The rows are 1 Rows' rows in a narrower column: the same 22 px title, the same hairline, the same hung meta at 200. What the split buys is a head that can be larger — title 40 instead of 34 — without pushing the first post below the fold.

The head column is A17's three-up cell width and the list column is the remaining two cells plus their gutter — the same division A17·6 used, so a site running both sections on one page gets one vertical edge rather than two. Inside the list, 620 of text and 200 of meta with a 36 px gap.
The head's title is 40 px, the ladder's Large, and this is the only design in A18 that draws it. A 40 px serif on a 416 px measure is four to five words a line — a shape that only works in a column, which is the argument for the split rather than an ornament on top of it. The note and the link move into the head column, where 1 Rows puts them under the list; they are the head's last two lines and they close the column.
The head does not stick, and that was drawn. A 416 px column of five elements pinned beside a scrolling list is a page pattern, not a section, and at Show 25 it leaves the head hanging in space beside row nineteen. A17·6 refused it, A8·6 refused it, and this design refuses it for the same reason — a section is a band, and its parts scroll together.

Head side is a grid-column assignment and never an order or a DOM change — A8·6's rule, reused verbatim in A17·6 and again here. The head is the section's heading; it comes first in the source at both values, so a screen reader always hears the section's subject before its posts. Head Right is the more useful setting than it looks. Two split sections stacked on a page with the same head side make a hard vertical seam down the middle; alternating them does not. It is a page-composition control that happens to live in a section, which is worth saying plainly since nothing in the panel can see the section above it. ⚑ The head column has no minimum content. At Head None — every head field empty — the design is 856 px of list beside 416 px of nothing, which is a worse 1 Rows. The panel says so under the Design picker rather than switching designs: “This design needs a title. Without one, try Rows.” Mine, and it is the only place in A18 where the picker gives advice about the design itself.
WHAT A 620 PX TEXT COLUMN CHANGES ABOUT THE ROW · drawn at 600 crop
1 ROWS' 820 AGAINST THIS DESIGN'S 620 The 69-character title fits one line at 820 and takes two at 620. That is the whole practical difference between the two designs' rows, and it is why the excerpt's default here is One line rather than Two — a two-line title above two lines of excerpt in a 620 column is a paragraph, and a list of paragraphs is not a list. The meta column stays 200 px. It could have shrunk with the row; it does not, because its width is set by the longest date string rather than by the column it sits in, and that is true at 1440 in every design in A18 that hangs one. Reusing the number keeps the vertical edge in the same place when a site switches designs. Six rows is the count this design is drawn for and ten is its comfortable ceiling. Past that the head column is a short paragraph beside a very long list and the split stops paying for itself — the count line advises 5 or 10 and does not enforce, as everywhere in the category.
WHAT IS REUSED HERE, AND FROM WHERE Split layout — A8·6, via A17·6, geometry unchanged. Section head — A1, with the title at Large 40. The row — A18·1, at a 620 measure. Meta column — A18·1, at its full 200. Focus ring — A6. Nothing in this design is new, which is the point of a design that exists to place two known things beside each other.

THE ONE CONTROL THIS DESIGN COULD HAVE HAD AND DOES NOT A head-width control — 416 or 306 — was drawn and cut. A narrower head gives the list 966 px and makes the row nearly 1 Rows' row, at which point the two designs differ only by where the title sits. A control whose extreme value turns one design into another is a control that should not exist, which is rule 2 applied to a panel rather than to a page. Meta keeps all four values here even though the 620 column is narrower than 1 Rows' 820. The meta column is unchanged at 200, so its content is unchanged too; the narrowing is entirely on the text side. That is a small thing worth stating, because it means switching between the two designs never silently drops a meta part. Head None is legal, unhelpful and disclosed. The section still renders, the list still draws, and the left column is empty — the design does not hand off, because an empty column is a visible mistake a site can fix in one field, where a silent switch to another design is a mystery. A17·11's hand-off rule applies when a design cannot exist; this design can exist, badly.
