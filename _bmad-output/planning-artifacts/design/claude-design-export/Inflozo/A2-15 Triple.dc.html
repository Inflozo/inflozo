1 · Skip link first, ahead of the bar. 2 · <aside aria-label="Announcements"> containing a <ul> of three <li>, in authored order, which is also the visual order left to right and the tab order. Labels are text in the same paragraph as their sentence. 3 · A clipped sentence is a real accessibility problem, not a visual one: the visible text ends in an ellipsis but the element's text is complete, so screen readers get the whole sentence and sighted readers do not. Where a slot clips, its link's accessible name is the full message — and the editor marks clipping per slot rather than hiding it. 4 · With Link style: Whole slot each cell is one link wrapping label, sentence and arrow; with Arrow link only the arrow and sentence are the target. Either way one link per slot, never two. 5 · The close sits outside all three links and last in focus order, on its own ground patch inside the third slot's reserved 24 px. It dismisses all three notices at once — there is no per-slot dismissal, and the sidebar says so, because a band that can lose a column mid-session would re-lay-out under the reader. Contrast and focus · sentence on surface 13.2:1, muted labels 5.4:1, accent label 4.9:1 at 12 px / 600. The focus ring is drawn inside the cell rather than at 2 px offset — the one departure from the library shape, because an offset ring on a flush cell sits on the divider.
