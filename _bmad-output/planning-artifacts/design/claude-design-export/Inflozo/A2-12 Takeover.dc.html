A 200 px band with a picture in it: image on one side, headline, body and a button on the other. The only design in the category that carries imagery, and the only one tall enough to be mistaken for a hero — which is the risk it has to manage rather than the effect it is going for. Used for a launch week, not for a notice.

1 · Skip link first — and it matters most here: this is 200 px of furniture, so the skip link is the difference between reaching the article and reading an advertisement first. 2 · The image is decorative by default with an empty alt — the headline already says what it says. An imageAlt field is offered and, if filled, is used; the editor does not invent alt text and does not use the file name. 3 · Eyebrow, headline and body are all <p> elements. A 26 px headline in the heading font is exactly the case that tempts an h2, and the category floor holds: the bar sits above the page's own h1 and must not enter the outline. 4 · One action, a link styled as a button. The band is not a link and the image is not a link — a 1440 × 200 px target above the header would swallow the page. 5 · Close is a labelled button in the band's top-right, 32 px in a 44 px hit area, 44 px at ≤ 767 where it sits on the image behind an 85% wash. Dismissal removes 200 px in one 160 ms collapse, with the scroll correction the category specifies — this is the design where that rule earns its keep. 6 · Contrast: headline on surface 13.2:1, body 5.4:1, eyebrow 4.85:1. On Background the scrim's 72% stop puts white headline at 9.1:1 and body at 7.4:1 over the darkest region of a mid-tone photograph; the editor cannot measure a photograph, so the scrim values are fixed rather than adaptive and Strong is offered for images that need it.
