Filter

A4·15's field above the accordion, narrowing the list as a reader types. With 7 Index it is one of the two designs that hold twenty-four questions, and the two are the same content answered opposite ways: an index shows a reader the shape of the whole thing; a filter shows them one part of it and hides the rest.

Two things are settled tightly because a filter can lie. It matches the question text and not the answers — a row whose visible words do not contain what a reader typed is a row they will not trust — and the count of matches is announced and drawn while the field has text, which is the one exception to A9-0's refusal of question counts and is flagged as such.

The resting state is the whole list with an empty field, which is the frame a reader who never types sees and the reason the design is honest: nothing is hidden until a reader hides it. The field is A4·15's — 48 px tall, surface, a 1 px border, the pack radius, 16 px text, a 19 px glyph inset 16 — on the same 780 measure as the list, 32 px above the first row. No shadow, no fill on the glyph, no button beside it: pressing Enter does nothing this design does not already do.
The placeholder says “Filter questions” rather than “Search”, because it filters twelve rows on this page and does not search the site. Ghost's own search is A4·15's field, this is not it, and the two should not look like the same promise. ⚑ Mine: the placeholder's wording, the 32 px gap and refusing a submit button.

The count is drawn only while the field has text — “1 of 12 questions”, 13 px muted, 12 px above the first row — and it is also the polite announcement. A9-0 refuses a question count and this is the stated exception: a resting count is a number a reader can see and that goes stale; a filtered count is the only way either a sighted or a screen-reader user knows the list got shorter. ⚑ Mine. The matched term is marked in the question with the hover surface, no accent and no bold — a <mark> taking a plane rather than a colour, because the question is already at 600 and there is nothing louder to go to. ⚑ Mine. It matches the question only, and the panel says so in the field's help line. Matching a closed answer would return rows whose visible words do not contain the term, which reads as a broken filter; for text inside answers the panel advises 3 Open List and a browser's own find. Matching is case- and accent-insensitive, on whole substrings, with no fuzzy matching and no stemming. “cancel” finds “How do I cancel?”, “cancelling” finds nothing, and the no-match state says what to do about it rather than guessing. Filtered-out rows are removed from the flow with no transition at any motion setting. A list that animates on every keystroke is unreadable, so there is nothing for reduced motion to turn off — this design's only behaviour has no animation in it.

The no-match state quotes the reader's term back and offers the section's own link — the authored linkLabel and linkUrl, not a new one invented for the state. With no link authored the second line is absent and the first stands alone. The rows are gone, not dimmed, and the field keeps its text so the reader can edit rather than retype. ⚑ Mine, wording included. Field width Narrow is 420 px centred over the 780 list, for a site whose FAQ is twelve rows rather than twenty-four: a full-width field over a short list looks like the page's search. Full is 780 and matches the list exactly. The clear button is a 44 px target with a 15 px cross, drawn only when the field has text, labelled with the catalog’s “Clear”. Escape clears the field too and keeps focus in it. A reader's text is not remembered, not written to the URL and not read from it. A deep link into a question loads the section unfiltered with an empty field — settlement 2's rule for this design — because a link that arrived pre-filtered would hide the eleven rows the sender did not mean to hide.

Two matches out of twelve, and both of them show the reader their own word. “letter” is marked where it occurs in each question, which is the whole argument for matching the question and not the answer: every row a reader is left with contains the term somewhere they can see it. Ten rows are gone rather than dimmed, and “What do I get with a membership?” is among them even though its answer mentions the letter twice — the cost of the rule, stated at the field.
A filtered row that a reader opens stays open while they keep typing, and closes only when it stops matching and leaves the list. In dark the mark is the dark hover surface at a 1.2:1 step against the ground, the count is muted at 6.8:1, and the field lifts to surface with its hairline — the one input in A9, and the only place the category draws a form control at all.

ACCESSIBILITY · THE ONLY INPUT IN THE CATEGORY A <input type="search"> with a visually hidden <label> from the theme’s translation catalog, default “Filter questions” — the placeholder is not the label, and a placeholder that disappears when a reader types is not a name. No <form> and no submit: there is nothing to post, and Enter is a no-op that does not reload the page. The count is a polite live region — aria-live="polite", debounced 500 ms so a reader typing “membership” hears one announcement rather than ten. It is the same element sighted readers see, not a second hidden one. The no-match line announces through the same region. Filtered-out rows are removed from the DOM, not hidden — the one place in A9 where content leaves the tree — because a reader who filtered asked for a shorter list, and eleven hidden rows would still be counted by some assistive technology. Clearing the field restores every row in authored order. ⚑ Mine. The mark is a <mark> with a plane and no colour change, so it survives forced colours as the system's own mark styling and needs no contrast ratio of its own. The question's text stays text at 600 either way. Tab order: the field, its clear button when there is text, then one stop per visible row. Focus is never moved for a reader — typing does not jump focus to the first match, and clearing keeps focus in the field. Contrast: field text 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, placeholder 5.6:1 / 6.8:1, glyph 5.6:1 / 15.1:1 when the field has text, count 5.6:1 / 6.8:1, focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1, field hairline 1.3:1. Print: the field is not printed and every row prints open, unfiltered, because a printed filter is a box a reader cannot type in. ⚑ Mine.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Questions and answers edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The answer raises the marks toolbar on selection with bold · italic · link · inline code and no underline — an underline inside prose is a link that is not one. Recorded as a PRD delta. Nothing in A9 is Ghost-owned, so nothing here says “Edit in Ghost” — every word on the page is the site’s own. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Questions are the P0·3 item list: Add question arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. The field’s placeholder and the no-match line are fields now, not theme literals: filterPlaceholder defaults to “Filter questions” and noMatch to “No question here mentions “{term}”.”, with {term} filled at render — a site can say it in its own language, and the default says what the field does. The input’s visually hidden label and the Clear button’s name stay catalog strings: they are the theme’s to translate, not the section’s to type.
