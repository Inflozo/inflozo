Overlap

A wide picture with a surface card sitting over its lower corner, 64 px into the space below it. The text is on a card and not on the photograph, so there is no scrim, no contrast measurement and no pale-image problem — the overlap does what 2 Full Bleed's scrim does, structurally instead of chemically.

A4·18 Overlap Card and A6·13 Overlap established the object; A19 uses it verbatim and changes only what is inside it. Like 2 Full Bleed and 4 Poster it draws Ground when the post has no picture — the picture's box becomes a flat field of the contrast colour and the card overlaps it exactly as always ⚑ changed in the design patch pass; the hand-off to 3 Card is deleted, and the panel advises it instead.

The overlap is 64 px and it is not adjustable. ⚑ Mine, and the reason is that every other value fails at some card height: less than 48 reads as a misalignment, more than 96 puts the card's title over the picture's subject. 64 is a quarter of the card's own height at the default settings and holds at every one of them, because the card grows downward from a fixed top edge.
The card is 560 wide, inset 48 from the picture's left edge, and carries A19's card values unchanged — surface fill, pack radius, md shadow, 32 px of padding. Its title is the spotlight's 34 rather than 3 Card's, and its excerpt clamps one line shorter than the control says at Two and Three lines, because a card that grows past 300 px stops overlapping and starts hanging.
The picture's lower 64 px is behind the card and is treated as lost: the crop is anchored to the top so nothing important sits in the band the card covers, and at Card position Centred the anchor stays at the top rather than following the card. No scrim, no tint, no border between the card and the picture — the shadow is the separation, which is the whole argument for the design.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES A disabled value with its reason, and this time the reason is geometry rather than contrast. Title Large is greyed: 40 px in a 560 px card is a three-line title on every post in the roster, and the card grows past the picture. Rule 4 says a value that fails is disabled with its measurement shown; the measurement here is the card's height against the picture's, and the panel states it in words rather than as a ratio. The overlap itself is not a control and that is the design's main refusal. Every value other than 64 fails somewhere, and a control whose values are mostly wrong is worse than no control — the user would have to test it against their own longest title to find out which ones work. Cut: a card fill control. The card is surface at both Image width values; an outlined card over a photograph is a rectangle with a hole in it. Cut: a second overlapping element — a tag chip, a date plate, an author card. One overlap is a composition; two is a collage.
