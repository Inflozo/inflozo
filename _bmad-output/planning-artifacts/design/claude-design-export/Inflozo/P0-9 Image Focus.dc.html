P0·9 · Image focus

Where a design crops a photograph into a frame of its own shape, Image focus says which part of the photograph survives the crop. It carries both axes: Centre · Top · Bottom and Centre · Left · Right. Centre and Centre are the defaults, which is the crop a design draws when nobody touches the control.

One control name means one set of values. A category places it, names the slot it applies to, and adds nothing — no fourth value, no renamed axis, no per-category vocabulary.

GHOST NEVER SEES THIS · IT IS A HINT THE COMPILER RESOLVES, WHICH IS WHY IT IS A CONTROL AND NOT A DATA BINDING
The user picks a value in the panel. The compiler resolves it into the crop the theme ships with — the position the image is anchored at inside its frame. Nothing about the choice reaches Ghost, and Ghost sends nothing back about it. That is the whole reason it is a control rather than a field: a data binding is something Ghost stores and the theme reads. Image focus is stored by the project, resolved at compile, and invisible on the far side. A category that describes it as Ghost data is describing something that does not exist.
It follows that focus survives a design switch, needs no server, and declares no behaviour module. There is nothing to run and nothing to fail with JavaScript off.

Focus is the one image decision a design cannot make for the user. A design owns the shape of its frame; only the person who chose the photograph knows what is in it. Two axes, six named values, one definition — and every category that draws an image now points here instead of writing its own.
