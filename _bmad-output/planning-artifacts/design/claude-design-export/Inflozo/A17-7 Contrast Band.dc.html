Contrast Band

1 Three Up's arrangement moved onto the contrast token, full-bleed across the viewport, at its own vertical scale. The ground is the entire design — same cells, same card, same gaps, one different token — which is why A17-0 uses it to make the point that ground is a slot in the tuple.

Everything interesting about it is a derivation problem. The pack supplies contrast and its text colour and nothing else — so the muted text, the hairline, the tag plate, the focus ring and the accent all have to be derived on that band, in twelve packs, in both modes. That is 5 derived values × 12 packs × 2 modes, and none of them is given. Four of the five derive cleanly as a step from the band. The accent does not, and that is this design's finding.

It also prints without its band. A band of ink across a sheet of paper is not a design decision, it is a cartridge decision, so print drops the band and the section reverts to the page ground — the one design in A17 whose printed form is a different design.

The band is full-bleed and the content is not. The contrast ground runs the whole viewport width; the cells stay on the 1,296 measure inside it, at the same 72 px page margin as every other design. A band that stopped at the content width would be a very wide card, which is 17 Panel. ⚑ Mine.
Its vertical scale is its own: 64 · 88 · 120 against the category's 64 · 96 · 132. A band announces its own edges, so it needs less air inside them to feel separate from what is above and below — 96 px inside a band reads as 120 px on a page. Comfortable 88 is drawn here. ⚑ Mine, and one of only two designs in A17 with a bespoke padding ladder.
Five values on this frame are derived rather than given, and each is one step from the band rather than an absolute: muted #A8A29A, the tag plate #332E27, the placeholder stripe, the link's accent lifted to #E8834F, and the focus ring the same. The photographs are untouched — no scrim, no border, no fade — because an image on a dark band is already framed by it.

The derivation rule, stated once: every value is a mix of the band toward its own text colour, and all three move in that one direction. Muted 60% · hairline 10% · tag plate 7% — as the build would write them, color-mix(in srgb, var(--contrast-text) 60%, var(--contrast)) and so on. On Paper's band that yields the three swatches drawn beside this panel: #A8A29A, #3A342B, #332E27. Nothing is ever mixed away from the text — a plate darker than its own band disappears into it, which is why A17-0's card rule says one step toward the text and this design follows it rather than inventing a second direction. Written as percentages of a pack's own two given colours, they survive all twelve packs, which is the only way this design can exist under rule 1. ⚑ The three percentages are mine, read off the drawn values rather than chosen first. The accent is the one value that cannot be derived by a step, and it is the finding this design exists to surface. A pack's accent is chosen against its page ground, and nothing guarantees it survives on contrast. Paper happens to clear it: #D96C3F measures 4.77:1 on #232019, just over AA's 4.5:1 for text — so no lift is required here, and the lift to #E8834F drawn on this frame buys headroom to 6.03:1 rather than fixing a failure. The risk is the eleven packs nobody has measured. Paper clears by 0.27, which is a margin thin enough that a cooler or paler accent will not, and A17 has no token to say what the accent becomes on a band. The architect needs an accent-on-contrast value in the pack definition — one value per pack, checked once — rather than eighteen section specs each deriving a lift by eye. ⚑ Mine, and the finding this design was drawn to raise. Three packs drawn, and only the radius and the heading face move — the band, the derived muted, the stripe and the plate all follow their pack's own contrast. Ink's band is nearly its dark-mode page, which is correct and looks odd: a cool pack's contrast band and its dark mode are almost the same colour, so this design is nearly invisible as a device there. Disclosed, not solved.

Band Inset keeps the ground and drops the bleed, which is the setting for a section between two other sections on the same page — full bleed twice in a row makes a stripe pattern. Inset is not 17 Panel: the ground is contrast rather than surface, there is no hairline, and the tuple's containment stays none — a band with a radius is still a band. ⚑ Mine, and the distinction most likely to be argued with. Print drops the band and reverts every derived value to its page-ground original; the grid then prints on white at the same cells, the same measure and the same rules ⚑ 29 August 2026. A17-0 states it; this frame draws it. The alternative — printing the band — costs a full ink coverage on every sheet and renders the derived muted as grey mud on most home printers. Which means this design has two forms and one spec. Field 4 carries the print reversion as a stated departure rather than a footnote, because a developer who misses it ships a section that looks broken on paper.

In dark mode the band goes light. contrast means “the inverted band colour”, and on a #171511 page the inversion of the ground is a pale one — #F7F4EE here, one step off Paper's light page. Every derived value flips direction with it: muted goes dark, the plate darkens instead of lifting, and the accent returns to its light-mode #D96C3F because it is now on a pale ground again.
This is the design that most tests “re-tuned, never inverted”, and it passes on a technicality: the band is not inverted by dark mode, it was already the inversion. A dark-mode band that deepened to near-black would be invisible against the page — the same failure Ink's light mode has, arriving from the other direction. ⚑ The whole of this frame's palette is mine, and it is the single most likely place in A17 for a pack author to disagree with me.
The consequence worth stating plainly: this design's two modes look like each other's opposites, and that is correct. A reader switching to dark on a page containing this section sees the rest of the page darken and this band lighten.
