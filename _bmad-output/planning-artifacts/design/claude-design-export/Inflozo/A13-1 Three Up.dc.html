Three Up

Three to six steps in equal cells, three or four across, each a numeral above a title above a paragraph. The design a site gets when it types “how it works”, the one A13-0's step card was drawn for, and the design five others hand off to.

All four settlements are on the primary frame at once: six numerals rendered from position rather than typed, an 80-character step title running to three lines without pushing its neighbours, a step with no body drawn as a numeral and a title alone, and no picture of any kind at any setting. It reads neither icon nor image — a picture above the numeral is 12 Media Top, and a picture beside it is 7 Alternating Media.

How it works

How a pitch becomes a Thursday letter

Six steps, about three weeks from the first email to the send.

  1. 01Send the pitchTwo paragraphs on what happened, why it matters now, and who you would talk to. No attachments.
  2. 02Two editors read itEvery pitch is read twice in the Monday meeting. You hear back either way within eight days.
  3. 03Agree the shape, the length, the deadline and the fee before any reporting startsA commissioning editor writes it down and sends it to you. Nothing is agreed on a call alone.
  4. 04Report and fileDraft into the shared document. Your editor comments in the margin rather than rewriting.
  5. 05Edit and checkA line edit, then a check against your sources. We may come back to you with questions.
  6. 06It goes out

Commissioning rates and the current calendar are on the contributors page.

ACCESSIBILITY · SIX STEPS, ONE TAB STOP, ONE HEADING <section aria-labelledby> named by the title, an <h2>; the steps an <ol> of six <li>; each step a <p> for the title and a <p> for the body. An <ol> and not a <ul> — this is the first ordered list in the library and the whole category uses one, because the order is the content. A reader hears “list, 6 items, item 1 of 6” without the theme generating a single string. A step title is never an <h3>: six headings under one <h2> would put a pitch process into the page's outline. Refused: <dl>, a table, role="list" on a <div>, and aria-level anywhere. ⚑ Mine. The numeral is a <span aria-hidden="true">, because the <ol> already says “1 of 6”. Announcing “01” as well would say the same thing twice, and it would go wrong the moment Numerals is None. The list markers themselves are list-style:none, which in Safari removes the list semantics — so the <ol> carries role="list" as the one ARIA attribute in the design. ⚑ Mine, and it is a build note rather than a design decision. Tab stops: one, the section link, or none where no link is authored. There is no per-step link in A13, no cell target, no “learn more” per step and no skip link. This is the least interactive section in the library so far and the frame is the whole design. Reading order is numeral, title, body — DOM order, in all fifteen designs, so switching designs never changes what a reader hears. At head Centred the order does not change: centring is text-align on the head block and nothing else, with no order property anywhere in A13. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, step title the same, body and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, numeral 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 — checked even though it is decorative, because a sighted reader is using it to follow the sequence. Rule and hairline 1.3:1 as non-text dividers. Link underline and focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. Reduced motion changes nothing, because nothing moves. The only transition in the design is the 160 ms underline on the section link. At 400% zoom the grid is one across, the 80-character title wraps to five lines, nothing overlaps and nothing scrolls sideways. Forced colours keep the rule and lose nothing else, since there is no fill anywhere in the design.
