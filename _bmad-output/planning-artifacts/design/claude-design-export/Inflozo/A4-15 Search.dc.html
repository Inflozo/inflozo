Search

One field where the actions would be, for an archive whose front door is a query rather than an argument. It is the hero for a reference site — documentation, a back catalogue, a long-running column — and its whole discipline is knowing where it stops: it never renders a result. A23 Search owns everything after the return key.

The field takes the actions' slot — same 32 px above, same 14 px to the note — and is 52 px rather than the form row's 46, the one measurement this design does not inherit: a search field is the page's primary action and has to look like more than an input, and 52 px is where it stops reading as a form field on a 720 px measure. The glyph is drawn in CSS at 17 px, sits 16 px in — 14 px at ≤ 767, where the page margin is 20 — and is aria-hidden — a magnifier is a convention, and the field's name comes from its label. There is no button: a search field with a Go button beside it is a field that does not trust the return key. Flagged as mine: the 52 px height, the CSS glyph, and the absent button.

Four states and none of them shows a result. On submit the field is a real GET to the site's search route and the browser navigates; where the theme uses Ghost's own search overlay instead, the field is the trigger that opens it and A23 Search owns the overlay, its results, its empty state and its keyboard model. Either way the hero's job ends at the query. Flagged as mine: the clear control's 44 px box, the submitted state's ground change, and the refusal of a suggestions dropdown — which would be A23 rendered inside a hero.

Nothing is pulled from Ghost — not a post count, not a tag list, not a suggestion. The note's “48 issues” is typed by the site owner like every other number in the category, and the editor's helper text says the theme does not count anything. Where search is unavailable — a theme built without Ghost's search, or a site that has disabled it — the design is not offered in the picker and the editor names the reason. This is the design most likely to be a page's hero rather than a home page's — a documentation index, an archive landing page — which is why its note and sub are both optional.

1 · Headline is the page's h1, eyebrow a <p>, section unlabelled — the shared floor. 2 · A real <form role="search"> — the category's only search landmark, and one of its two form landmarks alongside A4·11's labelled subscribe form. It is warranted here: a reader who lands here to look something up should find it in the landmark list. Inside it, <input type="search"> with a visually-hidden <label>Search Orbit Weekly</label>, enterkeyhint="search", autocomplete="off" and no autofocus — stealing focus on load moves a reader who came to read. The glyph is aria-hidden CSS; the clear control is a 44 px <button> named “Clear search” that returns focus to the field. 3 · The note is plain muted text, not the field's description: the field's name is its label, and a description that counts the archive is not something a screen-reader user needs read out before every keystroke. Where it stops · no results, no dropdown, no aria-live region, no combobox pattern. A hero that half-implemented a combobox would be a worse combobox than A23's and would own a keyboard model it cannot see the results of. Submitting navigates, or hands off to A23's overlay, and A23 announces the result count in its own region. Contrast · placeholder 5.1:1 light / 5.9:1 dark · glyph 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 rising to 12.1:1 on focus · typed text 12.1:1 / 11.8:1 · note 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · field border 1.4:1 and decorative, since the field's fill always differs from the ground. In dark, the field's darkened ground against the page is 1.3:1 — decorative, and the border is what draws the box.
