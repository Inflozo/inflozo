A18 POST LISTS · DESIGN 4 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SEVEN CONTROLS · THE DATE LEADS

Dated

A fixed 160 px date column at the left, every row hung from it. The date is the first thing in the row rather than the last, which changes what the list is: 1 Rows is a list of posts that happen to have dates, and this is a record of what was published when.

It is the closest design in A18 to grouping without grouping. A column of aligned dates does most of what a month heading does — it makes the shape of the publishing year visible — and it does it without a heading, a rule or a gap. 5 Grouped is the design that adds the heading, and the two are one click apart in the picker.

160 + 40 + 820 = 1,020, and the remaining 276 px of the content width is left empty at the right. That is deliberate and it is the design's one real cost: 1 Rows spends that space on a meta column and this design spends it on nothing, because a date at the left and a name at the right would put a reader's eye at both ends of a 1,296 px row for one post. The author's name moves under the excerpt instead, at 13 px, where it is a footnote to the post rather than a second column.
160 px holds “26 February 2026” at 15 px with 14 px to spare, which is the longest date English produces in this format. The date is 15 px rather than the meta row's 13 — one step up, because a column head-height object at 13 px reads as a caption and this is the row's first fact. ⚑ Mine.
Dates are not right-aligned to the column's inner edge, and that was drawn both ways. Ragged right against a flush left column reads as a list of dates; flush right reads as a table of figures, which is A17·10 Ledger's territory. Left-aligned keeps the two designs apart.

The column's width is a consequence of the format, not a separate control. Full 160 · Short 112 · Day and month 96, each measured against the longest string that format can produce in English. The text column takes back whatever the date column gives up, so Day and month is also the widest measure this design offers. ⚑ Day and month drops the year from the drawing and keeps it in the datetime attribute, which is the same arrangement A17 used for abbreviated dates. It is offered because a section showing this spring's posts repeats “2026” seven times for no information; it is not the default, because a list that outlives its year becomes ambiguous and nothing warns the site. Mine, and the risk is real enough to state in the panel: “Best for lists inside one year.” Localisation is the pack's problem and not this design's — a German date is longer than an English one and the column would need 190 px. Specified as a token-relative width rather than a fixed one: the column is the width of the longest rendered date in the set plus 24 px, floored at 96. That keeps the alignment in any locale and is the one measurement in A18 that is computed rather than named. ⚑ Flagged for the architect.

A date column is worth its 200 px when the dates are close together. Seven dates inside one month read as a publishing rhythm; seven dates spread over five years read as seven unrelated numbers, and the column is then 200 px spent on making that obvious. Nothing in the panel detects this and nothing should: a sparse archive is a fact about the site, and a section that quietly rearranged itself around it would be worse. The design depends on the list being in date order, and it always is. The Data group offers Newest first and Oldest first and nothing else — both are date orders, so a date column can never be out of sequence. That is the reason A17 refused shuffle paying off in a design nobody had drawn yet. Oldest first is the setting this design was drawn for and the panel says so — a record of what was published reads forwards. It is not forced: a site that wants newest first gets a perfectly good reverse chronology, and forcing an order would overrule a query the site owns.

WHY THIS PANEL HAS AN AUTHOR CONTROL WHERE EVERY OTHER DESIGN HAS A META CONTROL Meta cannot exist here, because the date has left the meta row. A17's meta is one string — name, date, reading time — and this design has taken the date out of it and put it in a column. What is left is a name and a reading time, so the control is called Author and has three values instead of four. The parts are the same parts and the strings are unchanged; only the grouping differs. ⚑ That is a genuine schema question and it goes to the architect. A site switching from 1 Rows at “Name and date” to this design at “Name” has lost nothing on screen — the date is still drawn, in the column — but the two designs write different fields, and switching back cannot know which of the four meta values to restore. Specified as: meta is preserved untouched and author is derived from it on first switch — Name and date → Name, Name date and reading time → Name and reading time. Mine, and the only derived default in A18. Cut: a date column side control. A date at the right of a row is 1 Rows' meta column, which already exists and is already the default there. Cut: a “group by month” toggle — that is 5 Grouped, and it is a structural claim rather than a setting; the picker's note on this design points at it in as many words.
