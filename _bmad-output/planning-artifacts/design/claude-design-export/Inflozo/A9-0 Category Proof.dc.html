A9 FAQ · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

Nine of the fourteen hide their own content until a reader asks.

A8's problem was that nothing came from Ghost. A9 reads nothing from Ghost either — a FAQ is authored in the section, in a repeater, exactly as a testimonial was — so the field list is settled quickly and the category's real problem is somewhere else. It is the first category in the library where the resting state of most designs withholds the thing the section is for. Nine designs draw closed rows. Five draw everything. Both are correct, and which one a site should pick is a decision the fourteen have to make legible.

The second new problem is the answer. A9 introduces the library's first rich-text field — an answer holds paragraphs, lists, links and inline code — which means every design has to hold formatted prose at a stated measure, and the field has to refuse everything that would turn a FAQ row into a documentation page. C Post Body owns full rich text; this is a deliberately short list of what an answer may contain.

This file carries the four settlements §8 asks A9 to make, the question ladder, the row component, the rules all fourteen share, the roster, the shared field list, and — added after the fifteen were drawn — the tokenisation proof, the stress frame and the consistency pass. Read it before the designs. Where a design's own panel and this file disagree, the drawn panel is the authority and the disagreement is a defect to be logged.

SETTLEMENT 1 · ACCORDION OR ALWAYS OPEN · AND HOW MANY ROWS MAY BE OPEN

Both exist, and they are designs rather than two values of one control. Nine of the fourteen are accordions and five are always open, and a site switching between them keeps every field. That division follows §3.2: an always-open FAQ and an accordion are two arrangements, not one arrangement with a switch, and a control that turned eight closed rows into eight open ones would silently rebuild the section under the site.

More than one row may be open, and opening one never closes another. There is no one-at-a-time value anywhere in A9. A reader holding two answers side by side is the ordinary case — a price beside a cancellation rule — and a section that closed the row they had just finished reading would be deciding on their behalf that they were done with it. Open on load has two values, All closed and First open, and there is no All open: that is 3 Open List, which is a design a site chooses.

SETTLEMENT 2 · DEEP-LINKING ONE QUESTION · AND WHAT IT DOES TO THE CLOSED ROWS

Every row has an anchor in every design, including the always-open five. The anchor is a stored field, anchor, seeded from the question the first time the item is saved and editable afterwards — stored rather than derived at render, so that editing a question does not break a link somebody has already sent. A derived slug is one rewritten headline away from a dead link, and a support reply is exactly the place these links get pasted.

Arriving at the anchor opens that row and leaves every other row exactly as the design would have drawn it. The closed rows stay closed. The row's question scrolls into view with 96 px of margin above it, focus moves to that row's button so a keyboard reader continues from the answer rather than the top of the page, and the row is open on arrival without the 160 ms animation — there is no closed state to animate from. No highlight, no flash, no pulse, no accent tint on the arrived row.

SETTLEMENT 3 · LISTS, LINKS AND CODE INSIDE AN ANSWER · AND THE MEASURE THEY SIT ON

The answer is limited rich text, 20 to 900 characters. Allowed: paragraphs, one level of unordered or ordered list, inline links, inline code, bold and italic. Refused: headings, images, embeds, tables, blockquotes, multi-line code blocks, buttons and nested lists. An answer that needs a heading is a page with sections, an answer that needs a table is a comparison, and an answer that needs a screenshot is documentation — all three are a link away, and C Post Body is where full rich text lives. The 900-character ceiling is hard; past it the editor names the post body and suggests a link.

The measure is 620 px at every width above 767, whatever the design's row is: 780 in 1 Accordion, 824 in 5 Split Head, 632 in a column of 2 Two Column — the question takes the row's full width and the answer is capped at 620 and sits at the row's left edge. 620 px of 16 px body is about 78 characters, and a FAQ answer read at 100 is a paragraph a reader loses their place in. Answer type: 16 px on 1.65 in text, not text-muted — an answer is reading matter rather than meta, which is where A9 parts from A8's muted role lines. Paragraphs 12 px apart. THE RULES ALL FOURTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE
Structure. <section aria-labelledby> named by its title, an <h2>; the set a <ul> of <li>. Every question is an <h3>, in all fourteen designs — the exact opposite of A8, which had not one <h3> in the category. A reader using a heading rotor gets the whole question list in one gesture, and that is the largest single accessibility win a FAQ section can offer. In the nine accordions the <h3> holds a full-width <button aria-expanded aria-controls> and the answer is a <div role="region"> labelled by that button; in the five always-open designs there is no button and no region, because a region nobody can toggle is a landmark for nothing. Three structures were considered and refused. <dl><dt><dd>: a question is not a term and a three-paragraph answer is not its definition, and the questions stop being headings. <details><summary>: it cannot carry a heading cleanly, its marker and its open state cannot be styled to the same rule across twelve packs, and its one real advantage — a browser finding text inside a closed row — is a cost we take instead and state. role="tablist": an accordion is not a tab set, and A5·12 owns tabs. ⚑ All three refusals mine. The closed answers are in the DOM with the hidden attribute — not removed, not lazily fetched — so nothing is missing from the page's source. They are still not on the page, so a reader scanning does not see them and a browser's find does not match them. Every accordion's panel states that and names 3 Open List for a site that wants every answer findable. ⚑ Mine, following A8·11's disclosure. No FAQPage schema in any of the fourteen. The section cannot know what the page is: three FAQ sections on one page would emit three graphs, and a marketing page with a FAQ band on it is not a FAQ page. A site that wants the markup adds it once, at the page level, in code injection — which is also the only place it can be written truthfully. ⚑ Mine. Motion: one behaviour, in nine designs. The answer's height animates 160 ms ease-out and the marker rotates in the same 160 ms, one transition per state change. Under reduced motion both are instant and the row still opens — the arrangement, the thresholds and the open state are unchanged. Nothing runs on a timer, nothing opens on scroll, nothing runs while editing: every frame in A9 is a resting state. 11 Tabs' tab row is the category's one other reader-driven behaviour ⚑ amended 31 August 2026, when 12 Filter was cut. Focus is A6's ring verbatim: 2 px accent, 2 px outside, the inner gap taking the ground it sits on, the carried colour on a band. The ring hugs the whole row, not the question's text and not the marker, because the whole row is the button. Forced colours keep every hairline, keep the marker in the system's text colour, and drop the band; 8 Contrast Band's rows keep their hairlines. Print: every row prints open, in every design, and the marker is not printed. A printed FAQ with eight closed rows is eight questions and no answers. ⚑ Mine. Section padding Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132, 80 at 834, 64 at 390; page margin 72 / 40 / 20; content width 1,296. Two designs carry their own scale: 8 Contrast Band's band at 44 · 64 · 88 (A4·9's) and 13 Slim's at 32 · 44 · 56 (A7·8's). Responsive floor. A one-column list of rows needs no collapse at any width, which is why ten of the fourteen change nothing but their type and their padding. What collapses is a second axis: 2 Two Column's two lists stack at ≤ 767, 4 Cards takes two columns at 1080 and one at 767, 5 Split Head puts its head above the rows at 1080, 7 Index moves its jump list above the rows at 1080, 10 Image Split stacks image over rows at 1080, 15 Ledger puts its answer under its question at 1080. No groups → the designs that read them draw one unlabelled set, or hide the container that had nothing to group. SEVEN STATED REFUSALS No Expand all, no Collapse all. Settlement 1's reason: that is 3 Open List, chosen once by the site. No question count, and no per-group count. “12 questions” above a list of twelve questions is a number a reader can see, and it goes stale the moment a row is added. No “Was this helpful?”, no votes, no thumbs. Ghost stores no answer to that question, so the control would post nowhere or to somebody else's analytics. No icon per question. Fifteen questions with fifteen glyphs beside them is a legend a reader has to learn; the marker is the only glyph in a row. No chat launcher, no “ask AI”, no search-the-site field. 12 Filter was cut on 31 August 2026, and it held the category’s only input, so no design in A9 draws a field of any kind and there is no form control left in it. No accordion inside an accordion, and no answer that opens a second answer. 11 Tabs holds always-open rows for the same reason: a disclosure inside a disclosure is two doors to one sentence. No “popular”, “new” or “important” flag on a row. Order is the site's statement of priority, and 9 Numbered is the design that makes order visible. ⚑ All seven mine.

WHAT HOLDS The 120-character question wraps to two lines and the marker stays on the first. It is aligned to the question's first line, not centred against the block, so a two-line and a one-line row have their markers on the same vertical relative to the text — which is what stops a long question from looking like a different component. The 900-character answer is 620 px wide and about fourteen lines, and nothing is truncated, clamped or faded. The row grows, the rows below it move down, and the section is 300 px taller than it would have been. The list keeps its 20 px indent and 8 px item spacing at this length, and the code chip wraps as a word. The 20-character answer under a two-line question is honest rather than embarrassing. “No — just the email.” is 19 characters and takes one line; the row is 100 px tall because its question is two lines and its answer is one. No row is stretched to match another — there is no equal-height mechanism anywhere in a one-column list. Display 48 over a 20 px question is the widest type gap in the category and the head still reads as the head, because the title is the only thing on the page in the heading font. The note is two lines on a 620 measure and the link label ends in an arrow the site typed. Both are inside their caps, and the arrow is a character in linkLabel rather than anything the theme adds.
WHAT THE FRAME COSTS, STATED The 900-character answer is the weakest thing in the category. It is legible, it holds its measure and it is still a wall of text inside a row a reader opened expecting a sentence. The editor's counter turns muted past 600 characters and names C Post Body — a page with a heading structure — and there is no design in A9 that makes this answer better, only designs that make it worse: 4 Cards would set the row's whole grid by it and 13 Slim refuses it outright. ⚑ The 600-character advice is mine. This frame is why the answer's measure is a cap and not a fraction. At 780 px the same answer would be 95 characters a line and unreadable; the 620 cap is doing more work here than in any other frame in the category. Two rows open at once, at these lengths, is a 1,400 px section — and A9 allows it, because closing one to open the other would be the theme deciding a reader is finished. The scroll is the cost of the settlement, and it is the right cost. One thing changed after drawing this frame: the question's marker was centred against the row in the first pass, which put it between the two lines of a long question and looked like a bullet. It is now aligned to the question's first line at every row height, and the change is in the row component in this file. ⚑ Mine.

Seven amendments, and the rules that survived contact with the drawings

This file was written before the designs. Where a drawn panel disagreed with it, the panel is the authority and the disagreement is a defect — seven were found, all seven are amended above, and every amendment is logged here. Nothing in the fifteen was changed to match this file.
