7 · Carousel

One photograph at a time on a snapping track, the frames either side showing at the margins so the reader can see there is more. Dots and a counter beneath, arrows beside the head.

It is the gallery for a set that is too big for a grid or too slow to scroll — twenty frames on a page that has other things to say. It is the only design in A14 where the reader sees one picture at a time by default.

REDUCED MOTION, AND WHAT SURVIVES IT · SETTLEMENT 1
The track is a native scroll-snap strip, so stepping works with no JavaScript at all ⚑ — the module adds the dots, the arrows and the counter, and the registry says so.Under reduced-motion the scroll is instant rather than smooth ⚑ — scroll-behavior: auto instead of smooth. Nothing else changes: same track, same snap points, same dots. The design is fully usable because it never moved on its own to begin with.This design declares two modules ⚑ — carousel and lightbox — and it is the first section in the library to declare two. The registry has no rule against it and no rule for it; that is a finding for the architect, not a new module name.

At one image the carousel is not a carousel ⚑ — no track, no dots, no counter, no arrows, and the frame draws at the full 1,296 as a single picture. At two there is one peek and two dots, and the track still snaps. The caption sits under the active frame and changes with it ⚑; at Captions: Off the line is removed and the frame does not grow to fill it ⚑ — the track holds one height so the section does not jump as the reader steps.

BEHAVIOUR · from the fixed registryTwo modules ⚑ — carousel and lightbox, both edit-safe: no. No autoplay at any value ⚑ — owner-ratified and stated here so it stops being re-litigated: a gallery that moves on its own takes the reading decision away from the reader, and reduced motion would switch it off anyway. No-JS, quoted: “The slide track is a native horizontally-scrollable scroll-snap strip — fully usable, only dots and arrow buttons are hidden.” And: “Each thumbnail is an <a href> to the full-size image; clicking opens it as a normal page.” Nothing else in the registry is claimed — no reveal, no shuffle, no load-more, and no module name is coined ⚑.
