Eight items in two columns of four, on a 1000 px measure rather than the full 1296 — a checklist is read, not scanned across a page, and 468 px a column is about 60 characters. The mark is 16 px with a 14 px gap, aligned to the line's baseline with a 3 px optical lift. The item title is 17 px in the body font at 500 weight, not the heading font — the one place in A5 the item title is not a heading-font size, because a one-line entry is a list item rather than a title, and Georgia at 17 px beside a check mark reads as a quotation. It is still an <h3>. These are the category's six items plus Office hours and the reading list, re-worded to one line each: the field is the same itemTitle, and a user switching to this design from 1 Three Up will want to shorten them, which the editor says. Flagged as mine: the 1000 px measure, the 17 px body-font title, the 16/14/3 px mark geometry, and the eight-item frame.

The mark is the one place this design spends the accent, and it spends it eight times. That is a stated departure from §2's once or twice, and the argument for it is that the mark is not decoration: at Check it is the only thing making eight lines a list of things included rather than eight lines of text. A site that finds it loud has Rule, which uses no accent. A check mark is a claim — that each line is part of what a reader gets — so Dot exists for a list that is only a list, and the editor says which is which in those words. Flagged as mine: the three values, the accent departure and its reasoning, and the check's geometry.

READING ORDER · down, then across
1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8

Items fill the first column top to bottom, then the second. Every other grid in A5 fills across, and this one does not, for one reason: it is the only arrangement where the visual order and the DOM order are then the same. A two-column list filled across reads 1, 2 / 3, 4 to the eye and 1, 2, 3, 4 to a screen reader, and on a checklist — where a user may well have written the items in order of importance — that difference is worth a rule. grid-auto-flow: column with a row count of ceil(n / 2). An odd count puts the extra item at the foot of the first column. Flagged as mine: the fill direction and the odd-count rule.

Density names the row gap — Compact 12 · Comfortable 16 · Spacious 24 — and at Rules Show the same number becomes the padding above and below each line, with a hairline between. The hairline runs to the end of the column, not across the two: two columns of ruled rows with a line across both would be a table, and a checklist is two lists side by side. Flagged as mine: the three densities and the hairline's extent.

WHAT THIS DESIGN DOES NOT HAVE
No body copy. itemBody is kept and not drawn, and the editor names 3 Four Up at Body copy Show for a site that wants a line of explanation under each entry. Settlement 4's title-only item is this design's whole subject, so a body here would undo it. No item link. Eight one-line entries with eight links is a navigation list, and A1's nav or A3's sitemap is where a list of links belongs. The section action is the design's only outbound path. No icons. Media has no Icon value on this design: a glyph and a check mark on the same line is two marks doing one job. The mark is the media. Flagged as mine: all three refusals and the two designs named as destinations.

1 · A5-0's structure, unchanged: the section is named by its h2 and the entries are a <ul> of <li>. Eight entries are announced as eight, which on this design is most of what a reader needs to know. 2 · The entry is an <h3> at 17 px in the body font. Visual size is not heading level — the same rule A4·16 states about its headline, applied the other way round. A run of eight h3s under one h2 is the outline this design has always had; nothing about the shorter text changes it. 3 · The mark is aria-hidden at all three values. A check mark read aloud as “tick” before every entry is eight repetitions of the section's own title, and the list's semantics already say these are items. The mark is 3.1:1 against the page at Check and Dot — non-text, so it passes — and 1.4:1 at Rule, where it is openly decorative. Nothing here is a checkbox. No role="checkbox", no aria-checked, no input: the marks are typographic, the items are not toggleable, and a reader who met eight checkboxes would reasonably try to click one. Contrast · entry 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · title 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · mark 3.1:1 / 6.1:1 · hairline 1.2:1 / 1.3:1. Nothing is interactive except the section action; no motion at any value.
