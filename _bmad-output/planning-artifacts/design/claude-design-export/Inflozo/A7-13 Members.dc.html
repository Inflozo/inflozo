The reader's own tier keeps its card and loses its button. In its place, between two hairlines, one muted line: the renewal date, which is the only thing a reader on that plan still needs from this section. A disabled button saying “Current plan” is not offered — a control a reader cannot use is a control that should not be drawn, and a 44 px grey rectangle in the middle of the row is louder than the sentence that replaces it.
The badge becomes an outline rather than a fill. “Your plan” is a fact about the reader and “Most popular” is a claim by the site; the same contrast colour drawn as a 1 px border and a text label says the second kind of thing. Where the reader's tier is also the recommended one, “Your plan” wins and the recommendation's badge is dropped: a site's suggestion is of no use to somebody who has already taken it.
The accent moves to the most expensive tier a reader is not on. On Reader, that is Quarterly; the free tier keeps the outline. This is the one place in A7 where the accent's position is computed rather than authored, and it is flagged twice — a theme deciding which upgrade to push is a theme making a commercial choice, and the sidebar offers Current tier Marked without it for a site that would rather not.

Four states and no fifth. Visitor, free member, paid member on a lower tier, paid member on the top tier. A cancelled or expired subscription reads as a visitor — Ghost's own status is “comped” or “canceled” and a theme guessing at the difference is a theme telling readers about their billing, which A30 Members Pages owns. The line under a member's card is one fact: the renewal date for a paid tier, the join date for the free one, both from Ghost, and neither is authored.

WHAT THIS DESIGN WILL NOT SAY · drawn at 560 crop
No name, no email, no avatar. The section reads three facts — signed in, paid, which tier — and draws none of the reader's own details. A pricing section that greeted somebody by name would be a pricing section that leaked one to anybody looking over their shoulder. No card details, no billing history, no invoice link, no cancel button. Portal owns all of it and #/portal/account is one link away. The note keeps saying “Cancel any time from your account page” and does not become a button. No proration arithmetic and no “you'll save €14” line. Stripe computes what a change costs and Portal shows it; a theme doing the same sum with different rounding is a theme publishing a number nobody will honour. No “downgrade” anywhere in the copy. Both directions say “Change plan” — the reader knows which way they are going, and the site does not need to name it. Nothing is remembered and nothing is announced. The state is rendered on the server; the other states' markup is not in the page, there is no live region, and nothing swaps after load. A6·14's rule, carried whole.
THE THREE PORTAL LINKS THIS DESIGN USES #/portal/signup/<tier>/<cadence> — a visitor #/portal/account/plans — a member changing tier #/portal/account — the note's account page

A member's actions all land on the same Portal screen — #/portal/account/plans — whichever card they came from, because Portal chooses the tier there and a theme deep-linking past that choice is a theme guessing. That is a real cost: three buttons, one destination. It is stated on the frame and in the panel rather than hidden behind three different-looking links.

1 · “Your plan” is plain text in the heading's row, exactly like “Most popular” — not aria-current, not a role, not a state. aria-current describes a position in a set of links a reader is navigating, and a subscription is not that. Membership is never exposed as a state, badge or role — A6·14's rule, carried whole. 2 · The renewal line is a paragraph where the action would be. It is not a disabled button, not aria-disabled and not focusable, so a keyboard reader tabs from the free card's action straight to the top tier's and meets nothing dead in between. Two tab stops on this frame, not three. 3 · All three member actions go to the same Portal screen, #/portal/account/plans, and each keeps its own visible label as its whole accessible name — three links named “Change plan” inside three named list items. No aria-label lengthens one into “Change plan to Quarterly”, because the link does not do that: Portal does. The state is rendered on the server and the other states' markup is not in the page — no hidden copies, no live region, nothing swapped after load, and therefore no announcement to make. A reader signing in gets a new page, not a new section. Contrast · as 1 Cards, plus: the outlined badge's label 12.1:1 with its 1 px border at 12.1:1 — the one border in A7 that is not a hairline token, since it carries a word rather than an edge. The renewal line is 5.4:1 muted between two hairlines, and it is the one place muted text sits where an action was.
