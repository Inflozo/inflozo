A17 POST GRIDS · DESIGN 8 OF 18 · PAPER PACK · SEVEN CONTROLS · MEDIA PLACEMENT BACKGROUND

Overlay

Tag and title set over the feature image at Portrait 4:5, behind a scrim, aligned to the foot of the cell. The only design in A17 where media placement is background — the image is not above the text, it is behind it.

Which makes it the one design in the category that cannot exist without photographs, and the one that spends the category's whole scrim budget. A17-0 permits exactly one scrim in A17 and this is it: a bottom-weighted linear gradient, never a flat wash, tuned 45% in light and 55% in dark.

Its hard case is not a missing image — that hands off to 9 Big Type. Its hard case is a pale photograph, where a scrim tuned for a night-time dock leaves white text on white sky. The panel states what the design does about it, which is: nothing automatic, and the reason why.

The gap is 24 in both directions and the cells are a fixed 416 × 520. Every other design in A17 lets its cell height come from its content; here the content sits inside the image, so the image's ratio sets the height and every cell in the grid is identical — the one design where a 69-character title and a 35-character title produce the same box. Post 2's title takes three lines inside its cell and post 4's takes one; both stay bottom-aligned and neither changes its cell.
Text is inset 24 from the cell's left, right and bottom, which is one 8 px step above the card's usual 20 and exists because text over an image needs more air than text on a ground. The tag sits above the title, not below it, keeping A17-0's DOM order — image, tag, title, meta — even though the image is now behind all three.
Post 3 has no feature image and is the frame's real subject. It draws the tag plate at 416 × 520, and its text is dark rather than white, with no scrim at all — because a scrim over a pale plate would darken a surface that has nothing behind it to darken. That inversion is per-cell, it is the only per-cell treatment in the whole category, and it is what keeps one imageless post from looking like a rendering fault. ⚑ Mine.

THE SCRIM · THREE VALUES, AND WHY IT IS A GRADIENT · drawn at 620 crop

LIGHT · .52 → 0 · FOR DARK PHOTOGRAPHY STANDARD · .72 → 0 · THE DEFAULT STRONG · .88 → .12 · FOR PALE PHOTOGRAPHY
The scrim is a three-stop gradient, never a flat wash. Standard runs rgba(28,27,26,.72) at the foot through .45 at 38% to transparent at 72%. A flat 45% wash over the whole image would dim the photograph everywhere to protect text that only occupies the bottom third — the gradient spends its opacity where the text is and leaves the top of the picture alone. That is the design system's “subtle scrims, never heavy black gradients” read as strictly as it can be: the scrim's colour is the pack's ink at partial alpha, not black. Only the foot stop is named in the control — Light, Standard, Strong — and the two upper stops move with it proportionally. A user choosing three numbers is a user choosing a gradient, which is a designer's job. Strong keeps 12% at the top rather than reaching transparent, because a pale photograph needs its whole surface pulled down a little to keep white text legible at the title's second line. It is the only value that touches the top of the image, and it is the value a user with sky-heavy photography needs.
THE PALE-PHOTOGRAPH PROBLEM, AND THE IMAGELESS CELL · drawn at 600 crop

InterviewInterviewRosa Ferreira on drawing machines THE IMAGELESS CELL · PLATE, NO SCRIM, DARK TEXTThe only per-cell treatment in A17. Everything else in the category writes one value onto the section and every item reads it; this inverts one cell's text colour because that cell has no photograph to sit on.
A pale photograph under a Standard scrim is this design's real failure mode, and the design does nothing automatic about it. Refused: sampling the image's brightness and picking a scrim. That needs the image decoded before the scrim is chosen, which means either a build step Ghost does not have or JavaScript at render time — and no module in the registry covers it. A design whose legibility depends on JavaScript fails FR-G4 outright. So the control is the answer, and the panel says so where the user will see it: “Pale photographs need the Strong scrim.” That is a real limitation, stated rather than hidden — a site with a mix of night-time docks and bright skies has one scrim for both, and the right value is whichever protects the worst case. The finding for the architect: the honest fix is per-post, and per-post is exactly what A17 cannot express. A feature_image_brightness value computed at upload and stored on the post would let the stylesheet pick a scrim per cell with no JavaScript — that is a Ghost-side change, not a section one, and it is the only clean solution I can see.

No image on a post → the tag plate at the cell's full size, no scrim, and the text inverted to suit the plate — the only per-cell treatment in A17, and the reason one imageless post does not look like a fault. No image on any post in the set → hands off to 9 Big Type, named in the panel before the switch: a wall of plates with titles on them is a text grid pretending to be photography. No tag → the title moves up inside the scrim. A pale photograph is not an empty state and has no automatic remedy — the Strong scrim is the answer and the panel says so. 9 · Behaviour module. None; core assumed. No-JS degradation: pixel-identical with JavaScript off. The scrim is a CSS gradient, the cell is an aspect-ratio box, the image is a background-image or a positioned <img> under absolutely-positioned text. Not declared: lightbox — the image is the link to the post, not to itself. Brightness-adaptive scrimming was considered and refused precisely because it would require a module and would fail FR-G4. Edit-safe. 10 · Accessibility. <section aria-labelledby> → <h2>; no accessible name at Head None. <ul> / <li>, title an <h3> in the card's <a>, and the DOM order is still image, tag, title, meta even though the image is visually behind the text. The image is decorative here and takes alt="" when it is a CSS background; when it is an <img> it takes feature_image_alt — the build should prefer the <img> form so an author's alt text is not silently discarded. The scrim is aria-hidden and pointer-events: none, so it never intercepts the card's click. A6's ring on the whole cell. Date a <time datetime>. Contrast: the honest statement is that it cannot be computed, because the text sits on a photograph nobody has seen yet. What the design guarantees instead, computed against a mid-grey image so the figure means something: the Standard scrim's foot stop composites to #383737 over mid-grey, where white measures 11.87:1; its mid stop composites to #535352 at 7.7:1. So the title clears AA by a wide margin on any image mid-grey or darker, and the margin narrows as the photograph pales — which is the failure mode, not a fixed ratio. Above that the Strong scrim is required, which is why the control exists and why the panel names it at the picker rather than burying it. ⚑ The scrim stops, the per-cell inversion and this contrast statement are all mine.
