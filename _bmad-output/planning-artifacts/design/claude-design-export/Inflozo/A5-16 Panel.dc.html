Panel

The whole set on one surface panel, inset from the page, divided internally by hairlines rather than separated by gaps. It is A3·12's card and A4·8's inset at section scale, and the only design in A5 whose items share a plane — which makes the divider, not the gap, the thing that separates one item from the next, and turns the set into a single object a page can hold.

One panel, 40 px inside the page's own margin, with 48 px of padding at its edges and 32 px between a cell and the divider beside it — so two items are 64 px apart across a hairline, and the panel's outer edge is 48. The plane is A3·12's card verbatim: surface, one hairline, the sm shadow, the pack radius. The dividers do the work the gutters do everywhere else in A5 — there is no gap between cells, only the 32/32 padding either side of a 1 px line — and that is the whole reason the design exists: six items read as one object rather than six. The head sits above the panel on the page's ground, at the page margin rather than the panel's, so the panel is the set alone. Flagged as mine: the 48/32 padding pair, the head's position outside the panel, and reusing A3·12's plane and A4·8's inset unchanged.

NONE · 1 Three Up's grid on a panel · the gaps return at 32 and 24

No divider ever reaches the panel's edge. Every hairline stops at the 48 px padding, which is what keeps the panel a panel rather than a table — a line that touches the border makes a cell, and a cell implies a column of comparable things. At Between rows the vertical lines leave and a 32 px gutter returns in their place, so the columns are separated by space and the rows by a line. At None the design is 1 Three Up drawn on a surface panel, which is a legitimate thing to want and the reason the value exists. Flagged as mine: the three values, the edge rule, and the gutter's return at Between rows.

SNUG 16 · the panel is nearly the page

WIDE 72 · the panel sits inside a second margin

The inset is measured inside the page's 72 px margin, so Wide puts the panel 144 px from the window's edge. A4·8 Card's values are reused unchanged and deliberately: a site running A4·8's hero and this section on one page reads one shape at two scales, which was the point of writing the inset down in A4 rather than inventing it twice. The gap under the head is always 48, whatever the inset — A4·8 fixed its own at 24 for the same reason, that a variable gap above a panel makes the head look attached to it.

RESTING · HOVER · the hovered cell takes a tinted block inside the divider, 16 px in from it

This is the one design where the hover needed a new answer. Everywhere else in A5 the plane steps from surface to hover surface — but here the plane is shared, so a step would light up the whole panel. Instead the cell takes the hover surface as a rounded block that stops 16 px short of the divider on each side, so the hairlines stay visible and the block reads as one item within the object rather than a new panel. The title underlines and the icon's tint inverts, as 4 Cards. Focus is the 2 px accent ring on that same block. Flagged as mine: the cell-block hover, its 16 px inset from the divider, and its 6 px radius against the panel's 8.

1 · A5-0's structure, unchanged: <section aria-labelledby>, a <ul> of <li>, h3 titles. The panel is not a landmark, a region, a group or a table — it is one <ul> with a background and a border, and the dividers are border properties on the list items. 2 · The hairline grid is the design's one real risk and the reason it is not a table. Six ruled cells look like one, and a reader who met role="table" here would ask what the columns mean — nothing, is the answer: the items are a list, the grid is how they fit, and there is no header row, no axis and nothing comparable across a column. 3 · The meta is 6 Rows' meta: plain text, third in the DOM after title and body whatever its visual position, no glyph and no badge. It sits on the icon's line rather than the title's on this design, because the cell's first row is where the panel's rhythm lives. At Whole item each cell is one <a> inside its <li>, named by the title, as 4 Cards. The hover and focus block is inside the cell's padding so the ring never crosses a divider — a focus indicator that overlapped the hairline would read as two objects at once. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · body 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · meta 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · dividers 1.2:1 / 1.3:1, decorative · hover block 1.1:1 with the underline carrying the state · focus ring 3.1:1. Forced colours keep the panel's border and the dividers, since both are real borders rather than background colours — which is the practical argument for drawing dividers this way.
