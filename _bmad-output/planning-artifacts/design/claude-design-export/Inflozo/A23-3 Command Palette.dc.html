3 · Command Palette

The overlay with its results grouped by what they are — posts, pages, tags and authors — and a ⌘K hint in the field. For a publication whose readers are the sort of people who know what ⌘K does.

It is the only design that shows the reader more than one kind of thing at once, and the grouping is what makes that legible: four short lists under four labels, rather than one list of mixed objects.

GROUPS HOLD THEIR ORDER WHILE THE QUERY RUNS ⚑
NO JAVASCRIPT · field and archive link only · results need JS ⚑
⌕ Search 412 essays and interviews NO JAVASCRIPT · THE ⌘K HINT IS HIDDEN · THE TRIGGER REMAINS The registry’s words, quoted: “The ⌘K hint is hidden; the visible search trigger remains and behaves as above.” The groups are the palette’s, so without the module a reader gets the /search/ route’s field and its archive link — and no list at all ⚑: Ghost cannot search server-side.
WHAT THIS DESIGN SETTLES
“Palette” names a pattern, not a capability ⚑. It navigates to posts, pages, tags and authors. It runs no commands — a public site’s search should not be able to change the site.Ctrl+K is always bound, whatever the hint shows ⚑. The hint is a label; the shortcut is the module’s, and a Windows reader gets the same behaviour with a different glyph.Tags and authors are one group, not two ⚑ — at three rows each, two labels for two short lists is more furniture than list. Flagged: the brief does not say, and a site with many authors may want them split.

A group with nothing in it is not drawn ⚑ — that is the state rule this design adds, and it is why the palette is the design that can show four kinds of thing without ever looking half-empty. Under three characters the groups have not run yet: recent searches stand in, ungrouped, because grouping three recent strings by object type would be a fiction.

THE KEYBOARD MODEL · A1·9'S, MATCHED
⌘KOpens and closes the palette. The hint in the field is this design’s own; the shortcut itself is registered by the module and works from anywhere on the site.
↑ ↓Moves the active row across group boundaries ⚑ — the labels are not stops. aria-activedescendant, focus stays in the field.
↵Opens the active row: a post, a page, a tag route or an author route. Nothing in this palette runs a command — it navigates, and the name is inherited from the pattern, not the behaviour ⚑.
escA1·9’s two steps: close keeping the query, then clear.
/The alternative hint value, for sites whose readers are not on a Mac. Ctrl+K is always registered alongside ⌘K whatever the hint says ⚑.
WHAT THE MODEL SETTLES
The palette navigates; it does not act. There are no commands in it — no “subscribe”, no “switch theme”, no “copy link” ⚑. A reader’s palette that performs actions is an admin tool, and this is a published site. Named for the pattern the reader recognises, and flagged as exactly that.Groups are announced, not just drawn. Each label is a real role="group" with aria-label, so a screen reader hears “Posts, 3 items” before the rows — which is the whole reason to group.One module, one hint. command-palette owns the shortcut; search-overlay’s panel behaviour is the same panel. Declaring both would be declaring the panel twice ⚑ — the registry’s palette entry already says the trigger remains.

THE SEARCH SOURCE · SEVEN ROWS · IDENTICAL WHEREVER RESULTS ARE DRAWN · NOT COUNTED
What it searches
Posts and pages▾
Posts · Posts and pages · Posts, pages and tags · Everything, including authors. Ghost decides what a theme can reach; this row says which of it this section asks for ⚑.
Search in
Titles and excerpts▾
Titles and excerpts · Full content. Full content is available here and is not in Ghost’s own bundle ⚑ — A23’s index is client-side over the Content API, which can return plaintext. It costs index size: roughly 4–8 kB a post, so a 400-post archive fetches a few megabytes before the first query. Titles and excerpts is the default, and this row is what the read-only line below reports.
Before a query
Recent searches, then featured▾
Recent searches, then featured · Featured posts · Latest posts · Nothing. Recent searches are the reader’s own and never leave their browser ⚑; a first visit falls through to the second value. “Most read” is gone ⚑ — Ghost gives a theme no view counts, so it was a promise the platform cannot keep.
Start searching after
3 characters▾
2 · 3 · 4 characters. A1·9’s three-character minimum and 200 ms debounce, carried ⚑.
How many results
20▾
10 · 20 · 50 · All matches. The cap is the section’s, not Ghost’s — the count line always names the true total ⚑.
Match highlight
MarkedPlain
A real <mark> at 16 % accent, 22 % in dark, in the title and the excerpt and never in the meta ⚑. At Plain it is absent from the DOM rather than untinted. Promoted here from 11 Thumb Rows, so every design that draws a result governs it in the same row ⚑.
What is indexed
Title, excerpt and slug🔒
Read-only, and it follows Search in ⚑. At Titles and excerpts it reads title, excerpt and slug; at Full content, title, excerpt, slug and body text. emptyText’s default follows the same value, which is why that sentence is a field and not a string.
EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESThe group labels edit inline — postsLabel, pagesLabel, tagsLabel, authorsLabel — with the P0·1 toolbar, and so do placeholder and shortcutHint. The state strings (countLabel, seeAllLabel, emptyHeading, emptyText, recentLabel, suggestLabel) are edited in the P0·6 switcher’s No query · Results · No matches states ⚑. Post titles, tag names, author names and post counts are Ghost’s ⚑ — “Edit in Ghost”. The keycap labels are translation-catalog strings ⚑.
DATA · GHOST’S OWN, AND NEVER AN AUTHORED LISTPosts, pages, tags and authors by scope: no Add, no Remove, no drag ⚑. A tag row carries its post count and an author row does not ⚑ — Ghost gives one cheaply and not the other. No Member Visibility row ⚑ — the palette navigates and never acts.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLESNo “what to group” control. The groups are the source group’s scope — search posts and you get one group; search everything and you get four ⚑. Two controls for one decision is how a sidebar starts lying.No command list, so no control for one. If a publication wants “Subscribe” in the palette, that is a section that does not exist yet and a finding for the architect, not a value here ⚑.Rows per group, not rows total. Three posts and one page is four rows; three posts and three pages is six. The palette’s height is content, and the panel never scrolls ⚑.Match highlight came from the source group in this pass ⚑ — the palette marks matched substrings in its titles, tags and author names, and the row that governs it is shared with the other twelve result-drawing designs.
