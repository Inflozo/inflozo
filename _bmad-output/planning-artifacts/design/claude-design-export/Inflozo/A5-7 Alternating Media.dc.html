Alternating Media

Each item takes a full row: a photograph on one half, the text on the other, and the sides swap row by row. It is the design for two to four features that each need showing rather than describing, and it settles the large item crop, the alternation rule and what a picture-led item does when the picture is missing.

These frames draw items 1–4 of the six the rest of A5 draws. Four rows is this design's advised maximum — six rows of a 413 px picture is 3,400 px of section, which is a page rather than a section. The other two items are kept, and at the fifth the editor names 8 Media Top. Flagged as mine, and it is the only place in A5 where a design's frames carry less than the category's authored set.

Two 620 px halves on a 56 px gutter, the picture at 3:2 — 620 × 413 — fixed, and not a control, agreeing with A24's post crop and A4·13's card so a site running all three shows one shape. 72 px between rows, which is more than any gap in A5 and less than the section's own padding: the rows have to read as separate items without reading as separate sections. The item title is 28 px, the largest in the category, because the item is half the page; the body holds its 520 px measure inside the 620 px half, so the text column has an edge the picture's does not. The picture is vertically centred against the text block, and where the text is taller than 413 px the picture centres against it rather than growing. Flagged as mine: the 3:2 crop, the 56 px gutter, the 72 px row gap, the 28 px title and the centring rule.

ALTERNATION AND PICTURE SIDE · the two controls together · drawn at 30%
PICTURE LEFT · ALTERNATION ON · the default
ALTERNATION OFF · every picture on the named side

Picture side names the first row, and alternation does the rest. Two controls rather than one because they answer different questions: which side the reader meets first, and whether the section zigzags at all. Alternation Off is the value for a set of screenshots that all show the same interface — a zigzag then makes the reader's eye do work the content does not repay. Alternation is by row and not by item, which matters at ≤ 767, where every row is stacked and the control has no effect: the panel says so rather than leaving a control that quietly does nothing on a phone.

FLUSH · the default · the picture is the plane
FRAMED INSET · A4·3's mount · 14 px of surface, one hairline, the picture at half the radius

A4·3 Split's mount, carried forward unchanged: a 14 px surface margin, one hairline, and the picture's own corners at half the pack radius so the two curves do not fight. It exists for pictures with pale edges — a screenshot of a white interface on a near-white page has no edge of its own, and the mount is what gives it one. The crop does not change between treatments; the mount is drawn outside the 3:2 box, so the row's height steps 413 → 441 px at Framed inset.

Whole item is not offered on this design. A 1,296 px row with a 413 px picture is not a target a reader aims at — the hover would cover half the window, and a pointer crossing the section on its way somewhere else would light it up. The picture does not darken on hover here for the same reason: there is nothing to darken it in response to. 4 Cards and 6 Rows are where a whole item is a link, and 8 Media Top is where a picture darkens.

1 · A5-0's structure: a <ul> of <li>, h3 titles, the section named by its h2. Each row is one list item containing a <figure>-less picture and a text block. 2 · The DOM order is picture then text on every row, whatever the visual side. Alternation is flex-direction: row-reverse on the odd rows, not a reordering of the source — so a screen reader and a keyboard meet the same sequence on row one and row two, and the phone's stacked order is that same sequence with no exception to state. 3 · alt is empty by default: the title is beside the picture and usually says what it shows. It is authorable per item, and the editor's help text asks for what the picture adds rather than what it is — a screenshot of the archive beside the words “Full archive” adds nothing by being described twice. The text tile that replaces a missing picture is aria-hidden in full: its title is a duplicate of the h3 that follows it, and a reader hearing the same three words twice learns nothing. This differs from A4·13, which repeats its title deliberately, and the difference is that A4·13's repeat is the only copy of a live post's title while this one is not. No whole-item link, so the only tab stop in a row is the text link where one is written. Nothing on this design animates, and the picture does not respond to a pointer. Contrast · item title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · body 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · link 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · text-tile title 11.2:1 on its hover-surface ground · the mount's hairline 1.2:1, decorative.

The no-JavaScript noticeThis design holds no subscribe or sign-in field, so it draws no no-JavaScript form notice. A5 holds no form anywhere: every sentence a reader sees is authored text, and the category makes no claim that anything can be subscribed to without JavaScript.
