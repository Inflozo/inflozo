Three cards of 416 on a 24 px gutter across the 1,296 px content width, stretched to the tallest — Reader's list is one line longer than Free's and the cards are honestly unequal rather than padded to match. Inside a card the separations are fixed, not controlled: 16 px from the name to the price, 12 to the description, 20 to the action, 20 to the hairline and 20 again to the first benefit, the benefit rows 10 apart. Head to tiers 48 px, tiers to note 24. The action sits above the benefits so three lists of unequal length keep their buttons on one line.
The recommendation is three signals and no new colour — the plane steps one step to surface with the sm shadow, the contrast badge sits at the right end of the name's line, and the accent is taken away from the other two rather than added to this one. Their actions become hairline outlines. Settled in A7-0 §2 and flagged there; the outline is a stated departure from A6's refusal of outlines anywhere.

1 · The tiers are a <ul> of <li> and each tier name is an <h3> under the section's <h2>, so a reader hears “list of 3 items” before the first price and can jump between tiers by heading. The card is a <div> — not a region, group or article. 2 · The badge is plain text inside the heading's row and not aria-hidden: “Most popular” is the site's claim and a reader who cannot see the plane step should still hear it. It is not aria-current, not a mark, and not part of the <h3> — a heading called “Reader Most popular” is a heading no reader wrote. 3 · The price is one paragraph read as one string — “€7 a month” — with no aria-label, no abbreviation and no visually-hidden alternative. The currency mark is real text at full size, which is why it is not raised: a superscript € is read out of order by some screen readers and looks like a footnote to everyone else. 4 · Actions are <a> elements to #/portal/signup/<tier>/<cadence>, never buttons, so a reader can middle-click them. Three links may share the label “Subscribe”: each sits inside a named list item, which is the accessible name's context, and lengthening one with an aria-label would make the visible label a lie. Three tab stops, in price order. 5 · Benefits are a nested <ul>, the mark aria-hidden and drawn from currentColor so forced colours keep it. Nothing is a checkbox: no role, no aria-checked, no input. A7 states no benefit as absent — a tier lists what it includes, never what it lacks, which is 2 Table's job and the reason that design exists. Contrast · title 12.1:1 · price 12.1:1 · description and note 5.4:1 · benefit rows 12.1:1 with the mark at 5.4:1 · badge 12.8:1 · accent label on fill 4.6:1 · outlined label 12.1:1 with its border at 1.4:1, which is why the outline never carries meaning on its own — the label does. Focus ring 3.1:1 against both card planes. Forced colours keep every action: the fill becomes a bordered link, the outline stays, and the recommendation survives as its badge.
