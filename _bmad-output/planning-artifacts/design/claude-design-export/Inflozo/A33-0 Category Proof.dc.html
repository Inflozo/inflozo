A33 KOENIG CARD TREATMENTS · CATEGORY PROOF · SIX TREATMENTS · PAPER PACK

A33 · Category Proof

A33 is not a section. It is the site-wide styling of the cards an author inserts into a post, and it lives on the same surface as C Post Body’s card module: one treatment picker at the top of the Cards panel, six treatments, and the per-card panels underneath it unchanged.

Six treatments, twenty cards each. What separates them is the plane under a card and what the author’s width class resolves to — never colour, type or radius, which the Style Pack owns. This frame carries the four settlements, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame, the roster, the shared field list, the findings and the cumulative component inventory.

WHAT A33 SETTLES · §8 OF THE BRIEF, ANSWERED
SETTLEMENT 1
Every card Ghost can renderAll twenty cards on ghost.org/help/cards are drawn in every treatment, in Ghost’s own order: image, markdown, HTML, gallery, divider, bookmark, email content, call to action, public preview, button, callout, GIF, toggle, audio, video, file, product, header, embeds, signup.Seven of the twenty are not the theme’s to arrange, and the roll says so on each one ⚑: markdown carries no wrapper class and is A25’s type verbatim; HTML is whatever the author pasted; embeds are the provider’s markup; gallery, toggle and audio ship Ghost’s own CSS and script, so A33 re-styles the shell only — and the toggle is a plain container with a heading and a button, not native disclosure markup ⚑, so with JavaScript off it does not open; email content never renders on the web at all ⚑ and is drawn dashed so a user can see that. The public-preview cut leaves no element at all ⚑ — Ghost emits only an invisible HTML comment there, so there is nothing for a stylesheet to reach and the marker component is deleted from this category; A32 renders the gate below the cut. Blockquote and the code block are text formats rather than cards and belong to A25’s measure ⚑.The call-to-action card renders on the web ⚑ — Ghost lets the author show it on the site, in the newsletter or both, so it is a themed card with a sponsor label, an image, a button and its own visibility. The line between a treatment and A25’s measure: A25 owns the column, the paragraph rhythm, the heading scale and the type for everything that is words on the page. A33 owns the plane under a card, the space around it, its caption and what its width class resolves to ⚑. A blockquote is A25’s. A callout is A33’s.
SETTLEMENT 2
Captions, credits and dark modeThe caption is 13.5 px in text-muted, at the measure by default, and it belongs to the <figcaption> of the card that owns it. Three values in every treatment: under-left, under-centred, hidden. Hidden never touches image.alt ⚑.Ghost has no credit field ⚑. The credit is the caption’s trailing <em> run — the convention editors already use — and the Credit line control decides whether it sits inline with the caption or drops to a 13 px second line. That is an invented reading of an existing field, and it is flagged in all six specs.Dark is re-tuned per card, not inverted: the warm shadows are dropped entirely rather than darkened ⚑, surfaces lift one step, the callout takes the dark hover token rather than a lightened copy of the light one, the placeholder stripes change pair, and the contrast band inverts the other way — a light band in a dark article.
SETTLEMENT 3
The relationship to C Post Body’s card moduleSame surface, one vocabulary ⚑. The six treatments are a picker at the top of the Cards module, where a section’s Design picker sits. Below it are the treatment’s own six controls, which write one value each, site-wide.The per-card panels stay exactly as C.1 drew them — thumbnail side, chevron side, emoji shown, which metadata rows show. What the treatment claims, the per-card panel no longer offers ⚑: plane, padding, caption placement, width resolution and corners are the treatment’s, in one place, for every card.This is the reading A33 chose and Prompt 2 Part C does not state it ⚑ — the module was drawn with per-card panels and no treatment layer. Finding 7 puts the product question plainly rather than assuming the answer.
SETTLEMENT 4
What a treatment may and may not doMay: choose the plane, the space, the caption, the credit, the emoji, and what regular, wide and full resolve to ⚑.May not: set a colour, a font, a radius or a width in pixels; style one card of one post; crop, tint, filter or dim a photograph (A19) ⚑; add markup to {​{content}​}; touch alt text; reach inside an embed’s iframe; or invert an image, a gallery or an embed at any control value ⚑.And one rule that reads like a restriction and is the point: A33 renders nothing of its own ⚑. With no cards in a post there is no wrapper, no padding and no trace — which is why the empty state of every treatment is the same three words.

All twenty editor cards drawn, in Ghost’s own order ⚑ — and the twelve are named because §8·1 asks for “every card Ghost can render” and a list that quietly stopped at ten would be the worse answer. Three of the twelve are A25’s, not A33’s, which is §8·4 answered: if it is words in the column it is the measure’s; if it is an object in the column it is a card’s.

The shared field list says what to style; this says what to select ⚑, and until this pass the category had neither a card class nor a width class written down anywhere. Every row marked Ghost docs is taken from Ghost’s own theme documentation; every row marked UNVERIFIED is flagged rather than guessed, because a wrong selector styles nothing at all and fails silently — the worst failure available in a stylesheet-only category. Inside a post’s body A33 owns the stylesheet and nothing else: not the markup, not the ARIA attributes, not the text. One build fact belongs with the list: Ghost injects cards.min.css through ghost_head unless the theme’s package.json excludes that card under card_assets — the excludable set is audio, blockquote, bookmark, button, callout, file, gallery, header, nft, product, toggle, video and signup — so a treatment’s rules only land for cards on the exclusion list. Settled by the owner, 1 September 2026: A33 replaces Ghost's card CSS for all twelve excludable cards — nft ignored, since this category draws no NFT card — and the theme always re-includes Ghost's own cards.min.js, so A33 owns the look and Ghost keeps the behaviour. One condition, tested first and with its fallback already written ⚑: an exclusion takes a card's assets rather than its stylesheet alone, so if the served bundle turns out to be filtered by the config, the seven static cards are excluded and toggle, gallery, audio, video and signup stay on Ghost's assets, styled by a deeper selector — excluding those five would leave a card that renders and does nothing.
FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · ELEVEN, FOUR ADDED BY THE PATCH PASS
FINDING 1
Ghost’s callout palette is nine literal colours and the pack ships seven roles. ⚑There is no mapping that keeps both. Collapsing the nine onto the pack’s planes loses the author’s intent — blue and green become the same plane; honouring them breaks §2 and every pack’s harmony. A33 offers the choice as one shared control (Callout colours: Pack tokens · Ghost’s palette) and defaults to tokens. The architect should decide whether that control belongs in the Cards module or in the Style Pack.
FINDING 2
Ghost’s gallery card ships its own row-ratio script and the registry has no module for it. ⚑FR-G7 is closed and none of the 31 modules describes it. With JS off the images render at equal widths, which is legible but not what the author arranged. The closest module is core, and A33 declares that. This is the second category to hit a Ghost-owned script with no registry entry — the toggle card is the first.
FINDING 3
C.1’s ownership matrix lists “corners” as a per-card setting for eleven cards. A33 answers with the pack’s radius token and offers no control. ⚑Two drawn artefacts now disagree, and the panel is the authority per this project’s own rule. A card that could mix radii would fail §3·1 the moment a user changed pack, so A33’s answer is the shippable one — but C.1’s matrix and its per-card panels should be amended rather than left to contradict it.
FINDING 4
A treatment re-resolves the author’s width class, and so does a section. ⚑A25·3 Sheet and A25·5 Full Bleed already overrule the Cards module for their own ground; A33·4 Wide and A33·5 Full Bleed now do it site-wide. Two layers can disagree about what “wide” means. A33’s answer: the section wins inside its own ground, the treatment wins everywhere else. That precedence is not written anywhere the user can see it.
FINDING 5
The toggle card’s open/close script is Ghost’s, not the theme’s. ⚑Tested against Ghost itself: the toggle is a plain container with a heading and a button, not native disclosure markup, so the registry’s quoted degradation — “native details, fully functional” — is simply false here and with JavaScript off the card cannot open. A33 still declares accordion as the nearest module and drops the quote. The registry needs either a degradation that matches Ghost’s markup or an entry that says the behaviour is Ghost’s own.
FINDING 6
A33 has no section, so its tuples describe the card. ⚑Every other category’s containment and ground slots describe a section on a page. A33 is a stylesheet over cards inside A25’s section. The tuple slots are read as the card’s, which the reconciliation pass should expect rather than flag — otherwise all six read none · page and the uniqueness check fails on a technicality.
FINDING 7
Six treatments plus per-card panels means a user meets up to twelve controls to style one callout. ⚑The treatment carries six and the callout’s own panel carries six more. That is within the letter of §3·5 and against its spirit. The product question: does the per-card panel collapse to “exceptions only” once a treatment is chosen — showing three controls instead of six — or does the treatment become a preset that writes the per-card panels and then steps out of the way?
FINDING 8
The HTML card emits no wrapper element. ⚑Ghost renders the author’s markup raw, so the Rules control and the Contrast Band have nothing to target and both drop it. The same fact raises a question this pass will not answer on its own: can 2 Card’s panel and 3 Panel’s plane reach the HTML card either? Asked of the owner rather than decided.
FINDING 9
The public-preview cut is a comment, not an element. ⚑Ghost leaves only an invisible HTML comment where it cuts the response. There is nothing to style, so the marker component is deleted from the inventory and the roll draws the absence instead of a label.
FINDING 10
Ghost gives the product card’s rating no text equivalent. ⚑The stars are Ghost’s markup and a theme cannot add the words. Recorded as a Ghost limitation rather than something this category fixes; a screen-reader user hears no rating at all.
FINDING 11
Ghost fixes the width of a gallery and of an embed. ⚑A theme may not override it, so neither can bleed and 5 Full Bleed’s value list drops to two. Whether the same fact removes them from 4 Wide’s step-up list is the second open question for the owner.

Two of the first seven are carried forward rather than new: the width-resolution conflict A25 raised at section level, and the Ghost-owned-script gap A14 raised about the gallery card. The five new ones are all the same shape — Ghost owns a piece of the card and the token system owns the rest, and the seam between them needs a decision the design pass can flag but not make.
