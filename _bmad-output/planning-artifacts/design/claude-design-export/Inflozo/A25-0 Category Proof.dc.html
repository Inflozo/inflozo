Twelve ways to wrap one article

A25 draws the body of a post: everything Ghost renders between the header and the footer, plus whatever the section puts beside it — an index, a share rail, a caption in a margin, a number in a gutter. It is the only category in the library whose content it does not control: the words, the pictures and the cards are the author's, and the design's whole job is where they sit and what sits next to them.

The category inherits its content model from C Post Body, which settled the section's six controls, the fixture it is drawn against and the two furniture positions. A25 adds the arrangement — and the specification for each of the twelve, which is the half the build reads.

Four things to notice. Nothing moved. Six frames, three radii, three accents, three heading families and one arrangement: the index, the measure and the rail sit in the same places at the same intervals in all six, and the drop cap sits on the same three lines.
The accent appears twice in every frame and both times on furniture — the current-entry rule and, unseen here, the focus ring. It never touches the prose, which is what lets Ink's blue and Tangerine's orange sit on a reading page at all: a pack whose accent would be unreadable as body text never has to be.
The drop cap is the element that does not survive a pack change untouched ⚑, and it is A25's version of A24's finding. Georgia's cap height, Bricolage's and Inter's are three different fractions of their em, so a three-line initial is three different heights: the section can ask for three lines, and only the pack can say what that is in pixels. A pack must supply its drop-cap ratio with its heading font — the second half of A24's finding about tracking and leading, and the two together mean a pack is a set of type metrics with a palette attached rather than a palette.
The hairline is the quietest and most load-bearing token in the six. Paper's #EBE5DB, Tangerine's #F0E0CE and Ink's #E3E3E8 carry 8 Index Top, 11 Ruled and every share button in the category; Ink's is the strongest and Tangerine's the weakest, and 11 Ruled is the design a new pack should be checked against first — it is drawn in nothing else.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT1 · A pack must supply its drop-cap ratio with its heading font. Three lines is a section instruction; what three lines is in pixels is a pack property, and Georgia, Bricolage and Inter give three different answers. A page layout may carry exactly one post content section, and the editor refuses a second. Two would print the article twice on the live page. The refusal happens at placement, with the reason shown — not accepted and warned about afterwards — because there is no reason to want a second, so nothing is lost by making it impossible. A control the post’s own content cannot honour is greyed too — ruled by the owner on 1 September 2026. The greyed-with-a-reason rule named controls switching controls; it did not say what to do when the article is the reason a value cannot be honoured. It is the same treatment: greyed, never hidden, never left accepting a value it will not honour, with the reason as a short sentence at the control — the sentence each panel already drew at that state. Twelve designs, twenty rows, one drawn example below: an index control on a post with two headings, a picture control on a post with no pictures, and a margin-note control on a post with neither caption nor quote. On an empty body every row greys with the section's own line. The rule reaches nothing else — no measure, no type scale, no drop cap, no rhythm and no share mark depends on what the post contains.
