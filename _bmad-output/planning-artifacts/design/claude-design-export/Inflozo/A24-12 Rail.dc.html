Rail

The facts in a 240 px rail down the left, the title and standfirst in the column beside it, the photograph beneath. The rail is the tag, the byline, the date and the reading time set one to a line — the same six facts every other design carries, turned ninety degrees and given their own column.

It is the design that reads the Post block's Meta field as a line count rather than as a row's terms — four values, four rail lengths — which is the category's one example of a design reinterpreting a block field instead of narrowing, setting, locking or ignoring it. Reading time is the subject here, so it is one of the two designs where it survives below 767.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES A design control may read a block field and must never write one. Picture ratio greys out when Feature image is Off — the same dependency 4 Image Top has — and Meta's value changes what the rail contains without the rail's own controls touching it. The reverse never happens: nothing in this panel can set Meta, and switching to another design leaves the user's Meta value exactly as they left it, meaning something different there. Rail width's three values keep one gap. 180 · 240 · 300 against a fixed 72 px gutter, so the column is 1,044 · 984 · 924 — and the title's 820 measure fits inside all three, which is why Title size needs no resolution rule here and 3, 7, 9 and 16 all do. At Narrow 180 the two-author case wraps to three lines and the panel says so rather than forbidding it. Cut: rail alignment. Left-aligned text in a left-hand rail, and left-aligned text in a right-hand rail: a right-aligned rail produces four ragged left edges against a title that starts on a straight one, and it is the one arrangement here that fails the squint test. Cut: a sticky rail. A rail that follows the reader down the article is A25's or A32's, and it is a different section from a post header: this rail ends where the header ends, and the only module it could have declared is scroll-spy, whose home is a table of contents.

Ghost finding · the feature-image caption renders differently on the two Ghost versionsReaches this design. Recorded because the shape is easy to get wrong: the count's element is never created without JavaScript, Ghost writes the number in with a script and prepends it to a bare noun, and a design that reads it aloud labels the surrounding element rather than the count.
