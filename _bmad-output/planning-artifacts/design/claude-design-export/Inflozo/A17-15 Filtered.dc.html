Filtered

A strip of tag links above a three-up grid, the current one marked. The only design in A17 with links outside its cards — everywhere else the card is the single destination and a tag inside it is plain text, because a link inside a link is invalid.

It declares filter-strip, and that module's registry line is the reason this design is straightforward rather than delicate: “Filters are <a href> links to Ghost routes and work perfectly — this module needs JS least of all.” The strip is real navigation with or without JavaScript; the module only spares the page reload.

Its hard question is not the strip but where the tags come from. The user does not author them any more than they author the posts — so the strip is a query too, and the panel settles what it lists, in what order, and what happens when a site has forty tags.

The active pill is the accent, and it is the third accent spend in the whole category — after the card title's hover underline and A6's focus ring. Tags: From these posts is deleted ⚑ 29 August 2026. A Ghost template cannot compute a distinct set of tags across a loop — there is no arithmetic in a template and no access to the previous item inside one — so a strip built from the posts actually drawn cannot be rendered at all. The values are All site tags, now the default, and Chosen tags, and a site with many tags uses Chosen tags to hold the strip to one line. Tags belonging to posts outside the count are not listed, which is the part that makes the guarantee hold: Data and Letters exist on posts 7 and 8, and at Count 6 they are absent from the strip rather than dead in it. ⚑ Mine, and the reason it is the default rather than “all site tags”. All site tags is offered and is where the design breaks. A publication with forty tags gets four lines of pills above its grid, and the grid starts below the fold — the section becomes a tag index with some posts under it. The panel warns rather than truncates: “Sites with many tags may want a chosen list.” Truncating to the first twelve was drawn and cut, because the order Ghost returns tags in is not an editorial order and the cut would be arbitrary. Chosen tags is the third value and the only one the user authors — a list they pick, in the order they pick. This is the one place in A17 where a user-authored list exists, and it is legitimate precisely because tags are not posts: choosing which filters to offer is an editorial decision about navigation, not about content. A17-0's no-repeater rule covers repeating items; this is a control's value set. ⚑ Mine, and the finding most likely to be challenged.
