Rows

One item per full-width row, a hairline between each, for a set whose items need a paragraph rather than a phrase. It is the only arrangement in A5 where an item has as much room as it wants: body copy is unclamped, the row's right-hand end carries a short fact, and the whole row can be a link because two hairlines give it an edge. A5's answer for six items that are all worth reading.

Six rows, 28 px of padding above and below each, a hairline between. The row is three parts: a 40 px container, a text block whose body sits on a 620 px measure, and the meta at the far right. The body is unclamped on this design — no four-line limit, because a row can be as tall as it needs and the hairline still lands where the item ends. Item title 24 px, the same as 2 Two Up: the row is wide, so the title is the wide title. The meta is 13 px muted, never wraps, and is top-aligned to the title with a 6 px optical lift — A4·17's meta rule, carried verbatim. Flagged as mine: the 28 px row padding, the 620 px body measure, the unclamped body, and the meta's alignment reusing A4·17's lift.

NONE · the padding is the separation · row padding steps 28 → 36, since nothing else is holding the rows apart

Drawn with the meta and no body copy so three rows fit one card; the rules are the subject here. None is the only value that changes a measurement — the row's padding steps 28 → 36 px, because with no hairline the 56 px between two titles was doing all the separating and it is not enough. Between is the default because it is the one value that makes the set's first and last edges the section's padding rather than a line. Flagged as mine: the three values, Between as the default, and the padding step at None.

RESTING · HOVER · FOCUS

Whole row is offered here and on 4 Cards alone among the first six designs. Two hairlines and the full content width give the row an edge, so a ground change has something to fill: the row takes hover surface across the content width and 16 px beyond it on each side, so the ground reaches past the text rather than stopping at it. The title underlines, the icon's tint inverts to surface as on 4, the meta does not change, and nothing moves. Focus is the 2 px accent ring on the row's box with the pack radius, which is the one place a row has corners. Flagged as mine: the 16 px overhang and the ring's radius on an otherwise square row.

1 · A5-0's structure: <ul> of <li>, h3 titles, the section named by its h2. Not a table. Six rows with a right-hand column of facts looks like one and is not: the meta is a fact about its own item, not a cell in a shared column, and nothing here is sortable or comparable across rows. 2 · The meta is plain text at the end of the item, read after the body — third in the DOM whatever its visual position. It is not a <dl>, not aria-describedby and never a badge. 3 · Icon aria-hidden, 40 px, first in the DOM. The hairline is a border-top on the <li> rather than an <hr> — a separator element between list items would be announced, and there is nothing to announce. 4 · “Members only” is a fact, not a state. Where a site uses the meta to mark access, it is the same plain text as “312 issues” — no lock glyph, no badge, no aria-disabled. A5 does not know a reader's membership, and A32 Paywall owns everything that does. At Whole row the row is one <a> inside the <li>, named by the title, wrapping icon, title, body and meta — 4 Cards' pattern exactly, and the same refusal of an aria-label over it. One tab stop per row. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · body 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · meta 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · hairline 1.2:1 / 1.3:1, decorative · hover ground 1.1:1 against the page, with the underline carrying the state rather than the fill.
