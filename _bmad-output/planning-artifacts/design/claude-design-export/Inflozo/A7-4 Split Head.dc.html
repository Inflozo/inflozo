Split Head

A5·5's division at pricing scale: the head held at the left margin, the tiers in a column of rows to the right of it. The design exists to answer one question the other fourteen do not have — where the cadence switch belongs when the head is not above the tiers — and its answer is that the switch is part of the head, not part of the tiers.

The switch belongs to the head. It is a control over the prices, not one of them, and a reader who has just read “Paid members get the archive” meets it in the same column as that sentence — 20 px under the sub, left-aligned with it, the computed saving line 10 px beneath rather than beside, because a 440 px column has no room for both on one line. The note goes to the foot of the head column, 28 px under the switch, where it reads as a term of the offer rather than a caption to the last tier. Everything the site writes is in one column and everything Ghost supplies is in the other; that is the whole idea of the design and it is what the division control is protecting.
The tiers are 3 Stack's rows at a narrower measure — 792 px, so the name column is 220 rather than 300 and the action 160 rather than 200 — cited from 3 Stack rather than re-decided, with the row gap, the top alignment and the plane step unchanged. The head is top-aligned to the first row and does not centre against the column: a head that drifted down as a site added a tier would be a head that moved for reasons a reader cannot see.

Two divisions and no third. Head narrow 440 leaves the tiers 792 — enough for 3 Stack's three parts. Head wide 560 leaves 672, at which the row loses its middle column and the benefits go under a hairline inside each row, which is the tablet arrangement drawn at desktop width, and the sidebar says so rather than pretending the value is free. An even split is not offered: 632 px of head is a paragraph with a title on it, and 632 of tiers is 1 Cards with a heading beside it.
The divider is a hairline in the gutter and never a border on either column — it is 32 px from both, it runs the height of the taller column, and it is a border token, never an <hr>. It is off by default: the 64 px gutter and two different rhythms already say there are two columns.

1 · One <section aria-labelledby> holding two columns, not two sections side by side: the head names the tiers and a reader who tabs out of the switch is still inside the region the title labelled. The columns are a flex row and the DOM order is head then tiers, which is also the phone's order. 2 · The switch is two <button>s in a group labelled “Billing period”, aria-pressed on the active one, and it is the first tab stop in the section — before any tier action, at every width and both divisions. That is the argument for keeping it in the head: a control that changed the prices from below them would be a control a keyboard reader met after the thing it changes. Changing it moves no focus and announces nothing; the prices cross-fade and the pressed state is the record. 3 · The note is a paragraph at the foot of the head column and not aria-describedby any action — A6's rule, carried: “Cancel any time” is a fact about the offer, not a description of three links. EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub and note edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Tier names, prices, descriptions and benefits are Ghost’s: clicking one says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL opens the Link Picker — a subscribe ask is its Portal · Sign up action — and every action takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label. The cadence words and the computed saving line are catalog strings, not fields.
DATA · from Ghost · outside the design's listTiers come from {{#get "tiers"}} filtered type:paid+visibility:public in Ghost's own price order; the free tier is its own type:free object. A Ghost list: no Add, no Remove, no reorder. Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript, elsewhere in the section: both of a tier's prices render side by side, each labelled in words, with no switch — and no saving line, which is the switch's own arithmetic.
