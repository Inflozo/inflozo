Split Head

The head, sub, note and link in a 416 column at the left; the posts in the 856 beside it, two across. The only design in A17 whose archetype is split rather than grid-of-N, which changes its collapse ladder rather than just its arrangement — A8·6's split, inherited.

Moving the head sideways buys two things. The title can go to Large 40, because it no longer sits above the grid competing with the card titles — it sits beside them, and a 416 measure at 40 px is four or five words a line, which reads as a masthead rather than a headline. And the note and the link finally have somewhere to live: in every other A17 design they trail under the grid, where nobody reads them.

It costs one thing, and the panel is explicit about it: the head column is empty space when the head is short. A two-word title and no sub leaves 416 × 400 of nothing beside the posts, and this is the one design in the category that needs its optional fields.

HEAD STICKY ON · position: sticky · THE HEAD HOLDS WHILE A LONG SET SCROLLS PAST IT
Head Sticky is one CSS declaration and declares no behaviour module. position: sticky with a top offset needs no JavaScript, so the registry's sticky-adjacent modules are not involved and neither is scroll-spy. There is nothing to degrade: with JavaScript off it behaves identically, which is why field 9 still reads “None”. It only earns its place above Count 6. With four posts the grid is barely taller than the head and nothing sticks; the panel discloses that rather than disabling the control — “Sticky has an effect from about six posts.” Off is the default for that reason. Sticky is dropped at 834 and below, where the head sits above the grid and sticking it would pin a 40 px title to the top of a phone screen for the length of the section. It also does not run while editing — not because of a module, but because the editor's canvas does not scroll the section's own container. ⚑ Mine. The head sticks to the section, never to the viewport top under a site header. A17 sections know nothing about A1's headers, and a sticky that assumes one is a section reaching outside itself. A finding for the architect: the top offset ought to come from a theme-level token, and A17 has no way to read one.
