Inline Form

The banner with A3·4's one-row form standing where the actions would be. It settles §8's fourth question for the whole category: a banner asks for an address inline only where the address is the entire ask, and every other design links out to Ghost Portal instead. This design and 14 Members are the two that draw a field.

The row is A3·4's footer form at A4·11's height. The field is 46 px, not the footer's 44, because a form standing in the actions' slot has to be the height of the actions it replaced — A4·11 made the same departure for the same reason and this is the third place it holds. Field and button 10 px apart, the button inside the row, the note 2 px under it at 13 px. The field is on background inside a surface banner, which is why Surface is this design's default ground: a field needs to be one step away from what it sits on, and the packs define exactly one step.
The five email* fields are shared with the footer's form and the hero's — placeholder, button label, note, success and short success — so a site changes its subscribe copy once and A3·4, A4·11 and this banner all follow. That sharing is the reason this design has no primaryLabel of its own.

Tested against two live Ghost servers: Ghost’s sign-up address refuses a plain form submission. A form here cannot degrade into a working form, so the earlier promise that it posts natively was wrong and is withdrawn. What ships without JavaScript is this notice — the field and the button are not in the page at all, so nobody types an address that goes nowhere, and the link goes to a page that works. The states above are untouched: they are Ghost’s own script’s, and they apply the moment JavaScript is available.

Every value here is A3·4's, including the ones that look like omissions. No red: the seven roles contain no error colour, so an invalid address is said in words at text weight and the field's border goes to text rather than a colour nobody defined. The library's focus ring is suppressed on the field alone, because a 2 px ring 2 px outside a field that sits 10 px from a button touches the button. The button's width is reserved for the longer of its two labels — “Subscribe” and “Subscribing…” — so the row does not twitch on submit; there is no spinner. Done is session-lived: a reload shows the empty form, because Ghost's confirmation email is the record and a banner claiming otherwise would be lying to a second reader on the same machine.

INLINE FORM VERSUS LINK-OUT · the category's fourth settlement · 560 crop
INLINE · 6 AND 14 ONLY The address is the whole ask. The form is A3·4's, the five email* fields are shared site-wide, and the banner owns four states.
LINK-OUT · THE OTHER THIRTEEN A signup ask is a link to #/portal/signup, which opens Ghost's own modal over the page. The banner draws no field, no states and no success — Portal owns all three, and a second form that also posts to the members endpoint would give a site two places to write its subscribe copy and two ways to fail.
MEMBER SIGNED IN · NEW THIS PASS · default Show a members line You’re on the list — manage your subscription from your account. The form asks members for an address Ghost already has, so a signed-in reader gets a members line instead of the field — an editable sentence with this default, its link a Portal · Account action. The other value hides the section for members. Rendered on the server, as 14’s states are; nothing swaps after load.

A3·4's substitution, carried: the field is replaced by the button alone, the placeholder and note are kept in the editor, and the sidebar states the substitution and links to the Ghost setting. The section does not disappear and nothing is silently broken.

The rule in one line: a field belongs in a banner only where the banner exists to collect an address. A banner whose ask is “read the archive”, “see the pricing” or “join the thread” has somewhere to send a reader, and sending them is cheaper than asking them to type. Flagged as mine — the brief gives the Portal hand-off but not which designs draw a form, and every count in the library depends on the answer.

In dark the field is surface on background — the step runs the other way. In light the banner is surface and the field is the page's ground, one step back; in dark the ground is the darkest thing and a recessed field would be a hole, so the field lifts to surface and takes the border hairline. This is the design's one structural difference between modes, and it is the reason Ground Background is drawn here rather than Surface: at Ground Surface in dark, field and banner would be the same colour and only the hairline would separate them. MEMBER ASK AND NO JAVASCRIPT · PATCH PASS · 29 AUG 2026 The ask is conditional. Where the primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting rather than shipping a button that fails. An ordinary link is unaffected. Ruled by the owner, 29 August 2026: the button keeps its place and its scale, and the site chooses what it says and where it goes — a fallback label and link (fallbackLabel ≤ 24 · fallbackUrl, in the panel beside the ask), so a reader meets a working button rather than a gap. Left empty, this design’s own rule applies: no primary label or URL, no section. Where the ask opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real href to a Portal address, so the page stays where it is and no window opens. No JavaScript: Ghost's sign-up address will not accept a plain form submission — tested against two live Ghost servers — so the form needs JavaScript. Without it the form is replaced by the designed notice drawn above: one line telling the reader what happened and a link that works. The empty, focus, invalid, submitting and done states are unchanged — Ghost's own script applies those.
