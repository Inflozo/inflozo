Faces

Eight to twenty-four people, four to eight across, a circle and a name and nothing else. §8.3's team of twelve, and the design a site should be sent to when it has more people than roles worth reading. It draws no role and no bio at any setting — the only design in A12 that refuses the role.

It also carries the one exception to settlement 1 in the whole category. Everywhere else a person's photograph is alt="" because the name is drawn beside it. At Names None there is no name beside it — so at that one value, and only there, the photograph takes the person's name as its alt text.

Names None is the one value in A12 that turns alt text on. Settlement 1 says a person's photograph is decorative because the name is beside it; here the name is not drawn, so the photograph carries it — alt="Marguerite Okonjo", the plain name, never “photo of”. Without that the section would announce a title and then twelve empty images. ⚑ Mine, and A12-0's settlement 1 is amended to say so. The initials block cannot take alt text — it is not an image — so at Names None it takes aria-label with the name and stops being aria-hidden. Its letters remain decorative: a reader hears “Rosa Ferreira”, not “R F”. ⚑ Mine. Names None is not the default and the panel argues against it: “Twelve faces with no names is a photograph of a team, not a list of people.” It exists because a masthead under a hero sometimes only needs to say that a team exists — and because A11·10 Slim made the same case for logos. Refused at both Names values: a tooltip, a hover label, a caption on the circle, a role, a number, and a “+4 more” badge. Everyone authored is drawn.

10 DIRECTORY CARRIES THE SAME ACTION · THE TWO DESIGNS THAT REACH TWENTY-FOUR

At twenty-four authored faces, alphabetical order was a want with no lever. The old ruling — no alphabetical control, no sort by posts, no drag-to-reorder of a Ghost list — held against controls, and it still does: nothing here writes a value onto the section, nothing appears in the panel, and reorder stays the only stored order. What is new is an edit, and an edit is undoable, which is why it can be offered where a control could not. It is refused everywhere else in A12: in the other thirteen designs the authored order is the hierarchy, and a masthead sorted alphabetically is a masthead nobody authored. ⚑ Mine: offering it on these two designs only, and sorting on the name as typed.

ACCESSIBILITY · WHERE THE ALT TEXT COMES BACK At Names Under the face this design is 1 Grid without the role line: a <ul> of <li>, an <img alt=""> and one <p> a person, no headings, and one tab stop per linked name. At Names None the photograph takes the person's name as its alt text and the initials block takes an aria-label — the one exception to settlement 1 in A12, and the reason it exists is arithmetic: twelve alt="" images with no text near them is a section that announces its title and then nothing. The alt is the plain name, never “photo of” and never the role. ⚑ Mine, and A12-0 records the amendment. At Names None a linked person is an <a> around the image, taking its accessible name from that alt — the one place in A12 where the target is a photograph rather than a run of text, and the target is then 56 × 56 or 44 × 44, which is at or above the touch minimum in both axes. At Names Under the face the target is the name, as everywhere else. Stated in the panel, because it means Names changes what is clickable. One flat list of up to twenty-four items, no grouping and no skip link — A11·13's position: the list-level jump is already in the reader's software. Twenty-four names and no structure between them is a stated cost of choosing density; 15 Groups is named for a team that wants sections. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 at 14 px — the smallest text-coloured type in A12, and it is 14 rather than 13 because 13 px is the library's meta size and a name is not meta; note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1. Nothing moves at any setting, and at 400% zoom the wall is three across with names wrapping.

It sits in the Image Picker’s popover on photo — A14’s ruling adopted: where a photograph is cropped is content, so the focus is a field on the image and never a section control. Live on the circle, including the off-ladder 44 px at Count Eight, where a badly centred file is most visible because the face is smallest. Image focus is stored the same way. An extension of P0·3, whose Ghost card was read-only throughout.
EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. linkUrl opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the section link takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Per person the name edits inline under the face — and at Names None it is edited in the item row instead, because nothing is drawn to select: it is still content there, being the photograph’s alt text and the initials block’s label, which is why the field stays required in a design with no visible type on a person. At Source Ghost authors the name and the bio are Ghost-owned: the toolbar is replaced by the plain-text lock pill and clicking says “Edit in Ghost”. The Role override is ours and stays inline-editable.
