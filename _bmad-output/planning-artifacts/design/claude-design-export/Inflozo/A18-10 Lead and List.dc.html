Lead and List

The first post drawn large with its picture, the rest as ruled lines beneath it. A17·5 did this with a grid; this does it with a list, and the difference is what the followers are — cells there, single lines here, which is why the lead can be four times their size instead of twice.

The lead is the first post in the query's order, never a flagged one. A17 spent a whole settlement on featured and this design ignores it: the lead is a position, so the site controls it by controlling the order, and Newest first means the newest post leads.

The lead is 864 wide — two thirds of the content width — and the followers are the full 1,296. That inversion is the design's one real idea: the lead is bigger by being taller and narrower, not by being wider. A lead that spanned the full width would put a 1,296 px image above 1,296 px rows and the page would read as an image with a list stuck under it; at 864 the lead is an object sitting in the page's left two thirds and the rows run past it.
The lead's title is 34 and the followers' are 22 — a 1.55 ratio, against A17·5's 1.2. A list can carry a bigger jump than a grid because the followers are lines rather than cells, and a line does not compete for attention with a picture. The lead also gets the excerpt at 17 px and the followers get none.
Only the lead has a picture. No follower draws one at any setting — a second image in the list would make the lead an accident of size rather than the design. That is what separates this design from 2 Thumb Rows with a larger first row.
40 px between the lead and the first rule, against 20 inside the list. The lead is not a row and is not ruled off from the section; the first rule belongs to the first follower.
