Drawer-First

No nav in the bar, at any width. A wordmark, a labelled Menu button, search, one action — and a takeover that holds the whole navigation at display size. The trade is deliberate: the page loses at-a-glance orientation and gains a header that never runs out of room, the right trade for a publication with 14 sections and no obvious five. It is also the design that absorbs the worst content: the stress case from A1·0 fits here without folding anything.

The takeover keeps the bar in place and fills the rest with surface: the Menu button becomes Close, the wordmark stays put, and nothing about the page’s position is forgotten. Two columns of sections in the pack heading font at 30 px, secondary links beneath the second column at body size, and one featured post — the drawer is the only place in A1 that renders content, and it renders exactly one item. Grandchildren indent under their child here, as in every dropdown. The “Latest” eyebrow is the featuredHeading field, editable inline (P0·1); Show date and Show excerpt govern the post’s meta. With Nav children = From Ghost navigation, the “Sections” and “More” headlines come from Ghost’s “+” labels (≤ 5 columns; more than 3 hides the latest-post column; past 5 the rest fold under the last) and unprefixed items render as the plain-link row — ARCHITECT: registry addition (nav-transform).

ACCESSIBILITY · the takeover is the whole problem
1The toggle owns the relationship. button aria-expanded aria-controls pointing at the takeover panel, with its accessible name changing from “Menu” to “Close”. The visible label and the accessible name are the same string — no aria-label overriding visible text.
2Focus is trapped and returned. Opening moves focus to the search field, or to the first nav item when search is off. Tab cycles inside the panel; Escape closes and returns focus to the toggle. The page behind is inert and does not scroll.
3Reading order matches the columns. The takeover is one nav containing two lists with their eyebrows as list labels, then the secondary list, then the featured post as a separate region — so a screen reader hears “Sections, 5 items” rather than ten unrelated links. Column layout is CSS, never DOM order.
430 px items still need a hit area. Display-size links are tall but narrow; each row is a full-width target with 44 px minimum height, so tapping the gap beside a word still works.
5No hover-only route. The takeover opens on click, never on hover — the entire navigation would otherwise be unreachable to touch and unstable to a tremor.
SPEC · A1·6 DRAWER-FIRST · full text in “A1 Headers — Spec.md”
Content fields Shared A1 list — and the only design that uses all of it, since nothing has to be hidden for room: navItems with no cap, children two deep, secondaryNav, socialLinks, signInLabel, cta pair, searchEnabled, darkModeToggle. Adds featuredPost (bound in the Data group), featuredHeading (“Latest”, inline-editable), and menuLabel/closeLabel (defaults “Menu”/“Close”).
Data Ghost primary nav fills column one, secondary nav the row beneath column two. The featured block populates from the P0·5 sources (single-post mode: Latest · Featured · By tag · By author · Hand-picked); if the site has 0 posts the block is dropped and the columns re-centre. Empty primary nav → the Menu button is not rendered at all, because a takeover with nothing in it is a dead end; the bar becomes wordmark + search + action. Ghost-prefix mode builds the takeover from “+” groups (read-only lists — “Edit navigation in Ghost”; ARCHITECT: nav-transform).
Responsive · empty · a11y Closed: 1440–768 narrows only; ≤ 767 wordmark 17, short CTA label, Menu keeps its label at 13 px. Open: two columns ≥ 1024, one column below, featured post beneath the list at 834 and dropped at ≤ 767. a11y: see the annotated frame — expanded/controls on the toggle, focus trapped and returned, page inert, one nav with labelled lists, 44 px rows, click-only.
