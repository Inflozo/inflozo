Contrast Band

1 Rows on the pack's contrast ground. The arrangement is unchanged and the ground is the whole design — which is exactly the case the tuple's ground slot exists to record: the same rows on page and on contrast are two designs, because a band is a decision about the page and not about the list.

Everything the band needs beyond its two tokens is derived by mixing toward the band's own text colour, using A17·7's ratios without change: muted at 60%, hairline at 10%. Nothing here is a new colour and nothing is hand-picked, which is what lets the band survive twelve packs.

The band is full-bleed and the content inside it keeps the page's 72 px margin, so the rows sit exactly where 1 Rows' rows sit and only the ground has moved. That is the test of the design: put the two frames one above the other and no element shifts.
Three derived values do all the work. Text is the band's own #FBF9F5. Muted is 60% of that toward the band — #A5A29D. The hairline is 10% — #39362F. A17·7 established the ratios and this design reuses them without adjustment, which is worth stating because a list needs the hairline to be right in a way a grid does not — six rules on a dark band is the strongest test the 10% mix has had.
The link's accent lifts to the pack's dark accent, #E0805A. Paper's light accent on this band is 4.77:1, which passes AA and looks muddy at 2 px under a 14 px label; the dark accent is 5.73:1 and reads as the same colour family. A17·7's rule, reused: the band takes the other mode's accent.

Inset is an object: 1,296 px wide, the pack's radius, the page visible either side of it — closer to A17·17's panel than to a band, and the reason the tuple's ground slot still reads contrast at both values is that what the rows rest on has not changed. At Inset the content inset drops from 72 to 48. A 72 px margin inside a rounded 1,296 box is a wide frame around a narrow list; 48 keeps the text measure at 820 and the meta column at 200 with the rows simply closer to the band's edge. The band's vertical padding is unchanged at 96, so switching values moves the horizontal edges only. ⚑ Inset is the value that changes what the design is worth on a page. Two full-width bands stacked make a striped site; two inset bands are two objects on one ground. Three packs, one formula, no hand-picked values. Each band is the pack's own contrast token carrying its own on-contrast text; the muted and the hairline are 60% and 10% mixes of that text toward the band. The radius changes because the pack's radius token changes — 8, 6 and 14 — and nothing else moves. ⚑ This repeats A17·7's finding rather than adding one: the packs should supply on-contrast directly. Three designs in A17 derived it independently, and this is the fourth. A derived colour is a colour the pack's designer never approved, and the mix that works on Paper's warm near-black is doing something slightly different on Studio's blue-black. The hairline is where a bad derivation would show first. At 10% the rule is visible and quiet; at 15% it is a table; at 5% it disappears on a dark ground and survives on a light one, which would make the design's separators mode-dependent. The value is A17's and it is reused rather than re-tuned.

THE BAND'S OWN PADDING SCALE, AND WHY A CONTRAST SECTION CANNOT BE COMPACT Padding's three values are 80 · 96 · 132 here against the category's 64 · 96 · 132. A17·7 set this and the reason is the same: a band is visible before its content is read, and a 64 px band reads as a strip rather than as a section. The name is unchanged and the quantity is not — the third instance in A18 of a shared control name carrying a design-specific value, after 3 Slim's row heights and 2 Thumb Rows' thumb sizes. Excerpt's default is One line here and on every other A18 design that offers it. On a dark ground a two-line muted excerpt at 16 px is the largest block of A5A29D on the page, and muted text on contrast is the pack's least legible pairing at 5.32:1 — comfortably AA, and still the place to spend the fewest words. Cut: a band colour control. Contrast is one token and a section that could choose between contrast, accent and surface grounds would be four designs pretending to be one. Cut: an inverted-in-dark toggle — the band already inverts in dark, because the pack's contrast token does, and a control that fought the token would break rule 1 by hand.
