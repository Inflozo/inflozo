Slider

A8's one sliding design, and A8-0's third settlement built rather than ruled. It is A5·14 Scroller's rail carrying quote cards — a list in a horizontally scrolling box, every quotation in the tree at all times, proximity snap, A1·14's outlined arrows in the head, no dots, one card per press, bleeding past the right margin. Nothing about the mechanism is new here; what is new is that the thing being scrolled is a person's words.

Which is why the settlement matters: this is allowed under reduced motion because the reader drives it. The arrangement is unchanged, the arrow's animated scroll becomes an instant jump and snap is dropped — A5·14's own answer. Nothing in A8 advances on a timer, cross-fades between quotations on its own, loops back to the first, or animates a hint that you could swipe.

At rest, at scroll position 0: the left arrow is at 40% and unavailable, the rail starts at the page's left margin, and the fourth card is cut by the window. That cut is the affordance — A5·14's rule — and it is why the rail bleeds rather than fitting three cards neatly into 1,296. The cards are the shared quote card at Three Up's width and padding: 416, 28, quote Card 20, avatar 36, the 20 px gap fixed, no shadow, equal height across the rail.
Equal height across a rail is different from equal height in a row: every card in the rail is as tall as the tallest quotation in the whole set, not the tallest on screen, because a card that changed height as you scrolled would make the section jump. That is this design's cost and it is the reason its ceiling is twelve rather than the wall's — one 288-character quotation makes twelve tall cards. The sidebar says so at the count. ⚑ The set-wide equal height and its disclosure are mine; the rail, the cut card, the arrows and the missing dots are A5·14's.

Reduced motion does not remove the rail, and that is the settlement. A scroll a reader performs is not motion the interface imposed: the box still scrolls, the cards still sit in a row, and the two things that were animated — the arrow's smooth scroll and the snap's easing — become instant. If the rail advanced on its own, reduced motion would have to stop it, and the honest way to avoid that argument is not to build the timer.
The rail is itself a focusable scroll container with tabindex="0", so a keyboard user can scroll it with the arrow keys without tabbing through twelve cards, and tabbing to a card that is off screen scrolls it fully into view with its ring. ⚑ The reduced-motion answer is mine; the focusable container is A5·14's.

A phone is the width this arrangement is for: a 300 px card on a 390 px window leaves 70 px of the next one, which is the clearest cut in the set. Rail controls default to None here — a thumb does not need arrows — and where a site keeps them they move under the rail at 44 px square, because a 38 px target beside a 28 px title is neither large enough nor aligned to anything. The second frame is the cost, drawn honestly: one 288-character quotation sets the height of every card in the rail, so Peter's 41 characters sit in a card with 180 px of space under them. A site that meets this either shortens the quote or picks 5 Wall, and the sidebar says so.

1 · Every quotation is in the DOM at all times, in authored order, in one <ul> of figures — nothing is display:none, aria-hidden or lazily inserted, so a screen reader reads twelve quotations as a list of twelve and Ctrl-F finds the eleventh. This is the difference between a rail and a carousel, and it is A5·14's rule. 2 · The arrows are two <button>s labelled “Previous quotes” and “Next quotes” — plural, because a press moves the rail by one card and not to “quote 4 of 12” — with aria-controls on the rail. At an end the button is disabled and drawn at 40%: 6.3:1 light and 6.2:1 dark, still legible, and the state is real rather than a visual dimming of a live control. 3 · The rail is a focusable scroll container, tabindex="0", with an accessible name from the section's <h2>; arrow keys scroll it. Tabbing to an off-screen card scrolls it fully into view, ring included, 2 px outside the card with the gap taking the ground. No card is a link and no card is a button — the focus stop exists because the card can hold a link one day, not because a quotation is clickable. Motion · one transition per state change, 160 ms ease-out on the arrow's scroll. No autoplay, no timer, no loop, no swipe hint, and no cross-fade. Under reduced motion the scroll is instant and snap is dropped; the arrangement is identical. Nothing runs while editing. EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Every avatar carries Image focus — Centre · Top · Bottom in its Image Picker popover, never as a hidden field. The arrow labels are catalog strings — “Previous quotes” and “Next quotes” are the theme’s translation catalog, not fields and not English shipped in a template. The arrows keep A1·14’s glyph pair and are not Icon-Picker editable: they are the module’s affordance, not an action. ⚑
