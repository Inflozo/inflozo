A28 · Comments

Ten wrappers around a block the theme does not own. Ghost renders its comments UI into a sandboxed frame, in Ghost’s colours, at Ghost’s type scale, with Ghost’s focus rings — and the theme cannot reach inside it. So this category designs the four things that are ours: the heading, the count, the house rules, and the ground the block stands on.

Everything the theme owns is drawn at full strength in these frames. Everything Ghost owns is drawn inside a dashed outline at reduced opacity, in its own greys rather than the pack’s, because that is the truth about where the line falls.

CONTROLS RECONCILIATION · 25 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED, AND ON WHICH FRAMES
ELEVEN FRAMES CHANGED · THIS ONE AND ALL TEN DESIGNS Every control panel was rebuilt. Padding retired into Vertical spacing and 1, 2, 6, 7 and 9's Divider into Top divider, both outside each design's list; a Content group added for the authored strings; the Comments block group recut as the Data group; an Editing group added naming the P0 primitives; header line, footer count and a Reconciled paragraph rewritten on all ten. Two section frames redrawn. 9 Big Count — the zero state is now the 15 px count line, with the display-scale 0 kept as a second drawn card at At zero: Figure. 8 Prompt — a new drawn state for the signed-in free member under paid-only commenting, and its shared body neutralised so one string serves both prompt states. Every other section frame draws what it drew before, because nothing else in this pass changes what the site renders at the defaults. Category-wide, on every frame: the count's three words became fields, Match the page resolves to auto on a dual-mode site, rules[] settled at one to four, and the <noscript> line arrived — drawn once, below, rather than ten times. The control budget. The old 4–7 norm is lifted; the ceiling is the PRD's ~15 visible controls plus the universal trio and the Data group. A28 ranges four to six — 8 Prompt is the largest at six and nothing came close to the ceiling, so no design was cut to fit. Quick Controls are the 3–5 rows named on each panel's header line.
WHAT THIS PASS DID NOT DO No layout was redesigned. Not one measure, division, ladder or breakpoint moved. The two redraws are new states, not new arrangements. No module was coined. 7's accordion and 8's member-form remain the category's only two; the upgrade action is a Portal link and the <noscript> line is markup, so no frame carries an “ARCHITECT: registry addition” note. No Preview control was removed, because none existed — rule 7 is satisfied by construction. Member Visibility lands nowhere. Nine designs draw no CTA at all, and 8 Prompt already carries a richer member-state model that rule 9's own exception defers to. Rule 10's Image focus lands nowhere either: A28 authors no image. Rule 11's button icon lands once, on 8's two buttons. The boundary is unchanged and stated louder. No control anywhere claims to style inside Ghost's component: mode, saturation, and whether the section renders at all.
THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · EVERY ANSWER HERE IS INVENTED AND IS FLAGGED HERE AND ON EVERY FRAME THAT DRAWS IT ⚑
1 · The frame only — what the section draws, and what it hands over. The section draws a head, a count, one line or one list of house rules, and the ground. It then calls {​{comments title="" count=false}​} — the block’s own title and count are switched off in all ten designs ⚑, because the section has just drawn them and Ghost’s would be the second pair on the page. The count is {​{comment_count singular=countSingular plural=countPlural empty=countEmpty}​} ⚑, which renders whether or not the block does. The three words were literals inside that call until this pass — countSingular “comment”, countPlural “comments”, countEmpty “No comments yet” — and are now authored fields with those defaults, because a non-English site could not change them and 9 Big Count draws one of them at display size.2 · Signed out, free member, paid-only. Ghost owns all three inside the block and its sign-up prompt cannot be suppressed, so nine of the ten designs draw nothing extra in any of them — a second prompt above Ghost’s is two calls to action in 400 px. 8 Prompt is the exception: it replaces the block with the publication’s own invitation for signed-out readers, and pays for it by not showing them the thread. What Ghost renders to a free member when commenting is paid-only is not documented ⚑ and could not be verified; nine designs assume Ghost handles it inside the block. 8 Prompt no longer assumes it: at paid-only commenting a signed-in free member gets the prompt in upgrade form ⚑ — a per-state heading and button, the shared body, Portal's account/plans action through P0·4, and no email field — which mirrors the member-state pattern the owner ruled for headers. ARCHITECT: verify stays on that frame: the live check is still owed, and it decides whether the other nine need the same arrangement. That is finding 5.
3 · Where it sits. A28 sits below A26 Post Footers and above A27 Related Posts ⚑ — the author card and the share row belong to the piece just read, the comments belong to the reader, and the related set is the offer to leave. Every frame in this category draws A26’s tail above and A27’s set below at half opacity so the seam is visible. A28 draws its own top padding and cannot know what precedes it ⚑ — the same route-awareness gap A26 and A27 both raised.4 · What the design must not style. Everything inside the outline: comment type, spacing, avatars, buttons, reply threading, the editor, the moderation menu, error states, and the focus ring on every one of them. Two attributes and one setting are the whole of the theme’s reach — mode, saturation, and whether the section renders at all. The block’s accent is Ghost’s accent colour setting, not the pack’s accent token ⚑, which is finding 1 and the one that has to be solved outside the theme.

REPEATING ITEMS · ONE AUTHORED ARRAY IN THE WHOLE CATEGORY
There is exactly one field in A28 that is an array the user authors: rules[], the house rules. Two designs draw it as a list — 2 Split Head and 6 Rail — and both take item-count few for it. The other eight draw rulesText, a single authored line, which is not an array. The comments themselves are not the section’s items ⚑: they are Ghost’s, inside the block, and no control in this category can add, remove, reorder, style or count them.Add an item. The list is P0·3’s shared item controls, unchanged: “+ Add a rule” at the foot of the list in the sidebar. A new rule lands at the end of the list, reading “Be kind” ⚑ — real placeholder text, never an empty row — and opens for editing.Remove an item. The ✕ on each row, never disabled. Removing the last remaining rule sets House rules to Off ⚑ rather than leaving an empty list; the list returns with one rule when the user sets it back to List.Reorder. Drag by the ⠿ handle. Order is meaningful in both designs — they are read top to bottom and the first is the one that gets read ⚑.Minimum and maximum. One to four ⚑ — settled in this pass, where the old text said “two to four” in one breath and “one rule is legal” in the next. One rule is legal and is drawn as one bulleted line; the Add control disables at four with its reason shown — a fifth rule in a 240 px rail runs past the block’s first comment, which is where the design stops working.At zero items. House rules resolves to Off and the section draws no list, no bullet, no heading and no reserved space ⚑ — 2 Split Head becomes a head and a count in a 320 column, 6 Rail a label and a count in a 240 rail. Neither widens the block.The two architectural rules, checked. Every design control writes one value onto the section — there is no per-rule styling anywhere in A28, and nothing in the category wants one. Inside an item the user edits content only: the rule’s text, up to 60 characters, inline with the P0·1 toolbar, and nothing else. A rule has no optional field, so there is no empty-field state inside an item ⚑ — a rule with no text is a rule that gets removed.
THE NO-JS STATE · CATEGORY-WIDE · NEW IN THIS PASS ⚑ · DRAWN ON 1 RULE AND IDENTICAL IN ALL TEN

Ghost's comments-ui is a script with no server-rendered equivalent, so with JavaScript off the frame renders and the block does not — finding 3. Until this pass the section said nothing about it: a head, a count and a rules line stood over 900 px of nothing. One line of markup fixes it, and it is not a module.

JAVASCRIPT OFF · THE COUNT STILL RENDERS (IT IS HANDLEBARS) · THE BLOCK DOES NOT · THE <noscript> LINE STANDS WHERE IT WOULD HAVE BEEN
THE RULINGS ON ONE LINE OF MARKUP It is a translation-catalog string, not an authored field ⚑ — comments.noscript, default “Comments need JavaScript.”. Rule 5 asks which of the two every visitor-facing string is: this one is chrome for a failure state, a publication has nothing to say in it, and a site in Portuguese needs it translated once rather than typed into ten sections. It takes the muted text token at the count's size, on the section's own ground, at the position the block would have occupied. It draws no box, no icon and no border — it is a sentence, not an error state. No module, and no change to any behaviour declaration. 7's bar still opens and closes without JavaScript and now reveals this line; 8's form still posts, which keeps it the one A28 design that does its whole job with JavaScript off. Drawn once, here, rather than ten times ⚑ — the line is identical in all ten designs, sits in the same place relative to the head, and changes no design's arrangement. Each design's panel and spec name it; this frame is where it is drawn. It does not answer finding 3. Whether the frame should render at all when the block cannot is still the architect's call. This line makes the honest version of “it renders” readable instead of silent.
TOKENISATION PROOF · A28·1 RULE · THREE PACKS · LIGHT AND DARK · drawn at a 600 crop, the block compact

Six frames, one markup tree, only tokens changing. The hairline is border, the label text-muted, the count text-muted, the rules text-muted, the head text, the ground background, the radius the pack’s. Nothing moves between packs — not the 720 measure, not the 26 px under the hairline, not the 36 px above the block.

And the thing this proof cannot prove: the block inside the dashed outline is identical in all six ⚑. Ghost’s greys, Ghost’s type, Ghost’s radius, Ghost’s accent. A pack change re-skins everything above the outline and nothing below it, which is finding 1 drawn rather than described.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · SEVEN · THREE OF THEM ARE PLATFORM FACTS RATHER THAN DESIGN GAPS · 7 IS NEW IN THIS PASS
1 · The block’s accent is Ghost’s, not the pack’s. Ghost’s comments UI takes its accent from the site’s accent colour setting. A theme in the Tangerine pack with a Ghost accent of blue shows a blue Reply button in every A28 section ⚑. Inflozo must write the pack’s accent into Ghost’s accent setting at deploy, or the category cannot keep its tokenisation promise. This is the one finding that has to be solved outside the theme.2 · Nothing inside the block can be styled or audited. Type scale, spacing, avatars, buttons, focus rings and contrast are all Ghost’s, inside a sandboxed frame. The category’s accessibility statement and its pack conformance both stop at the dashed outline ⚑, and every design says so in its own words rather than implying a coverage it does not have.3 · No JavaScript means no comments. All 31 registry modules degrade; Ghost’s comments-ui is not one of them and has no server-rendered equivalent. With JavaScript off, all ten designs render their frame and no block ⚑. 7’s bar still opens and closes, and 8’s form still posts — those two are the only A28 behaviours that survive. Worth a decision: whether the frame should render at all when the block cannot.
4 · Is a plane a ground or a containment? A25·3 Sheet called a raised plane card · surface. A26·3 and A27·4 called the same thing a ground with containment none. A28 follows the later reading ⚑ — 3 Panel is stack · none · surface — but the two readings cannot both be right and the reconciliation pass has to settle one. It affects the tuple of every plane in the library.5 · The free member under paid-only commenting. Ghost’s docs state the block appears when comments are enabled and the reader has access to the post. What is rendered to a signed-in free member when commenting is set to paid-members only is not documented and was not verifiable ⚑. All ten designs assume Ghost owns that state inside the block. This needs one live check before build, and if Ghost renders nothing, 8 Prompt’s arrangement becomes the pattern the other nine need too. Amended in this pass: 8 Prompt now designs the state rather than assuming it — the prompt in upgrade form, with a per-state heading and button and Portal's upgrade action — and the frame keeps its ARCHITECT: verify flag until the check happens.6 · Route awareness, again. A28 cannot know that an A26 footer sits directly above it, so the two paddings sit adjacent — 192 px at Comfortable. The third category in a row to raise it, and the same mechanism resolves all three.7 · The universal Background role has no “inherit” value. New in this pass ⚑. 10 Slim's ground is transparent — it declares none and shows whatever it is dropped onto, which is the whole of its difference from 1 Rule — and the universal control offers Background · Surface · Contrast, all three of which paint. The row is locked at Inherit on that design with the reason shown, but the value does not exist in the product yet: either the control gains it, or A28·10's ground slot has to change and the design loses the reason it exists. A28 is the first category to need it; every category with a transparent design will need it next.
