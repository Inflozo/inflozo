Big Type

A title at Display 48 on a 1,040 measure with three marks at Huge 64 beneath it, or four at Large 48. The category's loudest design, and the only one where the type is louder than the artwork — which makes it the answer to a question the other fourteen dodge: what a logo wall looks like when the sentence above it is the point.

It is the only design in A11 that offers Display 48, the second and last to offer Large 40, and the second to use the off-ladder Huge 64. Three logos is its floor: two marks under a 48 px line is a headline with a footnote.

The title takes a 1,040 measure rather than 780, because 48 px on 780 is four words to a line. That is the only measure in A11 that is not 780 or 620, and it exists for this size alone. Head to wall is 64 rather than 48: a Display line needs the extra 16 to stop reading as a caption for the marks. Cells are 1 Row's 416 at three and 306 at four, on the standard 24 px gutter.
Huge 64 in a 416 cell gives a 392 px max width, so every mark in the set is decided by the box rather than by its width — which is why Count Three is where this design works and why Huge 64 is unavailable at Count Four, where 282 px of max width would decide the height of every Wide mark. The sub is kept and not drawn: a 17 px paragraph between a 48 px line and a 64 px mark is read by nobody. ⚑ Mine: the 1,040 measure, the 64 px head gap, and refusing the sub.

Display 48 is offered here and nowhere else in A11. §2 allows one display moment per section, and in fourteen designs the artwork takes it — a 48 px line above a wall of 36 px marks would be two display moments arguing. Here the title takes it and the marks answer at 64, which works because a logo at 64 is a shape rather than a voice. ⚑ Mine, and the reason this design exists. The title ladder in this design is Large 40 · Display 48, with 40 the value for a long title. A 104-character title at 48 on 1,040 is three lines and 158 px of head; at 40 it is two lines and 106. Alignment Centred centres the title on its measure and leaves the wall alone, because the wall's cells already span the content width — the same rule 1 Row states for Head Centred. A linked mark's target here is the cell — 416 × 64 at Count Three — which is the largest in A11 outside 5 Cards. That is a by-product of the size rather than the point of the design: Big Type is chosen for the sentence, and a site whose logos are links should still choose 5 Cards. The panel says both. At ≤ 767 both title sizes draw at 28 and both logo sizes at 32 — the one place in A11 where two pairs of control values draw the same thing, which is A10·15's finding repeated for a different reason: 28 is the largest a 104-character title can take in a 350 px column without becoming six lines, and 32 is the largest box a 163 px cell can hold without every mark being decided by width. The panel states it rather than disabling either value, because the setting still means something at wider widths. Two across, short row centred, row gap 28.

ACCESSIBILITY · A DISPLAY LINE THAT IS STILL AN H2 The title is an <h2> at 48 px, not an <h1>. A section is a section however loud its type: the page's <h1> belongs to the page, and a wall of logos is not the reason a reader is there. Size is not level — the same rule every category in this library has held. Structure is 1 Row's exactly: <section aria-labelledby>, the <h2>, a <ul> of three or four <li>, each an <img> with a required alt. Switching between this design and 1 Row changes two type sizes and nothing a screen reader can detect. At 200% the title reflows on its measure and the wall holds three across; at 400% the title is 28 and the wall is two across, which is the mobile drawing. A 48 px heading at 400% is 192 px of type and it wraps rather than overflowing, because the measure is a max-width and not a fixed width. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, eyebrow and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1, hover fill 1.2:1 / 1.4:1 decorative. A logo takes no ratio, and at Huge 64 the editor's 3:1 advice matters least in the category — a 64 px mark survives a weak contrast that a 22 px one does not, which the field says. Reduced motion changes nothing. Print draws the title at its size and the wall as laid out; a 48 px line and three 64 px marks is 340 px of a printed page, which is the tallest single section in A11 on paper.

LOGOS · the item list · outside the design’s listLogos is the P0·3 item list. Add logo arrives with content — the editor’s placeholder tile, a 3:1 wordmark reading “Add a logo” at this design’s box height, alt seeded “Publication name”, the file picker open; an item still holding the tile is not published and the counter reads “11 of 12 placed”. Remove is never disabled; drag to reorder; per-item content only. Per item: logo req · logoDark opt · alt req ≤ 60 · url opt. Image focus is P0·9’s shared control — both axes, and the values are not restated here; it sits in the Image Picker’s popover on both image fields and is disabled here with its reason shown — nothing in A11 is cropped, so there is no discarded edge for either axis to choose: the file is drawn whole inside its box. Ghost never sees it: focus is a hint the compiler resolves, not a data binding. Add logo stops at twenty-four: “Twenty-four marks is the most a wall holds.” Past four the item is held — “4 of 6 drawn”, 3 Grid advised — because this design refuses a second row rather than wrapping.
EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. linkUrl opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the section link takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. alt is edited in the item row rather than on canvas, being the one required string this category never draws. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing answers “Edit in Ghost” — every word is authored in this section and every mark is a site upload. The sub is authored and never drawn here, so it is edited in the sidebar rather than on canvas.
