Fifteen ways to say “read this one”

A17 divides a page between posts and A18 gives one post a whole row. A19 gives one post a whole section — and the moment it does, three things that were free in the other two categories become decisions: what “featured” means, what happens when nothing is marked, and what a spotlight does when the picture it was built around is not there.

The content is still Ghost's. There is no repeater, no Add, no Remove and no drag handle in the category; the section is handed a query, Ghost answers it, and the designs draw what comes back. A17's post card, tag plate and Data group, A18's row and hairline vocabulary, A6's focus ring and A1's section head and meta row are inherited unchanged. What A19 adds is the spotlight: a post drawn at display scale, with a call to action on its own link.

Controls-reconciliation patch — 25 August 2026. The fifteen were audited against the PRD's control vocabulary and Ghost's verified data surface, and this frame carries the category-wide items: the universal trio outside every control list (Background role · Vertical spacing · Top divider, with every per-design Padding row retired into the second of them), the Data group in place of the Posts block — P0·5 “Populate from…” configured for posts, with Source: Hand-picked and Count as a 1–100 stepper — Tag: Show · Hide on the eleven designs that drew a tag with no way off, linkUrl deleted from all fifteen sidebars, the excerpt bound to excerpt everywhere except 13 Quote, and no invented reader-visible string left in the category. The shared editor primitives are P0's and are reused by name, never redrawn.

1 · What “featured” reads from, and what happens when nothing is marked. It reads Ghost's own featured boolean — the star in the post list — through the Data group's Source: Featured only, which is A19's default and is set for the user when the section is added. Nothing else is a source of truth: there is no A19 pin and no A19 star. ⚑ Amended by the reconciliation pass: there IS a per-section picker. Source: Hand-picked takes 1–5 posts through the Ghost-aware post picker, stores references and draws them in picked order — the old refusal (“the section cannot own a fact about a post Ghost already owns”) is overruled, because which post THIS section spotlights is a fact about the section, and Ghost's featured star is global: one hero and one featured grid cannot star independently today. Picking is not authoring, so every other refusal stands. ⚑ When nothing is marked, A19 does not quietly substitute the latest post. A section that says Featured over a post nobody chose is a section that lies, so the default is to hide — and because “hide” is occasionally the wrong answer for a home page that must not have a hole in it, the choice is the Data group's last field: When nothing matches — Hide the section · Show the latest post. It sits in the block rather than in a design's control list because it is a property of the query, and it is therefore identical in all fifteen and counts toward none of their totals. ⚑ Ghost cannot order featured posts by hand — so from this pass Hand-picked is the running order, and Order is disabled there because the drag order is the order. At every query Source the old rule holds: Order is a rule and never a sequence — Newest first · Oldest first — and the panel of every design that draws more than one carries A18·11's advice in A19's words: “For a fixed running order, tag the posts and use Oldest first.” shuffle is refused category-wide: a spotlight that changes on refresh is not a spotlight, and a reader who returns to find a different lead cannot tell whether the site changed or they misremembered. 2 · One spotlight versus three, and the hierarchy between them. ⚑ Amended: Count is the shared 1–100 stepper with per-design bounds — locked at 1 on nine designs and at 2 on 10 Pair, drawn disabled with the reason (A19's convention, and the right UI for it); min 1 · max 3 on 8 and 9, max 8 on 11 and 15, max 10 on 12. The ladder it replaces was 1 · 2 · 3 · 5, against A17's 3 · 6 · 9 · 12 and A18's 5 · 10 · 15 · 25 — the third ladder in three categories, and the clearest statement yet that count is a per-category enum. Nine designs draw exactly one post and lock Count at 1, showing the field disabled with the reason rather than hiding it. Six take more: 8 Lead and Two and 9 Alternating at 2 or 3, 10 Pair locked at 2, 11 Lead and Rail, 12 Picks and 15 Carousel at 3 or 5. Where a design draws more than one, exactly one is the spotlight and the rest are followers — the lead keeps the picture, the 34 px title, the excerpt and the call to action; a follower gets a 20 px title and a date. Hierarchy is size, never decoration: no design marks its lead with a chip, a fill, a border or a colour it does not give the others. Two designs are deliberately flat and say so — 10 Pair and 15 Carousel — and no design has two display moments.
3 · A featured post with no image, which is the common case. Four designs draw no picture at any setting — 6 Big Type, 12 Picks, 13 Quote and 14 Slim — so the question does not arise for them, and the vocabulary covers eleven. Reflow is the default answer and the one a site hits most, because the split designs are the ones most sites choose. 4 · Zero, one and many, and whether the section hides itself. Zero → the section does not render, unless the Data group's When nothing matches says otherwise: no head, no empty state, no sample post, and for 5 Contrast Band, 13 Quote and 14 Slim the band, the panel and the strip go with it. Zero in the editor → the design's outline at one lead plus one line naming the cause: “No posts are marked featured. Star a post in Ghost, or change Source.” ⚑ One → every design draws one and none apologises. The six multi-post designs each state what one looks like, and a design short of posts drops its follower column entirely rather than leaving a hole: 8 Lead and Two at one post is its own 848 lead with the follower column absent ⚑, 11 Lead and Rail is a lead with no rail, 12 Picks is a list numbered 01 and is weak, 15 Carousel is one slide with no dots and no arrows. Many → the section takes exactly Count and never paginates. There is no More link, no next arrow and no A34 attachment: a spotlight that offers to show you the rest is a list, and A18 is one click away in the Design picker with the query preserved. The count is the query's, not the design's. No A19 design has a control that adds or removes a post; where a design's arrangement changes with the count — 8, 9, 11, 12, 15 — Count drives it and the panel's count line reports what happened: “5 posts are featured. 3 drawn.”

Tuple uniqueness — the honest statement. All fifteen are distinct on the five closed slots, which A17 managed for nine of eighteen and A18 for nine of fifteen. The reason is not that A19 was designed more carefully; it is that a spotlight has more to vary. A list has one column, one ground and no containment, so its designs collide on the slots and rest on the emphasis phrase. A19 spends grounds (page, surface, contrast, image), containment (a card twice) and media placement (six of the seven values) because the whole category is about where one post sits.
