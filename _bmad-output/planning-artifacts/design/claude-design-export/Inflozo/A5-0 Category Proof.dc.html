A5 FEATURES · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

What the sixteen inherit, before any of them is drawn.

The four things §8 asks A5 to settle, the ladder all sixteen designs draw, the rules none of them restates, the media matrix, the roster and the shared field list. A5 is the first category whose subject is a repeated item rather than a single composition, so most of what follows is about the item: how many of them fit a row, what sits at the top of one, whether it is a link, and what it looks like when a user gives it a title and nothing else.

A5's frames draw the section alone on the page ground. A4 drew the site header above every hero because a hero sits under one. A feature section has an ordinary section above it and another below, so a header over these frames would say something false about where the section sits. Where a design's rule concerns its neighbour — 16 Panel's inset, 13 Spotlight's foot — the neighbour is drawn and the caption says so.

1 · THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · SETTLED BEFORE DRAWING

The questions §8 puts to the category

Every answer below is invented — the brief asks the questions and supplies none of the numbers. Each is flagged here, on the frames of the designs that draw it, and in the spec document, in the same words.

SETTLEMENT 1 · COUNTS · 2, 3, 4 — AND WHAT 5 AND 7 DO

The three counts are three designs, not one control with three values. 1 Three Up, 2 Two Up and 3 Four Up are separate entries in the Design picker, because §3·2 says the arrangement is the design and the number of columns is the arrangement. Switching count is switching design with the content preserved, which is the whole reason the shared field list exists. No design in A5 has a Columns control. What the item count changes is only which cells are filled.

A short last row stays left-aligned and keeps its column width. Two items under three do not widen to fill the row and do not centre themselves in it. The alternative — centring the remainder — reads as a second, smaller composition under the first, and it moves every item on the page when a user adds one more. Fewer items than the design's count divides the row by the item count instead. Two items in 3 Four Up are two half-width items, not two quarters with half the row left empty. This is the one place the arrangement is not the design's, and the editor says so on the frame rather than silently. Twelve items is the cap. Past twelve the editor asks for a second section rather than growing this one; a thirteenth item is refused with the reason named. A5 is a feature section, and a list of thirteen is a page. Flagged as mine: the three named counts, Three as the default, the left-aligned short row, the step-down under the column count, and the twelve-item cap.
SETTLEMENT 2 · MEDIA · AND WHETHER A PACK SUPPLIES ICONS

No pack supplies icons. The library ships one icon set and every pack draws it: 40 line glyphs at 24 px on a 1.5 px stroke, in text unless the design says accent. A pack changes the glyph's colour and its container's radius, and nothing else. A pack that shipped its own set would make the same section a different design in twelve places, which §3·1 refuses.

Media is one control with three values — Icon · Picture · None — and it is section-wide. A row of items where two carry photographs and one carries a glyph has no rhythm, so the choice is made once for the set. One stated exception: 9 Bento has two media controls, one for its large tile and one for the small ones, because an 852 px tile and a 410 px tile want different things — and it is the only design in A5 that does. An item with no icon of its own keeps its slot and shows the fall-back mark — a 10 px accent square at the pack radius. It is deliberately abstract: a question mark or a broken glyph tells a reader about the site's editing, and the row's rhythm survives either way. An item with no picture in a Picture section becomes a text tile: the item's crop filled with hover surface, carrying the item's title at 19 px, and the title is then not repeated below it. The crop holds, so the row holds. Crops are never controls. Each design states its own and keeps it at every width. Flagged as mine: the whole of it — the library icon set and its 40 glyphs, the four container treatments, the fall-back mark, the text tile, and the section-wide rule. The frames draw the glyph as a plain geometric mark; naming forty glyphs is not this pass's work.
SETTLEMENT 3 · IS AN ITEM A LINK · AND WHAT HOVER DOES

Item link is one control with three values — None · Text link · Whole item — and the three are exclusive, which is why they are one control. A card cannot carry both a whole-item link and a text link inside it: a link inside a link is not a thing a keyboard can describe.

Whole item is offered only where the item has an edge — a card, a tile, a panel row or a full-width row. A bare grid item standing on the page ground offers Text link and None, because a ground that changes with no border to contain it reads as a rendering fault rather than a hover. One stated exception: 8 Media Top offers Whole item at its Item plane None, where the item has no card but does have a picture, and the picture is the edge. The hover, in full: the plane steps surface → hover surface, the title takes a 1 px underline at a 3 px offset, and a picture inside it darkens 4%. No scale and no lift — A4·13 dropped A3·9's 1.02 for the same reason, and a grid of six items rising under the pointer moves more of the page than it explains. 160 ms ease-out, one transition. The text link is not accent. 15 px/600 in text with a typed → that moves 3 px on hover, and the underline appears on hover rather than resting. Six accent links in one section would spend the pack's one accent six times; §2 allows it twice. Flagged as mine: the three values and their exclusivity, the edge rule, the ground step, the underline offset, the 4% darken, the refusal of scale and lift, and the text link's colour and arrow.
SETTLEMENT 4 · A TITLE WITH NO BODY · AND 90 CHARACTERS OF TITLE
Print quarterly no body · the item ends here
A printed collection posted four times a year, anywhere in the world Sixty pages, sewn, on paper chosen for reading rather than for weight. 67 characters · three lines at Three across · one under the four-line cap
Body is optional per item and the item simply ends. Items are top-aligned in their row and nothing stretches text to make two items the same height. Where the design has a plane the planes stretch to the row's height so a row of cards is one band; the text inside stays at the top. Where no item in the set has body copy the row gap tightens one step — 40 px to 32 at 1440 — and nothing else changes. A grid of bare titles at a body-copy rhythm reads as six items with something missing. The title wraps to four lines at Two and Three across, three lines at Four across, and then clips, with the full title in the DOM — a measure rather than a count of lines in the abstract. The editor advises about 60 characters at Three across and says why; it never enforces it. 92 characters at Three across is four lines at 21 px, which is drawn on §7's stress frame. Body clips at four lines in a grid and is unclamped in 6 Rows and 13 Spotlight, where the item has a whole row to use. Flagged as mine: the title cap of four lines at Two and Three across and three at Four across, the 60-character advice, the four-line body clamp and its two exceptions, the plane-stretch rule, and the gap tightening.
2 · THE LADDER · SET ONCE HERE, DRAWN BY ALL SIXTEEN

The head, and the item

A5 has two type systems in one section: the head, which is one composition, and the item, which repeats. The item title's size is not a control: it steps with the column count, because the measure it has to fit is what changed.

3 · CATEGORY-WIDE RULES · TRUE OF ALL SIXTEEN, NOT REPEATED PER DESIGN

What every feature section inherits

PADDING · GAPS · MARGINPadding Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132 at 1440, 80 at 834, 64 at 390 — A4's values unchanged. Item gaps 32 px between columns and 40 px between rows at 1440; 24 and 32 at 834; 28 px stacked at 390. Page margin 72 / 40 / 20. Everything on the 8 px grid.
HEADINGS AND LANDMARKSThe section is a <section aria-labelledby> named by its own title, an <h2>. Item titles are <h3>. The items are a list — <ul> of <li> — because a feature set is a count of things and a screen reader should say how many. With no section title the section is not a labelled region and the item titles step to h2.
GROUND AND THE ACCENT BUDGETGround is a control on most designs — Background · Surface · Contrast — and 10 Contrast Band is the ground rather than offering it. Accent is spent once or twice: an icon container at Accent, or one section action, or a tab's active underline. Never on every item.
THE SECTION ACTIONA5 carries at most one section-level action, under the set, centred with a centred head and at the left margin with a left one. It is A1·1's button at its own size — accent fill, 14 px/600, padding 9×17 — not A4's hero scale: a button in the middle of a page is not the page's first ask. A row of actions is A6's.
RESPONSIVE FLOOREvery count is one column at ≤ 767 unless the design names another arrangement — 3 Four Up keeps two where its body copy is hidden, 11 Checklist keeps two, 14 Scroller keeps its rail. At 834 Four becomes two and Three becomes two; Two stays two. A design that changes arrangement at a width states the width in words.
DATA · EMPTY · MOTION · CONTENTNothing in A5 comes from Ghost. Features are authored — there is no feature object in the API, and every number a reader sees here is typed by the site owner. No items authored → the section does not render and the editor names the field. Motion is hover only at 160 ms ease-out, except 12 Tabs. Orbit Weekly content throughout.

Three things to notice. The radius token reaches the card and the icon container together, and at 14 the container is a squircle while at 6 it is nearly a square — the container inherits the card's radius rather than carrying its own, which is why nothing has to be re-decided per pack. The hover-surface tint is the token that carries the container, and it is the one A5 leans on hardest: six containers a section, three grounds deep on 4 Cards at Tinted, and every pack has to supply a value one step from its surface — Tangerine's is warm, Ink's is cold, and neither needed a rule. And the accent is spent once, on the section action, which is what makes Ink's blue survive at 4.8:1 against its page: it never has to work as a link colour or a rule. A5's two designs that spend the accent per item — 11 Checklist's mark and 15 Index's numerals — are the two the packs will pressure hardest, and both offer a value that uses no accent at all for exactly that reason.
