A17 POST GRIDS · DESIGN 12 OF 18 · PAPER PACK · SIX CONTROLS

Bento

Five posts in a fixed arrangement of deliberately unequal cells: one tall at the left, two wide stacked beside it, two square beneath. Not a grid that flexes — a named layout that holds exactly five posts, and advises in both directions when it is given a different number.

It is the only design in A17 with a fixed post count. Every other design takes whatever the query returns and arranges it; this one is a composition, and a composition with a hole in it is broken. Under five it hands to 2 Two Up; over five it draws the first five and suggests 1 Three Up — both disclosed at the picker, and A17-0's roster has been corrected to match.

Which makes it the design that most tests A17's premise that the user does not author the set. A five-cell composition wants five chosen posts; what it gets is a query. The panel is explicit about how uncomfortable that is, and about the one mechanism — a tag the site controls — that resolves it without a repeater.

Two 636 columns and three rows, on the 24 px gutter in both directions. The tall cell spans rows 1 and 2 in the left column; two wide cells stack beside it; two squares fill the third row. Every measure comes off A17's own ladder — 636 is the Two division, 795 is 636 at 4:5, 358 is 636 at 16:9, and the squares are 636 at 1:1 — so nothing here is a bespoke width, which is what separates a bento from an art-directed one-off.

The arrangement is fixed, so the count is fixed too. Four posts leaves the third row half empty; six has nowhere to go without inventing a fourth row and a new shape. Five is the only number this composition holds, and the two directions are not symmetrical. Under five it advises — the cells with no post are not drawn, and the panel may advise 2 Two Up, two 636 cells at the same measure with no hole — because four posts cannot fill the composition at all. Over five it does not switch design: it draws the first five and suggests 1 Three Up in the panel, because a site with twelve matching posts still wants its five-cell front page. One piece of advice, one truncation, both disclosed at the picker, and the roster says the same. ⚑ Exactly five is a hard requirement, and it is the finding this design raises. Count in the shared Data group offered 3 · 6 · 9 · 12 before the reconciliation pass — none of which is five. So this design needed a fifth value, a per-design control, or a forced five that ignored the control. The pass chose the first: Count is one stepper, 1–100, and this design's is locked at 5 with the reason drawn beside it. I have specified that it forces five and says so — “This layout shows five posts” — but that is a real breach of “the block is identical in all eighteen”, and the architect should choose between the three rather than inherit my default.
THE COMPOSITION-VERSUS-QUERY PROBLEM · drawn at 600 crop
TALL · HAS AN IMAGE WIDE · PLATE WIDE · PLATE
THE UGLY CASE · TWO IMAGELESS POSTS IN THE TWO WIDE CELLS · 16:9 PLATES ARE LETTERBOXES
A composition assigns meaning to positions; a query fills positions in date order. Those two facts do not cooperate, and this is where the tension is sharpest in A17. The tall cell goes to whatever post is newest — not the best-photographed, not the most important — and the two wide cells take posts two and three whether or not they have images. The mechanism that resolves it needs no new machinery: Source By tag, with a tag the site controls. A17-0 already names this as the answer to “I want to choose these five” — a tag such as Front page on five posts, newest first. The site then owns the composition through Ghost's own mechanism rather than through a repeater the category forbids. The picker says so: “Works best with a tag you control.” It still does not let a site say which post takes the tall cell, only which five are in the set — order decides that, and order has two values. ⚑ Closed by the platform, 29 August 2026 — a Ghost template cannot look across a loop to find which item is flagged, so the tall cell is the first post in the set’s order and Order decides which post that is. The old gap read: featured is the natural input for “this one takes the tall cell”, and I have not used it, because five designs already read that flag to mean a two-column span, and making one flag mean two different geometries is worse than leaving it unread here. A finding for the architect rather than a decision I should take alone.

SIX CONTROLS, AND WHY A SEVENTH WOULD BREAK THE COMPOSITIONTwo findings close here: the forced five is now expressible as a locked stepper, and featured has a use as the tall cell’s input.Tag and “View all” link added; Padding retired into Vertical spacing. The brief allows four to seven and this design sits at the floor. The arrangement is the design, the count is fixed, the cells' ratios are fixed by their shapes — so what remains is which side the tall cell sits on, how much excerpt to draw, how much meta, and the section's padding. Four. Every candidate fifth control I considered would have changed a cell's shape, and a cell's shape is the composition. Cut: Image ratio. The three ratios here are structural — 4:5 for the tall, 16:9 for the wides, 1:1 for the squares — and they are chosen so that the two columns' images come out close: two wide cells and their gutter are 358 + 24 + 358 = 740 px against the tall image's 795, a 55 px difference the right column absorbs because it carries two text blocks where the left carries one. The cell boxes themselves are equal automatically — a row-spanning grid item is as tall as the rows it spans, so the third row starts level whatever the ratios are. What the ratios buy is that the two columns' photography ends at roughly the same place, which is what the eye reads; get them wrong and one column's text floats in space. A ratio control would break that pairing, which is why this design has none. ⚑ Mine, and the reason this design has no ratio control while every other image-bearing design does. Cut: Per row and Columns. Two columns is the composition; three is a different one, and it is 5 Lead + Grid. Cut: a cell-count control (“five cells / seven cells”), which is two designs wearing one name. Excerpt All cells is offered and warned about. Two lines under each square cell adds about 60 px to the third row and pushes the section past 1,800 px tall — the note says which: “All cells makes the two square cells much taller.” Tall cell only is the default because the excerpt is part of how the tall cell is promoted, and giving every cell one flattens the emphasis this design exists for.
