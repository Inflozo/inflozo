Bento

One tile at four times the size of the others, with the rest filling the cells around it. It is the design for a set with one feature that matters more than the others and no wish to say so in words, and it settles two things nothing else in A5 does: an unequal grid whose cells are filled by item order, and a section where the large tile carries a photograph while the small ones carry glyphs.

Three 410 px columns and three 190 px rows on a 32 px gap. The first item takes two columns and two rows — 852 × 412 — and the rest fill the remaining cells in the order they are written: items two and three down the third column, items four to six across the bottom. All six tiles are 4 Cards' Hairline plane, unchanged. The large tile's picture takes whatever height the tile has left after its text, which at six items is 208 px on 852 — a 4:1 crop, and the one picture in A5 whose shape follows the tile rather than a named ratio, because the tile's height is set by the small tiles beside it. The large tile's title is 28 px, 7 Alternating Media's; the small tiles' are 19 px, 3 Four Up's, with body at 15 px and two lines. Flagged as mine: the 190 px row, the 2 × 2 span, the fill order, the picture's derived crop, and borrowing two title sizes from two other designs in one section.

Item order fills the cells and nothing is re-arranged to make a row look balanced. Six is the count this design was drawn for and the editor says so; below three it is refused with 4 Cards named, since a bento with one small tile is a large tile and a mistake. Above six the grid grows downward in rows of three, left-aligned, exactly as settlement 1 requires — and past nine the editor advises 4 Cards, because a 2 × 2 tile stops reading as the emphasis when eleven others surround it. Flagged as mine: the three-item floor, the six-item intent, the nine-item advice, and the downward growth.

MIXED MEDIA · settlement 2's one exception
Media is section-wide everywhere in A5 but here. This design has two media controls: Large tile media (Picture · Icon · None) and Small tile media (Icon · Icon in a tint · None). A photograph in a 410 × 190 tile is a 24 px band above two lines of text, and a glyph on an 852 × 412 tile is a 36 px mark in a field of white — the two sizes want different things, so they get different controls. The exception is stated in A5-0's settlement 2, on this frame and in the spec, in the same words, and it is the only one in the category. Every other design's Media control governs all its items. Flagged as mine: the exception, both control lists, and the decision that Picture is not offered on the small tiles at all.

The large tile is drawn hovered and the third small tile focused. One hover for both sizes: the plane steps to hover surface, the title underlines, a picture inside darkens 4%, nothing scales. A tile that grew on hover would push its neighbours out of the grid, which is the strongest version of A5's no-scale rule and the reason it holds here without argument. Focus is the 2 px accent ring 2 px outside the tile, as 4 Cards.

WHY THERE IS NO GROUND CONTROL
The tiles are the surfaces and the page is what they sit on. On a surface ground every tile would need 4 Cards' step-up, and six lifted tiles on a lifted ground read as one block with seams rather than six things — which loses the arrangement this design exists for. On contrast the same is true twice over. 10 Contrast Band is the inverted feature section, and it is a flat three-across grid for exactly this reason. The editor names it. Flagged as mine: dropping Ground and the named destination.
