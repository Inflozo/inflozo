Panel

The whole list inside one surface panel — a single plane holding every row, with the dividers inset to the panel's padding. 8 Row Cards gives each post a plane; this gives the section one, and the difference is what a reader counts: five objects there, one object here.

It is the only design in A18 whose containment is card and the only one whose ground is surface. A17·17 established the panel on a grid; the geometry, the two panel edges and the head-outside rule are taken from it unchanged.

The panel is the full 1,296 with 48 px of inset, so the list runs at 1,200 and the text measure is 760. A17·17 set the 48 and this design keeps it: the panel's inset is the one number that makes a container read as a container rather than as a border, and it is deliberately larger than the row's own 24 px of vertical padding.
The dividers stop at the panel's padding and never touch its edge. A rule that ran to the panel's border would cut the plane into compartments; inset dividers separate rows inside one plane, which is the whole distinction between this and a table. There is no rule above the first row or below the last — the panel's own edge closes the list, so Rule Boxed is drawn as Between rows here and the panel says so.
⚑ The tag plate is not reachable and the hover surface is: at Panel Fill the rows sit on surface, so a hover fill would need a step above white, which Paper does not have. The hover state is the title's accent underline alone, as everywhere else in the category. A17·17 recorded the same context-dependence for its plate; here it is a hover rather than a plate, and the finding is the same one.

WHAT THE PANEL IS FOR, AND THE PAGE IT BELONGS ON · drawn at 600 crop
THE CASE FOR ONE PLANE A panel says “this belongs together and is separate from the page”. That is worth saying when the list is a sidebar-ish object in a page of other things — a “More from this author” block, an index at the foot of a long page, a members-only reading list. It is not worth saying when the list is the page, and on an archive route this design is the wrong one: a panel wrapped around the only content on the page is a box around everything. The panel's cost is 96 px of width and the page's left edge. The rows are 1,200 rather than 1,296 and they start 48 px inside the margin every other section aligns to. Head Outside buys back the head's alignment; the rows' is gone for good, which A17·17 named as the honest argument against a panel and which holds here. ⚑ One post in a panel is 70% empty plane — A17·17 measured the same case and reached the same number. The panel suggests Panel Outline at a count of one, which halves the visual weight of the container, and does not switch designs.
REUSED HERE, AND FROM WHERE Panel geometry, edges and Head Outside — A17·17, unchanged. The row — A18·1, at a 760 measure. Meta column — A18·1, at its full 200. Section head — A1. Focus ring — A6. THE ONE CONTROL IN A18 WITH A MISSING VALUE, AND WHY MISSING IS BETTER THAN DISABLED Rule has two values here and three everywhere else. Boxed adds a rule above the first row and below the last, and inside a panel those two rules sit 48 px from the panel's own edge — a line parallel to a line, which is the definition of a mistake. The value is absent rather than disabled: a greyed-out option invites a user to wonder what they did wrong, where a two-value control simply reads as a two-value control. ⚑ Mine, and it differs from the library's usual practice of disabling with a reason shown — which is for values that fail contrast, not for values that make no sense. Padding sets the space around the panel and the panel's own inset is fixed at 48. Two nested paddings both called Padding would be unreadable in a sidebar; Row density is the control that changes the space a reader feels inside, and it does so by moving the rows rather than the panel's wall. ⚑ At Panel Outline the sm shadow is dropped, and in dark it is dropped at both values. A17 found near-black shadows invisible on a dark ground; this design and 8 Row Cards are the two in A18 that lose something in dark, and here what is lost is the panel's separation from the page. Paper's surface step carries it; a pack with a weaker step would need the hairline to do all the work, which it can, at the cost of the plane reading as an outline in dark and a fill in light.
