Contrast Band

1 Three Up inverted onto the contrast token, at the band's own vertical scale. Same cells, same numerals, same item rules, separated from it by ground alone — the pair A13-0's roster describes, and the only design in the category with a ground that is not the page.

A13's version of that pair costs nothing at all, and the reason is the finding this design exists to state: the category's item is type. A11 needed a second logo upload for a dark band and A12 needed a derived initials fill; a numeral, a title and a paragraph need one token pair and no uploads of any kind. It is also the one design in A13 where the accent is disabled with its ratio shown.

How a pitch becomes a Thursday letter

Six steps, about three weeks from the first email to the send.

  1. 01Send the pitchTwo paragraphs on what happened, why it matters now, and who you would talk to. No attachments.
  2. 02Two editors read itEvery pitch is read twice in the Monday meeting. You hear back either way within eight days.
  3. 03Agree the shape, the length, the deadline and the fee before any reporting startsA commissioning editor writes it down and sends it to you. Nothing is agreed on a call alone.
  4. 04Report and fileDraft into the shared document. Your editor comments in the margin rather than rewriting.
  5. 05Edit and checkA line edit, then a check against your sources. We may come back to you with questions.
  6. 06It goes out

↓ THE PAGE BELOW · THE BAND IS A background-color ON THE SECTION AND NOTHING ELSE

The band is a background-color on the section, and every colour inside it is derived from one token pair. contrast #232019 carrying #FBF9F5; muted is the carried colour at 72% — #BEBCB7 in light and #57544D in dark, A12's derivation carried over; a rule would be 18%. Nothing in this design needs a second upload, a plate, a scrim or a re-tint, because the item is a numeral, a title and a paragraph. That is the finding, and it is the cheapest ground swap in the library so far.
The accent is unavailable on the band at 2.3:1 in light and 2.1:1 in dark, and the section link's underline takes the carried colour instead. §7.4 applied: the value is disabled with its ratio shown rather than silently allowed. Band padding is the band's own scale, 44 · 64 · 88 — A4·9's, inherited — and it replaces the section Padding control rather than sitting beside it, which is why this design has six controls and not seven.

Band width: Full bleed · Inset. Full bleed runs the band to the viewport at every width and keeps the content on 1,296, so the cells are 1 Three Up's exactly — 416 or 306. Inset draws the band at 1,168 with the pack radius and 64 px of page each side, and the cells narrow to 372 or 274 inside it. The same list therefore draws smaller at Inset, which is a section value rather than an item one, and the panel states the cell width at both. Everything else is 1 Three Up's, without exception. Two to six steps, three or four per row, 48 px row gaps, the short last row at the left, the numeral rendered from position and aria-hidden, no picture at any setting, seven or eight → 14 Index. That is the point of the pair: a site switching between the two keeps every step, every control value that exists in both, and every line break. Rule Between columns takes the derived 18% hairline — #3F3B34 on the light band — rather than the pack's border token, which would be invisible. It is the one element in the design that is not simply the carried colour at an opacity the type already uses. ⚑ Mine, and the 18% is A12's figure reused. Print and forced colours both drop the band and the section becomes 1 Three Up. A band is a ground and a page has one; forcing an inverted ground onto paper spends ink on nothing. Stated here and on the frame in the same words.
