THE POST'S FEATURE IMAGE, IN THE ARTICLE · C POST BODY'S SURFACE, NOT THIS SECTION'S
⚑ FEATURE IMAGE IS INERT IN THIS DESIGN · THE FIELD STAYS IN THE PANEL, GREYED, WITH ITS REASON · WHETHER THE ARTICLE DRAWS THE PICTURE IS C POST BODY'S DECISION AND A24 DOES NOT MAKE IT — A FINDING, NOT A BEHAVIOUR
Seven of the sixteen designs draw no picture and all seven mark Feature image inert. That makes inert the block's most-used deviation by a distance, and it is worth saying plainly rather than burying it in seven panels: a post header is not obliged to show the post's photograph, and half this category takes that position. The finding this design raises is not its own. C Post Body does not currently state whether it draws feature_image at the top of the article, and three A24 designs assume it can — 2, 8 and 10. If the answer is that it cannot, those three designs lose the post's picture rather than relocating it, which is a different and worse claim. The architect should settle it; the design does not change either way.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES An inert block field is drawn, not hidden. ⚑ The row stays in the panel at the muted-border step with "Not used here" in the value slot and one line of reason beneath. Hiding it would be worse in the exact case it matters: a user switching from 1 Centred, where the picture was showing, needs to see where the control went, and an absent row reads as a bug in the editor rather than a decision in the design. Five controls, and the missing sixth is Alignment. This design has no alignment value: it is flush left and that is its name. Offering Centre would produce 1 Centred at a 720 measure — two designs that can become each other are one design with a control, and the picker is where a user changes their mind. Rule carries four values here and three in 1 Centred, and this is the design that needs all four: with no picture and no centring, the hairlines are the composition's only edges. At None the header floats on the page and relies entirely on the 96 above and the 64 below, which is a legitimate look for a site whose nav already ends in a rule — the panel's line names A1·1 Rail as the case. Cut: a drop-cap toggle. The first letter of the article is C Post Body's, not this section's, and a header that reaches into the article to style it is a header that breaks when the design is switched.
