One card per newsletter, side by side, each with its own name, description and field. The second answer to the multi-newsletter question, and the opposite of 10 Choice’s.

Where 10 Choice asks a reader to tick and type once, this asks them to choose by typing into the one they want — which suits two letters with genuinely different audiences and suits four badly.

ONE, TWO AND MORE NEWSLETTERS
1 newsletter → one card at the grid’s left, no stretch ⚑, and the panel names 2 Card as the design meant for one. The section is not switched.2 is what the layout is built for. 3 → three of 416; 4 → two rows of two ⚑.5 or more → the panel names 10 Choice ⚑, which holds any number in one field. Five cards is five forms and no reader fills in five.

The states are per card, not per section ⚑ — this is the one design in A22 where two of them can be on screen at once, one done and one empty. Card heights are equalised by the grid, so a done card does not shorten beside an empty one ⚑; the taller of the two states sets the row.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESEvery visible authored text edits inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored: the section’s eyebrow, heading and blurb, the button label and the note — shared across both cards, there being no per-card copy. The note is the consent line and now takes the link mark ⚑ — “…you agree to our Privacy Policy” links through the Ghost-aware Link Picker, which is what the field was missing. The five state strings are edited in the state that shows them through the P0·6 editor state switcher — Empty · Invalid · Done · Subscribed · Members off — inline, in place, never a sidebar preview row ⚑. The subscribe button takes an optional icon before or after its label from the P0·2 Icon Picker, with its size and colour-role popover. The endpoint, the Portal preferences target and the member count are Ghost’s and are never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking one says “Edit in Ghost”, and each newsletter’s name, description and visibility — including the generated meta line, which says “Edit in Ghost”. Translation-catalog strings ⚑ — “Subscribing…”, “Use a different address” and the visually-hidden “Email address” label: theme catalogue, not fields.
BEHAVIOUR · MEMBER-FORM · GHOST’S FORM CONTRACTmember-form, edit-safe. The form speaks Ghost’s own contract ⚑ new in this pass: data-members-form="subscribe" on the <form>, data-members-email on the field and data-members-name when Ask for a name is on. Ghost’s members script drives loading, success and error, and member-form maps those three onto the designed seven. The native POST to /members/api/send-magic-link/ stays as the no-JS floor. member-form is declared once and binds both forms ⚑; with script off both post natively and independently.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLESFive controls of its own after this pass. Cut: a per-card colour or accent (per-item styling is not expressible ⚑), a “recommended” badge (Ghost has no such flag on a newsletter ⚑), a card-order value (Ghost’s own order is the order ⚑), and a shared-field value — two cards with one field between them is 10 Choice. A paid-only card is the one exception to the pair being identical ⚑: its field goes and its button becomes the Portal route.There is no Add and no Remove. The cards are Ghost’s newsletters; the section cannot create, hide, reorder or rename them ⚑.Where the address goes is the shared group below: which newsletter, whether a name is asked for, what a signed-in subscriber sees, who sees the section at all ⚑ new in this pass, and what happens when members are switched off. The form always posts to Ghost ⚑ — the endpoint is read-only.
