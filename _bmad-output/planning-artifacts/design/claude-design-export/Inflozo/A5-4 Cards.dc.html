HAIRLINE · the default · surface, one border, sm shadow TINTED · hover surface, no border, no shadow · the icon's tint inverts to surface RAISED · surface, md shadow, no border · a hairline instead of the shadow in dark

Three treatments and one rule between them: a container inside a card steps away from the card, not away from the page. On Hairline and Raised the card is surface and the icon's tint is the pack's hover surface; on Tinted the card is that hover surface, so the icon's tint becomes surface and the step runs the other way. It is one rule, and it is why Media and Card treatment are two controls that cannot be read separately. Raised carries the md shadow in light and a hairline in dark — A4·18 Overlap Card's decision, carried verbatim, because a warm shadow on a near-black ground is invisible and a card with no edge in dark is not a card. Flagged as mine: the three values, the inverting tint, and reusing A4·18's dark substitution here.

Settlement 3, drawn on the design that owns it. The plane steps one value — surface to hover surface at Hairline and Raised, hover surface to surface at Tinted — the title takes a 1 px underline at a 3 px offset, and nothing scales, lifts or gains a shadow. Raised keeps its md shadow unchanged on hover: a card that rises when a pointer crosses it is the trope this library refuses, and a grid of six of them moves the whole page. 160 ms ease-out, one transition. The focus ring is the library's, drawn on the card's edge rather than the title's, because the card is the link.

Settlement 4's plane rule, drawn: the planes stretch to the row's height and the text stays at the top of each. The shorter card is not padded out, its title is not centred vertically, and its body is not stretched — the empty space at the foot of a card is the honest shape of an item with less to say, and a row of cards with ragged bottom edges is the alternative. This is the design the rest of the category points at for the rule.

1 · At Whole item the card is one <a> and its accessible name is the item's title. The <a> is inside the <li> and wraps the h3 and the paragraph, so the heading is still a heading and still navigable by heading — the pattern where the link wraps the card and the title is a plain span loses that. 2 · The body is inside the link and read after the title. That is the trade: one target instead of two, at the cost of a longer name when a screen reader reads the whole link. Nothing is aria-labelled over it — a card whose spoken name differs from its visible text is worse than a long name. 3 · Icon aria-hidden, inside the link, first in the DOM. At Card treatment Tinted its own fill inverts to surface; the glyph's contrast is measured against whichever fill it lands on and holds at both. 4 · Focus is on the card, not the title — a 2 px accent ring 2 px outside the card's edge, so it clears the hairline and the shadow. One tab stop per item, six for the set, and the tab order is the DOM order. The exclusivity is a11y, not taste. Item link is one control with three values because a text link inside a whole-item link is a link inside a link — invalid HTML that browsers repair unpredictably and that keyboards cannot describe. The panel disables rather than warns. Contrast · item title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · body 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 on the card's own fill at every treatment · focus ring 3.1:1 against surface, which passes the 3:1 non-text minimum · card against page 1.1:1 light and 1.2:1 dark, decorative, with the hairline carrying the edge. Reduced motion removes the 160 ms transition and the states change instantly. The states themselves do not change: hover still steps the ground and underlines the title.
