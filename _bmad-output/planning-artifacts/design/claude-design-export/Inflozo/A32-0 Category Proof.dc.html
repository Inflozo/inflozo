A32 Paywall / Content CTA — the category

Twelve designs for one element: the thing that renders mid-article where a members-only post stops. Each draws four gates — free signup, paid, upgrade for a signed-in free member, and a post sold to one named tier — from one field list, so a publication picks a design and gets all four.

The fact to read first is what the browser has. Ghost sends the free preview and nothing below it. The fade and the blur cover text the reader already has; the withheld text was never in the response. Every frame in the category carries that line, and no control in any design moves it.

This frame carries what belongs to the category rather than to any one design: the four questions §8 asked, the cut anatomised, the tokenisation proof, the stress frames, the roster with its twelve structural descriptors, the shared field list, the component inventory and the findings for the architect. The per-design frames are A32-1 … A32-12, and the written specification is A32 Paywall - Spec.md.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS §8 ASKED, ANSWERED
1 · THE CUT · WHAT FADES, WHAT BLURS, AND WHAT WAS NEVER SENT
The fade is a fixed-height gradient of the page ground over the last visible block — 96, 160 or 240 px, never over the gate and never over withheld text ⚑, because there is no withheld text in the page to cover. The fade belongs to the last visible block, not to the section ⚑. That is what makes it a stylesheet rule — p:last-child, ul:last-child, ol:last-child — rather than a script, and it is why a heading, a figure, a code block or an embed gets no fade at all ⚑: fading a heading promises a sentence that does not exist, and A19’s rule forbids tinting a photograph. Blur is offered and is labelled as decoration ⚑ — 2.6 px on the last visible paragraph, whose words stay selectable and stay in the page source. The truncation is the protection; the blur is a picture of one, and the panel’s help text says exactly that.
2 · FREE-SIGNUP VERSUS PAID-UPGRADE, AND THE COPY FOR EACH
Four gates, and Ghost picks which from post.visibility ⚑. Free signup — “Keep reading with a free account” · “Sign up free”. Paid — “The rest of this piece is for members” · “See membership”. Upgrade — “This one is for paying members” · “Upgrade”. Named tier — “This piece is for Patron members” · “See Patron” ⚑. All four sets are fields; the words above are defaults. The free gate is the only one a theme can complete itself ⚑ — an email address and Ghost’s members endpoint — which is why the email-field control value exists only there and is greyed on the other three with the reason shown. No design states a price it was not given ⚑. With many tiers the paid gate reads “From $6 a month”, with one it reads that tier’s price, and with none the price clause disappears rather than defaulting to a number ⚑.
3 · THE PORTAL HAND-OFF, AND THE SIGNED-IN FREE MEMBER
Every action opens Portal ⚑ — #/portal/signup for the free and paid gates, #/portal/signin for the secondary link, #/portal/account/plans for the upgrade. The theme draws the trigger and Portal draws the rest, over the page. A30’s boundary, carried verbatim. A32 does not draw the PORTAL ↗ tag ⚑. A30’s account rows carry it because they look like settings; these are calls to action, and a tag beside a primary button reads as a warning. The destination is annotated on every frame in mono, outside the section. A signed-in free member on a paid post gets the upgrade gate ⚑: no sign-in link, greeted by name where Ghost has one, and pointed at the plans panel. On a members-only post the same reader has access and A32 does not render at all ⚑ — there is no unlocked state to design, and no design may draw one.
4 · A PARAGRAPH, A HEADING, AN IMAGE · AND A25’S OPEN CONTRACT
After a paragraph the fade runs at its control value and the gate’s padding is the ladder’s. After a heading the fade is suppressed and the space above the gate grows ⚑ — 72 in the boxed and carded designs, 132 in 9 Big Type — so the heading is never read as the gate’s own title. After an image the fade is suppressed absolutely ⚑; two designs recommend another design at that landing (7 Tiers and 10 Cover) and neither forces it. A25’s finding 8 asked A32 to state what it expects to be handed ⚑. It is three things: the article’s ground or box closed above the cut (three A25 designs already do it by rule), the page ground unchanged at the seam, and the last visible block’s element type reachable from CSS. The third is the one that is not settled ⚑ — :last-child works only if Ghost’s truncation leaves the last block as the last child and not inside a wrapper. A32 asks the build for a class on the article naming the last block’s type, and that is a finding rather than a design.

THE FIVE HARD CASES, AND WHAT EACH DESIGN DOES
A preview of one sentence. A publication can put the cut in the first paragraph. The fade still runs at its set height ⚑ — CSS cannot count lines, so nothing fires by itself — and over 30 px of type a 160 px gradient reads as a grey block, which is why the panel advises Short 96 or None at this landing. The gate’s padding is unchanged, so the page reads as a very short article followed by an offer. A preview that ends mid-sentence. It is the normal case, not an edge one: Ghost cuts at a card boundary and the author wrote a paragraph that runs on. No design adds an ellipsis ⚑ — the fade is the punctuation, and an inserted “…” is the theme writing in the author’s voice. A 104-character heading. It wraps in eleven designs and is refused at 60 in 9 Big Type, which is the only enforced limit in the category ⚑. A member with no name on the upgrade gate → the greeting drops to “You are signed in on the free plan”, never “Hello, friend” ⚑. A30’s finding, carried: most members have no name. One included line where three are expected → the row does not stretch and the hairline stays ⚑.
REFUSED CATEGORY-WIDE · EACH WITH ITS REASON
An overlay or modal over the preview ⚑ — there is nothing to cover. An overlay says the article is behind it; the article was never sent. It would also trap a reader who wants to re-read what they did get. A blur over withheld text ⚑ — same reason, and it is the misconception the anatomy frame exists to kill. “2 of 3 free articles this month” ⚑ — Ghost has no metering, so the number would be invented. No module counts reads and none should be requested for it. A countdown or a limited-time price ⚑ — the registry’s countdown is for a deadline a publication authored, not for pressure a theme manufactures at a paywall. A card form ⚑ (Stripe’s, and PCI’s) · a member count or “join 4,000 readers” line ⚑ (not queryable) · a permanent dismissal ⚑ (a paywall a reader can hide forever is a paywall a publication cannot use) · an accent left border on a box ⚑ · a per-item control of any kind ⚑.

TUPLE UNIQUENESS · THE HONEST STATEMENT
All twelve are distinct on the five closed slots ⚑, and three slots do the work. Containment separates 1, 2 and 5 — none, card, box — and ground separates 1, 3, 4 and 10 — page, surface, contrast, image. Those six are deliberately the same components six ways, which is the case those two slots exist for. Archetype separates the four that share none · page · none · none: 1 is article body, 9 is stack, 12 is bar, 6 is split. Item-count separates 7 and 8 — few queried tiers against many authored rows — and media placement separates 10 alone, at background. Containment is none in ten of twelve ⚑. 7 Tiers’ cards are the items’ geometry, not the section’s — A21·2’s rule, and the slot most easily got wrong.
WHAT THE CHECK CANNOT PROMISE
9 Big Type and 1 Fade differ on archetype and on nothing a machine can see ⚑ — what actually distinguishes them is scale, and no machine reads scale. 12 Meter’s bar claim rests on the meter being the arrangement rather than an ornament above a stack; that is a judgement. 3 Panel and 11 Sticky Bar are both surface grounds and are separated by archetype alone. 2 Card and 5 Boxed are one design in two containments — a real and visible difference at rest, a thin one written down. Each design’s panel names the ones it is closest to, by number ⚑, and says which control value would turn it into its neighbour. That is the honest version of a uniqueness claim.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · EIGHT, SIX OF THEM NEW
The fade and the blur protect nothing, and the product copy must say so ⚑. Both are drawn over text the browser already received. The protection is Ghost’s truncation, which is real and total. This is a documentation finding rather than a design one, and it is first because it is the thing a publication will get wrong.A25’s finding 8 is answered, except for one part ⚑. A32 expects the article’s ground or box closed above the cut, the page ground unchanged at the seam, and the last visible block’s element type reachable from CSS. The third needs the build’s help: :last-child only works if Ghost’s truncation leaves the last block as the article’s last child. A32 asks for a class on the article naming that type — one class, set server-side.Whether post.reading_time is computed on the whole post or on the truncated preview must be verified ⚑. 12 Meter depends on it entirely: if it is the preview, the meter always reads full and the design cannot be built as specified. It is the only design in A32 with a single-point dependency.Whether Portal can be opened at a named panel — #/portal/account/plans — from a themed link needs verifying ⚑. A30·11 raised it; here four designs’ upgrade gates assume it. If it cannot, the upgrade action opens Portal’s default panel and the member has to find plans themselves, which is a worse gate rather than a broken one.
There is no metering in Ghost and A32 does not ask for one ⚑. “Two free articles left this month” is the commonest paywall pattern on the web and the number would be invented. It is refused category-wide rather than left unmentioned, so it is clear the omission is a decision.An overlay paywall is refused for a structural reason, not a taste one ⚑ — an overlay claims to cover something, and there is nothing under it. If a thirteenth design is ever requested it should not be that, and this is where to look first.The registry already names A32 #12, and this pass rewrote what it is honouring ⚑ — reading-progress’s degradation read “A32 #12’s meter renders at its server-known static value”, which assumed a value a template cannot compute. The contract is now split in two: the words are a static line built from post.reading_time and need no script, and the proportional fill is the module’s — drawn from the reader’s scroll, hidden on a very short post, and the only thing that disappears with JavaScript off.
