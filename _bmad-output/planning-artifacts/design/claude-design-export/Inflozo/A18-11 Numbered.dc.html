Numbered

A numeral in a 64 px column at the leading edge of every row. It is the only <ol> in 485 designs, and the only place the library makes a claim about sequence rather than just drawing things in an order.

⚑ The number is a position, not a rank. Ghost does not tell a theme what is popular, and nothing in this section can. A site that means “our ten most read” must build that order itself — with a tag, or with the query — and the panel says so in as many words rather than letting the numeral imply something the data cannot support.

The numeral is the title's size and the meta's colour — 22 px in the heading font, text-muted, tabular figures. That pairing is the whole design decision: at the title's size it is structural, and at the meta's colour it is quiet. The alternatives were drawn and cut — a 13 px numeral is a footnote, a 48 px numeral is a countdown, and a numeral at text competes with the title it is labelling. ⚑ Mine.
64 px holds two digits with 24 px of gap after them. Tabular figures are what make the column work: without them “01” and “11” are different widths and the titles do not line up, which is the same requirement A17·10 Ledger raised for its figures and could not state at pack level. This is the second design to need it, and it is now a pack requirement rather than a design's preference.
The date column narrows from 200 to 140 because the numeral has taken 88 px from the row. 140 holds “26 February 2026” at 13 px with 32 px to spare; the author's name is dropped from Meta's default rather than the date — a numbered list is about sequence and the date is the fact that supports it.
The head is different content here, and that is deliberate. “Seven pieces to read before the rest” is what a numbered list is for; the archive head every other A18 design carries would make the numbers look like an accident of chronology.

Zero-padded is the default because a list of ten looks like a list of ten. “01 … 10” is a set; “1 … 10” is a sequence that grew. At Show 5 the padding is still two digits — the numeral's format does not change with the count, or a site adding a sixth post would see all five renumber. Plain left-aligns its single digits rather than right-aligning them into the column. Right alignment would put “1” under the “0” of “10” and make the column read as a ledger; left alignment keeps the numeral attached to its row. The titles still align, because the column is fixed at 64 either way. Cut: Roman numerals, letters and a leading hash. Roman is a typographic costume; letters imply a lettered index the content does not have; “#1” imports a ranking claim the section cannot support — which is the same reason the panel's note says “position in the list, not a ranking”.

The numeral counts the drawn list, from the top, always. It is not a post's position in the archive and not its position in the query before Show cut it — it is where the row sits on this page. That is the only definition a section can honour without knowing anything the theme does not have. Which means the same post can be 01 today and 04 next week, and at Order Newest first it will be. A site that wants a stable numbered set uses a tag as the source and Oldest first as the order, so new posts join the end rather than displacing the top. The panel's note points at the tag and does not enforce it. ⚑ This is the design most likely to be read as a ranking, and nothing prevents that. A reader who sees a numbered list of posts assumes an editor put them in that order, and at Newest first no editor did. The honest mitigation is the head, which the site writes — “Seven pieces to read before the rest” makes the claim the numbers imply, and the panel's placeholder head says as much.

WHERE THE PANEL GIVES ADVICE ABOUT THE QUERY RATHER THAN ABOUT THE DESIGN “For a fixed set, use a tag and Oldest first.” That line sits in the Data group, not in this design's controls, and it is the only place in A17 or A18 where a design's needs reach into the shared query panel. It is advice rather than a constraint, and it is there because the numeral is the one drawn element whose meaning depends on the query's settings. A number size control was drawn and cut. Small, Medium and Large numerals — 15, 22 and 34 — looked like three designs rather than three settings: a 34 px numeral beside a 22 px title inverts which one the row is about. One size, tied to the title's, keeps the relationship fixed however the pack scales its type. ⚑ The numeral is not accent-coloured, and that is a refusal worth recording. Accent on a numeral is the most obvious thing to do with it and the library spends accent twice a section — on a hover underline and a focus ring. Seven accent numerals down a page is decoration, which §2 rules out in as many words. Mine, and the design is quieter for it. Cut: a thumbnail. A number, a picture and a title is three things at a row's leading edge; the numeral has taken the position a thumbnail would occupy, and 2 Thumb Rows is one click away for a site that wants the picture instead.
