A6 CTA BANNERS · DESIGN 12 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · TWO ASKS, NOT ONE ASK TWICE

Pair

Two asks side by side, each with its own words and its own action. It is the answer to the second half of §8's question about one action or two: where the two things are genuinely alternatives rather than an offer and its fallback, they are not a primary and a secondary — they are two columns, and this is the only design in A6 that spends the accent twice.

Two accent fills, and the section says why. §2 spends the accent once or twice per section and A6's own rule is once; this design spends it twice because the two actions are the same offer at two prices, and making one of them a ghost link would tell a reader which to pick. That is a decision the site makes with its words, not one the design should make with its colour. Where the two asks are not really alternatives — a subscribe and a “read about us” — the editor names Actions Text link, which spends no accent at all, or 1 Centred with a secondary.
The eyebrow sits above the pair and is the only thing that does. There is no section title: a title over two titled columns is three headings in 400 px, and the columns are the asks. The eyebrow is 13 px tracked at the page margin, 32 px above the cards.
The columns stretch to the taller of the two and their text stays at the top — A5·4's row-stretch rule, carried — so the shorter card's action sits where its own text ends rather than being pushed to the foot. Two asks of unequal length are honestly unequal.

1 · This is the one design in A6 with no section heading. The eyebrow is a paragraph, not a heading, so the <section> is named by aria-label instead of aria-labelledby, and the label is the eyebrow's own text — the only place A6 uses aria-label on a section. With no eyebrow the section is not a labelled region and the two h2s stand alone. 2 · The two columns are a <ul> of two <li>, each carrying an <h2>: a reader hears “list of 2 items” before the first ask, which is exactly what the section is. Not a table, no role="group", and nothing comparable across them — A7 Pricing and Tiers owns anything a reader is meant to compare feature by feature, and the editor names it where a site starts writing matching lists in both columns. 3 · Two <a>s, one per column, in DOM order, and neither is marked as recommended, default or featured: no aria-current, no badge, no “most popular” ribbon. Visible labels as whole names, which is why both include their price or their absence of one. Contrast · titles 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · bodies 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · buttons 4.6:1 / 8.1:1 · divider 1.2:1 / 1.3:1, decorative. Zoom: at 200% text the columns stack in the phone's arrangement, with the horizontal hairline where the vertical one was. Forced colours keep the cards' borders and the divider; at planes None the two asks are separated by space alone and forced colours leave them so, which the specification states rather than fixes.
