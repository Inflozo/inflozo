A8 TESTIMONIALS · DESIGN 12 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · NO HEAD, NO PLANES, NO ACCENT

Rows

Quotations as rows between hairlines: the words at the left on a 780 px measure, the attribution in a 300 px column at the right, and no title, no eyebrow, no card and no shadow. It answers the question the quote card raises by existing — does a quotation need a box round it — with the plainest thing the category can put on a page.

It also does work for three other designs. 6 Split Head hands off here when no title is authored, 11 Faces hands off here on a phone and at two quotations, and A8-0 names this design and 14 Slim Line as the two in the category with no <h2>. A section with no heading takes no accessible name rather than being labelled with text nobody wrote.

PRIMARY · desktop 1440 · light · padding Comfortable 96, quotes shown Four, attribution Right column, rules Between, quote size Card 20, row padding Comfortable 32 · 4 of 7 authored quotes drawn

780 · 56 · 300 leaves 160 px of the content width unused at the right, and that is deliberate — the attribution column is a caption column, not a second text column, and filling the row edge to edge would make the two halves look equal. Both halves are top-aligned to the same baseline, so the name always sits level with the quotation's first line whatever length either runs to.
There is no avatar in this design at any value. A 36 px photograph in a 300 px caption column beside 20 px type is the only decorated thing in an otherwise undecorated section, and once it is gone the row is two pieces of text and a rule. The note and the link are drawn where authored, 32 px under the last row and above its closing rule where one exists. ⚑ The 780/56/300 division, the unused 160 and dropping the avatar are mine.

RULES NONE · QUOTE SIZE FEATURE 27 · nothing but type and 44 px of air between rows

Rules None is the quietest setting in the whole library, and it works only because the row padding doubles as the separation: at None the space between rows is the row padding's Spacious value whatever the control says, because 32 px of nothing between two quotations is not enough to end one. Rules Above and below closes the set into a block, which is the value for a page that needs this section to have an edge without giving it a card. Feature 27 is available here and in no other multi-quote design: on a 780 px measure with nothing beside it, four quotations at 27 px is a page of reading rather than a grid of cards. ⚑ The forced spacing at Rules None and offering Feature here are mine.

232 CHARS ABOVE 41 · the rows are honestly different heights · nothing equalises

Rows have no equal-height problem at all, which is the structural reason this design can hold six quotations of any length: each row is as tall as its own quotation, the rule sits at the row's own foot, and a 232-character quotation above a 41-character one is a long row above a short one. The step-down still applies — past 180 characters that first quotation would be at Small 17 — because the ladder is about how a quotation reads, not about how a grid fits.

DARK · desktop 1440 · padding Spacious 132, quotes shown Six, attribution Right column, rules Above and below, quote size Card 20, row padding Compact 24

Drawn at six rows, Compact 24 and Rules above and below, which is the densest this design gets: six quotations in a bounded block on 132 px of section padding. In dark the hairline is the only non-text thing on the page — #332E27 at 1.5:1 against background — and it holds, because a rule between two blocks of type is doing a job that a reader can also infer from the spacing. Quote and name 15.6:1, role 6.8:1. There is no accent anywhere in this frame: no link authored, no tabs, nothing focusable, so the section has no accent pixel in it at all — which A8-0 says is true of most of the category and is most literally true here.

TABLET 834 · the attribution moves under the quote

At 1080 and below the right column has nowhere to be: 780 + 56 + 300 needs 1,136 px, so the attribution goes under the quotation at every Attribution value and the control reads “Under the quote at this width”. That is the design's whole collapse — the rows stay rows, the rules stay where they are, and nothing else moves. EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Every avatar carries Image focus — Centre · Top · Bottom in its Image Picker popover, never as a hidden field.
DATA · nothing from Ghost · outside the design’s listNothing here comes from Ghost. There is no testimonial, review or endorsement resource, so every word is authored in this section: no Populate from… panel is offered on any A8 design and there is no missing-data branch. The only failure available is an empty repeater, and then the section does not render. Comments are refused as a source (a reader who replied under a post agreed to be read there — A28 owns it) and members are refused (a name and an email in Ghost’s members table are private data). Refused on record, at every value: no rating, score, star or “verified” mark (Ghost stores none of it), no per-item link, no decorative quote glyph, no autoplay.
