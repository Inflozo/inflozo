Cards

One person per card on a surface plane with a hairline and the pack's radius: circle photograph, name, role and a bio. The design to choose when the team has paragraphs, and the only design in A12 where a person gets a plane of their own — everywhere else a person is type and an image on the section's own ground.

It is also the one place in the category that amends settlement 2. Elsewhere a person with no bio is drawn short and sits at the top of its cell; here the card matches its row's height and the space falls at its foot, because a row of planes at four different heights is a ragged edge rather than a composition. The rule changes; the person does not.

ACCESSIBILITY · A PLANE IS NOT A CONTROL The card is a presentational <div> inside the <li>: no role, no label, no tabindex, not a <figure>, not a nested <section>, not an <article> — so the announcement is identical to 1 Grid's and switching between the two changes nothing a reader hears. A11·4's rule for its panel, applied twelve times over. The link is the name and the card is not clickable anywhere else. A11·5 made the whole card the target because a logo has no text to attach a link to; a person does, so the target here is the name's own box and the rest of the card is inert. No overlay anchor, no click handler on the div, no nested link, no title attribute. ⚑ The category's settlement 4, and the clearest place its cost is visible: a linked card and an unlinked one are the same picture. Name and role are two <p>s and the bio is a third; the photograph is alt="" and the initials block is aria-hidden. No heading inside a card at any level. Reading order is photograph, name, role, bio, and the equal-height stretch is align-items: stretch — it adds nothing to the DOM and announces nothing. Forced colours keep the border and lose the fill, at both Cards values, which is why the hairline is not optional: in forced colours it is the only thing that says where one person ends and the next begins. Contrast: name 15.8:1 on #FFFFFF and 14.6:1 on #211D17; role and bio 5.4:1 light on the card's own fill and 5.8:1 dark — checked against the card, not the section ground, because that is what they sit on; hairline 1.3:1, which is what a hairline is. Initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1. Nothing moves except the name's underline. No card transition exists to disable, so reduced motion changes nothing. At 400% the cards are one across and the bios reflow inside them.

Image focus: Centre · Top · Bottom sits in the Image Picker’s popover on photo — A14’s ruling adopted: where a photograph is cropped is content, so the focus is a field on the image and never a section control. It moves the crop on the axis the box actually crops, so a landscape file in a circle or a 4:5 box — cropped left and right — is the case these three values cannot answer: ARCHITECT: the Image focus vocabulary is short a horizontal axis. Three to twelve at Three or Four per row; one → 8 Founder, over twelve → 1 Grid, which drops the bios. Removing one person re-equalises its row and can shorten its neighbours. At Source Ghost authors this becomes P0·3’s Ghost-sourced card: no Add person, no drag handles, no Remove. Name, photograph and bio are read-only and answer “Edit in Ghost”. One field stays editable per row — Role — stored in the theme and keyed by the author’s slug, because Ghost has no role. Image focus is stored the same way. An extension of P0·3, whose Ghost card was read-only throughout.
EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. linkUrl opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the section link takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Per person, name, role and the ≤ 240 bio edit inline in the card, the bio as prose with the toolbar’s five marks and nothing else. At Source Ghost authors the name and the bio are Ghost-owned: the toolbar is replaced by the plain-text lock pill and clicking says “Edit in Ghost”. The Role override is ours and stays inline-editable. A Ghost bio has no length limit and is clamped to three lines — no ellipsis and no “more” link, so the clamp is visible on canvas rather than in the field.
BEHAVIOUR · from the fixed registryNone, and this is the design most likely to be assumed to have one. The equal heights are a CSS grid row track, not a measurement. The fill, hairline and radius are stylesheet; there is no link on the card to handle. JS off: identical.
