Panel

The whole grid inside one surface panel with a hairline. The only design in A17 whose tuple has a containment other than none — and A17-0 warns that this is the slot most often got wrong, so it is worth being exact: the section sits in a box. The posts do not.

That distinction is the whole design. 4 Cards gives each post a plane and leaves the section bare; this one gives the section a plane and leaves the posts bare. They are the category's only two planes, they are opposites, and putting both in one design would be a box of boxes.

What a panel is for: it makes a grid legible as one object on a busy page. A section between two full-bleed bands needs an edge to belong to itself, and a hairline on a lifted surface is the quietest edge available.

One panel: surface fill, a 1 px border hairline, the pack's radius, the sm shadow, and 56 px of inset. Nothing else is added and nothing inside changes — every card is A17-0's card exactly, with no fill, no border and no radius of its own.
The cells are 378, not 416, and that is the design's only real cost. A 56 px inset on both sides takes 112 px out of the 1,296 content width, leaving 1,184 for three cells and two gutters. A panel is a 9% tax on every cell in it, paid so the section reads as one object. ⚑ Mine, and the number worth knowing before choosing this design.
The row gap comes down from 48 to 44, because the panel's own edge is now doing part of the work of separating the grid from the page. The head sits inside the panel by default — a panel containing only the grid, with the head above it on the page, reads as a caption above a box rather than as one section. Head Outside is offered for the case where a page has several panels under one heading.
The sm shadow, not md. A17-0 gives md to 4 Cards on hover, where a card lifts under a cursor; a panel never lifts, so it takes the resting shadow and keeps it. The whole depth budget of this design is one hairline and 1 px of blur.

Three edges, and each is a different answer to “what is this panel resting on?” Hairline is fill plus border plus sm, and works on any page ground. Fill only is the quietest and fails on a pack whose surface and background are close — in Paper they are #FFFFFF on #FBF9F5, a difference of 2 lightness points, which is nearly invisible. ⚑ A finding: Fill only is pack-dependent in a way the tokens cannot express, and the panel's note says so plainly rather than the design pretending otherwise. Outline only takes the page's own ground and draws a border round it. It is the setting for a page that already has surfaces elsewhere and does not want a third. It is also the only one of the three where the tuple's ground slot is arguably wrong — the section shows the page beneath it, which A17-0 defines as transparent. The tuple records the default, surface, and I am flagging that one control value moves a closed slot rather than silently ignoring it. The posts stay bare in all three, and that is not negotiable within this design. A card fill inside a panel fill is two surfaces one step apart, which in most packs is invisible and in the rest is muddy — and it is exactly the “box of boxes” A17-0's containment note warns about. A site that wants planed posts wants 4 Cards.

Head Outside does not change the panel's inset, so the cells stay 378 — the head simply moves to the page's own measure above the box, where it aligns with every other section's head on the page. That alignment is the setting's whole purpose: inside the panel the head is inset 56 px from where the page's other heads sit, which is invisible in isolation and obvious in a column of sections. ⚑ Which is the honest argument against this design, and it belongs on the frame rather than in a footnote: a panel breaks the page's left edge. Every other section in A17 starts its text at the same x; this one starts 56 px in. Head Outside recovers the head's alignment and the grid's is still lost — that is the trade, and a site that values a hard left edge across a whole page should not use this design. The inset ladder is Compact 32 · Comfortable 56 · Spacious 80, giving cells of 394, 378 and 362 at three across — (1296 − 2·inset − 48) / 3, so each 24 px step of inset costs 16 px a cell. Spacious costs 160 px of content width — an eighth of the page — and at that point the panel is more visible than the posts, which the note says: “Wide insets make the panel the main event.”

PADDING AND INSET · WHY BOTH EXIST AFTER THIS PASSThe “no Padding control” absence is closed ⚑ — Vertical spacing is the page’s space around the panel and Inset is the panel’s own, so the two are not indistinguishable in use after all. Two vertical ladders, two names, both honest.Meta was missing and now ships with five values; Tag and “View all” link added. Every other design in A17 ends its list with Padding: Compact · Comfortable · Spacious. This one does not, and Inset takes its place. Two vertical-space controls on one design would be indistinguishable in use — a user adding space cannot tell whether they want it outside the panel or inside it, and the two compound: Spacious padding with a Spacious inset is 212 px of vertical space above the first image. So the section's own vertical padding is fixed at Comfortable 96 and is not exposed. ⚑ Mine, and it is the one design in A17 where a user cannot change the section's height directly — a real reduction in control that buys a comprehensible one. A finding for the architect if consistency across the eighteen sidebars matters more than that. Inset controls both axes, and that is why it can replace Padding. Compact 32 · Comfortable 56 · Spacious 80, applied on all four sides — so the panel's top inset and the section's fixed 96 stack to 128, 152 and 176 px above the head. Those are the real vertical steps, and they land close to A17's own 64 · 96 · 132 ladder without being it. Cut: a panel radius control. Radius is the pack's token and A17-0 forbids mixing — a panel that could be square in a rounded pack is exactly the re-skinning failure rule 1 exists to prevent. Cut: a panel colour. ⚑ The sm shadow is dropped in dark, and this is the design that exposes why. A17-0's warm shadows are rgba(28,27,26,.06) — a near-black at 6% — and a dark shadow on a dark ground is invisible. The shadow question generalises: A17 has exactly two shadowed things — this panel and 4 Cards' hover — and both lose their shadow in dark for the same reason. The category's depth is really hairlines and one surface step.
