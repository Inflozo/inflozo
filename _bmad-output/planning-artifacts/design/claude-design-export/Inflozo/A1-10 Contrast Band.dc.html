Contrast Band

Rail's arrangement on the contrast token: an inverted band at the top of the page. The structure is settled by design 1, so the whole of this design is one question — what happens to the accent when the ground goes dark. The answer is that the accent yields: on contrast the primary action takes the pack's lightest surface, and the accent returns only where it still clears AA.

ACCESSIBILITY · contrast is the whole design, so it is the whole risk
1Muted text is 76 %, not 60 %. Surface at 76 % on the Paper contrast band measures 8.1:1; at 60 % it drops under 5 and starts failing in the paler packs. 76 % is the floor for every pack, and a pack that cannot meet it renders muted items at full surface instead.
2The accent value is disabled, with its number shown. Paper’s accent on Paper’s contrast is 3.1:1, so “Primary action: Accent” is unavailable and the sidebar says why. Other packs may enable it. A control that produces failing contrast is worse than no control.
3The focus ring is surface, everywhere on the band. Fixed, not pack-dependent, and it takes a band-coloured gap when it sits on a surface fill. Off the band — in the dropdown, which opens over the page — the ring returns to accent.
4The band is not a mode. It does not change the page’s colour scheme, so color-scheme stays as the site’s and form controls elsewhere on the page are unaffected. Readers using forced-colours mode get system colours and a real border, since the band’s meaning is carried by its background alone.
5Semantics are Rail’s, unchanged. One header landmark, one nav labelled Primary, the wordmark as the home link, dropdown triggers as buttons with aria-expanded, skip link first. Inverting colour changes none of it.
