Big Type

The headline set large enough to be the page, flush left across the whole measure, with everything else kept small beneath it. The category's display moment, and the one design with type rules of its own: the eyebrow leaves by construction, the sub steps down to 17 px, and the actions sit under a hairline rather than in the composition. A4·6 is what A3·7 is to the footer, and the two are deliberately the same idea at opposite ends of the page.

The headline takes the whole 1296 px measure at 96 px with line-height 0.98 and tracking −0.03em — one notch tighter than the ladder, because at this size the ladder's −0.02em leaves gaps between words that read as errors. Below it a hairline runs the full measure, and under that the sub and the actions share one row: sub left on its 560 px measure, actions right. That row is the whole reason the design works — it keeps the small things small and in one place, so the headline is the only thing competing for attention. Flagged as mine: the 0.98 leading, the −0.03em tracking, the 44/28 px rule spacing, and putting the sub and actions in one row rather than stacking them.

Fill width solves for the size at which the headline's last line ends nearest the measure, then clamps: below 64 the design has stopped being Big Type and hands off to 1 Centred; above 160 a two-word headline becomes a texture rather than a sentence. Leading and tracking step with the size — 1.0 / −0.028em at Display, 0.98 / −0.03em at Huge, 0.94 / −0.035em at Fill — and in dark each tracking value opens by 0.001em, the correction A3·7 established. Flagged as mine: the 64–160 clamp, the solve rule, and all six type values.

What happens: the scale steps down one named value, once. If five lines survive at the lower value the design hands off to 1 Centred and the sidebar says why: “This headline is longer than Big Type can hold. Showing Centred.” The user's scale choice is remembered and the design returns the moment the headline is shortened. Why a cap at all, when the rest of the category refuses to clamp: five lines at 96 px is 470 px of headline before anything else, and the display moment has become a wall of type with no focal point — it fails the squint test, which is the one test in §3 the category cannot argue with. The hand-off is the same device A1·11 and A1·15 use, named here as they named it.

The rule is a hairline in the border token at the measure's full width, and it is the only line in the category that is part of a composition rather than a division. It is off by default in the packs whose border token is heavier than Paper's — flagged as mine, and the one pack-dependent default in A4, which is a compromise: a rule under 96 px type has to be a hairline or it becomes a second element.

The second mobile state shows what Fill width is for: a 14-character headline resolves to 64 px — the clamp's floor rather than its solve, since two words at 350 px would otherwise reach 96 px and lose their second line to a hyphen. Words are never hyphenated and never broken; the clamp is what protects that. This frame carries a shorter headline than its siblings because the state is a shorter headline, and it is the only frame in the design that does.

1 · The headline is the page's <h1> and is real text at a large size — never an image, never SVG, never letter-spaced with markup. It reflows at every zoom and Fill width recomputes under text zoom, so the headline never overflows horizontally. Words are never hyphenated or broken mid-word. 2 · The eyebrow is dropped by construction, not hidden. It is not rendered and not visually-hidden: a 13 px label above 96 px type is a mistake at every size, and a hidden one would put text in the accessibility tree that no sighted reader is being shown. The field is kept and returns on switching to any other design. Flagged as mine — this is the only design in A4 that refuses a field the category has. 3 · The rule is a border on the row, aria-hidden by being CSS rather than an <hr> — it is a compositional line, not a thematic break, and announcing “separator” before the sub would be wrong. Under forced colours it disappears and the 28 px gap is what remains, which is why the gap opens when the rule is off. Reading order · headline, sub, primary, secondary at every width. The desktop row puts the actions to the right of the sub visually and after it in the DOM, so the two agree; nothing is re-ordered in CSS.
