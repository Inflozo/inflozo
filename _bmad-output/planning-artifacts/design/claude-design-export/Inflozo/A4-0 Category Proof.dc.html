A4 HEROES · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

What the seventeen inherit, before any of them is drawn.

The four things §8 asks A4 to settle, the rules all seventeen designs share, the type and action ladders that make the set one family, the roster, and the shared field list. Heroes are also the pilot for control density, so the control vocabulary is set here and spent differently by each design.

The tokenisation proof and the category stress frame are at the foot of this file, both drawn on 3 Split — the category's most-used arrangement and the one that carries a picture, actions and the full type ladder at once.

1 · THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · SETTLED BEFORE DRAWING

The questions §8 puts to the category

1 · Where A1·4 Overlay lands

Every A4 design declares one of two header modes, and it is a property of the design, not a control. Overlap: the header has no ground of its own and sits on the hero's picture. The condition is narrow: the picture must span the header's full width, because A1·4 has one colour setting for the whole bar. Four designs qualify — 4 Full Bleed, 10 Video Poster, 14 Full Height, 18 Overlap Card. Those heroes add the header's own measured height to their top padding (76 px at A1's Comfortable, 64 at 834, 60 at ≤ 767), so the eyebrow never starts under the bar, and the reservation is measured from the header rather than typed, so A1's own Height control stays free. Below: the other fourteen have no full-width picture at the top edge, so A1·4's own rule already applies — no transparent header over a flat pack colour — and it renders on its own ground instead (the rule that no design ever turns into another design). A4 does not override that, including on 9 Contrast Band, where a transparent header would be measurable but would make the pairing a different design.

Where the header overlaps, the hero draws the header's scrim, not the header: one element, one gradient, never two stacked. Values in §3 below. Flagged as mine: the two modes, the four designs that take Overlap, measuring rather than typing the reservation, and giving the header's scrim to the hero.

Corrected while drawing 12 Offset Image. This settlement first listed five Overlap designs, on the assumption that a picture at the hero's top edge was enough. 12's picture covers only the right-hand end of the bar, where no single colour setting keeps both the nav and the actions legible — so the condition is full width, not merely present, and 12 declares Below.

2 · Image, no-image and video-poster variants

One picture field serves all three, so switching designs never loses it: image + imageAlt everywhere, imageCaption where a caption has somewhere to sit (5 and 18). A video adds videoUrl, videoPoster and videoDuration; with no poster set the poster is image. Nothing ever autoplays behind text — the poster is a button that opens the player in a dialog, and A15 Video and Embeds owns the player itself.

No-image is not a state to survive, it is a state each image hero names for itself. A published site never shows a striped placeholder — that rule comes from A3·14 verbatim; the stripes are the editor's. The no-image states are in §4.

3 · How the set scales 1440 → 390 without dropping the actions

One ladder, drawn below, shared by all seventeen: eyebrow holds at 13 px at every width, the headline steps by its named size, the sub steps 19 → 18 → 17, and the actions never leave. At ≤ 767 the pair stays on one row while both labels fit the measure and stacks to two full-width 48 px buttons when they do not — measured at build, not guessed from a character count. The authored pair, Subscribe and Browse the archive, fits. Two actions is the cap in the whole category; a third is refused and A6 CTA Banners named.

4 · No feature image, and templates other than home

A hero draws authored content, with one named exception. Seventeen of the seventeen never borrow a post's feature image, title or excerpt: A24 Post Headers owns the top of a post and A19 Featured and Spotlight owns any section whose subject is a post. The exception is 13 Latest Post, where the right half of 3 Split's arrangement is the newest published post as one card — image, title, tag and date, no excerpt, no second post, no grid. The authored headline stays the page's h1 and the post is the card beneath it, which is the line between this and A19: A19's subject is the post, A4·13's subject is still the page.

So “no feature image” for A4 means the section's own image is empty, and every image hero names its own no-image state in §4. On 13 it means two things — a post with no feature image, and no published post at all — and both are in that table.

A4 is offered on home, pages and custom routes. It is not offered on tag or author archives, where A29 Archive Headers owns the top, nor on posts. 17 Slim exists for the inner-page case — an About page wants a hero one step tall, not the home page's. 13 Latest Post is offered on home and custom routes only: a latest-post card on an About page is a section that has wandered in from the home page. Ghost supplies the site title (7 Masthead) and the one post (13); everything else is authored. Flagged as mine: the authored-plus-one rule, the route lists, and the boundary lines with A19, A24 and A29.

3 · CATEGORY-WIDE RULES · TRUE OF ALL SEVENTEEN, NOT REPEATED PER DESIGN

What every hero inherits

Placement and padding One A4 per page, the first thing inside <main>, above every other section. Vertical padding Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132 at 1440; 56 / 80 / 108 at 834; 48 / 64 / 84 at ≤ 767. Page margin 72 · 40 · 20, as A3. Where the header overlaps, its measured height is added to the top value and to nothing else. No hero draws a hairline at its top edge — it has nothing above it but the header. The bottom edge is the next section's problem, so no hero draws one there either: the ground change or the following section's own padding is the join.
The scrim, in two parts and no more Text scrim — one linear gradient in the contrast token, running from the edge the text is anchored to: 60% at that edge, transparent by 70% of the field. Bottom-anchored runs up, left-anchored runs right. Centred text over a picture is anchored to the lower third, never the optical centre — which is what lets one gradient family serve all three arrangements instead of a flat wash. Header scrim — only where A1·4 overlaps: contrast at 30% at the top edge, transparent by the header's height plus 24 px. Drawn by the hero, once. In dark both weaken: text scrim 48% in the background token, header scrim 24%. Never black, never a flat overlay, never a blend mode. Both are aria-hidden siblings, so forced colours leaves text on a solid ground.
Imagery Crops are fixed per design, never a control: 16:9 full-bleed and video (14:9 at ≤ 767), 4:3 beside text, 3:2 for a pair, 2.4:1 for a band under text. Centre anchor, object-fit: cover, pack radius except where the image is full-bleed. Explicit dimensions, the space reserved before the file arrives, loading="eager" and fetchpriority="high" — a hero image is the page's largest paint and the only image in the library that is not lazy. Alt defaults to empty where words sit over the picture, and to the editor's prompt where the picture sits beside them: a hero photograph beside a headline usually is content.
Accent, depth, motion Accent is spent on the primary action and the focus ring, and nowhere else in the category — not the eyebrow, not a rule, not an underline. Where a design has no action, it spends nothing and the hero is type on a ground. On any contrast ground the primary action is the pack's lightest surface or an outline, never accent: A1·10 measured Paper's accent carrying its label on the band at 3.1:1 and disabled the value there, and A4 carries that control and that measurement forward rather than re-taking them. The rule reads the same in 1 Centred, 8 Card and 9 Contrast Band. Flagged, and it is a pack problem rather than a section one: on a light ground the same accent carries the primary button's label at about 3.2:1 at rest and 4.0:1 on the darkened hover step, both under the 4.5:1 floor for 15 px / 600. Earlier categories record 4.6:1 for the darkened step; the drawn colours do not support it. A4 keeps A1·1's button rather than inventing a second one, states the measured figure in each design's accessibility notes, and says plainly that the fix belongs to Paper's accent token — darkening it by about one step clears the floor without changing a single section. Warm sm and md shadows only, on 8 Card and 18 Overlap Card; dropped in dark, where the raised surface and the hairline carry it. 160 ms ease-out, one transition per state change. The category's whole motion budget: button and link hovers, 10 Video Poster's play affordance, 14 Full Height's scroll cue. No parallax, no entrance animation, nothing that moves while the section is being edited.
Accessibility floor The headline is the page's <h1> on home, pages and custom routes — one per page, and the hero is where it lives. The eyebrow is a <p> above it, never a heading and never part of the h1's text. The hero is a plain <section>, unlabelled — it is the first thing in <main> and its own h1 announces it. Actions are links styled as buttons, except the subscribe submit on 11 and the play control on 10, which are real buttons because they do something. Hit targets 44 px at ≤ 767, 46 px everywhere by construction. Contrast measured against the scrimmed image, never the raw one, exactly as A1·4.
The control vocabulary · heroes are the pilot Four controls recur across the seventeen and mean the same thing on each: Padding (Compact · Comfortable · Spacious), Headline size (Medium · Large · Display), Actions (Both · Primary only · None), Ground (Background · Surface · Contrast, where the design has a choice). That leaves each design two or three of its own, and none exceeds six plus the Design picker. Alignment is not a shared control: a design whose arrangement can be centred or flushed left is two designs, and the set has both. Flagged as mine — it is the one place where minimal controls and §3·2 pull against each other, and structure wins.

Three things to notice, and one that matters more than the others. The radius token reaches three places here — picture, primary, secondary — and at 14 the picture's corners are the softest thing in the hero while at 6 they are the hardest; nothing else about the composition responds. The heading fonts set the same headline at different widths, up to about 6% apart, and the 7/5 division absorbs it because the text column is a fraction rather than a fixed measure. And the accent is spent once, on the primary action, which is the reason Ink's blue survives: it never has to work as a link colour, an eyebrow or a rule, so it only has to pass on one label. A pack whose accent failed there would need its own carried pair, which is a pack problem and not a section one.
