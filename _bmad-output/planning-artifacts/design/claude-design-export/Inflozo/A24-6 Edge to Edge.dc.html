Edge to Edge

The header's words sit on the page margin like any other section, and then the photograph breaks out of the measure entirely and runs from viewport edge to viewport edge with no radius and no margin. The break is the design: the reader gets a title at reading scale and then a picture at cinema scale, and the section's own boundary is the picture's top edge.

It differs from 4 Image Top on one tuple slot and from 5 Full Bleed on two, and the three of them are the category's argument about what a page's edges mean. 4 keeps the picture inside the grid; this one lets it out; 5 puts the words inside it. Only this one keeps the type entirely on the page's own ground, which is why it needs no scrim, no derived colours and no pairing with A1·4.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES The hand-off to 2 Flush Left is deleted — no design ever turns into another design. This design reflows instead: the picture, its caption and the gap above them are not drawn, the words keep this design's own measure and order, and the article's 64 is measured from the byline. The panel may still say that 2 Flush Left is the design built for a site with no photography — as advice under the picker, never as a switch. Two designs in A24 lock Feature image and they lock it for the same reason and with different consequences. 5 loses its ground; this one loses its subject but keeps its layout. The lock is what makes the missing picture legible in the panel: the field cannot be turned off, so the only way to reach the hidden-picture state is for the post to have none, and the panel's line describes exactly that case. Cut: an "inset the picture at wide viewports" value. On a 2,560 px monitor a full-bleed 21:9 crop is 1,097 px tall and it is fair to ask whether it should stop at 1,800. It should, and that is a page-level maximum rather than a section control — the finding goes to the architect, because every full-bleed section in the library has the same problem and none of them should solve it privately.
