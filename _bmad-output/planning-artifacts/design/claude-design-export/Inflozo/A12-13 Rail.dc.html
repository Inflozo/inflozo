Rail

One line of portrait cards wider than the window, scrolled by the reader. A11·9's rail carried over verbatim — the full-bleed track, the fades, the two arrow placements, the measured precondition, the role="group" and the generated accessible name — with logos swapped for the person card at a named crop.

It is one of two designs in A12 with a behaviour, and the behaviour is entirely the reader's: nothing auto-advances, nothing loops, and the rail starts at the start on every load. It is also the design that draws the longest card in the category — a 4:5 crop, a name, a role and a 240-character bio, one above the other — which is why it is a rail rather than a grid.

The fade appears at an end only when there is content past it, A11·9's rule verbatim: at rest a reader sees one fade and knows which way the rail runs. The fade is 96 px at 1440, 72 at 834 and 48 at ≤ 767, drawn in the section's background token, and it is the full height of the card rather than of the image. A click moves one card plus one gutter — 330 px at Standard, 264 at Narrow — so a card never lands half-shown after an arrow press. Scroll snap is not used, because a reader dragging a rail past the last card should not be pulled back. Scroll position is not remembered and not restored: the rail starts at the start on every load, at every width. ⚑ Mine, and A11·9's position carried. The behaviour does not run while editing. In the editor the rail draws its resting state and the arrows are inert, which is why the resting state is the primary frame. Under reduced motion the rail still scrolls and stops animating: scroll-behavior drops to auto and the jump is instant. Nothing auto-advances, nothing loops, and there are no dots. A12 has no carousel in it: a masthead that moves on its own is a masthead a reader cannot finish reading.

The precondition is measured, not counted — A11·9's words. Five people is the floor for choosing the design at Standard and six at Narrow, but the test at render is whether the cards are wider than the content width; if they are not, the section draws as 1 Grid with no arrows, no fades and no scroll container. A rail that cannot scroll is a row that looks like it should. The arrows are the reason this design always spends the accent, whatever else is authored: they are controls, so hover and focus take accent. That is A11·9's finding, and in A12 it means 13 Rail and 14 Reveal are the two designs in the category that cannot be drawn without an accent pixel. A disabled arrow stays in the DOM, stays focusable and stays 38 px, with aria-disabled rather than disabled, so a keyboard reaching the end of the rail does not lose its place. Its glyph is 2.1:1 and the panel shows the ratio at the value, which is §7.4 applied to a state rather than to a control. One crop for the whole rail, named and never computed — A8·8's rule, and settlement 1's: Portrait 4:5 or Square 1:1, chosen once for every card. Landscape 3:2 is not offered, because a landscape crop in a 306 card is 204 px tall and the rail stops being portraits. ⚑ Mine.

ACCESSIBILITY · A SCROLL CONTAINER A KEYBOARD CAN REACH The track is a role="group" with a generated aria-label — “Team, scrollable” — and tabindex="0", so the arrow keys reach it. That is A11·9's mechanism verbatim, and it is the only generated string in A12: everywhere else the section's words are the site's. ⚑ Flagged there, flagged here. The arrows are <button>s named “Scroll left” and “Scroll right”, outside the group, in the DOM after it at Arrows In the margins and before it at Above the rail — so the reading order follows the drawn order at both values. A disabled arrow takes aria-disabled and stays focusable. A linked name inside the rail is an ordinary link, and tabbing to one scrolls it into view — the browser's own behaviour rather than a script — so a keyboard reader reaches the twelfth person without touching an arrow. The card is not a target and the crop is not a target: settlement 4 holds here as everywhere. The photograph is alt="" and the initials block is aria-hidden, because the name is under it in every state. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 at 20 px; role and bio 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1; a disabled arrow's glyph 2.1:1, disclosed at the state. Under reduced motion the rail scrolls without animating. In print the rail is not a rail: it draws as a wrapped grid at three across, every person and every bio, because a page has no viewport to scroll and a printed masthead that stops at card five is a printed mistake. ⚑ Mine, and one of two print exceptions in A12.
