A15 · Video and Embeds

Fifteen ways to put a film on a page: one player, one player on a plane, on a band, at the page’s full width, under a head that sits on it, in a grid, with a lead, on a track, beside a queue, with a contents list, in a box that names its provider, in ruled rows, on one line, and behind a tab strip.

This frame carries what belongs to the category rather than to a design: the tokenisation proof, the stress frame, the roster, the shared field list, the component inventory, the four settlements and the two findings. Every design’s own frames and written spec are in A15-1 through A15-15.

THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 OF THE BRIEF, ANSWERED
1 · POSTER FRAME VERSUS AUTOPLAY-MUTED, AND WHAT PLAYS ON A PHONE
A poster frame, everywhere, and nothing autoplays at any value in any design ⚑. There is no autoplay control, no muted-loop control and no hover preview ⚑ — a film that starts because a pointer passed over it is autoplay with extra steps. Playback begins on an action the reader took, in all fifteen.On a phone the film loads in the frame, playsinline, in the same reserved box ⚑. A15 never asks for full screen and never hands off to a native player of its own; if the browser takes the film full screen that is the platform’s decision and the section does not fight it.The one thing that changes at 390 is the theatre ⚑ — Plays: In a theatre falls back to In the frame, because a dialog on a 390 screen is the frame with a close button on it. 14 Slim Bar is the exception: it has no frame to fall back to, so it opens the theatre at every width ⚑.
2 · HOLDING THE ASPECT RATIO WHILE THE EMBED LOADS
The box is reserved in CSS before anything loads ⚑. Every frame is an aspect-ratio box at the section’s Aspect value; the poster, the notice and the player are all position:absolute; inset:0 inside it. Nothing on the page moves when a film is played, because nothing changes size.There is no “as the provider reports it” value ⚑ — the ratio has to be known before the request is made, and asking the provider for it is the request. A film at another ratio letterboxes inside the box ⚑ rather than resizing the section: 15 Tabs shows it, and the stress frame draws a 9:16 film in a 16:9 box.One exception, and it is a control value rather than a hidden case ⚑: 12 Embed Card’s Audio bar is a height — 160 px, 120 at 390 — because an audio player has no aspect and reserving 16:9 for one leaves a hole.
3 · A CONSENT OR CLICK-TO-LOAD PLACEHOLDER
Every third-party embed is click-to-load, and there is no value that turns it off ⚑. No request reaches the provider until the reader acts. A control whose value leaks the reader’s address on page load is a control that will be set wrongly, so A15 does not offer one.The poster is the author’s image, and if there is none it is the plate ⚑ — never the provider’s own thumbnail, because fetching that thumbnail is the request the facade exists to prevent. The plate names the provider and the film, and holds the same box.The notice is on the frame, drawn at the moment the reader acts ⚑ — “Loads from YouTube. Playing this film sets cookies in your browser.” 12 Embed Card moves it above the frame and has no Off value ⚑: that design’s job is to say what the embed costs before the reader spends it.
4 · CAPTION, CREDIT AND TRANSCRIPT, AND WHERE A TRANSCRIPT LIVES
Three different things, three different fields ⚑. description is the film’s own line, drawn under the frame in 13 Thumb Rows and 15 Tabs and stored everywhere else; credit is the section’s, under the set in fourteen designs, and belongs to the films rather than to the page; transcriptUrl is per film.A transcript is a link, never a panel ⚑ — A15 does not host transcript text. transcriptUrl points at a Ghost page, a post or a file, and A25 Post Content Layouts owns the page it points at. An accordion of transcript text inside a video section was considered and refused: it is an article, and articles have a category.Where a design has no room for a meta row the transcript travels into the theatre ⚑ — 6 Cover and 14 Slim Bar — and the panel says so rather than dropping it quietly. Meta: Off never deletes the link ⚑; it means “not under the frame”.

A fifth question the brief did not ask, answered anyway: a section with no video does not render ⚑, at every design. No head, no empty box, no placeholder — and in the editor, one paste field.

It carries its URL as its title until the author types one ⚑, so a new row is never a blank shell; the poster, duration and transcript are empty and the frame draws the plate.Remove is on the row and is undoable. At one film Remove is disabled with the reason shown — dropped 24 Aug 2026 ⚑: it contradicted this category’s own zero state, a paste field at 0. WHAT IS EDITABLE INSIDE AN ITEM, AND WHAT IS NOT
Selecting a frame on the canvas opens that film’s own fields and nothing else ⚑ — URL, title, description, poster, duration, transcript link, plus tabLabel in 15. Everything but the URL is optional ⚑.No layout, spacing, alignment or emphasis is editable inside an item ⚑. Every design control writes one value onto the section and the stylesheet reads it, so “make film 3 bigger” is not expressible by construction. Where a set genuinely needs one film larger, that is 8 Lead and Grid — and the lead is item 1, chosen by reordering the list ⚑, not by a control on the item.What an empty optional field looks like: no title → the sidebar row shows the URL and the cell is its poster and meta alone ⚑ (10 Playlist is the exception — its row shows the URL, because a queue row with no name cannot be chosen); no poster → the plate, naming the provider; no duration → no pill and the meta row closes up; no transcript → the link is absent rather than disabled; no description → the row or caption closes up.11 Chapters carries the only second list ⚑ — chapters[], 1–24, time and label both required, added rows landing last carrying “New chapter”, never sorted ⚑, and a backwards timecode published as typed with a warning dot in the sidebar.
