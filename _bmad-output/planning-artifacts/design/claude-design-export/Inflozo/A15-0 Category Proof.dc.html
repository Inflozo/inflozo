A15 · Video and Embeds

Fifteen ways to put a film on a page: one player, one player on a plane, on a band, at the page’s full width, under a head that sits on it, in a grid, with a lead, on a track, beside a queue, with a contents list, in a box that names its provider, in ruled rows, on one line, and behind a tab strip.

This frame carries what belongs to the category rather than to a design: the tokenisation proof, the stress frame, the roster, the shared field list, the component inventory, the four settlements and the two findings. Every design’s own frames and written spec are in A15-1 through A15-15.

CONTROLS RECONCILIATION · 24 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED ACROSS ALL FIFTEEN PANELS
THE UNIVERSAL TRIO, AND THE PADDING ROW IT REPLACES
Background role · Vertical spacing · Top divider now sit outside every design’s own list. The per-design Padding row is retired ⚑ — it was Vertical spacing under another name, with the same three values and the same 64 · 96 · 132 numbers.Three designs lock Background role, with the reason shown in the row ⚑: 4 Contrast Band (the inverted band is the design), 5 Full Bleed (the section paints no ground — the film is the only fill) and 6 Cover (the picture is the ground). 6 Cover gains Vertical spacing, which it never had: it sets the space above and below the picture, and Height still sizes the picture.Ladders that are genuinely different keep their own names ⚑ — 6 Cover’s Height, 12 Embed Card’s box padding (no control) and 14 Slim Bar’s 88 px bar height (no control). In 4 Contrast Band Vertical spacing resolves onto the band’s inner padding rather than sitting beside it.Control counts after the pass: five of its own in eleven designs, six in 6 Cover, four in 14 Slim Bar — plus the trio and the Data group in all fifteen. Quick Controls are three per design and named on each panel’s footer. A15 shipped no Preview control, so rule 7 removes nothing.
THE UPLOAD BRANCH · SPECIFIED, AND ITS OWN PLAYBACK ROW
Every rule the category wrote was embed-shaped ⚑, and this pass names the other branch. Source is per film — Embed or Upload, set by what Add received rather than by a section value, and one list may hold both.Source: Upload renders a native <video controls playsinline preload="none" poster="…"> ⚑. First-party, so no consent notice and no facade; the no-JS state is the native player itself; the duration reads from the file and is never typed.On uploads only: Playback — Click to play (default) · Ambient loop ⚑. Ambient is muted, looped and playsinline, and the help line says plainly that muted is what makes autoplay browser-legal. The poster stands in under reduced motion and with no JavaScript. Struck through in 14 Slim Bar (a 96 × 54 thumbnail is not a loop) and at 12 Embed Card’s Audio bar.Embeds keep the blanket autoplay refusal ⚑ — settlement 1 is unchanged where it applies. Gating an ambient loop on prefers-reduced-motion is behaviour no registry module covers: flagged ARCHITECT: registry addition on every panel, never named.
EDITOR-SIDE SEEDING, AND WHAT SETTLEMENT 3 ACTUALLY PROTECTS
Pasting a recognised URL fetches its oEmbed title and duration once, in the editor ⚑, and seeds both fields. “Use provider thumbnail” downloads the image and re-hosts it as the poster ⚑.Settlement 3’s privacy goal is the reader’s browser, not the editor’s ⚑ — a re-hosted poster is served first-party, so the facade still prevents every third-party request a reader would otherwise make. The rule “never the provider’s thumbnail” is amended to “never fetched by the reader”.The typed-duration chore is gone ⚑, and with it the caveat 10 Playlist carried: its running total is now as right as the provider or the file, and still absent if any film has none. Both fields stay editable and a typed value wins.Recognised providers, named at last ⚑ — video: YouTube · Vimeo; audio, for 12 Embed Card: Soundcloud · Spotify · Apple Podcasts · Bandcamp · Mixcloud. An unrecognised URL takes 12 Embed Card’s link-out fallback in all fifteen designs: the frame is drawn, the label reads the domain, and the frame is a link rather than an iframe.
ITEMS, STRINGS AND THE TWO FLAGS
The videos block is the P0·3 item list ⚑: add arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag reorders, per-item content only. The old “disabled at one film” rule is dropped — it contradicted the category’s own zero state, a paste field at 0 — and removal may now empty the list.New per item ⚑: start ≤ 8 characters, appended to the watch-page href and to the loaded player — 11 Chapters’ mechanics, and “start at 1:32” is the commonest per-film ask. Every poster carries Image focus (Centre · Top · Bottom) from the Image Picker popover.No fixed English visitor-facing string ships ⚑. Authored fields: the action labels (playLabel “Play”, watchLabel “Watch”), openLabel, transcriptLabel “Transcript”. Catalog strings: the notice sentence and its load action, the theatre’s close and counter, “Now playing”, the running-total prefix, the dead-URL line, 7 Grid’s new “Film on {provider}” and 15 Tabs’ “Film {n}”. The duration suffix stays generated.Two flags now stand on the frames ⚑: ARCHITECT: tabs registry ruling on 10 Playlist and 15 Tabs (the no-JS heading branch, settled once with A13’s Walkthrough) and ARCHITECT: registry addition on 11 Chapters (seeking a loaded player) and 12 Embed Card (a facade for a non-video embed). Member Visibility lands nowhere in A15 — no design carries an auth or subscribe action; a members-only film is A32 Paywall’s ground. Recorded rather than invented.

No section frame was redrawn in this pass, and that is a claim rather than an omission. Nothing here changes a drawn mark at a drawn value: the trio ships at Background · Comfortable · None, the icon slots are off by default, start and Image focus are fields, and Source: Upload draws the same reserved box with the browser’s own controls inside it instead of the facade. What the build gains is a second branch and a shorter list of typing chores.

THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 OF THE BRIEF, ANSWERED
1 · POSTER FRAME VERSUS AUTOPLAY-MUTED, AND WHAT PLAYS ON A PHONE
A poster frame, everywhere, and nothing autoplays at any value in any design ⚑. There is no autoplay control, no muted-loop control and no hover preview ⚑ — a film that starts because a pointer passed over it is autoplay with extra steps. Playback begins on an action the reader took, in all fifteen.On a phone the film loads in the frame, playsinline, in the same reserved box ⚑. A15 never asks for full screen and never hands off to a native player of its own; if the browser takes the film full screen that is the platform’s decision and the section does not fight it.The one thing that changes at 390 is the theatre ⚑ — Plays: In a theatre falls back to In the frame, because a dialog on a 390 screen is the frame with a close button on it. 14 Slim Bar is the exception: it has no frame to fall back to, so it opens the theatre at every width ⚑.
2 · HOLDING THE ASPECT RATIO WHILE THE EMBED LOADS
The box is reserved in CSS before anything loads ⚑. Every frame is an aspect-ratio box at the section’s Aspect value; the poster, the notice and the player are all position:absolute; inset:0 inside it. Nothing on the page moves when a film is played, because nothing changes size.There is no “as the provider reports it” value ⚑ — the ratio has to be known before the request is made, and asking the provider for it is the request. A film at another ratio letterboxes inside the box ⚑ rather than resizing the section: 15 Tabs shows it, and the stress frame draws a 9:16 film in a 16:9 box.One exception, and it is a control value rather than a hidden case ⚑: 12 Embed Card’s Audio bar is a height — 160 px, 120 at 390 — because an audio player has no aspect and reserving 16:9 for one leaves a hole.
3 · A CONSENT OR CLICK-TO-LOAD PLACEHOLDER
Every third-party embed is click-to-load, and there is no value that turns it off ⚑. No request reaches the provider until the reader acts. A control whose value leaks the reader’s address on page load is a control that will be set wrongly, so A15 does not offer one.The poster is the author’s image, and if there is none it is the plate ⚑ — never the provider’s own thumbnail, because fetching that thumbnail is the request the facade exists to prevent. The plate names the provider and the film, and holds the same box.The notice is on the frame, drawn at the moment the reader acts ⚑ — “Loads from YouTube. Playing this film sets cookies in your browser.” 12 Embed Card moves it above the frame and has no Off value ⚑: that design’s job is to say what the embed costs before the reader spends it.
4 · CAPTION, CREDIT AND TRANSCRIPT, AND WHERE A TRANSCRIPT LIVES
Three different things, three different fields ⚑. description is the film’s own line, drawn under the frame in 13 Thumb Rows and 15 Tabs and stored everywhere else; credit is the section’s, under the set in fourteen designs, and belongs to the films rather than to the page; transcriptUrl is per film.A transcript is a link, never a panel ⚑ — A15 does not host transcript text. transcriptUrl points at a Ghost page, a post or a file, and A25 Post Content Layouts owns the page it points at. An accordion of transcript text inside a video section was considered and refused: it is an article, and articles have a category.Where a design has no room for a meta row the transcript travels into the theatre ⚑ — 6 Cover and 14 Slim Bar — and the panel says so rather than dropping it quietly. Meta: Off never deletes the link ⚑; it means “not under the frame”.

A fifth question the brief did not ask, answered anyway: a section with no video does not render ⚑, at every design. No head, no empty box, no placeholder — and in the editor, one paste field.

THE VIDEOS BLOCK · ONE REPEATING UNIT, IDENTICAL IN ALL FIFTEEN DESIGNS · THE P0·3 ITEM LIST
ADDING, REMOVING AND REORDERING
Add is one paste field ⚑ — a provider URL or an uploaded file — and the new film lands last. It carries its URL as its title until the author types one ⚑, so a new row is never a blank shell; the poster, duration and transcript are empty and the frame draws the plate.Remove is on the row and is undoable. At one film Remove is disabled with the reason shown — dropped 24 Aug 2026 ⚑: it contradicted this category’s own zero state, a paste field at 0. Remove is never disabled and removal may empty the list ⚑; at 0 the section does not render on the published page and the editor draws that one paste field.Drag reorders, and authored order is drawn order in all fifteen designs ⚑. Order is meaningful in ten of them: it is the lead in 8, the running order in 9 and 10, the tab order in 15, and the first film in the eight single-film designs. It is not meaningful in 7 Grid and 13 Thumb Rows beyond reading order, and those panels say so.1–24 ⚑. Below the floor: a design written for many draws what it has — 7 Grid at 1 is one cell at its column width, 9 Carousel at 1 loses its dots and arrows, 15 Tabs at 1 loses its strip and is 1 Player — and every one of those panels names the design that fits rather than refusing to draw. At 24 Add is disabled with the reason shown.At 0 the section does not render on the published page ⚑ and the editor draws one paste field at the section’s full width. That is the same empty state in every design.
WHAT IS EDITABLE INSIDE AN ITEM, AND WHAT IS NOT
Selecting a frame on the canvas opens that film’s own fields and nothing else ⚑ — URL, title, description, poster, duration, transcript link, plus tabLabel in 15. Everything but the URL is optional ⚑.No layout, spacing, alignment or emphasis is editable inside an item ⚑. Every design control writes one value onto the section and the stylesheet reads it, so “make film 3 bigger” is not expressible by construction. Where a set genuinely needs one film larger, that is 8 Lead and Grid — and the lead is item 1, chosen by reordering the list ⚑, not by a control on the item.What an empty optional field looks like: no title → the sidebar row shows the URL and the cell is its poster and meta alone ⚑ (10 Playlist is the exception — its row shows the URL, because a queue row with no name cannot be chosen); no poster → the plate, naming the provider; no duration → no pill and the meta row closes up; no transcript → the link is absent rather than disabled; no description → the row or caption closes up.11 Chapters carries the only second list ⚑ — chapters[], 1–24, time and label both required, added rows landing last carrying “New chapter”, never sorted ⚑, and a backwards timecode published as typed with a warning dot in the sidebar.

FINDINGS · TWO GAPS IN THE MODULE REGISTRY, AND ONE DISAGREEMENT RESOLVED
FINDING 1 · SEEKING A LOADED PLAYER
11 Chapters needs to tell a player already on the page to move to 5:03, and no module covers that. video-facade covers the swap from poster to player and stops there.What the design does instead ⚑: every chapter row is a real <a href> to the canonical URL carrying the provider’s own time parameter, so with no script it navigates and the film opens at that point, and with the module running the frame reloads at the new start time. One behaviour rather than two, and no registry addition needed to ship it.Named for the architect: closest module video-facade. A15 does not invent a name and does not use an M-prefixed alias.
FINDING 2 · A FACADE FOR A NON-VIDEO EMBED
12 Embed Card puts an audio player, a map or a data frame behind the same click-to-load placeholder, and the registry’s facade is written for video — its degradation names a poster and a watch page.What the design does instead ⚑: it declares video-facade and reads the degradation as the embed’s canonical page — the episode on its host, the location on the map service — so with no script the reader gets there in one navigation and only the in-page frame is lost.Named for the architect: a general embed-facade is the architect’s call. A15 does not name one.
RESOLVED · A4·10’S BUTTON-VERSUS-ANCHOR DISAGREEMENT
A4·10 Video Poster left a finding: its design specified a labelled <button> while video-facade degrades to an <a href>.A15 settles it ⚑: the trigger compiles as the anchor — an <a href> to the canonical watch page wrapping the poster — and the module upgrades it in place to the control that loads the player or opens the theatre. The design and the registry no longer disagree, and the no-JS path is the registry’s own sentence rather than a special case.This is the rule in all fifteen designs and in A4·10, which calls A15’s theatre.

Refused, each with its home named: lightbox — A14’s module is not declared anywhere in A15 ⚑, because a still opens an image overlay and a poster opens a player, and the two are different behaviours; reveal — a film that fades in as it is scrolled to is decoration on a frame the reader is already looking for; load-more and infinite-scroll — both count a query and A15’s list is authored; count-up — there is no number to count; accordion for transcript text — that is an article, and A25 owns it ⚑.

THE SHARED FLOOR · WHAT EVERY DESIGN OBEYS UNLESS ITS OWN ENTRY SAYS OTHERWISE
THE FRAME, THE CONTROL, THE META LINE
The frame is a <figure> whose anchor wraps the poster; the whole frame is the target and its accessible name is the film’s title plus its duration ⚑, never “Play”. Hover grows the play control 2% and underlines the title; focus draws A6’s 4 px accent ring 3 px outside the frame ⚑ (inset at a page edge, A17·18); reduced motion drops the growth and keeps the underline ⚑.The play control is 64 px on a single-film frame, 44 in a grid cell and at 390, 88 at full bleed and on 6 Cover ⚑. It is never the accent ⚑ — a coloured fill over a photograph nobody has seen cannot be checked for contrast, which is A14·11’s finding in a new place.The meta line is 13 px in text-muted: duration · transcript, with the provider added at the values that name it. The credit is one 13 px line under the set ⚑, in fourteen designs, and belongs to the films rather than to the page.The type: eyebrow 13 uppercase tracked .08em · heading 40 · 34 · 28 by width (34 in 3 Panel, 46 on 6 Cover) · blurb 17, 16 at 390 · film title 17 in a cell, 24 in a row, 22 in a queue · description 15 · meta and credit 13 · mono 10–12 for frame labels and timecodes. A15 has no Big Type design ⚑ — the display moment is the film.
THE COUNT, THE SEAM, THE COLLAPSE, THE PRINT
The count. There is no Show ladder in A15 ⚑ — A17’s 3 · 6 · 9 · 12 counts a query and there is no query here. 1–24 ⚑, and eight designs draw item 1 and keep the rest ⚑.The seam. A15 draws its own padding — 64 · 96 · 132 at 1440, 80 at 834, 64 at 390 — A17’s ladder. 4 Contrast Band has no padding of its own: the band carries it ⚑. 5 Full Bleed keeps vertical padding and has no side padding at any width ⚑. 6 Cover has no padding row at all — Height is its size ⚑.The collapse. Every archetype’s own ladder, with the departures named on each design. Two designs collapse at 1,080 rather than 767 ⚑ — 2 Split and 10 Playlist, A16·1’s number. Grids go to one column at 390 rather than two ⚑ — A14’s category-wide step, carried. The aspect never changes with the width ⚑ in any design.Print. Every design prints its poster, its title, its meta line and its URL ⚑ — a printed film is a still and an address, and the address is what makes it findable. 4 Contrast Band prints as 1 Player on white (A17·7’s rule); 9 Carousel prints its slides stacked, all of them, in authored order; 15 Tabs prints every panel stacked with its label ⚑, which is its no-JS rendering exactly.

Accent, once or never. The focus ring in thirteen designs; the active dot in 9 Carousel; the active underline in 15 Tabs; the action in 2 Split and 14 Slim Bar. None at all in 4 Contrast Band and 6 Cover ⚑, where the ring takes the carried colour with a 1 px dark outline — A29·3’s finding that Paper’s accent measures 4.0:1 on the band, and the same argument over an unknown poster. A15 never dims, tints or filters a poster ⚑ — not for dark mode, not on a band, not behind a caption. The ground changes; the pictures do not.
