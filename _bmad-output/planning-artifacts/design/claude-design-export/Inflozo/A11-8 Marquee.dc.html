A11 LOGO WALLS · DESIGN 8 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SEVEN CONTROLS · THE CATEGORY'S ONE BEHAVIOUR

Marquee

Eight to twenty-four logos on a track that runs continuously in one or two rows. The only design in A11 with motion, and the only one in the two categories before it with a behaviour at all — which is why every panel in A10 and thirteen panels in A11 name it while saying they have none.

It is also the one design in the category with no cell grid: a track has no columns, so the logos sit at their natural widths on a fixed 64 px gap and only their heights are normalised. Two things follow from the motion and are drawn here: a visible pause control, because WCAG 2.2.2 requires one, and a track that stands still under reduced motion — still this design, with no pause button, because nothing is moving.

REDUCED MOTION · THE TRACK STANDS STILL · STILL 8 MARQUEE · NO PAUSE BUTTON, BECAUSE NOTHING IS MOVING
Under reduced motion the track stands still and the design stays itself. A static full-bleed row, natively scrollable by the reader, the fade or hard edge as authored, and no pause button, because nothing is moving. The old rule drew 3 Grid here; a placed design is the design that renders, so the track loses its motion rather than the section losing its design. Nothing is hidden: every mark the track would have carried is in the row, and the reader can reach the rest of it by scrolling. ⚑ Mine, and the one state in A11 decided by a setting on the reader's machine rather than by the content. The pause button is required, not offered. WCAG 2.2.2 asks for a mechanism to stop motion that starts automatically and runs for more than five seconds, and this track runs indefinitely. Hover is not that mechanism — it does not exist for a keyboard or a touch reader — so hovering the track does not pause it and there is no control to turn the button off. At Rows Two the set is split in authored order — the first half on the top row, the second on the bottom — not interleaved and not balanced by width, so the top row can be visibly shorter than the bottom and the copies absorb it. One pause button stops both rows, because two buttons for one behaviour is two behaviours. ⚑ Mine.

On a phone the button moves from the right margin to under the track, on the left margin, 20 px clear. A 38 px button beside a 350 px viewport takes a ninth of the width off the artwork, and the track needs it. Pausing holds the track exactly where it is — no snapping to a logo, no easing to a stop, no returning to the start — and the glyph becomes Play. ACCESSIBILITY · THE ONE SECTION IN TWO CATEGORIES THAT MOVES 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide is met by a real button, not by hover and not by a preference. <button> with aria-pressed, an accessible name that changes between “Pause the logo wall” and “Play the logo wall”, a 38 px box in a 44 px target, and it is the first thing after the heading in the DOM so a keyboard reader meets the control before the moving content. It cannot be turned off by any control. 2.3.3 and reduced motion are met by not moving at all: at prefers-reduced-motion: reduce the track is drawn stationary, with no duplicate copy and no button — the same row JavaScript-off gets. Nothing is hidden in either state: the row carries every logo the track would have carried, reachable by scrolling. The duplicate copy is aria-hidden="true" and its images take alt="" — the one place in A11 where an empty alt is correct, because the same twelve names announced twice is a fault. The real list is one <ul> in authored order, announced once, in the order it was written rather than the order it happens to be scrolled to. Links inside a moving track are allowed and advised against, and the panel says why: the target moves, so a reader has to chase it. The advice is to pause first, which the button makes possible — and focus does pause the track, unlike hover, because a keyboard reader tabbing onto a moving link cannot chase anything. That is the one automatic pause in the design. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, eyebrow and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, the button's glyph 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 and its hairline 1.4:1 / 1.6:1, focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. The fade is a mask, so it does not sit over the button or the head, and nothing under it is focusable while it is hidden — the fade only ever covers a logo mid-exit. Print draws the wrapped wall, not the track — the same drawing reduced motion gets — because a printed page has no viewport to scroll and half a logo at the paper's edge is a defect.
