A29 · Archive Headers

The head of a route that lists posts: a tag, an author, or a date collection. It has one job the post header does not — it has to say what the reader is looking at before they have read anything, and it has to do it from three different Ghost objects with one design.

Fourteen designs, one shared field list, and four questions the brief asked: can one design carry all three archive types, what happens when the description and the count are missing, what happens when the tag has no feature image, and what a zero-post archive looks like. Every answer below is invented where Ghost does not decide it, and every invented answer is flagged here and on the frame that draws it.

THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · EVERY ANSWER HERE IS INVENTED AND IS FLAGGED HERE AND ON EVERY FRAME THAT DRAWS IT ⚑
1 · One design, three archives. A tag, an author and a date collection are one section with one field list. The eyebrow is what changes — "Tag", "Author", "Archive" ⚑ — and the name, description and count come from whichever Ghost object the route provides: {​{#tag}​}, {​{#author}​}, or the route's own title. Ghost has no native date archive ⚑: a date collection is a routes.yaml entry and its title is authored there, which is finding 1. Only 12 Portrait is built around one archive type and it draws the other two without breaking. Reconciled: because the eyebrow defaults by route, clearing the field brought the default straight back — so the label could never be turned off. Eyebrow: Show · Hide now exists in all fourteen ⚑.2 · Description and count, and an archive with neither. Most tags have no description ⚑ — Ghost leaves it empty and most publications never fill it. So absence is the normal case, not an empty state: the stack closes up and nothing is reserved. The count is always available from {​{pagination.total}​} and is the one fact every archive has. An archive with neither is a name and a label, and 1, 7, 8 and 9 are the four designs that still read as compositions at that size.
3 · A tag with a feature image, and one without. Two designs of the fourteen draw the image — 5 Full Bleed and 6 Image Split. 6 uses it: with none, the picture column goes and the text keeps its measure. 5 is built around it, and with none it renders itself ⚑: the frame keeps its Height and its bottom-left type and the picture slot resolves to the pack's contrast ground. Amended in the patch pass — the hand-off to 1 Centred is deleted, because no design ever turns into another design. The panel advises “no picture on this tag — 1 Centred reads better here” and never switches.4 · The zero-post archive. It is reachable — an empty tag, a new author, a month with nothing in it — and A17 Post Grids does not render at all when the query is empty. So A29 owns the message ⚑: the head renders in full, the count reads the archive.count_empty catalog string — "No posts yet" by default, and a template literal no longer ⚑, and emptyText renders under it. Hiding the head is not offered ⚑ — with A17 silent, hiding it leaves a blank route. 4 Panel and 10 Boxed are the two designs whose zero state looks finished rather than broken.

REPEATING ITEMS · ONE AUTHORED ARRAY IN THE WHOLE CATEGORY
There is exactly one field in A29 that is an array the user authors: links[], the tag list. Two designs draw it — 11 Filter as a pill row and 13 Index as ruled columns — and it is authored only at Tag row / Index list: Chosen tags. At the other two values the list comes from Ghost, ordered by post count or alphabetically, and is not editable as items ⚑ — it is a query, and the user changes it by changing the query.The posts themselves are never the section's items ⚑. A29 draws no posts; A17 and A18 do. No control here can add, remove, reorder or count them.Add an item. "+ Add a tag" at the foot of the list. It opens Ghost's tag picker rather than a blank text row ⚑ — a tag that does not exist has no archive to link to — and the chosen tag lands at the end of the list with its real name and count.Remove an item. The ✕ on each row. Never dimmed, and never silently emptied ⚑. At two tags the ✕ is still drawn and still clickable, and clicking it says why it cannot go lower — “a filter row needs at least two tags”, “an index needs at least two tags”. Amended in the patch pass: removing the last one used to return the control to Most used, which took the user's list away at a click.
Reorder. Drag by the ⠿ handle. Order is meaningful in both designs — 11 reads left to right and wraps; 13 reads down each column and then across ⚑, which is how a printed index reads and is why the two designs order the same list differently on screen.Minimum and maximum. Two to twelve ⚑. 11 Filter is designed for 6–10 and is correct at 2–12; at one pill it is "All posts" and one tag, which reads as a mistake and the panel says so. 13 Index is designed for 12 and is correct at 6–12; below the column count the last columns are empty ⚑ and the panel says to use Two. Above twelve, both truncate silently at the query limit ⚑ — finding 5.At zero items. The row or the index does not render at all and each design is its own head alone: 11 is its head left-aligned on the content box, 13 is its head on the content box ⚑. Neither becomes another design — amended in the patch pass. Neither draws an empty container, a placeholder pill or a "no tags yet" line, because on a live site a tag archive implies at least one tag exists.Inside an item the user edits content only — the label and the link ⚑. Both come from Ghost when the tag is picked and the label is editable, the link is not ⚑: it is the tag's archive URL and re-pointing it would break the active state. There is no per-item styling anywhere in A29, by construction: every control writes one value onto the section.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · EIGHT THINGS A29 COULD NOT SETTLE ON ITS OWN · 3 AND 5 AMENDED, 8 NEW
1 · Ghost has no date archive. §8 asks for tag, author and date archives from one design, and Ghost provides the first two. A date collection is a routes.yaml entry the publication has to write, and its title comes from the route file rather than from any Ghost object ⚑. Every design carries it; none can create it.2 · There is no related-tag query. 11 Filter and 13 Index draw sibling tags and Ghost cannot return "tags that co-occur with this one" ⚑. Both designs therefore draw the site's tag list, ordered by post count, and mark the current one. On author and date routes nothing is marked.3 · There is no focal point on a feature image. 5 Full Bleed crops 1440 × 440 from the centre and 6 Image Split crops 3:2 from the centre, because Ghost stores no focal-point data ⚑. A tag image with its subject at the top will lose it. Amended in this pass: imageFocus — Centre · Top · Bottom — now ships on 5 and 6 ⚑ and is applied to the Ghost image as well as an upload, so the crop is authored rather than computed. The finding stands for what Ghost stores: nothing. It is also short a horizontal axis, as A12 and A13 both found — Centre · Top · Bottom has nothing to say about a 3:2 crop of a portrait file.4 · pagination.total and count.posts can disagree. A29 counts what the archive query returns; A20 counts what the tag holds ⚑. On a site with member-only posts a reader can see "42 posts" on a tag card and "37 posts" on its archive. One of the two has to change and it is not a design decision.
5 · The tag list truncates silently at twelve. Both list designs cap at the query limit and draw nothing to say there are more ⚑ — a "+8 more" chip would be a link to a tag index page that, as A20 also found, Ghost does not provide. The cap is honest; the silence is a decision the architect should confirm. Amended in this pass: 11 Filter now closes its row with an All topics link ⚑ — a 44 px text link, never a pill — and 13 Index deliberately does not, because a thirteenth ruled line in a printed index is not an exit. The link's target is the product’s tags-route decision and is unset until that route exists; with no target it does not render.6 · There is no sticky module for anything but the site header. 14 Sticky pins with CSS and does not shrink, because a shrinking bar needs a scroll listener and the registry's only scroll module is header-scroll, which is A1's ⚑.
