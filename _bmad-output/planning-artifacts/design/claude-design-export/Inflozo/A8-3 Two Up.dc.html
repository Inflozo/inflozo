Two Up

Two cards of 632 px on a 32 px gutter, the quotation at Feature 27 inside the card. Every control is 2 Three Up's, at this card's own scale, so a site switching between the two keeps all six values — and what it buys with the switch is room for a quotation that runs long.

It settles one thing for the category, and it is an exception rather than a rule: this is the only design where the step-down stops at one step. A 300-character quotation lands at Card 20 here and not at Small 17, because a 632 px card at 20 px sets a 34-character line and holds 300 characters in eight of them. Everywhere else in A8 the second threshold applies.

Card padding is Comfortable 32 on this card and Comfortable 28 on Three Up's — the same named value at a different scale, which is how a named-value control survives a card that is half again as wide.

THE SAME 288 CHARACTERS AT BOTH STEPS · the argument for the exception, drawn

A 632 px card at Small 17 is a 62-character line, and a reader's letter set at a footnote's size in a card with 300 px of white under it reads as small print. At Card 20 the same string is eight lines on a 34-character measure and looks like something a person wrote. So the second threshold is suspended here and the sidebar says which threshold applied: “Feature · stepped down to Card, 288 characters · this design stops at one step”.
The neighbour is untouched — the step-down is per quotation, not per row, so 70 characters beside 288 stays at Feature 27 and the row is honestly unequal in content while equal in height. ⚑ The exception, its wording in the sidebar, and the 34-character measure argument are mine.

ONE QUOTE AUTHORED · one card of 632 centred · this design does not hand off

One quote does not hand off to 1 Single here, and that is the difference between two cards and three. A single 632 px card centred in a 1,296 width is a composition — Three Up's single 416 card is a fragment of a row that is not there — so this design keeps its own frame at a count of one and the panel says “1 of 7 quotes drawn” without naming another design. A list of two is a list, and at one quote a list of one is still a list — a reader switching designs hears the same structure at every count. 2 · The step-down is a visual size and never an announcement. Nothing in the DOM says a quote was set smaller: no attribute, no class read aloud, no “long quote” label. A screen reader hears the same sentence at every step, which is the point of not truncating. 3 · One focusable element, the foot link, with A6's ring. EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Every avatar carries Image focus — Centre · Top · Bottom in its Image Picker popover, never as a hidden field.

The only failure available is an empty repeater, and then the section does not render.
