A7 PRICING AND TIERS · DESIGN 15 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SETTLEMENT 1'S OTHER HALF

Both Prices

Both cadences on every card — yearly at the price size, monthly muted beneath — and no switch at all. §8 asked whether the monthly / yearly choice is a control or content; the other fourteen designs answer “a control”, and this one answers “content”. It is the design for a site that would rather show a reader both numbers than ask them to find one.

One line under the price carries the other cadence — “or €7 a month” — at 15 px muted, 6 px under the number. It carries no saving and no percentage. Both prices are Ghost's own strings; the whole-month saving that the switch computes elsewhere in A7 is the switch's script doing arithmetic, and this design has no script — a template cannot add, so the line a site would want here cannot be built. Nothing is struck through and no percentage is ever shown. Nothing here is authored and nothing is struck through.
The free tier's line is “No card, no renewal” — fixed copy, drawn where the second price would be, so the three cards keep one shape and the free card does not have a hole in it. That is invented and flagged; the alternative was 20 px of empty space in the free card at every width.
The action's Portal link takes the lead cadence, so at Lead price Yearly the button opens Portal on the yearly plan and a reader who wanted monthly changes it there. That is a real cost of drawing both and not offering a choice — it is stated on the frame and in the panel, and it is the argument for the switch that the other fourteen designs have.

TWO PRICES · both at price sizes — 48 and 34 — with a hairline between them · the loudest value, and the only one that changes the card's height

Two prices is the value a site reaches for and the one the editor advises against, with the reason stated at the control rather than the value hidden: two numbers of comparable size in one card is a card that asks a reader to do arithmetic before it asks them to subscribe. It exists because some sites genuinely price the two cadences as two products, and where they do, the second price keeps the price ladder's Medium 34 rather than a size of its own — the ladder has three steps and this is the second.

1 · The lead price is one readable string — “€70 a year” — as everywhere in A7, in its own paragraph. 2 · The second line is a second paragraph, not part of the first, and its middot is a real separator character read as a pause rather than a word. A screen reader hears two sentences: the price, then the alternative and the saving. DOM order is price, second line, description, action, benefits — A7's shared order with one element inserted, which is this design's only structural difference from 1 Cards. 3 · The action's URL carries the lead cadence and its label says neither. “Subscribe” at Lead price Yearly opens Portal on the yearly plan; the label is not lengthened to “Subscribe yearly”, because the visible label is the whole accessible name and a reader can change cadence in Portal. The cost is stated in the panel rather than papered over with an aria-label. No switch means no <button> anywhere in this section, no aria-pressed and no cross-fade — the one design in A7 with no behaviour of any kind, and the reason a site with a reduced-motion or no-JavaScript audience might choose it. Contrast · lead price 12.1:1 · second line 5.4:1 · everything else as 1 Cards. At 200% text the second line wraps to two lines and the card grows; nothing truncates.

Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript: this design is identical with script on or off — it declares no module — and it draws no saving line at all, since nothing server-side can compute one.
