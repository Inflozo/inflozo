1 Row's cells with a numeral after each value and a numbered list at the foot. The design for numbers that came from four different places — a subscriber count, an open rate, an archive index and a payment ledger are four claims with four sources, and a single section-level line saying “our own records” would be covering three of them.

It is the one design that reads note, and the one that does not draw the section's own source line: four footnotes under a sentence that already sources the section is two sourcing systems arguing. With no note on any stat no markers and no note list are drawn, the cells are unchanged, and 1 Row is advised.

The marker is a numeral in the body font at 13 px, superscript, muted, set tight against the value or its suffix. An asterisk at superscript size is a speck, a dagger is a convention most readers do not hold, and a bracketed numeral is a citation in a paper. The marker is not a link and does not jump — the notes are 200 px below and already on the screen, and a jump inside a section a reader can see is motion for nothing.
The notes sit 32 px under the stats on a 620 measure, 6 px apart, in text-muted at 13 px — the section source line's own position and treatment, holding a numbered list instead of a sentence. The numeral in the note is followed by a middot, not a full stop or a bracket, so it reads as a label rather than an ordinal. ⚑ Mine: the numeral, its size and colour, the middot, the 620 measure and the 6 px gap.

Two placements, and each is right for a different set. At the foot is for four notes that are variations on one thing — four dates from four records — and keeps the row of numbers clean. Under each stat is for notes a reader needs beside the figure, and it costs the row 30 px of height and the numerals their reason to exist: at Under each stat there are no markers at all, because a numeral pointing at a line 12 px below it is a footnote to itself. ⚑ Mine: both placements and the marker rule. The marker is noteMarker when typed and the stat’s own position when not, so a set with a gap reads 1 and 3 unless the site types otherwise. A stat with no note takes no marker and reserves no number. Reordering renumbers every marker left at its default, and a typed one travels with its stat. ⚑ Mine. The note is plain text at 60 characters and takes an optional noteUrl. With no URL it is muted text after its numeral and nothing is focusable; with one, the footnote’s text is the link at the section-link treatment — the accent’s fourth and last job in A10, and this design’s only tab stops besides the section link. Two sourcing systems in one section leaves a reader deciding which one governs; the notes are the more precise of the two, so they win here and 1 Row draws the other.

ACCESSIBILITY · ANNOTATED · A CITATION THAT IS NOT A LINK The marker is inside the stat's <p> as a <sup>, so the stat announces as “41,800, 1, readers on the Thursday letter”. A bare numeral in the middle of that string is not useful, so the <sup> carries aria-hidden="true" and the note is joined to the stat with aria-describedby instead — the reader gets “41,800, readers on the Thursday letter” and then, as a description, “Subscriber records, 1 January 2026”. ⚑ Mine, and the one place in A10 that uses a description relationship. The notes are an <ol> with the numeral drawn as text rather than as a list marker. A CSS counter would put the number outside the line box and the middot would have nothing to attach to; the text numeral keeps “1 · Subscriber records” as one string for both a reader and a screen reader. The list keeps its <ol> because the order is the numbering. ⚑ Mine. Nothing here is a link and nothing jumps. No href="#note-1", no back-reference arrow, no role="doc-noteref" and no role="doc-footnote": the DPUB roles describe a printed book's apparatus, support for them is uneven, and a section 400 px tall does not need in-page navigation. At Under each stat the description relationship is dropped, because the note is the next line of the same paragraph. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, label 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, marker and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 — the same as the label, not a step below it, because a citation a reader cannot read is not a citation. Link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. One tab stop per linked note, plus the section link. No motion. Print keeps the markers and the notes exactly as drawn, which is the one design in A10 that paper suits better than a screen.
