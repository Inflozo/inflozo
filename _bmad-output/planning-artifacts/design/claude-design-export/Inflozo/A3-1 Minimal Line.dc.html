Minimal Line

One row of brand and social above a hairline, with the legal line on its own second row beneath — reconciled this pass from the original single row. This is A3's baseline, and it is the design that settles the pieces the other fifteen inherit — the legal line's order, the locked attribution, the legalLinks repeater, the social row's disappearance when Ghost has nothing set, and the hairline that separates every footer from the page above it. No link columns, no form. It spends no accent.

© 2026 Orbit Weekly·Privacy·Terms·Built with Inflozo 4
1 · The footer is <footer role="contentinfo">, a sibling of <main>, and the last landmark on every page. Exactly one per document — the sticky design, A3·16, is the same landmark moved, never a second one. 2 · The lockup is one link to / wrapping mark and wordmark, accessible name “Orbit Weekly, home”. The mark is aria-hidden — it repeats the word beside it. 3 · Social is a <ul> with an aria-label="Orbit Weekly elsewhere"; each link's accessible name is the platform name, not the glyph, and carries rel="me". 34 px box inside a 44 px hit area at desktop; a real 44 px box at ≤ 767. 4 · The legal line is a <ul> labelled “Legal”, with the copyright as plain text in its first item. The middots are CSS, not characters, so a screen reader does not read “dot” four times. No heading. Minimal Line has no visible heading and adds no hidden one: with only three groups, a visually-hidden “Footer” heading would add an outline entry that describes nothing. The designs with link columns do add headings — those are real groups. Contrast · muted legal text on background is 5.4:1 light, 6.4:1 dark. Glyphs are 5.4:1 and are also labelled. Hover's 35% underline is decoration; muted → text is the state change that carries.

Reconciled this pass: Padding, Ground and Top edge are retired into the universal Vertical spacing, Background role and Top divider outside the design’s list; the new controls are in the updated spec.
