Three lines, authored, 40 characters each, and the theme writes none of them. That is the settlement: a claim about payment is a claim the site is making, so the field is empty by default and the editor offers the three drawn here as suggestions a site can accept, edit or ignore. A theme that shipped “Secure checkout” as a default would be publishing a security claim on behalf of a site that never read it.
The strip replaces the note rather than joining it — note is kept and not drawn, because “Cancel any time from your account page” and “Cancel in two clicks, any time” under the same hairline is the same sentence twice. The mark is A6·11's, at 40 px in text-muted, and the lines are 15 px at 500 in text: one weight above the benefits inside the cards, because they are the things a reader hesitating over a price actually wants.

Mark Rule is the default here where A6·11's was Check, and the reason is the tick's other job: a check mark 40 px below three cards full of check marks reads as a fourth benefit list. A 40 px rule reads as a heading's underline instead, which is what these lines are — three short headings with nothing under them. Check is still offered for a site whose assurances really are things included; Dot for a site that wants neither. The mark is text-muted at all three values and an accent mark is not offered — A6·11's rule, and there is an accent button 40 px above it.

Suggestions, not defaults. Nothing appears here until you write it — these three are true of every Ghost site using Stripe, and they are still yours to make.
No badge, seal, lock, shield or card-brand logo at any value. A row of payment-card marks is a claim about which cards a site takes, which comes from Stripe's own settings and changes without the theme knowing. The lines are words, and the only glyph in the strip is the mark. No money-back guarantee, trial length or refund window unless a site writes one, and where it does, the words are the site's alone — the theme has no way to check it and does not pretend to. A fourth assurance is refused. Three fit the three-column grid and the fourth would make a 2 + 2 that reads as a list rather than a strip. The editor names A5·11 Checklist for a site with more to say.

One to three lines, each ≤ 40 characters — A6·11's field exactly, including the cap and the advice to keep them under 20 at Stacked. Where a site writes one, it is drawn alone at the left of the strip rather than centred: a single sentence centred under three cards reads as a caption to the middle one.

1 · A <ul> of one to three <li>, after the tier list in the DOM at every arrangement and never inside it — the assurances are about the transaction, not about a tier, and a reader who has heard three tiers should not then hear a fourth item in the same list. They are not headings: 15 px at 500 is emphasis, and the section already has its one heading. 2 · The mark is aria-hidden at all three values and drawn from currentColor, so forced colours keep it — the rule is a border, the dot a background, the check a glyph. Nothing here is a checkbox: no role, no aria-checked, no input, exactly as A5·11 and A6·11 settled it. No assurance is ever a link. A site that wants to link its terms writes that in the note on another design; a 40-character line with a link inside it is a footnote pretending to be a promise, and the tab order of this section stays at one stop per tier. The hairline is a border-top on the strip, never an <hr>: it separates nothing semantically, and at one authored line it is still drawn, since the line still needs to sit apart from the cards. Contrast · assurance text 12.1:1 at 500 · marks 5.4:1 · hairline 1.4:1, decorative. At 200% text the strip stacks at every width, which is the phone's arrangement, and each line keeps its own mark.

DATA · from Ghost · outside the design's listTiers come from {{#get "tiers"}} filtered type:paid+visibility:public in Ghost's own price order; the free tier is its own type:free object. A Ghost list: no Add, no Remove, no reorder. Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript, elsewhere in the section: both of a tier's prices render side by side, each labelled in words, with no switch — and no saving line, which is the switch's own arithmetic.
