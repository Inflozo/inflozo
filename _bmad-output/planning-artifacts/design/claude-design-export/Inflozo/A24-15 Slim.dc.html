Slim

The title at 28 px with the meta beside it and a hairline under both. One line, 48 px of space above it, and then the piece. It is the quietest way A24 can start a post, and it exists for the sites where the writing is the event — a daily letter, a linked list, a notebook — where a 50 px title and 96 px of air above it would announce something the post is not.

It has its own title ladder at 24 · 28 · 34 and its own padding ladder at 48 · 64 · 96, and it is the only design in the category with both. It draws no standfirst and no picture. It is also the only design where the tag is not above the title ⚑ — it is the meta line's first term, because a line above a 28 px title is a second header.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES Two of this design's five controls exist to keep it from becoming another design. Title size is capped at 34 — six px below the shared ladder's Small — and the panel names 2 Flush Left rather than offering a fourth value. A slim header with a 40 px title is 2 Flush Left with a rule, and the moment a control lets a user cross that line the two designs stop being distinguishable in the picker. Its padding ladder is the only one in A24 that is not 64 · 96 · 132. ⚑ The reason is proportional rather than aesthetic: 96 px of space above a 44 px header is more than twice the header's own height, which makes the space the composition. At 48 · 64 · 96 the ratio at each step is close to what the other fifteen get, and Spacious 96 is the value a site uses when this design sits under a tall masthead. Measure is the only control in the category that lets a header be wider than the article it opens, and it is here because it is only affordable here: at 28 px a title across 1,296 is still one or two lines. Above 34 px it would be a masthead, which is A4's business, and that is the second reason the ladder stops where it does. Cut: alignment. Cut: an avatar step. The floor offers Off · Small 24 · Medium 36 · Large 44 and this design takes Off at every setting — the one design in A24 that draws a byline with no avatar anywhere. Cut: a date format. The long form is the floor's, and a slim header is exactly where an abbreviated date would look like a saving rather than a decision.
