Tabs

The tier names as a tab row and one tier's card shown at a time. A5·12 established the tabs; this design takes them verbatim and adds A7's second and last behaviour. It is the only design in the category where two tiers cannot be seen at once, which is a real cost, and the frames say what it buys: one full card on a phone instead of three stacked, with every benefit visible without scrolling past a price.

The tabs are the tier names and nothing else — no prices in the tab row, because a reader who can see three prices does not need to switch, and a tab row carrying them is 8 Slim Row with a panel under it. The selected tab opens the recommended tier by default, not the first or the cheapest, which is the one place in A7 where a recommendation changes what a reader sees rather than how it looks — and it is flagged twice, here and in the panel, because a default selection is a stronger nudge than a badge. With no recommendation authored, the free tier opens.
The badge rides in the tab rather than in the panel: the tab row is where the tiers are compared, so that is where the mark belongs. The panel is 7 Highlight's large card, verbatim — 360 for name, price, description and action, benefits behind a vertical hairline — at a stated 880 px measure rather than the full content width, because a single card 1,296 px wide is a band.

TAB 3 · QUARTERLY · the panel's height follows its own content and the section grows and shrinks

The panel does not hold a fixed height. Reserving the tallest tier's height would put 40 px of nothing under the shortest, and a section that changed height under a reader's cursor is only a problem if the change is above the fold — this one is under it, since the tabs are above the panel and stay put. The panel cross-fades at 160 ms and nothing slides: no carousel, no direction, no track. Under reduced motion the change is instant. The behaviour does not run while editing — the editor shows the tab the sidebar selects, which is how the frames above were drawn. The module is the registry’s tabs, with price-toggle beside it at Billing period Both, and all three panels are server-rendered: with JavaScript off they render stacked and visible, each preceded by its tab label as a heading — “All panels render stacked and visible, each preceded by its tab label as a heading”, the registry's own sentence and an acceptance criterion. Nothing in this design depends on script.

THE ACCENT, COUNTED Two accents are on screen at once here — the selected tab's 2 px underline and the recommended tier's button — and that is a stated exception to A7's one-accent budget, allowed because the underline is a state rather than an object: it says where you are, not what to do. A1·1 set the same 2 px accent underline for an active nav item and A5·12 carried it; this design carries it a third time rather than inventing a quieter mark. At Tab style Pill the accent is spent none — the active tab is a surface plane on the hover surface, the switch's own shape — and a site that wants the strict budget uses that value. The sidebar says so at the control.

1 · A real tab set: role="tablist" labelled “Membership tiers”, three <button role="tab"> with aria-selected and aria-controls, one role="tabpanel" with aria-labelledby its tab. Left and right arrows move between tabs, Home and End to the ends, and the tab row is one tab stop — A5·12's keyboard model, unchanged. Selection follows focus and no announcement is made; the panel is what changed and it is next in the reading order. 2 · The tier name is still an <h3> inside the panel, repeated from its tab, so a reader navigating by heading finds the open tier and a reader who arrives at the panel directly knows which one it is. The tiers are not a <ul> here — the tablist is the list — which is A7's one structural exception besides 2 Table's, and it is stated. 3 · All three panels are in the DOM, server-rendered. The unselected two are hidden by CSS scoped to the tabs module's own enabled class, which is what the registry's degradation requires — this corrects the claim this frame previously carried, that only the open panel was rendered. The reader-facing consequence stands as stated: one tier is legible at a time, so the editor names 1 Cards where a site's tiers are close in price and the comparison is the point. The badge is inside its tab as text and moves under the row on a phone, still text, still not aria-current. The default selection is the recommended tier — a nudge in behaviour rather than in colour, flagged twice.
