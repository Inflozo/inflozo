Numbered

Every H2 — and optionally every H3 — numbered from the article's own heading order, the numeral hanging in a 72 px column left of the measure or sitting above the heading inside it.

It is the only design in A25 that generates content on the page, and the only one whose furniture survives with JavaScript off. The four index designs build their lists from {{content}} in the browser and lose them without it; these numerals are CSS counters, so an author who inserts a heading renumbers the article by publishing and a reader with no scripts sees the same page.

The domains are the tell. Fifteen years on the same address, renewed every October by someone who never expected an audience and got one anyway. I have been keeping a list since 2019, and it has never once grown.

What they share is not taste. Some are beautiful and most are not. It is a refusal to move — off the address, off the software, off the habit — which turns out to be the rarest thing on the web.

💡If you are looking for the list itself, it is at the end of this issue — and every link on it was checked the week this went out.
01

Who is actually paying

Three of the four hundred are paid for by estates. Two are paid for by universities that forgot they were paying. The rest are individuals, and their renewal dates cluster in October for a reason nobody has explained to my satisfaction.

A site that outlives three platform migrations is not nostalgic. It is just paid for.Kicks Condor, 2019

I asked eleven of them why October. The answers divided cleanly:

·Four said the date meant nothing at all, and were mildly annoyed to be asked.
·Three said it was simply when they had money, which is the honest version of the same answer.
·Two said October was when their first host used to bill them. That host closed in 2013.

The renewal arithmetic

A domain costs about twelve dollars a year. Hosting a static site costs nothing at three of the four common hosts. The whole obligation, then, is one dollar a month and one afternoon a decade.

What the pages look like

Every H2 is numbered and the numeral hangs in a 72 px column 32 px left of the measure, right-aligned against the gutter so the numerals form a straight edge and the headings form another ⚑. It is a smaller column than 6 Hanging Heads' 240 on purpose: a numeral is two characters, and a 240 column around it would be a margin with a speck in it.
The numbers are CSS counters, not written by the author and not counted by a script ⚑ — an author who adds a heading in the middle renumbers the article by publishing, and a post that is edited on a Tuesday does not need the theme rebuilt. It is the only design in A25 that generates content on the page and it does it with no module at all.
Numerals are the pack's mono face in text-muted, never the heading font and never accent ⚑: a numbered heading is one thing, and a numeral at the heading's weight and colour would be two.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESNo authored field. The numerals are generated and cannot be edited ⚑ — a user writes a heading and a number appears. The article is Ghost’s ⚑ — paragraphs, headings, captions, quotes, lists and every card: clicking one on the canvas selects the section and the sidebar says “Edit in Ghost”. A25 has no authored URL field ⚑, so the Ghost-aware Link Picker never opens in this category, and no image field, so Image focus lands nowhere — the pictures are the author's and the Cards module sets their width. No authored array anywhere in A25 ⚑, so P0·3's item controls land nowhere, and P0·4 and Member Visibility land nowhere either — an index entry is navigation and a share link is not an offer. No fixed English visitor-facing string ships ⚑: the authored labels carry defaults and the rest are theme translation-catalog strings — “Link to this section”, the disclosure's heading count, and 8 Index Top's “{n} sections” and “min read”. The share strings are gone with the destinations ⚑: Ghost’s own share modal supplies the platform names and the copy confirmation, so the theme ships neither.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLESThe numerals are generated and are not content ⚑, which is what lets this design exist at all: an author cannot edit them, a translation does not have to carry them, and a heading inserted in the middle renumbers everything after it with no republishing. It also means they are not selectable and do not copy — a reader who copies a heading gets the heading, which is the correct behaviour and is stated in the spec because it surprises people.Cut: a starting number. A post that begins at 04 is a post whose numbering means something outside itself, and Ghost has no series primitive to mean it with — A24 recorded that finding and this design is the second place it bites. Cut: a numeral style — roman, alphabetic, decimal. The mono face is the pack's and a roman numeral in a proportional serif is a different design; a pack that wants roman numerals can supply them, and the section would not change. Cut: numbering the paragraphs, which is a legal document rather than an article.Cut: an index. A numbered article and a numbered index are the same list twice, and 8 Index Top at Index style Numbered is the design for a site that wants the list at the top — the panel names it. This is the pair the category came closest to duplicating, and the difference is that one numbers the article and the other numbers a summary of it.

The numeral is text-muted at 6.2:1 in dark and 5.4:1 in light, and it is deliberately quieter than the heading beside it ⚑ — it is a label on the section, not part of its title, and a numeral at text would read as the first word of the heading.
An accent numeral was drawn and refused: it is the third design in A25 to consider spending accent on something that repeats, and the floor's answer is the same each time — accent marks one thing, and eleven accent numerals down a margin mark nothing. The design spends its accent once, on A6's focus ring, and has none left over, which the spec's accessibility line states.
Measured: numerals 6.2:1, headings 15.1:1, body 12.4:1, captions 6.2:1.
THE WRITTEN SPEC · ALL TEN FIELDS
1 · Descriptor. Every H2 — and optionally every H3 — numbered from the article's own heading order, the numeral hanging in a 72 px column left of the measure or sitting above the heading inside it. The category's one design that generates content, and it does it with CSS counters and no module. 2 · Structural descriptor. article body · none · page · many · inline · numbered section breaks
Count many — five or more, which is what a numbered article implies — and the only many in A25. It is what separates this from 2 Plain, whose closed slots are otherwise identical: the numbered sections are a repeating unit the design lays out and 2 Plain has none. 3 · Archetype. article body. One departure: the hanging numeral column resolves to Above the heading below 1,200 ⚑ rather than collapsing to nothing, because the numbering is the design and a design that stopped numbering would be 2 Plain. 4 · Responsive rule. 1440 numeral column 72 px, 32 px gutter, hanging 104 left of the measure, right-aligned, mono 13 px in text-muted, zero-padded to two digits; section gap 44. 1,200 and below In the margin resolves to Above the heading, numeral 12 px with a 6 px gap. 834 measure 754, body 18, section gap 40. ≤ 767 measure 350, body 17, numerals 12 px and decimals 11 ⚑, section gap 34. 5 · Content fields. Read: {{content}} whole and specifically its heading order, post.access. Generated: the numerals ⚑ — not stored, not authored, not in the source, and not copied with a heading. Authored: none. Unread: indexLabel, indexNote, shareLabel. 6 · Controls, in sidebar order. Measure Narrow 620 · Comfortable 720 · Wide 840. Type scale Compact 17 · Comfortable 19 · Spacious 21. Drop cap Off · Drop — three lines · Raised. Paragraph rhythm Spaced · Indented. Numbering H2 only · H2 and H3. Number position In the margin · Above the heading. Anchor links Off · On hover · Always. Seven, and the universal trio outside them. Cut: a starting number, a numeral style, numbered paragraphs, an index.
7 · Data. {{content}} whole. Headings: 0 → no numerals and the design is 2 Plain ⚑, which the panel names; 1 → a lone 01, which is drawn and is the design's weakest state; many → as drawn, zero-padded to two digits and right-aligned so the column never moves; past 99 the padding stops and the numeral grows into the 32 px gutter, which holds to 999. An H3 before any H2 takes 00.1 ⚑. At the paywall cut the numbering stops at the cut and does not resume — a member who unlocks the rest sees 05 follow 04 because the counter is recomputed on the full page ⚑, not continued from a partial one. 8 · Empty state. An empty {{content}} renders nothing. A body with no headings renders the measure alone with no numeral column reserved ⚑. In the editor, one greyed line: "This post has no headings. There is nothing to number." A single heading renders 01 and nothing else, and the panel does not warn about it — one numbered section is a legitimate post, not an error. 9 · Behaviour module. None; core assumed. No-JS: pixel-identical ⚑ — CSS counters are a stylesheet feature, so the numbering survives with JavaScript off, which is what distinguishes this design from every index in the category: 1, 4, 7 and 8 all lose their list without toc, and this one loses nothing. It is the fourth A25 design that is the same document either way, after 2, 6 and 11. Edit-safe. 10 · Accessibility. <article> with no h1; heading levels as Ghost emitted them; scroll-margin-top: 96px. The numerals are ::before content and are aria-hidden by being generated ⚑ — a screen reader announces "Who is actually paying", not "01 Who is actually paying", and a reader who copies the heading copies the heading. The anchor link is inline with the heading and the numeral is never the link ⚑, so the 44 px target is on the ⌗ and not on two mono characters. Measured light: numerals 5.4:1, headings 13.1:1, body 13.1:1. Accent is spent once, on the focus ring, and nowhere else. Print: numerals print, above their headings ⚑. Repeating items. One, generated: the numbered sections. The user cannot add, remove, reorder or edit a numeral — they write a heading and a number appears, which is the plainest possible case of the rule that a control writes one value onto the section. Numbering and Number position each apply to every numeral at once. Selecting the section gives no text fields. Reconciled. Paragraph rhythm joins the quartet; seven controls. The universal trio outside the list, Vertical spacing inert. This is the design the category now recommends for a no-JS site ⚑ — CSS counters need no script, where the four indexes lose theirs; finding 6 is corrected to say so.
