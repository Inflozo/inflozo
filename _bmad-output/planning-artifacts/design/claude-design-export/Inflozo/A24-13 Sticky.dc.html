A header that leaves something behind it. At rest it is the title on 820 with a byline under it; once it has scrolled past, a 56 px bar holds at the top of the viewport carrying the title at 15/600 and a 2 px progress meter. The resting state is the primary frame, because behaviours do not run while editing and because the resting state is what a reader arriving at the page sees.

It is the one design in A24 that truncates a title, in the bar only, and that is allowed for one reason: the complete title is 200 px above it in the same document. It declares reading-progress for the meter and nothing else — the bar itself is position: sticky, which needs no module at all.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES The standing warning is A34's pattern, inherited: a design that declares a module carries a line above its controls saying what the editor will not show and what happens without JavaScript. Here it has to say two different things, because the bar and the meter degrade differently — the bar is CSS and survives, the meter is the module and does not — and a single sentence that covered both would be wrong about one of them. Bar height keeps one value at every width and that is the design's least obvious decision. ⚑ Every other spacing value in A24 steps down on a phone. A bar is furniture, not composition: 56 px is the smallest box that holds a 15 px line and a 44 px target, and a 48 px bar on a phone would take the target below the floor's minimum. Compact 48 is therefore desktop-only and resolves to 56 below 767, with the panel's line saying so. Cut: a bar position control. Bottom-of-viewport was drawn and refused — a bar at the bottom of a phone screen sits where the browser's own controls are, and the meter reads as a page-load indicator. Cut: a meter colour. It is the accent and it is the third accent A24 spends; the floor names it. Cut: a "back to top" glyph in the bar — the title already links there and a second control for the same destination is two targets 12 px apart. Cut, and this is the one worth recording: a share button in the bar. It is the most-requested thing a sticky reading bar carries. It belongs to 14 Share Row, which draws the row where a reader can see it before they start reading, and a design that declares two modules is a design that has stopped being one design.

WHAT THE DARK FRAME RECORDS The bar is background in both modes, not surface ⚑. A surface-coloured bar over a page-coloured article reads as a panel that has come loose; a bar in the page's own colour with a hairline under it reads as the page's top edge, which is what it is. In dark that means #171511 over #171511 with the hairline doing all of the work — which is why Bar edge None is disabled in dark ⚑, drawn greyed with "Needs an edge in dark mode": without either a hairline or a shadow the bar and the article are the same colour and the text scrolls under invisible furniture. Bar edge Shadow resolves to Hairline in dark, following A17's rule that warm shadows are dropped in dark. That makes two of the three values identical in dark and the third unavailable, so the control is effectively light-only and the panel says so rather than pretending otherwise. The meter is accent in both modes — #D96C3F light, #E0805A dark — on a border track. It is 2 px of non-text colour and is exempt from AA, but it is measured anyway: 3.1:1 light and 4.4:1 dark against the track, which is above the 3:1 non-text threshold in both. Measured on the bar: title 15.1:1 dark and 13.8:1 light, reading time 6.2:1 and 5.4:1.
