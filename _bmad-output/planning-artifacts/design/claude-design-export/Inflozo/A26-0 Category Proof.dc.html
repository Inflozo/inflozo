Fifteen ways to end a post

A26 draws everything between the last line of the article and the site footer: the tags, the author, the share row, the subscribe prompt and the two destinations. A25 owned the article; A3 owns the site. This category owns the space in between — the thing A25’s seventh finding asked for.

Four blocks and one prompt, in a fixed order, arranged fifteen ways. Nothing in the category is authored except its labels, two kickers, one URL and the subscribe strings: the tags, the authors and the neighbouring posts are Ghost’s, and no design offers an Add, a Remove or a reorder for any of them.

This frame carries the four settlements, the shared floor, the roster, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame and the shared field list. The per-design specifications are in A26 Post Footers - Spec.md.

THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · EVERY ANSWER HERE IS INVENTED AND IS FLAGGED HERE AND ON EVERY FRAME THAT DRAWS IT ⚑
1 · The author bio block, and a post with two authors. The block is a 56 px avatar, the name in the heading font at 19, two clamped lines of bio and a link to the author’s archive ⚑ — 1 Author Bio draws it and eleven designs reuse it unchanged. Two authors are two rows with a hairline between them ⚑, each with its own avatar, bio and link. Three or more collapse to one row of overlapped 40 px avatars and a names line with no bios ⚑ — except 11 Portrait, which draws the primary author alone ⚑, the owner's ruling of 30 August — and from three upward the line reads "Rosa Menendez and others", with no number. Ghost’s own author order is kept and is not selectable anywhere in the category ⚑.
Three designs answer it differently and each says so: 10 Big Type puts both names in one display line and drops the bio, 11 Portrait draws two half-width portraits, 14 Ledger writes one line and never reads the bio at all.2 · Tag row, share row and next/prev, and their order. The order is Tags · Author · Share · Subscribe · Next and prev, it is fixed, and it is not a control ⚑. A design may draw fewer blocks; no design may reorder them. The argument is that a reader who meets two posts on one site meets the same shape twice, and an order control makes that the user’s problem to keep consistent.
Eleven panels open with a Blocks control — one control, five named values, from Author to Everything — rather than five switches ⚑, because five switches have thirty-two combinations and four of them are empty footers. The four single-block designs (7, 8, 9, 12) have no Blocks control at all.
3 · The newsletter CTA here versus A22. A26 yields ⚑. A22 Newsletter owns the newsletter section — pictures, benefits, tiers, counts, a full-bleed ground — and A26 keeps one prompt in one design: 12 Subscribe, a surface bar with a line, a sentence, one field and one button. The other eleven reach it only through Blocks Everything, which draws that same bar ⚑ rather than a second one.
The route rule: where an A22 section is on the route, the footer prompt does not render ⚑ — and the same mechanism gives 9 Share Row the only share block on a page and resolves A25’s Share rail Under to Off. One of a kind, per route: it is one rule with three uses, and it is a finding for the architect, because nothing in the product expresses it today.4 · A post with no tags, no next post and no author bio. Every absent block is absent — no placeholder, no "Untagged", no em dash, no reserved height ⚑, and every hairline goes with the block it belonged to. No bio → the avatar, name and link stay and the row loses one line. No photograph → A1’s one-letter avatar, or A19’s plate at 11 Portrait’s crop. No next post → the remaining destination takes the full width and keeps its own label ⚑. No tags at all → 8 Tag Row renders nothing at all ⚑ — no label, no row, no reserved height. No design in A26 turns into another design: a footer hides what does not apply, and where a panel prefers a different design it says so as advice.
With every block empty a footer renders nothing at all, and the editor draws the absent blocks dashed and labelled.

All fifteen are distinct on the five closed slots. Archetype carries the category — stack five times, bar three, split two, one each of nav, form, edge rail, table and grid-of-N — because containment and ground barely move: fourteen none and no card at all ⚑ (3 Card’s plane is its ground, not a box round the section); eleven page, three surface, one contrast.

TOKENISATION PROOF · A26·1 AUTHOR BIO · THREE PACKS · LIGHT AND DARK · drawn at a 640 crop with the measure at 592 and the avatar at 48

Four hairlines, one plate and a pill: the smallest surface a section can present to a pack

A25’s proof was a type proof at reading scale. This one is a proof about edges — the footer’s whole vocabulary is border, text, text-muted and one avatar plate, and a pack changes all four at once. Six frames, three radii, three heading faces, three accents, three hairline colours. Nothing moves. The pills are the pack’s radius, the plate is the pack’s hover surface, the hairlines are the pack’s border, and the arrangement is identical to the pixel in all six.

Four things to notice. The pill is the whole argument: at radius 14 a tag pill is a lozenge, at radius 6 it is a chip, and neither needed a different layout ⚑. Second, the avatar is a circle at every radius — it is the one shape in A26 that does not take the token, and that is deliberate: a rounded-square avatar at radius 6 is an app icon ⚑. Third, no accent appears in any of the six, because 1 Author Bio spends none at rest — the packs differ here only in ink and edge. Fourth, Ink’s heading face is its body face, so the name reads as bold body text rather than as a heading, and the block still holds because the size and the weight carry it ⚑.

What holds. The tag row wraps to two lines and the block grows; the second author’s row is a name and a link with no bio line and no reserved height ⚑; the four share links fit the content width with the long label; the single destination takes the full measure, keeps its Next label and clamps at two lines ⚑.
What is uncomfortable and is not corrected. Seven pills is a heavy first block for a footer, and 1 Author Bio has no tag limit — 8 Tag Row’s Tags shown is the answer and it is a different design ⚑. The two author rows are 190 px of footer before the share row starts. Both are the honest shape of this content, and the panel’s advice is 14 Ledger, where the same post is six lines.
What would break and does not exist: a tag longer than about 34 characters would push a pill past a third of the measure — Ghost caps tag names at 191 and nothing in the theme caps them shorter ⚑, which is a finding.

Nineteen authored, ten read, one derived — thirty in all after the reconciliation pass, and every authored field is a label, a kicker, a URL or one of the subscribe strings: revisionNote is cut ⚑, so nothing in A26 is authored about the post rather than about the section — which is what made that one field wrong.
Switching between any two of the fifteen is lossless. tagsLabel survives dormant in the eleven that do not draw it, the three subscribe fields in the fourteen without a prompt, the seven label sets in the designs that do not draw them, and 12’s five member strings in the fourteen without a prompt. No design needs a field the category does not have, and no field has nowhere to live — which is rule 3, checked design by design.
Derived, never stored: the one letter in an avatar fallback, "and others", the author’s post count, the long form of a date, and the fact that a post has been updated at all ⚑.
POST FOOTERS PATCH · 30 AUGUST 2026 · THE CATEGORY-LEVEL CHANGES AND THE RULE THAT REQUIRED EACH
Bylines and tag counts carry no number“Rosa Menendez and three others” → “Rosa Menendez and others” on 1, 2, 3, 4, 14 and here; “+4 more” → “+ more” on 8 Tag Row. Ghost’s templates cannot count, add or remember, and inside a loop they cannot see the previous item, so a number in either place could only have been hard-coded. Both are catalog strings, and no number appears in a byline or a tag row anywhere in the category.
“More from” uses the full name“More from Rosa Menendez”, never “More from Rosa”. Ghost gives a theme one display name and no separate first name on the versions we support. authorLinkLabel’s default changes with it in all seven designs that read it, and in 10 Big Type, which reads it as a prefix.
Captions that carry a link14 Ledger’s Photography row takes Post Headers’ treatment: feature_image_caption is rendered as HTML, so a photographer’s credit link survives. The old rule — plain text, never a link — is withdrawn. The row is still not itself a link, the caption is still Ghost’s and says “Edit in Ghost”, and a post with no caption still draws no row.
Avatars with no photographOne letter, never two — “Rosa Menendez” shows R. Every person in A26 comes from Ghost, which cannot split a display name, so two initials were never available. Changed on 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 13, 14 and 15, including 11 Portrait’s plate, which is now the one-letter plate. Sizes, colours and crops are unchanged.
No design ever turns into another designThree hand-offs deleted and six phrases reworded. 8 Tag Row no longer hands off to 6 Slim: an untagged post renders nothing at all. 7 Next and Prev no longer falls back to 1 Author Bio: with no neighbours nothing renders. 11 Portrait no longer hands off at three authors: it draws the primary author alone — the owner's ruling of 30 August — {{#primary_author}} giving the portrait, name, bio and archive link, with the co-authors as one muted line and no number.
