Big Type

Four posts, four titles at 48 px, and nothing else on the page but a tag and a date. The category's one display moment, spent on the posts rather than on the section head — which is the inversion that makes it a different design rather than 1 Rows with a bigger font.

It is the only A18 design whose item count is few. A 48 px serif title is 90 px of page a row, so five posts is a screen and fifteen is a mistake — the count line says so and the design does not enforce it, which is the category's standing position on advice.

The section head is 28 px and the post titles are 48. That is the design: the head steps down to Small so the posts can step up to Display, and a page cannot have two display moments. It is the only design in A18 where the head is smaller than the rows it introduces, and the only one where the head is at Small by default.
The title measure is 1,100 rather than the row's full 1,296. A 48 px Georgia line at 1,296 px runs to about 62 characters, past the comfortable maximum for a display size; 1,100 holds it near 52 and lets the 69-character title break onto a second line where it belongs. Titles wrap and are never clipped — A17's card rule, and this is the design where it matters most, because a clipped 48 px title would be the most conspicuous failure in the library.
The tag has moved into the meta line, after A17·9 and for the same reason. A 13 px tracked eyebrow above a 48 px title is a caption on a poster — the size difference is so large that the eyebrow stops reading as part of the same object. “Reporting · 12 March 2026” at 14 px under the title is one quiet line under one loud one, which the page can hold. ⚑ It is the second design in the library to depart from the card's DOM order, and A17 already flagged the first.

Two values, and the second is not for everyone. Display 48 is the library's Display step, already used by A17·9 and by nothing else; Huge 64 exists above the ladder and this is the only place it appears in 485 designs ⚑. It is offered because a three-post list of long-form essays is a real page and 48 does not carry it; it is not the default because 64 px turns a 69-character title into three lines and a five-post list into a scroll. Line height tightens as the size grows: 1.08 at 48, 1.05 at 64, with tracking from −0.02em to −0.025em. Both are the pack's display settings rather than this design's — a pack whose heading face needs different values supplies them, which is the mechanism that keeps a 64 px title from falling apart in the packs with a wider face. Row density is 20 · 28 · 40 here, not 16 · 24 · 36. Large type needs more room above and below it before a rule reads as a separator rather than as an underline. The fourth design in A18 to keep a shared control name and change its quantities, and the pattern is now regular enough to be a finding rather than four separate notes.
ALIGNMENT CENTRED · AND WHY THE RULE STAYS FULL WIDTH · drawn at 600 crop

Centred is the one alignment control in A18 and it belongs here. Every other design hangs something at an edge — a date, a meta column, a picture, a number — and a centred row with a hung anything is incoherent. This design hangs nothing, so it is the only one that can centre. The measure drops from 1,100 to 900 at Centred, because a centred line that nearly fills its container reads as justified text rather than as a centred object, and the ragged ends are what make centring legible. The rule stays at the full 1,296 — a centred rule under a centred title is a certificate. The head centres with the list at Centred and the link centres under it. ⚑ Mine: an alignment control that left the head alone would produce a left head over a centred list, which no site would choose deliberately. One value, one page-wide effect — the same principle as every other control in the library writing a single value onto the section.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESEvery visible authored text edits inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored: the eyebrow, the title, the sub, the note and the link label. Post content is Ghost’s and is never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking a post title, tag, author name or excerpt says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL field opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the head link’s target defaults to Matches the query so it cannot go stale when the query does. This design draws no image at any setting, so rule 10’s Image focus has nothing to attach to ⚑. No button, so rule 11’s optional icon has nothing to attach to ⚑ — the head link is text with an accent underline, and an icon on it would make it look like a button. No fixed English visitor-facing string ships: the head strings are authored with no defaults and this design draws no furniture text of its own; the main feed’s empty pair is authored, with defaults. No authored list exists in A18, so the P0·3 item controls have nothing to govern: the rows are Ghost’s and there is no Add ⚑. There is no Preview control and there never was one to remove.

Member visibility is not offered anywhere in A18 ⚑ — every action here is navigation, and a list that hid itself from readers would hide the site’s writing rather than an offer.
THE ONE DESIGN IN A18 WHERE THE META CONTROL'S VALUES ARE DIFFERENT Meta reads None · Date only · Tag and date · Name, tag and date — the tag is in the list because it lives in the meta line here rather than above the title. A17·9 made the same substitution and this design takes it verbatim, which means a site switching between A17·9 and A18·9 keeps its meta value intact and a site switching from 1 Rows gets the nearest equivalent. Excerpt is not offered, and this is the design where its absence is most obviously right. A 16 px sentence under a 48 px title is two type sizes too far apart to be one object; the title is the excerpt here, at four times the size, and adding a second one would say the design has nothing to be big about. The count line carries the only standing warning in the category: “Display titles read best at five or fewer.” It does not cap Show, because a site running fifteen 48 px titles has made a choice that is loud rather than wrong, and the library's position is that the panel advises and the site decides. At Title size Huge the advice drops to three. Cut: a hover fill and a hover colour. At 48 px the accent underline is 2 px under a very large word and reads clearly; a colour change on a display title is a flash rather than a state. Cut: a number or ordinal — 11 Numbered exists, and a numbered display list is a magazine cover rather than a section.

Light type on a dark ground looks heavier than the same type inverted, and at 48 px that is visible rather than theoretical. The library's answer is to change nothing: no weight step, no tracking adjustment, no optical size switch between modes, because each of those is a rule-1 violation dressed as craft. What is specified instead is the pack's responsibility — a pack whose heading face bulks up in dark should ship a variable-weight axis or a lighter display weight, and A18 cannot fix it from a section.
Three tokens change and the hairline is the fourth. Contrast is 15.64:1 on the title and 6.89:1 on the meta.
THE WRITTEN SPEC · ALL TEN FIELDS
1 · Descriptor. Three to five posts as display-size titles on an 1,100 px measure with one quiet meta line each, the section head stepped down to Small so the posts can be the page's display moment. 2 · Structural descriptor. feed · none · page · few · none · display-size titles
Unique on item count — the only few in A18. 3 · Archetype. feed. One departure: the archetype's title ladder steps 22 · 21 · 19 across widths and this design's steps 48 · 38 · 28, because a display size cannot narrow proportionally with a row. 4 · Responsive rule. 1440 title 48 on an 1,100 measure (900 at Centred), meta 14, row padding 28, head 28. 1080 title 44, measure 936. 834 title 38, measure 674, head 26. ≤ 767 title 28, head 21, meta 13, row padding 20. Section: the six, and the sub is rarely drawn — a 17 px sub under a 28 px head above 48 px titles is a third size the page does not need; it is offered and not recommended. Query: all four. Post: title, url, published_at, primary_tag (drawn in the meta line, not above the title), primary_author.name at Meta's fourth value. Never reads excerpt, feature_image or reading_time — the shortest field list in A18 after 3 Slim's. 6 · Controls. Title size Display · Huge. Alignment Left · Centred. Rule Off · Between rows · Boxed. Row density Compact · Comfortable · Spacious (20 · 28 · 40). Meta None · Date only · Tag and date · Name, tag and date. “View all” link Matches the query · Custom. Six, plus the universal trio (Background role · Vertical spacing · Top divider) and the Data group, both outside this list; Padding is retired into Vertical spacing. No Tag row — the tag lives inside the Meta enum here (Tag and date · Name, tag and date), the same exception A17·9 records. No Excerpt, no image, no meta position.
7 · Data. #get "posts" per the Data group, Count a stepper with this design's bounds. Designed for 3, 4 and 5; correct at 1 to 6, legible to 10, and long past that. The count line advises five or fewer, three at Huge, and does not cap Show. 0 published: no render. 0 in the editor: three outlined title blocks at the set size plus the query line — the largest editor empty state in the library, which is appropriate, since the setting's cost should be visible before the posts arrive. 1: one 48 px title on a page is a composed statement and this is the strongest single-post case in A18; it is close to A19 Featured and the panel says so without switching. More than Show: not drawn. 8 · Empty state. No tag at Meta Tag and date → the date stands alone with no separator dot, drawn on post 4 in the primary frame. No author at Meta's fourth value → tag and date only. No image case exists and no excerpt case exists — this design reads neither, which makes it, with 3 Slim, one of two in A18 that cannot be damaged by a thin post. Its exposure is the opposite one: a very long title at 48 px is three lines, and the design absorbs it because titles wrap and are never clipped. 9 · Behaviour module. None; core assumed. No-JS degradation: pixel-identical with JavaScript off. Edit-safe. Considered and refused: reveal, whose degradation is “content renders fully visible” and which would be harmless — refused because A17 refused it on a grid and a display list is the design most likely to make an animated entrance look like a slideshow. Also refused: typewriter, which the registry has and which a big title invites; the section is a list of destinations, not a headline. 10 · Accessibility. <section aria-labelledby> → <h2>, which is visually the smallest heading on the page and structurally still the parent — worth stating, because the design's whole idea is that visual size and document structure disagree here. <ul> of <li>, each one <a>, title an <h3>. ⚑ DOM order departs from A17's card: title then tag, because the tag is in the meta. A17·9 made the same move and it is the same flag — the card's field order now has two exceptions across the two categories, and the architect should write them into the card. Date a <time datetime>. A6's ring on the row, with 28 px of padding to sit in. Contrast 15.46:1 and 5.56:1 light, 15.64:1 and 6.89:1 dark.
