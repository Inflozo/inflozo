Overlap

A card that crosses the footer's top edge, so the last ask on the page and the end of the page are one object. It answers §8's third question directly rather than by rule: most banners sit above the footer and pretend not to notice it, and this one is built out of the join. It is also the only design in A6 with a precondition — no footer beneath it, no design.

The card is 4 Card's plane at Raised, and the overlap is 88 px of it hanging into the footer. A4·18 Overlap Card settled the mechanics for a hero and its own card: the overlap is never more than 40% of the overlapping element's height, past which the two objects stop reading as a join and start reading as a mistake. Carried here, a 380 px card gives a ceiling of 152 and the two named values sit well under it. The footer needs a plane for the join to be visible, so the design draws the footer on the hover surface — a background footer under a surface card would leave the overlap invisible and the card would just look badly spaced. That is the design asking something of a section it does not own, and it is stated on the frame, in the panel and in the spec: A6·13 requires the footer's ground to differ from the card's, and the editor names the footer designs where it does.
The footer's top padding absorbs the overlap — 132 px here rather than its own 40 — which is the one place A6's above-the-footer rule is not enough: the theme's normal step-down does not apply, and the footer instead adds the overlap to its top padding so its first row still sits 44 px below the card's bottom edge.

THE CEILING · 40% OF THE CARD'S HEIGHT A4·18's rule, carried: past 40% the card's bottom edge crosses into the footer's own first row rather than passing behind its top margin. A short card clamps the overlap rather than the design refusing to render — a title-and-button card at 220 px takes 88 px of overlap as its ceiling, and Standard behaves as Shallow there with the sidebar saying so.

Both values are absolute rather than proportional, and the ceiling is what makes that safe. A proportional overlap would move every time a site edited its sub, which is the kind of number that looks fine in a design and drifts in use.

THE PRECONDITION · when the design hands off · 560 crop
NOT THE LAST SECTION ON THE PAGEThere is nothing to overlap. The design is 4 Card at Raised, the overlap dropped, and the editor says which section is below it.
THE FOOTER'S GROUND MATCHES THE CARD'SA surface card on a surface footer has no visible join. The card steps to the hover surface and keeps its hairline, which is 4 Card's Tinted treatment used as a substitution rather than a choice. The sidebar states it and names the footer setting.
THE FOOTER IS A3·5 CONTRAST BAND OR A3·14 IMAGE BANDBoth work, and both are the design at its best: a light card crossing onto an inverted band or a photograph is the clearest join the library can draw. On A3·14 the card keeps its md shadow in both modes, since a photograph is not a ground a hairline reads against.
TWO OVERLAPPING SECTIONS ON ONE PAGEA page running A4·18's hero card and this banner has an overlap at both ends. Allowed, and advised against once: the shape stops being a join and becomes the page's habit.

§7·8's hand-off, used deliberately for the second time in A6 — 8 Image Split uses it for a missing picture, this design for a missing neighbour. The rule is the same both times: where a design cannot exist, it becomes the design that can and the editor names it, rather than rendering something broken or rendering nothing at all.

1 · The overlap is visual only. The banner is a <section> and the footer is a <footer>, complete, adjacent and in order — one does not contain the other, and nothing about the join reaches the accessibility tree. A reader hears a section with a name, then a footer landmark. 2 · The card is a <div> with position: relative and z-index: 1; the overlap is a negative bottom margin on it and matching top padding on the footer. No transform, no absolute positioning, no fixed heights — so the card grows with its content, the footer's padding follows, and nothing is clipped at any zoom. 3 · The footer's first row never sits under the card. Its top padding is its own value plus the overlap, so there is always 44 px of clear footer between the card's bottom edge and the first link. At 200% text this is checked against the grown card, not the authored one, and the padding is computed from the card's real height. Contrast · as 4 Card: title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · sub and note 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · button 4.6:1 / 8.1:1 · the card against the footer's ground 1.1:1 light and 1.2:1 dark, which is the honest number for this shape — the join is carried by the hairline and the shadow, and in forced colours by the hairline alone. Reduced motion changes nothing: the overlap is a position, not an animation, and nothing here moves on scroll.
