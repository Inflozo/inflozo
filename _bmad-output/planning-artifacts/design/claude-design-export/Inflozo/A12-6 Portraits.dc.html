Portraits

Two or three photographs at a named crop, filling their cells, with the name under the image or set over it. The first design in A12 to leave the circle — and the design that answers what happens when the photograph stops being a mark beside a name and becomes the thing the section is made of.

It has a hard ceiling of three. Above three people it hands off to 1 Grid, so its frames draw a three-person list rather than the twelve the rest of the category uses — the same three people, in their authored order, including the one with no photograph. Four 4:5 portraits at 306 px is a contact sheet, and A12 has one of those: 9 Faces.

CAPTION OVERLAID · THE ONE SCRIM IN A12 · drawn at 640 crop
Marguerite OkonjoEditor and founder RF SCRIM · A4·4'S RULE · contrast AT 72% AT THE FOOT, 42% AT 55%, 0 AT THE TOP THE SCRIM COVERS THE BOTTOM 45% AND IS DRAWN ONLY AT CAPTION OVERLAID A PERSON WITH NO PHOTOGRAPH KEEPS THE CAPTION UNDER THE BLOCK AT BOTH VALUES · NAME OVER INITIALS IS TWO KINDS OF TYPE IN ONE BOX
This is the only place in A12 where type sits on a photograph, and therefore the only scrim in the category. It is A4·4's — a gradient of the pack's contrast from 72% at the foot to nothing at 45% of the height — and it is never a flat black overlay and never a full-height wash, both of which take the photograph down with the type. ⚑ The percentages are mine. The scrim is what makes the contrast measurable. The theme cannot measure an uploaded photograph, so it does not try: the name is drawn on the scrim's own darkest band at 12.1:1 against contrast at 72%, and the role at 7.4:1. Without the scrim the same white name on a pale photograph is unreadable and unmeasurable — so Caption Overlaid without a scrim is not a value. At Caption Overlaid the name and role take the band's carried colour, not text — #FBF9F5 and a 90% step of it — in both light and dark, because the scrim's lightness does not follow the mode's. A11·7 made the same finding about its band. Refused: a hover that lifts the scrim, a caption that fades in, a gradient in the accent, a border on the image, a shadow, and a duotone.

Two counts: three at 416 and two at 632 on a 32 px gutter — A8·8's even division and its wider gutter, because two planes need a wider gap than three to read as two things. A 632 × 790 portrait is the largest image in A12, and the panel says what it needs: 1,264 px on the long edge. Name Large 27 exists here and nowhere else in the category. Under a 520 px image a 20 px name is a caption; 27 makes it the second element rather than the third. It is A8's Feature quote size, borrowed rather than invented, and it is still not a heading — the name is a <p> at 27 px, because size is not level. Over three people → 1 Grid, named in the panel. The ceiling is the design: this is the one place in A12 where adding a fourth person changes which design the site is using, and the editor says so before it happens.

Ground rule 10 fixes Centre · Top · Bottom; this case is the one the instruction cited, and it crops on the other axis.

“Upload a different file” was the old answer and it is withdrawn. Image focus ships on photo in the Image Picker’s popover — a field on the image, never a section control, which is A14’s ruling and the only reading that survives A12’s own rule that nothing about an item’s size, crop or spacing is exposed: the ratio stays the section’s and a per-person crop is still refused. It is live in all fourteen designs that draw a photograph, because a circle covers too, and load-bearing in the three that name a crop — this design, 8 Founder and 13 Rail. Where it cannot help is drawn beside it: a landscape file in a 4:5 or 1:1 box is cropped left and right, and three vertical values cannot move it. ⚑ Mine: adopting the field on the circle designs as well, and the architect note.

ACCESSIBILITY · A 520 PX PHOTOGRAPH WITH NO ALT TEXT The photograph is still alt="", at 416 × 520 as at 56 × 56. The rule is not about the image's size but about what is beside it: the name is drawn under or over every portrait at every setting, so alt text would repeat it. The one image in A12 with real alt text is 4 Story and Team's group photograph, and it has no name beside it. ⚑ A12-0's settlement 1. Not a <figure> and not a <figcaption>, at either Caption value. A11·11 settled this for its captioned wall: a figure is a self-contained unit referenced from elsewhere, and a person in a team is a list item. The overlaid caption is the same two <p>s in the same DOM position as the caption under the image — only the box they sit in moves. The scrim is a background-image on the caption's own box, not a separate element, so there is nothing extra in the DOM and nothing to announce. In forced colours the scrim is dropped and the caption falls back to under the image, because white type on an unknown photograph with no scrim is the one state this design cannot allow. ⚑ Mine. Contrast: name and role under the image 15.8:1 and 5.6:1 light, 15.1:1 and 6.0:1 dark. Overlaid: 12.1:1 and 7.4:1, measured against the scrim at its 72% band rather than against the photograph — the theme cannot measure an upload, and the scrim exists so that it does not have to. Initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1. Nothing moves. No parallax, no reveal, no hover on the image; the only transition is a linked name's underline. At 400% zoom the portraits are one across and the overlaid caption keeps its scrim.

Image focus: Centre · Top · Bottom sits in the Image Picker’s popover on photo — A14’s ruling adopted: where a photograph is cropped is content, so the focus is a field on the image and never a section control. This is the design the field was adopted for: a 4:5 or 1:1 crop of a 632 × 790 cell discards most of a tall file, and Top is what keeps a head in frame — “upload a different file” was never an answer. The crop itself stays a section value: one named ratio for every cell, and a per-person crop is still refused. It moves the crop on the axis the box actually crops, so a landscape file in a 4:5 or 1:1 box — cropped left and right — is the case these three values cannot answer: ARCHITECT: the Image focus vocabulary is short a horizontal axis. Two or three, one crop each; a fourth does not break the section, it changes the design — the panel reads “4 people — 1 Grid draws all four” before the add. Two → two 632 cells, one → 8 Founder. At Source Ghost authors this becomes P0·3’s Ghost-sourced card: no Add person, no drag handles, no Remove. Name, photograph and bio are read-only and answer “Edit in Ghost”. One field stays editable per row — Role — stored in the theme and keyed by the author’s slug, because Ghost has no role. Image focus is stored the same way. An extension of P0·3, whose Ghost card was read-only throughout.
EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. linkUrl opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the section link takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Per person, name and role edit inline in the caption, at both Caption values; at Overlaid the toolbar sits above the caption on the scrim and the text stays in the carried colour while it is edited. At Source Ghost authors the name and the bio are Ghost-owned: the toolbar is replaced by the plain-text lock pill and clicking says “Edit in Ghost”. The Role override is ours and stays inline-editable.
