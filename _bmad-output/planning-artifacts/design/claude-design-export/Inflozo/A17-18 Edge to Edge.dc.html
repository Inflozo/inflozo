Edge to Edge

Images across the full viewport with no gutter and no page margin, text beneath each. The only full-bleed design in A17 and the only one with no page margin at all — the photographs touch each other and touch the edge of the screen.

Removing the gutter is a larger change than it sounds. A gutter is what makes each cell a separate object; without one the row becomes a single band of imagery that happens to be divided. That effect is what this design is for — a publication showing that it has pictures — and it is why the design cannot exist without them.

It is the third and last of the image-dependent designs. With no feature images anywhere in the set it hands off to 9 Big Type, alongside 8 Overlay and 11 Masonry — and of the three it fails hardest, because a gutterless band of tag plates is one solid rectangle of empty surface.

Three 480 px cells filling the 1,440 viewport exactly, with zero gutter — 1440 / 3, not (1296 − 48) / 3. The category's 1,296 content width and 24 px gutter are both abandoned here, which makes this the one design in A17 that does not use A10's divisions: a full-bleed row divides the viewport rather than the content column. ⚑ Mine, and the reason this design's cell widths appear nowhere else in the category.
The images have no radius, and that is the strongest single argument against this design existing. A17-0 says radius comes from the pack token and is applied consistently; a rounded corner on a full-bleed image would round away from an edge that is not there. So this is the only squared image in the category — a departure from the pack rather than from the category's taste, and the one place where rule 1's “only tokens change” is strained. ⚑ Flagged rather than argued away.
The text is inset 24 px inside its cell; the head keeps the page's own 72 px margin. Without the first a title would start hard against the screen edge; without the second the head would not align with any other section on the page. So the head and the grid sit on two different grids — deliberate, and the exact inverse of 17 Panel's problem, where the head is the thing pushed out of alignment.
Row gap 48: the images touch side to side and never top to bottom. Touching in both directions is a mosaic, and a mosaic has no reading order. Post 3's plate is a 480 × 320 rectangle of flat surface, and it is the only plate in A17 with no radius and no gutter. Several designs draw a larger one; every one of those carries the pack's radius and sits inside a 24 px gutter, so it reads as a card that happens to have no picture. This one shares a raw edge with the photograph beside it, so it reads as a hole punched in the band rather than as a cell in it. ⚑ Mine, and the distinction the dark frame's finding rests on — it is about containment, not size.

TEXT OVER · 8 OVERLAY'S SCRIM, GUTTERLESS · AND THE PLATE CELL CANNOT DO IT
Text Over borrows 8 Overlay's scrim and inherits its whole problem set — 45% in light, 55% in dark, and the pale-image case. What it cannot inherit is the plate: a post with no image has no scrim to sit on, so its title stays in text on a plate while its neighbours are in white on photographs. Two title colours in one row is the worst thing this design can produce, drawn above so it is not discovered later. ⚑ Mine. Which is why Text Under is the default and Text Over is offered for a site that has an image on every post. The note says so: “Text over images needs a picture on every post, and a scrim — the Scrim row below is the condition of this value existing ⚑.” It is not disabled — a site knows things about its own posts that the section cannot. The real risk of a gutterless band is two adjacent photographs of similar tone reading as one image. Two harbour pictures side by side with no gutter and no border become a panorama, and the eye finds the boundary only at the caption. Refused: a hairline between cells — that is a gutter drawn in ink, and it defeats the design. Accepted and disclosed instead, because the alternative is not being this design at all.
WHY THE SHORT LAST ROW IS THIS DESIGN'S HARDEST CASE · drawn at 600 crop
FOUR POSTS · THE ORPHAN KEEPS ITS 480 AND TWO THIRDS OF THE VIEWPORT IS EMPTY
A17-0's short-last-row rule says the orphan keeps its cell width and sits at the left, and this design obeys it at the highest cost in the category. A full-bleed row one third full leaves 960 px of empty page beside one image, and unlike a gutter grid there is no whitespace convention making it look intentional. Stretching the orphan to full width is a different design, and it promotes a post the site did not promote. So the counter matters more here than anywhere else: “Full-bleed rows look best when your count divides. Try 3, 6 or 9.” ⚑ It is advice, not enforcement — the Count stepper reaches every number now, so a count that does not divide by three is one keystroke away, and the orphan also appears whenever the query returns fewer posts than Count, which no control can prevent. Under three posts it does not hand off, and that is a deliberate difference from 11 Masonry and 12 Bento. One full-bleed image with two thirds of the viewport empty is poor, but an image touching the screen's edge is still recognisably this design, where four posts in three staggered columns is not recognisably masonry. The threshold that matters here is images, not count.

Head Inset or Edge is a control no other design in A17 needs, because no other design has two grids in it. Inset keeps the head at the page's 72 px margin, aligned with every other section on the page; Edge moves it to the grid's own left, 24 px from the screen, aligned with the first cell's text and with nothing else. Inset is the default because a page is usually more than one section, and a head that agrees with its neighbours is worth more than one that agrees with the row beneath it. ⚑ Mine. Portrait is available here and is refused in 14 Dense, because the constraint is the cell's absolute width rather than the ratio: a 480 px portrait cell is 600 px tall and is a poster, where a 196 px portrait cell is 245 px tall and is a postage stamp. At Per row Four the 360 px cell makes Portrait 450 px tall, which is still a picture. All three ratios are offered at all three counts, and this is the only design in A17 where a ratio control has no restriction on it at all. There is no Excerpt control — the second design after 14 Dense with none, for a different reason: an excerpt in a 480 px cell would fit comfortably, and it would break the band. A band of imagery with three lines of body text under each cell stops being a band and becomes 1 Three Up without gutters, which is a worse 1 Three Up. The design is imagery and captions, and a caption is a title, a tag and a date. Cut: a gutter control — “Gutter: None · Hairline · 24” would make this design contain 1 Three Up, and the picker already offers that as its own entry. Cut: a radius control, which would only ever be used to undo the squared corner this design exists to have.
