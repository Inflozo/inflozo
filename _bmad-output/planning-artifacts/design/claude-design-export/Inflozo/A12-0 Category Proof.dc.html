A12 ABOUT AND TEAM · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

Fifteen arrangements of a person

A12's content is people: a photograph, a name, a role, sometimes a paragraph, sometimes a link to a page of their posts. It is the first category in this library whose list has two sources — a site can author people[] by hand, or point the section at Ghost's authors — and the designs cannot tell which they were handed. That is where most of A12's decisions come from.

It is also the first category where the missing image is a face. A11 answered a missing logo with a second upload; a missing photograph cannot be answered that way, so the initials block is a first-class state here rather than a fallback, and a row mixing photographs with initials is the ordinary case, not the degraded one.

This file carries the four settlements §8 asks for, the person card, the two photo families and their ladders, the rules all fifteen share, the fifteen-field shared list, the roster, the tokenisation proof and the stress frame. The prose companion is “A12 About and Team — Spec.md”. Where the two disagree, the drawn panel is the authority. Every invented decision is marked ⚑.

The photograph is optional and the initials block is its equal, not its apology. It takes the pack's hover surface — #F4F0E8 in Paper light, a derived #2A251E in dark — the family's own shape, and initials in the heading font at 0.34 × the box height in text-muted, tracked .02em. ⚑ The fill, the ratio and the tracking are mine; the component is A1·14's avatar at four times the size. Two letters in a list the site types itself: the first letter of the first word and the first letter of the last word. At Source Ghost authors the block shows one letter — “Jane Doe” gives J — because Ghost cannot produce two initials from a name. One-word names take one letter. Particles are not skipped — “van der Meer” gives VM — because a theme that decides which parts of a name do not count will eventually decide wrong. ⚑ Mine. Refused: a silhouette icon, a generated illustration, a hashed hue per person, an empty box, and the site's own logo in the hole. Twelve hashed hues is twelve accents, and a silhouette in a masthead reads as a person who was removed. A row that mixes photographs and initials is the ordinary case and nothing marks the difference — same box, same size, same position in the card. This is the one place A12 does not follow A11: a logo missing its dark file gets a plate that asks for the file; a person missing a photograph gets a block that does not ask. ⚑ Mine.

A person's photograph is either a mark or an image, and the two are different compositions. A circle is a fixed size that does not change when the cell does; a crop fills the cell's width and its height follows the ratio. No design in A12 offers a control that switches between them — that control would move every other element on the frame, which is §3.1's definition of a wrong design. ⚑ Mine, and the reason there are fifteen designs rather than eight with more controls. The crop is named and never computed — A8·8's rule, carried verbatim, and the reason is unchanged: a theme that decides where to cut a photograph of a person will eventually cut through a chin. object-fit: cover, centred, at the pack radius; a circle is border-radius: 50% and does not take the pack radius, which is the one place a pack's radius token has no effect in this category. Refused in both families: a ring, a border, a shadow, a duotone, a greyscale filter and a hover zoom. A11 filtered logos because a logo is artwork; a photograph of a person is content and is drawn as uploaded. The one treatment in A12 is 6 Portraits' scrim under overlaid type. Reconciled: the refused focal-point control is now a focal-point field — Image focus (Centre · Top · Bottom) in the Image Picker’s popover on every photo, A14’s ruling that where a photograph is cropped is content. The refusal keeps its aim: the ratio is the section’s, there is no per-person crop, and no control moves a crop. Upload advice, not a limit: 2 × the drawn box on its longest edge — 192 px for a Large circle, 832 px for a 4:5 crop in a 416 cell. The editor warns below it and draws it anyway. ⚑ Mine.
THE RULES ALL FIFTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE
Two sources, one contract. Source: Authored · Ghost authors is the Data group’s first row on all fifteen panels, and at Ghost authors it reveals three more — Authors: All · Hand-picked · Limit ≤ 24 · Order: Name A–Z · Most posts. Only authors with at least one published post appear: the Content API returns no others, so an invited colleague who has not published cannot be drawn at any Limit. From Ghost: name, bio, profile_image and the author page URL. Not used: cover_image, location, website, twitter, facebook, post counts and “latest post”. Ghost's bio is not length-limited, so an over-length Ghost bio is clamped by line rather than by character — three lines in a card, four in a row, with no ellipsis and no “more” link. ⚑ Mine. Reconciled: twitter and facebook leave the not-used list — the socials row reads them, version-gated — and post counts leave it too, being what Order: Most posts sorts on. cover_image, location and website are still not used. Order. Authored: the repeater's order, and nothing re-sorts it — in a masthead the order is often the hierarchy. From Ghost: Order: Name A–Z · Most posts, set here in the Data group. Deleted this pass: “the API’s default author order, which the site changes in Ghost rather than here” — Ghost Admin has no author reorder, so the claim was never true and the row above is the fix. Three things to notice, and the first is the category's whole tokenisation problem. The photographs do not change in any of the six frames — a person's photograph is content, drawn as uploaded, so A12 inherits A11's inversion: the pack re-colours the ground, the card, the hairline and the type, and the pictures sit there. The one thing that does re-skin is the initials block, which takes each pack's hover surface and each pack's heading font, and is therefore the only place in this category where a missing photograph looks like it belongs to the pack.
The radius is the visible difference between the frames: Tangerine's 14 and Ink's 6 land on the card and nowhere else, because a circle is 50% in every pack and eleven of the fifteen designs draw circles on the section's own ground. In eleven of the fifteen, six frames of this proof would be six frames of type on six grounds.
The name is in the body font in all six, which is the person card's rule doing its work: the head changes face between Georgia and Bricolage and the twelve names do not, so a re-skin never turns a masthead into twelve headlines. ⚑ Tangerine's and Ink's surface, border and hover-surface values here are mine — those two packs are specified in this library by ground, text, muted and radius, and the rest is inferred: #F2DFCC / #FBE7D4 / #2A1C16 / #34231B and #E3E3E8 / #EAEAEF / #191920 / #22222B.
