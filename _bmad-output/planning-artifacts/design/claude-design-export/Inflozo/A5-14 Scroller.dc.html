RAIL CONTROLS · the three named values
ARROWS · the default · A1·14's icon button, outlined, in the head's right · 40% and unavailable at an end
A RULE · a 3 px track under the rail, 240 px wide, the filled part being what is shown · no drag, no click
NONE · the cut card is the only affordance · the value for a phone-first site, and the default at ≤ 767

No dots. A row of dots under a rail counts pages that do not exist here — the rail is continuous, not paginated — and a dot small enough to be tasteful is under any reasonable target size. A rule is not a scrollbar: it is a 3 px indicator that cannot be dragged or clicked, because a custom scrollbar is worse than the browser's at every size. Where a reader's platform draws its own scrollbar, the rail keeps it and the rule sits above it. Flagged as mine: the three values, refusing dots, and the rule's 240 px width.

WHERE THE LIBRARY WILL NOT USE THIS
A rail hides content, and the reader has to act to see it. That is acceptable for a set where the first two items carry the argument and the rest are more of the same. It is not acceptable for a set a reader must see in full, and the editor says which is which in those words. So: not for pricing, not for anything a reader compares, and not for a set of three. Three cards fit on one screen at 1440 and a rail with nothing off-screen is a grid with a scrollbar — below five items the design is not offered and the editor names 4 Cards. Above nine it advises 4 Cards too: a rail nine cards long is an archive, and A17 Post Grids is where a long set of anything belongs. One rail per page. Two sections that both scroll sideways make a page whose horizontal gestures mean different things in different places. The editor flags the pairing rather than preventing it. Flagged as mine: the five-item floor, the nine-item advice, the one-rail rule, and naming A17.

The card's width decides the item's type sizes by the same measure rule 5 Split Head uses: 280 and 320 are Four Up's column, so the title is 19 and the body 15; 380 is close to Three Up's, so they step to 21 and 16. The picture is 16:9 at every width, so a Wide card is 380 × 214. The gap stays 24 px at all three — it is the rail's rhythm rather than the card's margin.

4 Cards' hover and 8 Media Top's picture darken, unchanged: ground steps, title underlines, picture 4% darker, nothing scales. Focus is where a rail differs from a grid: tabbing to a card that is off-screen scrolls the rail until the whole card is in view, at the same 160 ms, and the focus ring is never clipped by the rail's edge — the ring sits 2 px outside the card, so the scroll allows for it. A card half-cut by the window is not a valid focus position, and a keyboard reader never has to guess what they have landed on.

Field notes
1 · A5-0's structure: a <ul> of <li> with h3 titles. Not a carousel, not a tablist, not a slideshow — it is a list in a box with overflow-x: auto, and every item is in the accessibility tree at all times whether or not it is on screen. 2 · The arrows are two <button>s labelled “Previous features” and “Next features” in words, not glyphs, with the glyph aria-hidden. At an end the button is disabled rather than removed, so the pair does not move. They are a convenience, never the only way: the rail scrolls by wheel, trackpad, touch, scrollbar and keyboard without them, which is why Rail controls None is a legitimate value. 3 · The rail itself is a focusable scroll container — tabindex="0" with an aria-label naming the section — so a keyboard reader can scroll it with the arrow keys without stepping through every card. Firefox and Chrome both need that tabindex; it is the one place in A5 a non-interactive element takes a tab stop, and the label is what makes it survivable. 4 · Tabbing to an off-screen card scrolls it fully into view, ring included. The ring is 2 px outside the card and the scroll allows for it, so a focused card is never half-cut by the window. Under reduced motion the scroll is instant. Nothing moves on its own. No autoplay, no timer, no infinite loop back to the start, no swipe hint animation. The library's one rail is a scroll container and nothing more.
