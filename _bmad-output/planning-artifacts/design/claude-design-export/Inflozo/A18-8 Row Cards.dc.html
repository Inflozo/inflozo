Row Cards

Every post on its own full-width plane: surface fill, hairline, the pack's radius, 16 px between cards and no rule anywhere — the plane is the separator. The thumbnail sits at the trailing edge, inside the card, which is the one place in A18 an image can go at the right without ending up on the far side of the page from its title.

It inherits A17·4 Cards' two exceptions verbatim: a plane of its own, and the library's only hover lift. Everything else about the card — the DOM order, the title face, the untruncated title, the whole-card link — is the post card unchanged.

The card is 1,296 wide with 24 px of padding, so its content box is 1,248 — text takes what the 160 px thumbnail and the 32 px gap leave, and the excerpt is held to 760 so a one-line sentence does not run the card's whole width. Vertical centring, not top alignment: a 107 px picture beside a three-line text block looks wrong pinned to the top and right centred, which is the opposite of 2 Thumb Rows and is a consequence of the card's padding making the row an object rather than a band.
16 px between cards and no rule. A17·4 established that a plane is a separator; a rule as well would be a card in a table. This is the only design in A18 with no Rule control, and the gap is not adjustable either — Row density changes the card's internal padding, which is what a reader actually feels.
The meta sits under the excerpt rather than hanging at the right, because the right of this row is where the picture is. A card cannot hang a meta column and carry a trailing thumbnail at the same time, and the picture is the more useful of the two.

Card has two values and they are the same two A8·2 drew for its quote cards. Surface is a raised plane with the sm shadow; Ground is the page's own colour with a hairline and nothing else. Ground is the setting for a page that already has a surface-coloured section above or below it, where a white card on a white band is a card that has disappeared. The hover lift is sm → md in 160 ms and it exists only at Card Surface. At Ground there is no shadow to grow, and adding one on hover would make the card materialise under the pointer. The title's accent underline is the hover state at both values, so nothing is lost — the lift is an addition, not the mechanism. ⚑ In dark the shadow is dropped and the lift with it. A17 found that a near-black shadow on a dark ground is invisible and a pale glow is a different design language; this design inherits that and it costs more here than it did in A17·4, because the lift is one of only two things distinguishing a card from a ruled row. What remains in dark is the surface step, which the pack does provide.

A card has an edge and a bare row does not, and that is the entire argument for the trailing thumbnail living here. Inside a 1,296 px card the picture is bounded by the card's right padding; in a bare row it floats at the page's edge with the title 900 px away. 2 Thumb Rows puts its picture at the leading edge for exactly this reason — the two designs answer §8's thumbnail question at opposite ends, and only one of them needed a plane to do it. Picture has three values: Off · Small 112 · Medium 160, all Landscape 3:2, all measured against the card's 24 px padding rather than the page. There is no Large: a 200 px picture in a 24 px-padded card is 17% of the card's width and starts to look like a two-column card, which is A17·13 rotated. Picture Off is a real setting and the card survives it — the plane is what makes a row a row here, not the picture. That is why this design does not hand off when no post has an image, unlike 2 Thumb Rows: it simply draws at Picture Off and the editor says so.

WHAT A PLANE COSTS, AND THE ONE CONTROL IT REMOVES Rule is gone and Card takes its place. That is the cleanest control substitution in A18: both controls answer the same question — what separates one post from the next — and a design answers it once. A site switching from 1 Rows keeps its Rule value untouched on the section and simply does not see it here. Row density means card padding here, not row padding. 16 · 24 · 32 inside the card, with the 16 px gap between cards fixed at every value. A gap that grew with the padding would double the effect and turn Spacious into a list of distant objects; holding it fixed keeps the cards a single group at all three settings. ⚑ The gap is 16 and the cards' corners are the pack's radius, and those two numbers are related. At radius 14 — Ember's token — a 16 px gap between two heavily rounded cards reads as a wider gap than the same 16 between Studio's radius-6 cards. The gap is specified as a constant rather than as a multiple of the radius, which is the simpler rule and the one that keeps every pack's list the same height. Mine, and worth the architect's attention because the alternative is defensible. Cut: a card-width control. A narrower card centred on the page is a panel, and A18·12 Panel is a different design with a different tuple. Cut: a per-card accent border — A17 refused accent as decoration and this library has not spent it on a container yet.
