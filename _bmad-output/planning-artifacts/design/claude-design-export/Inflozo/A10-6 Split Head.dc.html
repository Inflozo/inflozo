Split Head

The head in a 416 px column at the left, two or four stats in an 824 px block at the right, on A8·6's 56 px gutter. The design for a section whose sub is doing real work — a 178-character sentence beside the numbers instead of above them, which is where a reader looks for it once the numbers are the first thing on the line.

Inherited unchanged from A8 and A9: the 416 / 56 / 824 division, both columns top-aligned, the head never sticky and never vertically centred, and the split leaving at 1080. What A10 adds is that the stats block is a 2 × 2 rather than a list, which makes this the only design where four stats appear in a column narrower than the content width.

Foot Under the stats takes a hairline above it, spans the 824 block and sits 24 px below the last row — the same hairline the dividers use, which makes the foot read as the last thing in the stat block rather than a caption to it. A 104-character title at Medium 34 in a 416 column is five lines, which is taller than the head's sub and foot together and is still drawn. The stat block does not move down to meet it and the head does not shrink: both columns start at the same line and end where their content ends. The vertical divider runs the full height of the block, so it passes a labelless cell as well as a three-line one. This is the one place in A10 where a rule is longer than the content beside it, and it is correct: the rule divides the block's two columns, not the cells. Two hand-offs out. No title → 1 Row, because a split head with no head is a row with an empty third of a page beside it — A9·5's hand-off inverted. One stat → 4 Single. Three or five authored at Count Four draws what exists in the 2 × 2, the last cell absent. Six → the panel names 3 Grid.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWO COLUMNS, ONE LANDMARK The two outer columns are a flex row, not two landmarks — no <aside>, no roles, no second heading. A8·6's rule verbatim. The stats are one <ul> of two or four <li> in a CSS grid, never two lists of two, because the 2 × 2 is a wrap and not a grouping. The head is read first at both Head column values, and the Foot control changes reading order as well as position: at Under the head the source and link are read before the numbers, at Under the stats after them. Both are legitimate and the sidebar says which is which. A9·5's disclosure, cited. The vertical divider is a border-left on the right-hand cells, so it is decorative and inherits the cell's box; it is not an <hr> and never a separator role. In forced colours it survives as a system border, which means the two columns stay divided when the fills are gone. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, label 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, hairline 1.3:1 / 2.3:1, underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. One tab stop at most — the link. At Count up Off no behaviour; no sticky, no hover. Print: the split prints as drawn at 1440 and stacks at narrower paper.

STATS · the item list · outside the design’s listStats is the P0·3 item list: Add stat arrives with content — value 100, label “What this number counts” — Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Per item: value ≤ 12 · prefix ≤ 3 · suffix ≤ 6 · label ≤ 60. note, prev and share held. Value source: Authored · Member count · Paid member count. At Member count the value renders Ghost’s {​{total_members}} string verbatim — “1,200+” in, “1,200+” out, no maths and no formatter, which is the category’s own no-formatter rule — prefix and suffix are disabled and the label stays authored. {​{total_paid_members}} is offered only when the site has paid members enabled. Eight is the ceiling of stats[], raised from six in this pass: “Eight numbers is as many as a reader holds in one look. A ninth is a table, and a table is a different section.” 3 Grid is the only design that draws eight; everywhere else seven or eight authored names it. Seven or eight authored → 3 Grid; three authored at Count Four still draws three cells with the fourth absent, and no title still sends the design to 1 Row.
EDITING · the P0 primitivesValue, prefix, suffix and label edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The value raises no marks toolbar — it is plain text, so bold, italic and a link inside a figure are withheld rather than offered and refused. Eyebrow, title, sub, source and the link label take the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. A value bound to a member count is not inline-editable: clicking it says “Edit in Ghost”, because the figure is the site’s member list and not a word anyone typed here. The head column’s eyebrow, title and sub edit where they sit in the column, not in a sidebar field. sourceUrl and linkUrl open the Link Picker; the section link takes an optional P0·2 icon.
BEHAVIOUR · from the fixed registrycount-up, from the fixed registry, and only at Count up On. Its own degradation is the argument: “The final value renders as static text — it is already in the HTML before JS ever runs.” JS off, reduced motion and assistive technology all get the figure rather than the animation, and the module does not re-run on the way back up the page. Edit-safe: it does not run while the section is edited, so every frame here is still a resting state. At Count up Off — the default — this design declares no module at all: Head column, Foot and Dividers are values on the section, the head moving above the stats at 1080 is a media query, and the head is never sticky, so nothing tracks the scroll.
DATA · Ghost’s two member helpers · outside the design’s listTwo figures in A10 can come from Ghost, and both are public theme helpers: {​{total_members}} and {​{total_paid_members}}, each rendering a pre-rounded string. They are reached from the item’s Value source and not from a “Populate from…” panel — there is no list to populate. Everything else is authored in this section. Refused on record: a post count from a tag (a number nobody wrote and nobody checks), any admin-API figure — opens, revenue, growth — a live figure from a third party, and the four ornaments: sparkline, dial, donut, pie. 9 Bars’ single proportional bar is as far as A10 goes.
