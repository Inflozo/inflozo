Index

A jump list of group labels at the left margin, every group of rows at the right. The design for a FAQ that has outgrown a list — up to twenty-four questions in up to six groups, all of them drawn, none of them behind a tab or a filter — and the one place in A9 where a reader can see the shape of the whole thing before reading any of it.

It carries two decisions the rest of the category does not. It is the only design in A9 that draws a copy-link affordance — settlement 2's anchors made visible, as a mono # after each question — and it is the only sticky element in the library's sections so far, which is flagged twice: once here and once at the control that turns it off.

240 · 56 · 780 = 1,076, and the remaining 220 px of the content width is left empty. The index sits at the left margin and the rows keep 1 Accordion's 780 measure: a FAQ's rows do not get wider because the page has an index beside them. The head and the foot sit on the rows' column, not the section's, so the whole right-hand block reads as one thing.
The index item is A1·1's nav item, cited verbatim — 15 px, muted at 500 resting, text at 600 with a 2 px accent underline for the group a reader is currently in. That is a fourth place A9 spends the accent and amends A9-0's “three places”, logged rather than applied quietly. The current-group mark follows the scroll; it is not a timer, nothing animates, and under reduced motion it is unchanged.
The copy-link # is drawn at 14 px in the mono face — a typed character rather than an icon, so it needs no glyph set and survives every pack. Muted on the row a pointer is over or a keyboard has focused, and border-coloured at rest, which keeps ten of them from reading as a column of marks. ⚑ Mine: the 220 px of unused width, the # and its two colours, and the index heading “On this page”.

THE STICKY INDEX, AND THE STATIC ONE · schematic at 620
STICKY · THE INDEX HOLDS AT 24 PX FROM THE TOP WHILE THE ROWS PASS IT
STATIC · THE INDEX SCROLLS AWAY WITH THE FIRST GROUP
Sticky is the default and it is A9's only sticky element. A8·6 established that a split head never sticks, and that rule stands for a head — a title that follows a reader down a page is a title asking to be read twice. An index is not a head: its whole use is arriving somewhere else, and at twenty-four rows the section is over 2,000 px tall, so an index that leaves after the first group is an index a reader can use once. ⚑ Mine, and the exception is stated at the control. Four conditions, all stated: it sticks 24 px from the top of the viewport; only above 1080, where there is a left column to stick in; only while the rows are taller than the viewport, so a six-row FAQ never has a floating list beside it; and it stops at the foot of the rows rather than travelling into the next section. Static is the second value, not a fallback. A site whose header is already sticky has a reason to refuse a second fixed thing, and the panel says so in one line. Position is not motion. Nothing about the sticky index animates, and reduced motion changes none of it — the only thing a reader with that preference notices is that the jump is instant, which it is for everybody.

The # is a <button> beside the question’s summary, never inside it — interactive content inside a <summary> is not reliably operable, and nesting it would make the whole row ambiguous to press. It is 44 px tall, 24 px wide, always in the DOM and always in the tab order, so a keyboard reader reaches it whether or not a pointer ever hovered the row. Its accessible name is the catalog string “Copy link to this question”. Pressed replaces the character with the catalog’s “Link copied” for two seconds, in mono at 10 px, and the change is announced once through a polite live region. No toast, no tooltip, no icon that turns into a tick. ⚑ Mine, including the two seconds. Copy links Hidden removes the button entirely rather than hiding it from pointers: a control that only some readers can reach is worse than one nobody has. The anchors still exist and links into the section still work, which is the whole point of settlement 2's stored anchor. An arrival from an anchor opens that row, leaves every other row closed, and marks its group in the index — the index's current-group underline moves to the group the row is in, which is the one thing an arrival changes that the other fourteen designs have no equivalent for.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWO NEW THINGS, AND WHAT THEY COST The index is a <nav> labelled with the catalog’s “On this page”, holding a <ul> of in-page links. It is the only <nav> inside a section in A9, and it earns the landmark: a reader can reach it directly instead of scrolling past ten questions to find it. The current group takes aria-current="location", not aria-current="page": the page has not changed. Group label <h3>, question <h4>, one <ul> per group — 6 Grouped's structure and the same amendment to A9-0. An index link points at the group's <h3>, which carries tabindex="-1" so the jump moves focus as well as the scroll. The copy-link button is a sibling of the question’s summary, never nested inside it, 44 px tall, always in the tab order, named with the catalog’s “Copy link to this question”, with a polite live region announcing “Link copied” once. Its cost is one extra tab stop per row — twenty extra stops at twenty-four questions — which is why it has a control and why the control's help line says what Hidden is for. Sticky and forced colours: the index keeps its hairline and its aria-current, and the accent underline becomes a system-coloured one. In forced colours the index also stops sticking, because a floating column with a transparent background over moving text is unreadable when the system picks both colours. ⚑ Mine. Contrast: index resting 5.6:1 light / 6.8:1 dark, current 15.8:1 / 15.6:1, its underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1; group label 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; question and answer the same; the resting # is the border token at 1.19:1 and is not information — the row is pressable without it and its name is read from the button, not the character. Print: index dropped, every row open, group labels kept.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Questions and answers edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The answer raises the marks toolbar on selection with bold · italic · link · inline code and no underline — an underline inside prose is a link that is not one. Recorded as a PRD delta. Group labels edit inline too — they live in a second repeater, so renaming one on canvas renames the group for every question in it, and the confirm says so. Nothing in A9 is Ghost-owned, so nothing here says “Edit in Ghost” — every word on the page is the site’s own. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Questions are the P0·3 item list: Add question arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Three visitor-facing strings are theme catalog strings, not fields: “Copy link to this question”, “Link copied” and the index’s “On this page”. A non-English site cannot ship them as English literals, and they are the theme’s to translate rather than the section’s to type.
