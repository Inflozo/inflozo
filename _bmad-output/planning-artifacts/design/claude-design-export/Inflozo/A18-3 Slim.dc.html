Slim

One post, one line: the title at the left, one trailing item at the right, a hairline between. 44 px a row at Compact, which is A18's answer to §8's first question — the smallest row that still reads — and it is a number borrowed rather than invented: A1 set 44 px as the touch-target minimum, and a row that is a link should not be smaller than a button.

One line has a consequence the rest of the library never had to face. ⚑ This is the only design in 485 that truncates a title — A17 wrote “wraps, never clamped, never truncated” into the post card, and a single-line row cannot honour it. The compromise is drawn and specified below: the clip is visual only and the whole title stays in the DOM, so a screen reader and a search engine still get all of it.

Ten posts in 440 px of page, and the whole spring above the fold. 1 Rows draws six posts in 768 px at the same section padding; this draws ten in 440. That ratio is the design's only argument, and it is why the panel's description reads “Every post as one line. Good for long archives.”
44 px is 12 above, 12 below and a 19 px title on a 1.3 line-height — 24.7 rounded to 20 of visible type plus its leading. The eight-point grid is honoured at the padding, not at the total, which is stated because A18-0 claims an 8 px internal grid and 44 is not a multiple of 8. ⚑ Mine: the touch minimum wins where the two rules disagree, and 48 would have been the grid-honouring alternative at a 9% cost in density.
The date column is not a column here — it is a trailing item that shrinks to its own content. 1 Rows fixes 200 px because it holds two lines; one line of “26 February 2026” is 108 px at 13 px, so a fixed column would leave a 92 px hole beside the shorter dates. The titles are ragged at the right and the dates are flush, which is the opposite of 1 Rows and correct for one line.

⚑ The departure, stated plainly: A17's post card says a title wraps and is never clamped or truncated, and this design clips it. There is no arrangement of a single-line row that keeps that promise — a 90-character title in a 350 px row is four lines, and four lines is not this design. The alternative was to refuse the design, and a library of 485 sections that cannot draw a compact archive list is missing something a great many sites want. What limits the damage is where the clip lives. It is white-space: nowrap plus text-overflow: ellipsis on the visible box: the full string is in the markup, is the link's accessible name, is what a screen reader announces and is what a crawler indexes. Nothing is lost except pixels, and only at widths where nothing would have fitted anyway. At 1,296 px the clip almost never fires. A 19 px Georgia title averages 9.2 px a character, so the row holds about 128 characters before the date; the longest title in the library's content set is 69. The clip is a phone-width behaviour that the desktop frame inherits for consistency rather than for need. A finding for the architect either way — the card's promise now has one exception and the exception should be written into the card, not left in this design's spec.

Trailing is one item, never two. Date · Tag · Reading time · None. The row has one line and one line has room for one fact; “Reporting · 12 March 2026 · 9 min read” beside a title is 4 Dated's job with the parts in the wrong order. A missing value draws nothing — no dash, no “Untagged”, no reserved gap. Columns Two is the only place in A18 where a list runs in two columns, and it reads down one column and then down the other, never across. That is a real risk and it is why the setting is not the default: two columns of dated rows look like a table, and a reader who scans across gets the wrong order. The rules are per column, so nothing lines up across the gutter to invite it. Twenty posts at Columns Two and Compact is 440 px of page — the same height the primary frame spends on ten. It is the densest arrangement in the library and the point at which the panel's advice stops: past 30 rows the answer is A34's pagination or a search page, not a longer list.

EDITING · THE P0 PRIMITIVESEvery visible authored text edits inline on canvas with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the link popover carrying Open in new tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored: the eyebrow, the title, the sub, the note and the link label. Post content is Ghost’s and is never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking a post title, tag, author name or excerpt says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL field opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker, and the head link’s target defaults to Matches the query so it cannot go stale when the query does. This design draws no image at any setting, so rule 10’s Image focus has nothing to attach to ⚑. No button, so rule 11’s optional icon has nothing to attach to ⚑ — the head link is text with an accent underline, and an icon on it would make it look like a button. No fixed English visitor-facing string ships: the head strings are authored with no defaults and this design draws no furniture text of its own; the main feed’s empty pair is authored, with defaults. No authored list exists in A18, so the P0·3 item controls have nothing to govern: the rows are Ghost’s and there is no Add ⚑. There is no Preview control and there never was one to remove.

WHAT A ONE-LINE ROW REMOVES FROM THE PANEL, AND WHAT IT HAS TO ADD Excerpt and Meta both go, and one control replaces them. An excerpt needs a second line; a meta row of three parts needs one too. Trailing is what survives of both — a single fact at the right edge, chosen from four. That is the trade the design makes, and it is why switching to 1 Rows restores an excerpt the site never lost: the value is stored on the section and ignored here, never cleared. Title size has two values and A18's ladder has three. Large 22 is the category default and this design refuses it: a 22 px title in a 44 px row leaves 11 px above and below and stops reading as a list. Small 17 exists for Columns Two, where the measure halves. ⚑ Mine. ⚑ Row density's numbers are this design's own: 44 · 56 · 72, against the category's 16 · 24 · 36 padding steps. The names are shared and the values are not, because here the control sets a row height and everywhere else it sets a padding. Same control name, different quantity — a second instance of the pattern A17 flagged around ratio, cycle and thumbSize, and worth the architect's attention for the same reason. Cut: a hover fill on the row. A full-width tinted band on hover is what a table does, and at 44 px it flickers as the pointer crosses the list. The accent underline on the title is the hover state here as everywhere. Cut: an index number — that is 11 Numbered, and adding it here would make the two designs one.

Ten rules 44 px apart is the hardest test the pack's hairline gets anywhere in the library, and it passes on the same measured step every other design uses: #332E27, 9.8 lightness points above the dark ground. A heavier rule would turn this list into a table and a lighter one would lose the rhythm — the two failure modes are 3 px apart in lightness, which is worth recording as the reason the step was measured rather than chosen.
Three tokens change: ground, text, muted. No image, no plate, no shadow — this and 1 Rows are the cheapest dark modes in A18.
