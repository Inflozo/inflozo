1 · Skip link first, ahead of the bar. 2 · The sentence names the deadline in words — “ends Sunday 23 August” — so the bar is complete without the clock. That is the accessibility requirement that shapes the design: a reader who never sees the digits still knows when the offer ends. 3 · The clock is a <time datetime="2026-08-23T23:59+01:00"> carrying a visually-hidden “2 days, 14 hours and 9 minutes left”, with the boxes marked aria-hidden. aria-live="off": a clock that announced itself every minute — or every second in the last hour — would make the page unusable with a screen reader. The hidden text updates silently and is read on demand. 4 · The action is a link styled as a button, label standing alone. Focus order: action, then close. The clock is not focusable and has no hover state. 5 · Close is a labelled button, 32 px in a 44 px hit area, top-right when the bar has two rows. 6 · Contrast: sentence 13.9:1; digits 13.9:1; the 9 px unit labels at 66% carried measure 7.2:1, and they are duplicates of words already in the sentence — that duplication is what allows 9 px at all.
