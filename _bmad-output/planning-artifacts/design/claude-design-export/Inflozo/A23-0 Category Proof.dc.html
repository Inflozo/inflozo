A23 Search — the category

Fifteen designs for the surface a reader lands on when they want one thing out of an archive. Thirteen of them draw results; two hand the query to the search route and stop. One of them, 2 Overlay, is the panel every search affordance in A1 Headers opens — drawn once here so no header has to invent its own.

This frame carries what belongs to the category rather than to any one design: the four questions §8 asked, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame, the roster with its fifteen structural descriptors, the shared field list, the component inventory and the findings for the architect. The per-design frames are A23-1 … A23-15, and the written specification is A23 Search - Spec.md.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS §8 ASKED, ANSWERED
1 · THE SURFACE A1·9 OPENS, AND ITS KEYBOARD MODEL
2 Overlay is that surface, and every search affordance in A1 opens it ⚑ — A1·9’s persistent field, A1·2’s drawer field, A1·14’s ⌕ button. One panel, drawn once. A header does not get its own results treatment. One row, two densities ⚑. The suggestion row is A1·9’s: title, then “section · author · date”, and its accessible name is those four things in that order. The result row is that row with an excerpt, and 11 Thumb Rows adds the picture. Nothing else differs. Combobox keys in two designs, not fifteen ⚑. 2 Overlay and 3 Command Palette are comboboxes: aria-activedescendant, arrows that never move focus, Escape in A1·9’s two steps. The other thirteen are documents — their results are a
    and the arrow keys are the browser’s. Drawing a page of results as a listbox is the commonest search-page accessibility defect.
2 · EMPTY QUERY, NO RESULTS, MANY RESULTS, AND THE COUNT
Empty query is not an empty state — it is the recent-search state: the reader’s own last three queries, then the site’s featured posts, then the field alone. The reader’s queries never leave their browser ⚑. “Most read” was struck in the reconciliation pass ⚑ — Ghost gives a theme no view counts. 5 Tag Chips ignores all of it: its chips are the pre-query state ⚑. No matches is a content state. No red, no icon: the query, one line of why, the section chips, an archive link. And the line names the index — title, excerpt and slug, not body text ⚑ — because the worst state in this category is a query that plainly exists in the archive and returns nothing. The count line is the announcement ⚑. Visible text and a visually-hidden aria-live="polite" region where results replace in place; no live region where the query was a page navigation — A34’s rule. The count always names the true total, never the capped one, and the cap is the source group’s.
3 · FILTERS — THE DESIGN OR A CONTROL?
Three designs are the filter; twelve have none ⚑. 5 Tag Chips draws the publication’s own tags as an authored row, 13 Facet Rail draws Ghost’s tags, authors and years in a column, 15 Grouped splits the answer by object type. Everywhere else a filter would be a second query the reader did not ask for. One facet at a time, and that is a platform limit rather than a simplification ⚑. A Ghost theme is given one taxonomy route at a time, so “Reporting, by Ida Brandt, in 2026” has no URL. 13 Facet Rail is single-select and says so in the rail; the finding is the architect’s, not a new module. Filters are links, so they work with everything off — filter-strip, declared by 5 and 13, and the module in the registry that needs JavaScript least. Two counts, two meanings: 13’s facet counts are the archive’s, 15’s group counts are the query’s, and both panels say which ⚑.
4 · WHAT IS INDEXED, AND WHAT THE SPEC PROMISES
The promise is title, excerpt and slug — or the body too, if the author asks ⚑. Search in, added in the reconciliation pass, offers Titles and excerpts (default) and Full content: A23’s index is client-side over the Content API, which can fetch plaintext, so the body is reachable here even though Ghost’s own search bundle cannot reach it ⚑. The panel states the index-size cost, and the read-only “What is indexed” row reports the value rather than asserting a floor. Tags and authors are matched on their names, not their descriptions, and only where the source group’s scope asks for them. Pages are searched by default — a publication’s About page is a legitimate answer to a query and excluding it surprises people. Flagged, and the first finding: what the index carries is a build decision — title, excerpt and slug at the default, plus body text at Full content ⚑ — and every empty state’s wording follows the value, which is why the wording is a field and not a hard-coded string.

AND THE ONE THAT BREAKS EVERY OTHER CATEGORY
A query with results the reader cannot see. A word that appears only in an article’s body returns nothing, and the archive plainly contains it ⚑ — the single worst state in A23, because the section looks broken while working exactly as specified. Every empty state names the index for this reason, and it is the category’s first finding. A one-word title where the mark is the whole title. The 16 % tint over 21 px of heading font reads as a highlight, not a fill — checked, and the reason the mark is a tint rather than a background swap ⚑.
WHAT HOLDS AND WHAT IS ALLOWED TO GIVE
Holds: the 700 text measure, the 160 thumb, the 24 row padding, the meta on one line at 13 px, the field at 52. Gives: the title wraps to three lines, the excerpt is absent where the post has none, the thumb is absent where the post has none, and the meta wraps to two lines past 64 characters ⚑ rather than truncating a writer’s name. Refused: a placeholder image, an “Untitled” fallback, a truncated title, and a minimum row height ⚑ — three rows of different heights is a real archive, and forcing them level is how a list starts lying about its contents.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · ELEVEN AFTER THE RECONCILIATION PASS · TWO NEW, TWO REWRITTEN
What the index carries is a build decision, and the reconciliation pass made it a control. Search in: Titles and excerpts (default) · Full content ⚑ — the client-side index can fetch plaintext from the Content API, at a stated cost in index size. Every empty state’s wording follows the value, which is why the wording is an authored field. Verify the fetch budget on a large archive before build ⚑.Two facets at once has no Ghost route. Routes give a theme one taxonomy at a time, so 13 Facet Rail is single-select by construction. The closest module is filter-strip, which it declares; multi-facet filtering would need a query the platform does not expose to a theme.There is no year archive route by default. 13’s year facet needs a collection in routes.yaml. At the default Facets value no year rows are drawn and the editor names the reason ⚑.No cross-type relevance score exists. 15 Grouped’s order is an arrangement — Posts first, or Most matches first — and “best match” is deliberately not offered, because a theme cannot compute it.And no view counts reach a theme at all ⚑ — which is why “Most read” was struck from the pre-query row in this pass and replaced by featured and latest. If the platform ever exposes counts, the row gains a value and nothing else changes.
A section cannot scope a query to the route it sits on. 9 Slim Bar wanted “search this section” and cannot have it: A23 assumes page sections around it and cannot know what they are — the same route-awareness gap A21, A22 and A26–A29 each raised. Five categories have now reported it ⚑.2 Overlay and 3 Command Palette render nothing in the page flow. No ground, no padding, no place in the page order — and therefore no universal trio. ARCHITECT: editor-surface ruling ⚑ — the editor needs a surface that lists a panel with the header that opens it rather than in the page stack. A product decision, drawn as a note on both frames.Ghost gives a tag’s post count cheaply and an author’s not at all. 3 and 15 draw one and not the other, which looks like an inconsistency on the frame and is a data limit. Named in both panels.There is no server-side search in a Ghost theme. A template cannot read ?q=, so the no-JS floor across all fifteen is a field and an archive link ⚑ — 8 Big Type’s and 15 Grouped’s server-side claims were struck in this pass. Ghost’s numbered /page/2/ links page the archive, not the query.8 Big Type echoes reader input at 96 px — the only place in the library that renders a reader’s string at display size. Escape it, and cap the echo at 60 characters: a build requirement, written into the spec rather than left as a design note ⚑.search-expand is in the registry and A23 declares it nowhere ⚑. A23’s fields are persistent or in a panel, so nothing expands from a trigger. It belongs to A1’s header affordances, and if no category claims it the registry has an unused entry — a finding for the architect.

None of these is a request for a new behaviour module. The registry is closed and A23 uses five of its thirty-one — search-overlay, command-palette, filter-strip, accordion, load-more — with two designs declaring none at all. Where a design wanted behaviour no module covers, it says so and names the closest one ⚑.
