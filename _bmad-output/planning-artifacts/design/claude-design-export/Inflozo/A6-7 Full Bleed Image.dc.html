The ask over a photograph that fills the section. It is the third of §8's three grounds — band, card, picture — and the one where the section's height is set by its own content rather than a crop, since a banner is not a hero and 400 px of photograph is not a 16:9 frame.

The scrim is a flat 56% wash of contrast over the whole picture, and that is a stated departure from A4·4. A hero's scrim is a gradient — 60% at the bottom fading over 70% of the height — because a hero is 16:9 and its text sits in the lower third with clear photograph above it. A banner is as tall as its own content, about 340 px at Comfortable, and a gradient across 340 px puts the eyebrow in unscrimmed picture while the note sits in 60% black. A flat wash treats every line the same. It is still not a heavy black gradient: 56% of a warm near-black, which lets the photograph's shape through and its detail go.
The accent is spent once, on the button, and the button is the only opaque object over the picture — which is the argument for a fill here rather than a link, since its label's contrast is measured against its own fill and not against an unknown photograph. The secondary goes to the full carried colour with a 50% underline, not the 72% muted value the other designs use: muted text over a photograph is the one place in A6 where a reader would have to work.

Band edges is 5 Contrast Band's control with the band replaced by a picture, and at Page margin it drops the 56 px inner padding: a photograph does not need to be held away from its own edge the way text on a colour does, so the padding is the section's alone. Text position is A4·4's control minus its third value. A4·4 offers “Over the picture: everything / headline and actions / headline only”, moving what it drops onto the page's ground below the picture. That value cannot exist here — the picture is the section's height rather than a crop above it, so there is no page ground underneath to move anything onto. The editor names 8 Image Split, where the text has its own half.

The focus ring is the carried colour rather than the accent — 5 Contrast Band's exception, extended here for the same reason and one more: an accent ring around an accent button on a photograph is one colour surrounded by a slightly different version of itself. With no picture the design is 5 Contrast Band: the scrim's flat wash becomes the contrast token at full strength, the accent action is substituted for the carried-colour fill at 5's 3.2:1 refusal, and the editor names the design the section has become. That is A4·4's empty state — a flat contrast panel with the scrims removed — carried without change.

1 · The picture is a CSS background, not an <img>, because it carries no information a reader needs — the words are all in the DOM. imageAlt is therefore kept in the editor and unused on this design, and the editor says so rather than quietly discarding what a site typed. Where a site's picture does carry information, 8 Image Split draws a real <img> with a usable alt. 2 · A scrim cannot guarantee a ratio against an unknown photograph. A4·4 said this plainly and it is said here again: 56% of near-black over a light-heavy picture leaves the sub at about 8.4:1 and over a white-sky picture it can fall below 4.5:1. The specification's answer is the same as A4·4's — the wash, a stated minimum picture width, and an editor note asking for a photograph with low detail through the middle third — and the honest admission that the last 10% is the site's choice of picture. 3 · The accent button is the one element whose contrast is guaranteed: its label is measured against its own opaque fill, 4.6:1, whatever is behind it. That is the argument for a filled action on a picture and against a text link. The secondary is the full carried colour with a 50% underline, so it is not identified by colour alone over a ground that could be any colour. Contrast against the scrim · title 11.2:1 · sub, eyebrow and note 8.4:1 · button label on its fill 4.6:1 · focus ring in the carried colour 11.2:1 against the wash. Forced colours drop the picture and the scrim entirely and the section renders on the user's own background — which is the same result as the no-picture state and needs no extra specification. Reduced motion changes nothing: there is no parallax, no zoom, no fade, and the picture never moves.
