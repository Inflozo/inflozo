Sixteen ways to open one piece of writing

A24 draws the top of a post: a tag, a title, a standfirst, a byline and, when there is one, a photograph. Six facts, and the whole category is the argument about their order and their scale. Like A34 it owns no query — it reads the route's post object and draws what is already true — so there is no Posts block, no Show ladder and nothing to choose. Unlike A34 it carries the page's one <h1>, which is the constraint every design in here is built around.

A17, A18, A19 and A34 are inherited whole — A1's nav item, eyebrow, avatar and meta row, A1·2's 38/44 box, A6's focus ring, A8's named ratio ladder, A17's measure and page margin, A17·7's on-contrast derivation and raised Compact padding, A19's missing-picture vocabulary and its scrim rule, A19·3's card and A19·7's overlap, A34's derived dark hover step. A24 adds one thing and it is the thing the category exists for: type at reading scale. Everything else is arrangement.

TUPLE UNIQUENESS — THE HONEST STATEMENT All sixteen are distinct on the five closed slots — A17 managed nine of eighteen, A18 nine of fifteen, A19 fifteen of fifteen, A34 ten of ten. A24 reaches it with the narrowest vocabulary of the five categories in one slot and the widest in another. Media placement carries the category: eight of the nine values are spent, only inline unused. Ground barely moves — thirteen of sixteen are page, with one image, one surface and one contrast. The count slot needed an interpretation and it is stated once, here, for the whole category. ⚑ The gloss counts repeating units, and a post header draws one post — under the literal reading all sixteen would be one and the slot would carry nothing. So A24 reads it as the number of repeating units the design lays out: one where the header is a single composed block (thirteen designs), few where the design's own unit repeats — 11 Dateline's three or four cells and 14 Share Row's two to four links — and none for 10 Big Type, which draws a title, a byline and nothing that repeats at all. Three of the sixteen therefore rest on the interpretation and it is fair to say so: variable is unused because nothing in a post header follows an authored array. Where two designs come closest it is 2 Flush Left and 15 Slim — the same facts, the same ground, no picture in either, separated by archetype and by scale: a stack at 50 px and a bar at 28. The next closest is 1 Centred against 16 Two Column, both page · one · bottom, separated by archetype — one axis against two columns. If one of the sixteen has to go it is 14 Share Row, which is 1 Centred with a module; the reason to keep it is that a share row placed above the article is a different decision from one placed below it, and A26 owns below.

Four things to notice. Nothing moved. Six frames, three radii, three accents, two heading families and one arrangement: the eyebrow, the title, the standfirst, the meta and the picture sit in the same places at the same intervals in all six, which is the whole test. The accent appears in none of them — a resting post header spends it only on a hover and a focus ring, so the pack's colour arrives through the greys and the ground, and Ink's frame is the proof that a header can be a pack away from Paper's and still be this design.
The title's optical size is the one thing that does not survive a pack change untouched ⚑. Georgia at 40 and a grotesque at 40 are not the same size on the page: the derived Tangerine and Ink frames both need −.03em and a 1.08 line-height where Paper takes −.02em and 1.1. That is a pack property, not a section one, and it is the strongest finding in A24: the pack must supply the title's tracking and leading with its font, or every one of these sixteen designs is drawn against Georgia's metrics and re-skinned into a pack that does not share them.
The picture's radius follows the pack token in all six — 8, 14, 6 — and it is the only place in a post header where the radius token is visible at all, which is why 4, 6 and 9 (whose pictures meet an edge and take no radius) are the three designs a new pack should be checked against last rather than first.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT 1 · A pack must supply its title tracking and leading with its heading font. The tokenisation proof needs −.02em/1.1 for Georgia and −.03em/1.08 for both derived faces. Sixteen designs drawn against one font's metrics is the largest tokenisation risk in the library so far. 2 · Eleven of the twelve packs have no recorded heading font. A24 is the first category where that is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. 3 · Ghost has no series primitive. The eyebrow is a tag. A real series needs an internal tag convention plus a route, and A20 Tag Collections should own the decision. 4 · A post header cannot know its own reading position, so 13 Sticky's meter is the only element in A24 whose value is not in the markup at render. It is the reason reading-progress exists and the reason its no-JS branch hides it. 5 · A1·4 Overlay is a precondition, and the editor cannot enforce it. 5 and 9 both state the pairing in the panel; a section that needs another section set a certain way is a new kind of dependency and the picker should carry it. 6 · The avatar's letter fill has no token. A24 uses border with text initials in every pack; A12 and A21 will both want a real one. 7 · A26 Post Footers should reuse 11 Dateline's cells for its own byline block, and A29 Archive Headers should reuse 1 Centred wholesale — an archive header is this design with a tag name instead of a title. 8 · The updated-date line is owned here now. ⚑ The refusal is withdrawn: Meta's Author, updated date and reading time reads post.updated_at past a day's gap, the same threshold A26·14 Ledger uses. Ghost stamps updated_at on every save, including a typo fix — the threshold is the theme layer's invention and either Ghost needs a real revision flag or this stays a convention. 9 · A page can render this section as nothing. ⚑ @page.show_title_and_feature_image suppresses the title and the picture, which is all A24 draws, so the section disappears. The picker should say so before a user places a header on a page, rather than the panel saying it afterwards. 10 · Share destinations are a site setting and the platform has nowhere to put one. ⚑ ARCHITECT: site setting — one ordered, enabled list of ten destinations plus a Mastodon instance, read by A24·14, A25·9 and A26·9. Three categories were fixing three different lists; a section field cannot fix that and a module is the wrong shape. 11 · A focal point is a section control after all. ⚑ The category refused it — the crop belongs to the image — and the refusal is withdrawn: only the section knows it is cropping a 1,440-wide 21:9 strip. Ghost still owns the image, so Image focus is a crop hint the theme applies, not a value written back to the file. 12 · The editor now shows 13 Sticky’s bar. P0·6's Simulate scroll entry closes what was this category's tenth finding; no Preview control was added, and A24 never had one.

The rule that required itOne control name means one set of values. Two dozen category specs each enumerated their own copy of Image focus, which is that rule’s worst case in the library. A category may place the control, name the slot it applies to and narrow an axis with its reason; it may not restate the values, rename an axis, add a value, offer a percentage or draw a draggable point.
