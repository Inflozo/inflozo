Dense

Twelve to twenty-four marks at Tiny 22, six to ten across. For a site whose honest answer is “a lot of them” — where the point is the number of names rather than any one name, and a wall of twelve at Medium 36 would run to four rows and 400 px.

It is the one design in A11 where the aspect classes stop governing: at Count Ten the cell gives an 84 px max width, and most of the set is decided by its width rather than by the box. The wall still reads level, for a different reason — and the panel states the trade rather than hiding it.

Eight across at 141 on the standard 24 px gutter — 141 × 8 + 24 × 7 = 1,296 — with a 22 px box and rows 32 apart. Twenty logos is 8 + 8 + 4, the last row left-aligned. The gutter never tightens in this design: the marks are already small, and 16 px between a 22 px wordmark and the next one is a paragraph of logos. What tightens is the row gap, 24 or 32, which is the only spacing control here.
Tiny 22 is off the box ladder and this is the only design that uses it. There is no Logo size control at all: a dense wall is dense because the marks are small, and a control that could set them to Medium 36 would turn twenty logos into 3 Grid with a worse gutter. The note is doing real work here — “four more asked not to be listed” is the kind of sentence a wall of twenty needs and a wall of five does not. ⚑ Mine: the 141 cell, Tiny 22, the fixed gutter, and dropping Logo size.

The aspect classes stop governing and object-fit: contain takes over — which is the same rule doing its job, and the reason the wall still reads level is that every cell is identical and every mark is centred in one. Four across at 78 on a 12 px gutter, box 18, row gap 20 or 16 — the only design in A11 that keeps more than two across on a phone, and the only one whose gutter tightens below 24. Twenty marks at two across would be ten rows and 400 px; at four across it is five rows and 170. An 18 px mark is small and the panel says so — “On a phone, marks draw at about 18 px. 3 Grid keeps them at 28.” Both states draw the same twenty marks in the same order.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWENTY-FOUR NAMES IN A ROW The wall is one flat <ul> of up to twenty-four <li>, each an <img> with a required alt — no grouping, no rows in the markup, no <table>. A screen reader announces “list, 24 items” and then twenty-four names. That is long, and it is the honest cost of the design: the section's content is the number of names, and shortening the announcement would mean not announcing some of them. No skip link and no aria-expanded “show all”. A list is the one structure every screen reader already lets a reader skip — the list-level jump is built into the software — so adding a bypass would duplicate a control the reader already has. A linked mark's target is the cell plus 8 px, which is 157 × 38 at eight across and 94 × 34 on a phone — past 44 px in one axis and not the other at the smallest size. That is stated as a limitation rather than solved: the fix would be padding the cell to 44, which at four across on a phone would take the wall back to two across and undo the design. The panel names 5 Cards for a wall of links, whose targets are 135 × 88. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, eyebrow and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, hover fill 1.2:1 / 1.4:1 decorative. A logo takes no ratio, and at Tiny 22 the editor's 3:1 advice matters more than anywhere else in A11 — a mid-grey mark at 14 px of drawn height on a pale ground is the worst case the category contains, and the field says so in this design. Reduced motion changes nothing. At 400% zoom the wall is four across at 18 px, which is a real strain and is stated: a site whose readers zoom should choose 3 Grid. Print draws all twenty-four as laid out.
