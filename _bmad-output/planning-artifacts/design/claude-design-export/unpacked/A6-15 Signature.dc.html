Signature

The ask written in the first person and signed by the person making it: a portrait, a name, a role. It is the last design in A6 and the only one whose copy has an author, which changes the section's job — a signed banner is a note rather than an offer, and everything about it is one degree quieter than 1 Centred.

The signature is A1·6's avatar and meta row, moved and grown. A1·6 sets a 24 px circle with “Name · date” at 13 px beside it; here the circle leaves the row and becomes the portrait at the left, and the name and role take its place as two lines — 15 px at 600 and 13 px muted — behind a 40 px rule that stands in for the pen stroke. Both are stated departures. The role is not a link, the name is not a link, and there is no “more from this author”: A21 Author Showcases owns the author as a subject, and here the person is the reason for the ask rather than its object.
The title is Medium 34 by default where every other design in A6 defaults to Large 40, and Display 48 is not offered at all. A sentence in the first person set at 48 px is not a person speaking; it is a slogan with a photograph next to it. The signature sits above the actions, not below them — a reader should know who is asking before they are asked — and that is the one ordering decision this design makes that the rest of the category does not have to.

THE UGLY TEST · a 148-character title with no sub · the portrait stays at the top and the rule holds its place

The portrait is top-aligned to the title and stays there however long the words get — the alternative, centring it against the text block, makes a 96 px circle drift down the page as a site edits its copy. Nothing about the portrait is a state: it does not respond to a pointer, it is not a link, and it does not change size on hover. The actions are 1 Centred's exactly, which is worth saying because this is the fifteenth design and the button has not changed once in the whole category.

The signature becomes one row — rule, name, role — because a centred two-line block under a centred paragraph is four centred things in a column and the composition stops having a foot. 1 · The portrait is a real <img> with alt="" by default and no way to author it — the one image field in A6 with no alt of its own. The name is beside it in text, so a described portrait would say the same name twice; A1·6 reached this answer for its 24 px avatar and it holds at 96. The initials fall-back is aria-hidden for the same reason. 2 · The signature is not a heading and not a <cite>. It is two paragraphs — a name and a role — and the rule beside them is a border on the block, not an <hr>. Nothing here is marked up as an author: no rel="author", no microformat, no link to an author page. A21 Author Showcases owns the author as a subject, and A6 does not pretend a banner is a byline. 3 · The DOM order is eyebrow, title, sub, signature, actions — the signature before the actions at both positions, so a reader hears who is asking before they are asked. That is a change from A6's shared order, and it is the only one in the category. Two tab stops; the portrait, the name and the role take none. Zoom: at 200% text the portrait goes above the words in the phone's arrangement. Forced colours keep the portrait (it is an image) and the rule (it is a border); the design does not depend on either.
