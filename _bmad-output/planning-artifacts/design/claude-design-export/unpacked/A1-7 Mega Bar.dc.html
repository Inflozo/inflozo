The whole two-line block is the target and the hover surface, not the title alone — a description that is not clickable invites mis-taps. Tag chips in the third column use the same hover surface with their hairline darkened one step.
OPEN AND CLOSE Opens on click or on Enter, and on hover after 120 ms of intent — never instantly, or crossing the bar strobes the page. Closes on click outside, on Escape, on the pointer leaving both bar and panel for 200 ms, and on opening another trigger. It never closes while focus is inside it. Under reduced-motion the fade is dropped, not the delay.

There is no mega panel at 390 — the drawer is the panel. Children keep their descriptions at 13 px (the only place in the library where drawer items carry a second line), and a child with grandchildren gets its own caret, so the second level is reachable without a second screen. The featured post is dropped, as in A1·6.

ACCESSIBILITY · two levels without a keyboard trap
1Triggers are buttons, panels are groups. button aria-expanded aria-controls; the panel is a labelled group whose name is the trigger’s text. Column eyebrows are the lists’ headings, so “Writing, 3 items” is announced rather than eleven loose links.
2Focus is not trapped here. Unlike a drawer, Tab moves through the panel and then continues to the next bar item — a mega panel is an expansion of the page, not a modal. Escape closes and returns focus to the trigger; the panel never closes while focus is inside it.
3The second level is a nested list. Grandchildren are a ul inside their child’s li, always visible when the panel is — never a hover-revealed flyout, which is the pattern that makes two-level menus unusable with a trackpad.
4Description is part of the name. Each link’s accessible name is title plus description, read as one string, because the description is the disambiguator between “Reviews” and “Field Notes”. It is not aria-describedby, which many screen-reader users have off.
5Hover-open respects intent and pointers. The 120 ms open delay applies to fine pointers only; touch and keyboard open on activation. The featured post’s image is decorative, its heading is the link.
SPEC · A1·7 MEGA BAR · full text in “A1 Headers — Spec.md”
Content fields Shared A1 list, and the design that consumes the most of it: navItems (4 shown, 5+ fold), children ≤ 8 with descriptions ≤ 60 chars, grandchildren ≤ 6 per child, plus panelTags (tag list, opt) and featuredPost (reference or “latest”, opt). Descriptions are held but not rendered by every other design in A1 — this is where they earn their place.
Data Ghost primary nav for the bar. Every panel column carries its own source, set in the Data block: Authored links · Posts in a tag · Posts by an author · Latest posts · Tag list. A bound column adds tag/author, order (Newest · Oldest · Featured), count (Three · Four · Six), second line (Date · Excerpt · None), foot link (Tag archive · Custom · None) and an empty rule (Hide column · Show fallback). One source per column, never mixed inside one — sort order cannot be defined across authored and bound items. Bindings belong to the content, so they survive a design switch: A1·1’s dropdown shows the same bound posts as a flat list. A parent with 0 children renders as a plain link with no caret; 1 child → a 248 px dropdown, not a panel. 0 posts → rail dropped, columns re-flow.
Responsive · empty · a11y ≥ 1024 columns as set (2/3/4) plus the fixed rail. 768–1023 the columns stack in order, grandchild groups intact, rail dropped; bar keeps all items, search and ghost Sign in to the drawer. ≤ 767 no panel at all — the drawer carries children with descriptions and a caret for grandchildren. Empty: no descriptions → titles tighten to a 14 px row list and the panel loses half its height, which is the honest look of a site that has not written them. a11y: see the annotated frame; notably focus is not trapped, and the second level is always-visible nested lists, never flyouts.
