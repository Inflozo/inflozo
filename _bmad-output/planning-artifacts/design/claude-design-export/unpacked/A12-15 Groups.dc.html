A12 ABOUT AND TEAM · DESIGN 15 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONE REGROUPING · SIX CONTROLS

Groups

People under labelled headings — Editorial, Art and photo, Operations. It is the one design in A12 that regroups the list, and it does it by reading an authored field rather than by sorting: group is 24 characters on a person, and the section draws one block per distinct value.

The groups appear in the order their first member appears, and inside a group the authored order is kept — so a masthead that put the editor first still puts the editor first. A person with no group is drawn last, in a block with no heading, and the panel says how many. ⚑ All three rules are mine, and the last one is the design's sharpest case: Henry Osgood has no group, and he is drawn rather than dropped or invented into one.

ACCESSIBILITY · THE ONE DESIGN IN A12 WITH A SECOND HEADING LEVEL <section aria-labelledby> named by the title, an <h2>, then one <h3> and one <ul> per group — four lists on the primary frame rather than one. That is the whole accessibility argument for this design: a reader jumping by heading gets the groups, and a reader jumping by list gets the same blocks. The unlabelled block is a <ul> with no heading before it, which is what a group nobody named should sound like. No aria-label is invented for it — “Other” read aloud is a group the site did not author. ⚑ Mine, and the one place in A12 where a list has no name of any kind. At head None the <h2> goes and the group labels stay <h3>, so the outline skips a level. Promoting them to <h2> is refused: the level would change with a control, and a heading that moves when a title is switched off is worse than a skipped level. ⚑ Mine, and disclosed at head None. Group label Beside the row changes the DOM not at all — the <h3> precedes its list at both values, and Beside is a two-column grid over the same order. Tab stops: one per linked name, and the section link; the labels are not focusable, because nothing about a group is interactive. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; group label 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 at 13 px, which is the smallest heading in the library and passes as normal text; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; role and bio 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; hairline 1.3:1 as a non-text divider. Nothing moves at any setting, and print draws every group as it stands.
