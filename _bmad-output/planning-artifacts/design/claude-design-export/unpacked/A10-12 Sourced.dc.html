1 Row's cells with a numeral after each value and a numbered list at the foot. The design for numbers that came from four different places — a subscriber count, an open rate, an archive index and a payment ledger are four claims with four sources, and a single section-level line saying “our own records” would be covering three of them.

It is the one design that reads note, and the one that does not draw the section's own source line: four footnotes under a sentence that already sources the section is two sourcing systems arguing. With no note on any stat the section draws 1 Row.

The marker is a numeral in the body font at 13 px, superscript, muted, set tight against the value or its suffix. An asterisk at superscript size is a speck, a dagger is a convention most readers do not hold, and a bracketed numeral is a citation in a paper. The marker is not a link and does not jump — the notes are 200 px below and already on the screen, and a jump inside a section a reader can see is motion for nothing.
The notes sit 32 px under the stats on a 620 measure, 6 px apart, in text-muted at 13 px — the section source line's own position and treatment, holding a numbered list instead of a sentence. The numeral in the note is followed by a middot, not a full stop or a bracket, so it reads as a label rather than an ordinal. ⚑ Mine: the numeral, its size and colour, the middot, the 620 measure and the 6 px gap.

A STAT WITH NO NOTE TAKES NO MARKER · THE NUMBERING RUNS 1, 2 OVER THE STATS THAT HAVE ONE
Two placements, and each is right for a different set. At the foot is for four notes that are variations on one thing — four dates from four records — and keeps the row of numbers clean. Under each stat is for notes a reader needs beside the figure, and it costs the row 30 px of height and the numerals their reason to exist: at Under each stat there are no markers at all, because a numeral pointing at a line 12 px below it is a footnote to itself. ⚑ Mine: both placements and the marker rule. Numbering follows the notes that exist, in authored order. A stat with no note takes no marker and no number is reserved for it, so four stats with two notes read 1 and 2 — not 1 and 3. Reordering the repeater renumbers the markers and the list together, which is why the numeral is not a stored field. ⚑ Mine. The note is plain text at 60 characters and takes no URL. A footnote that is also a link is a citation, and A10 spends the accent on the section link only. A site whose sources are pages puts one URL on the section link and names it “How we count”, which is one destination instead of four. ⚑ Mine. The section's source and sourceUrl are kept and not drawn, and the panel says so. Two sourcing systems in one section leaves a reader deciding which one governs; the notes are the more precise of the two, so they win here and 1 Row draws the other.

THE ONLY FOCUSABLE THING IN THE SECTION · A MARKER IS NOT A LINK AND A NOTE TAKES NO URL
The marker holds 13 px at both value sizes and follows the suffix. Scaling it with the value would make it a second unit at Large 52; keeping it at the note's own size says it belongs to the list below. It sits after the suffix because the value and its units are one word — a numeral between 3.4 and hrs would read as an exponent. ⚑ Mine. Dividers are offered at Three and Six and unavailable at Four, which is A10-0's first settlement: three hairlines inside 306 px cells is a table without a header row. At Six the rules run down the two gutters of a three-column grid and stop at each row, never across it. With no note on any stat the section draws 1 Row and the panel reads “No stat has a note. Drawing 1 Row.” — a set of markerless cells with an empty list beneath them is this design with its subject removed. One stat with a note is drawn as one cell and one note, not handed to 4 Single, because 4 Single does not read note and the note is the reason this design was chosen. That is the one hand-off in A10 that goes the other way, and it is stated in the panel. ⚑ Mine.

Six is where this design earns its place and where it is at its heaviest: six markers, six notes, and a list that is 108 px tall on its own. The two rows keep the 48 px gap and the rules stop at each row rather than running through both — a hairline continuing past the first row's shortest cell would draw a table's column. Six is also the count where Under each stat is the better placement, and the panel says so rather than switching it: six notes at the foot are read as a block by nobody, where six notes in six cells are read with the numbers they belong to.
In dark the marker is muted #A79E8F at 6.0:1 and the note is the same, which puts the whole citation apparatus at one contrast step below the label — it is the quietest text in the category, and it is still 15 px in the label's case and 13 in the note's.

ACCESSIBILITY · ANNOTATED · A CITATION THAT IS NOT A LINK The marker is inside the stat's <p> as a <sup>, so the stat announces as “41,800, 1, readers on the Thursday letter”. A bare numeral in the middle of that string is not useful, so the <sup> carries aria-hidden="true" and the note is joined to the stat with aria-describedby instead — the reader gets “41,800, readers on the Thursday letter” and then, as a description, “Subscriber records, 1 January 2026”. ⚑ Mine, and the one place in A10 that uses a description relationship. The notes are an <ol> with the numeral drawn as text rather than as a list marker. A CSS counter would put the number outside the line box and the middot would have nothing to attach to; the text numeral keeps “1 · Subscriber records” as one string for both a reader and a screen reader. The list keeps its <ol> because the order is the numbering. ⚑ Mine. Nothing here is a link and nothing jumps. No href="#note-1", no back-reference arrow, no role="doc-noteref" and no role="doc-footnote": the DPUB roles describe a printed book's apparatus, support for them is uneven, and a section 400 px tall does not need in-page navigation. At Under each stat the description relationship is dropped, because the note is the next line of the same paragraph. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, label 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, marker and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 — the same as the label, not a step below it, because a citation a reader cannot read is not a citation. Link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. One tab stop. No motion. Print keeps the markers and the notes exactly as drawn, which is the one design in A10 that paper suits better than a screen.
