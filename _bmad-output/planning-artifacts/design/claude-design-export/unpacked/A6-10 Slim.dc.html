Slim

One line of text and one action on a single row, at the tightest padding in the category. It is the banner for a page that already has a hero and a footer and needs a third ask that does not act like one, and it is the design A6 names whenever a site has no title to write.

Everything about this design is one step smaller, including the button, and that is the point. The padding scale is Compact 20 · Comfortable 28 · Spacious 36 — its own, not A6's 64/96/132 — and the action is A1·1's button at A1·1's size, 14 px/600 and 9×17, rather than A4's hero scale: a 46 px button inside 28 px of padding is a button with a strip around it. It is the only design in A6 whose action is not at hero scale, and the reason is arithmetic rather than taste.
The line is the title, at 21 px in the heading font — the same size A5·6 gives a row's title — with the note under it at 13. There is no sub: a slim bar with three lines of text is 1 Centred with the padding turned down. The rules are what make it a section rather than a stray row, and Above and below is the default here where every other design in A6 draws no line of its own. Directly above the footer the lower rule is dropped and the footer's own top hairline serves — two hairlines 96 px apart with nothing between them is a mistake a reader can see, and the frame above draws exactly that case.

Centred takes no note. A centred pair is one object about 520 px wide; a 13 px line under the left half of it hangs off the composition, and centring the note under both makes the row three lines tall, which is 1 Centred at Compact. The control disables Note with the reason shown. At Ground Contrast and Surface the rules are dropped — the plane's own edges are the rules — and Rules is unavailable, which is the same argument 2 Flush Left makes for refusing its Contrast ground and the reason this design keeps it: a slim bar can afford a plane because it has no measure to protect.

WHAT THIS IS NOT · A2 ANNOUNCEMENT BARS A2 owns the strip at the top of the page: pinned above the header, dismissible, one per site, sometimes sticky. This is a section in the flow — it scrolls, it is not dismissible, it carries no close control, a page may hold two of them, and it appears wherever the site puts it. The two look alike at 25% zoom and are different objects; a site wanting the top strip is told to use A2·1 Rule or A2·2 Split, and the editor names them at this design.

The states are A1·1's, since the button is A1·1's: 6% on hover, 10% pressed, a 2 px accent ring 2 px out. The arrow does not translate on hover — the library has no moving arrows, and a 2 px slide at 160 ms on a 13 px glyph is a twitch rather than a signal. The whole row is never a link. A 1,296 px row with a button in it cannot also be a target, and a slim bar whose text was clickable would make the button decorative.

Padding 28 → 24 at Comfortable, the line 21 → 20, the gap 48 → 32, and the row holds — which is the whole reason to draw this frame. A 40-character line at 20 px is 390 px, the button 118, so the row needs 540 of 754 and the design's real limit is the length of its own line rather than the width of the window. The editor advises 60 characters and says what happens past it: the line wraps to two, the row grows to 78 px, and the note is what a site should have written instead.

1 · The line is still the section's <h2> at 21 px, naming the <section aria-labelledby> as everywhere else in A6 — size is not level, in both directions. With no line authored the section is not a labelled region, and this is the one design where that is a supported state rather than a hand-off: the bar is then an action and its note, and the sidebar says the section has lost its name. 2 · The note is a paragraph after the heading and before the action in the DOM — the one design where the note is not last, because here it reads as part of the line rather than as terms under a button. It is not aria-describedby anything. 3 · One <a>, one tab stop, the visible label as its whole name. At Text link the target is 44 px tall and extends past the label's box, so the smallest action in A6 is still the library's minimum target. The row is not a link at any value. Contrast · line 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · note 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · button 4.6:1 / 8.1:1 · rules 1.2:1 / 1.3:1, decorative and unannounced, drawn as border-top and border-bottom rather than <hr>. Not A2: no role="alert", no live region, no dismiss control, nothing remembered in storage — a reader who reloads sees the same bar, because it is a section of the page and not a message about it.
