Slim

A 96 to 144 px band: a line of overlapped faces, one sentence, and the section link at the far end. It is the shortest section in A12 and the only one with no heading of any kind — the sentence is title set at 15 px in the body font, so this section takes no accessible name, which is A11·10 Slim's position carried over.

It carries three departures the panel states at the value. A 32 px circle, off the category's ladder and the smallest face in A12. A 2 px ring in the background token, which exists to separate overlapped circles and is refused in every other design. And no name is drawn, so there is nothing here to link: this is the one design in A12 that keeps url and never draws it. ⚑ All three are mine, and the roster's “96 to 132 px” is amended to 96 to 144 by the arithmetic below.

ACCESSIBILITY · A SECTION WITH NO HEADING AND ONE SENTENCE A bare <section> with no aria-labelledby and no <h2>: the sentence is a <p>, so this design takes no accessible name at any setting. That is A11·10 Slim's position carried forward verbatim — a 96 px band under a hero is not a landmark, and promoting a 15 px sentence would put it into the page's outline above the content it introduces. ⚑ Flagged, and stated on every frame. The faces are a <ul> of <li> holding <img alt="">, and the initials block takes aria-hidden. A reader hears the sentence and the link and nothing else. The list is kept rather than replaced with a single decorative <div> so that a reader using a list-level jump still finds a list of twelve where twelve people were authored. One tab stop, and only when a link is authored. No face is focusable, nothing has a tooltip, and there is no hover state anywhere in the band except the link's. A section with no link authored has no interactive content at all, which makes this the quietest design in the category. Contrast: sentence 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 at 15 px in text rather than text-muted, because it is the only sentence on the frame; link label 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 with a 3.6:1 accent underline; initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1; the 2 px ring is background against a photograph and is not asked to meet a ratio, since it separates two images rather than carrying information. Nothing moves at any setting and there is no behaviour. At 400% zoom the band is the stacked arrangement with the faces wrapped onto three rows. Print is the light mode as drawn, including the ring, which prints as the paper colour.
