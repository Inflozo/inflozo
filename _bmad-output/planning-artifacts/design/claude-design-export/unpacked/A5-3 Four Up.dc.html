Four Up

Four items across 300 px each, the densest grid the item ladder allows. Four is the last count offered because the fifth column would put the body copy under the brief's 15 px floor. This design settles that floor, the 19 px title, the Body copy control that makes eight items readable, and the short last row — six items in fours is four and two, which is the first frame in the category where the rule is visible rather than described.

HIDE · eight titles in two full rows · the row gap tightens 40 → 32

Body copy Hide is what makes eight items a section rather than a wall. It hides the field rather than clearing it — a user who turns it back on has their copy — and it is the design's answer to settlement 4: with no body anywhere, the row gap tightens one step, exactly as the settlement says. The two extra items here are Office hours and Reading list, authored beyond the six the rest of the frames draw; they appear only in this frame and the caption says so, because §7·1 asks that a frame carrying different content say why.

The link label is 14 px on this design, not 15 — the only place in A5 it is not the settlement's size, because a 15 px label under a 15 px body on a 300 px column reads as a second sentence rather than an action. It is the same step the item title and the container took. Flagged as mine, and it is the design's one departure from settlement 3.

WHY FIVE COLUMNS ARE NOT OFFERED
A fifth column on the 1296 px content width is a 233 px item. At 233 px a 19 px title takes about 12 characters a line and the body would have to drop to 13 or 14 px, under the brief's floor of 15. So the answer is Body copy Hide at four columns rather than a fifth column — eight titles in two rows of four say what ten titles in two rows of five would say, and they stay readable. A site that wants ten features has a page, and the editor says so at the twelfth item. Flagged as mine: the 233 px arithmetic and the refusal.

the second row: two items, left, at the same 300 px column4
1 · A5-0's structure, unchanged: <section aria-labelledby> named by the h2, items a <ul>, titles h3. 2 · The title is 19 px and the body 15 px, and neither is smaller than that anywhere in A5. The 15 px floor is the brief's, and it is why a fifth column is refused rather than sized down. 3 · Icon aria-hidden, container 36 px. At Icon in accent the glyph is the pack's background colour on the accent fill, 6.1:1 in dark and 4.9:1 in light — both measured against the fill rather than the page, since the fill is what the glyph sits on. 4 · The short row is a layout fact, not an announced one. Six items are six <li> in one list whatever the grid does with them; nothing tells a reader which row an item is in, and the empty cell has no element. Body copy Hide removes the paragraph from the DOM rather than hiding it visually — a paragraph a sighted reader cannot see and a screen reader still reads is a worse outcome than either. The field keeps its content in the editor. Contrast · title 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · item title 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · body 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · link label 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · glyph on tint 11.2:1 / 11.9:1 · glyph on accent 4.9:1 / 6.1:1. Zoom. At 200% text the four columns become two, and at 400% one; the 36 px container does not grow, as in 1 and 2.
