Split Head

The head held in a 416 px column at the left margin, the accordion in an 824 px column at the right, on A8·6's 56 px gutter. The division is A5·5's and the rules for it are A8·6's, carried without change: both columns top-aligned, the head never sticky and never vertically centred, and the foot's two values settled where they were settled.

What A9 adds is a column that changes height while a reader uses it. The head does not move when a row opens: it is not centred against the list, so a list that grows by 90 px grows downward and the head stays where the page put it. That is the whole reason the top alignment was worth inheriting rather than reconsidering.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWO COLUMNS, ONE SECTION The two columns are a flex row, not two landmarks — no <aside>, no roles, no second heading. A8·6's rule verbatim. One <h2> in the head column, one <ul> in the other, every question an <h3> holding a full-width <button aria-expanded aria-controls>. The head is read first at both Head column values, because row-reverse moves pixels and not the DOM. At foot Under the head the note and link are read before the questions; at Under the rows, after them — the two values differ in reading order as well as position, which is the honest consequence of moving a footer. Tab order: the section link in the head, then one stop per row, in authored order. At foot Under the rows the link comes last. Enter and Space open; no arrow keys. Closed answers are hidden, with the same cost as every accordion in A9: not found by a browser's find, not seen by a reader scanning. The panel names 15 Ledger for a site that wants the answers visible beside their questions and 3 Open List for one that wants them underneath. Contrast: question 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, answer the same, marker 5.6:1 / 6.2:1 resting, title and sub 15.8:1 and 5.6:1 / 15.1:1 and 6.8:1, link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. Every row prints open, in one column, with the head above.
