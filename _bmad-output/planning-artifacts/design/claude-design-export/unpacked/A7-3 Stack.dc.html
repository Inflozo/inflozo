A7 PRICING AND TIERS · DESIGN 3 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ARRANGEMENT WITH NO TIER COUNT

Stack

One full-width row per tier: the name and price at the left, the benefits in the middle, the action at the right. It is the only arrangement in A7 that does not care how many tiers there are — four rows read exactly like three — which is why every columned design in the category hands off to it at 1080 and why the sidebar names it whenever a site adds a fourth tier. It is also the design that controls a plane's padding, so its own section padding is fixed.

Three parts on one line, in the order a reader decides in: what it is called and what it costs at the left on a fixed 300, what comes with it in the middle, the action at the right on a fixed 200. The two fixed columns are what make four rows agree — a row whose price column shrank with its benefit count would put three prices at three different distances from the margin — and the 64 px gutters either side of the benefits are the same at every row. The parts are top-aligned, never centred vertically: a tier with one benefit and a tier with six then start their text on the same line.
The row is the plane, so Row padding is this design's control and the section's own padding is fixed at 96 — A5·16's rule, carried by every design in the library that controls a plane. The recommendation is settlement 2's three signals with the plane step landing on the row rather than a card.

1 · A <ul> of rows, each an <li> with the tier name as an <h3> — A7's shared structure, unchanged by the horizontal arrangement. The row is a <div> and never a link, at all three plane values. 2 · DOM order is name, price, description, action, benefits — the same order as every other design — and the visual order at this width is name, price, description, benefits, action. This is A7's one design where the two differ, by one element, and both readings are complete: a reader hears the offer then what comes with it, and sees what comes with it then the offer. Tab order follows the DOM, so a keyboard reader reaches the action before the benefit list, which is the order they would want. 3 · One tab stop per row, in price order. The action's width is fixed at 200 rather than the row's — A7's fill-the-card rule applies to a card, and a 1,232 px wide button is not a button. The 44 px height and everything else about it is unchanged. Two columns of benefits are one list. CSS columns, not a grid of two lists, so the reading order, the DOM order and the visual order are the same sequence and a screen reader announces one list of four rather than two of two. Contrast · as 1 Cards, with one addition: at Row planes Hairline between the recommended row's plane is 1.1:1 against the ground and the badge carries the recommendation, which is why Row planes None keeps the badge rather than dropping the mark entirely. At 200% text the three columns become the tablet's two lines at every width.
