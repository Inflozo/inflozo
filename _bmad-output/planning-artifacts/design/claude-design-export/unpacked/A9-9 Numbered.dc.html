Numbered

Questions numbered in the pack's heading font at Display 40, the question at Feature 24 beside the numeral, the answer beneath it. Always open. Order is the only thing a FAQ can say about priority — A9 refuses “popular” and “important” flags outright — and this is the design that makes the order the site chose visible.

It costs something to draw. The title is capped at Medium 34 here and has no size control, because §2 allows one display moment per section and the numerals have taken it: six 40 px numerals under a 48 px title would be two systems arguing about which is the section. The numerals are also the only place in A9 outside a section title where the heading font appears.

72 · 32 · 676 = 780. The numeral column is 72 px because “06” at Display 40 in a serif is 48 px wide and a two-digit numeral at twelve would be wider; holding 72 at every count keeps the questions on one vertical whether the list is four rows or twelve. The numeral sits on the question's cap height, not its baseline — 40 px of Georgia beside 24 px of Inter aligned on baselines puts the numeral's shoulder above the question's ascender.
The numeral is text-muted at 40 px, which is large text and passes at 5.6:1. It is not the accent — A9 spends the accent on links, the focus ring and 11 Tabs' active tab, and six accent numerals would be the loudest thing in the category. ABOVE THE QUESTION · NO COLUMN · THE TEXT TAKES THE FULL 780
Padded or Plain changes the character, never the column. “1” and “01” both sit in 72 px, left-aligned, so switching does not move a single question. Padded is the default because a padded numeral is a set and a plain one is a count — 01 to 06 reads as a numbered thing, 1 to 6 reads as six of something. Numerals are drawn, never counted by CSS. They come from the item's position in the repeater, so reordering renumbers, and a site cannot type its own numbers — there is no numeral field, and a list whose sixth row says “09” is a list with a mistake in it. ⚑ Mine. Above the question is the value for long questions. A 120-character question in a 676 px column is three lines; the same question on 780 with the numeral above it is two, and this value exists for that content rather than for variety. The numeral is aria-hidden at both positions, and the list is an <ol>: the order is in the markup, so a screen reader announces “1 of 6” and does not also read the drawn “01”.

At Rules None the rows sit 48 px apart at every padding value — 3 Open List's rule at this design's scale, and 48 rather than 40 because a 40 px numeral needs more air above it than a 20 px question does. This is the one design where Rules None is as strong as Between: the numerals already say where a row starts, which is what a rule is for. Six is the count control's ceiling and twelve is the field's. Past six the design still draws — twelve rows of Feature 24 with their answers is a very long section — and the panel names 1 Accordion at that point rather than refusing the content. At ten and above the numeral column is still 72, because it was sized for two digits from the start. There is no interactive element in this design at all unless a link is authored, which it shares with 3 Open List and 15 Ledger. No marker, no button, no region, no hover, no motion, no state. A deep link scrolls to the question's <h3> with 96 px above it and puts focus there, using the same tabindex="-1" as 3 Open List. The numeral is not part of the anchor and not part of the accessible name.

ACCESSIBILITY · THE ONLY ORDERED LIST IN A9 The list is an <ol>, not a <ul> — the only one in the category — because the numbers are part of what the design says. The drawn numeral is aria-hidden and the list's own numbering carries the order, so a screen reader hears “1 of 6” once instead of “01, one of six”. One <h2>, an <h3> per question with tabindex="-1" and the anchor as its id; the answer is its following content. No button, no region, no marker, no hover, no motion — 3 Open List's structure with a numeral column. The numeral is not in the heading's accessible name. It sits outside the <h3> in both positions, so the rotor reads six questions and not “01 What do I get with a membership?”. Contrast: numeral 5.6:1 light / 6.8:1 dark as large text, question and answer 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, inline code 15.4:1 / 14.6:1 on its chip, link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. In forced colours the numeral takes the system's text colour and stops being quieter than the question, which flattens the hierarchy to size alone — stated, not corrected. Print and screen are the same drawing, as in 3 Open List: nothing is hidden and nothing opens.
