Rail

One line of logos wider than the window, scrolled by the reader. The distinction from 8 Marquee is the whole design: a marquee moves whether anyone is watching, and a rail moves only when a hand causes it. Nothing on this frame is in motion at rest, and nothing ever starts on its own.

It is the one design in A11 with a precondition — if the logos fit the viewport there is nothing to scroll, so the section draws as 1 Row — and, because its two arrows are real controls rather than artwork, the one design in the category that always spends the accent, on their hover and focus, whatever the site authored.

The rail is full bleed, its content starts on the left page margin, and the logos sit at natural width on the gap the site chose — 8 Marquee's geometry exactly, with no cells and no counts. The arrows sit on the page margins, on the track's centre line, over the fade: 38 px boxes, surface, hairline, pack radius, A1·14's outlined icon button. At the start the left arrow is disabled at 40% and still focusable, so a keyboard reader can tell it exists and that it does nothing yet.
One press moves the rail by the viewport minus 96 px — 1,248 at 1440 — so the logo that was at the leading edge is still on screen after the press and nothing is stepped over. Scroll snapping is refused: a snap point has to be a logo's edge, and a 216 px wordmark and a 26 px square mark cannot share one rule without one of them sitting off-centre. The rail stops where the reader stopped it. ⚑ Mine: the overlap, the arrows over the fade, and refusing snap.

The fade appears at an end only when there is content past it, so at rest a reader sees one fade and knows which way the rail goes. That makes the fade the affordance and the arrows the mechanism — and the fade is a mask rather than an overlay, so it works on any pack's ground without a colour being written into the design. The rail ends on the page margins at both ends: 72 px of padding at the head of the track and 72 at its tail, so the first and last logo line up with the title. A rail whose last logo stops against the window edge looks broken, and the tail padding is what prevents it. ⚑ Mine. Scroll position is not remembered and not restored. The precondition is measured, not counted. Six logos is the floor for choosing this design, but the test at render is whether the list is wider than the viewport — so the same six logos are a rail on a phone and 1 Row on a desktop, and the hand-off can happen at one width and not another. That is the only hand-off in A11 that depends on the window rather than the content. ⚑ Mine. The arrows are the reason this design always spends the accent. They are controls, so their hover and focus take accent — A6's treatment for an icon button — while every logo stays untinted. Fourteen of the fifteen designs in A11 can contain no accent pixel; this one cannot. A disabled arrow stays in the DOM, stays focusable and stays 38 px. aria-disabled="true" rather than the disabled attribute, drawn at 40% — so the pair never changes width and the rail never shifts sideways when a reader reaches an end. ⚑ Mine.

At ≤ 767 the arrows are dropped and the rail is scrolled by touch, with the fade as the only affordance — two 38 px buttons over a 350 px track cover a fifth of it, and a phone has a better mechanism than a button. Arrows stays enabled and has no effect below 767, and the panel says so. The track keeps tabindex="0" at every width, so a keyboard attached to a phone still reaches it with the arrow keys. ACCESSIBILITY · A SCROLL CONTAINER A KEYBOARD CAN REACH The track is a role="group" with tabindex="0" and an accessible name taken from the section's title — “The letter, elsewhere, scrollable”. That is what makes the arrow keys work, and it is required rather than decorative: a scroll container with no tab stop is content a keyboard cannot reach, which is 2.1.1 whatever the arrows do. With no title authored the name is “Logo wall, scrollable”, the one generated string in A11. ⚑ Mine. The arrows are <button>s named “Scroll left” and “Scroll right”, with aria-disabled rather than disabled at the ends so focus is never dropped mid-interaction. They are after the track in the DOM at Arrows At the ends and before it at Above the rail — the drawn order in both cases. Under reduced motion the rail still scrolls; it just stops animating. scroll-behavior: smooth becomes auto and a press jumps. This design does not hand off under reduced motion, and 8 Marquee does, because a rail's motion is the reader's own — the preference is about motion nobody asked for. A linked logo inside the rail is an ordinary link and tabbing to one scrolls it into view — the browser's own behaviour, not a script — so unlike 8 Marquee there is nothing to chase. This is the design to choose when the logos are links and there are too many for 5 Cards. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, arrow glyph 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 resting and 3.3:1 / 6.3:1 on hover and focus, where it takes the accent; a disabled glyph at 40% is 4.1:1 / 3.9:1, above the 3:1 non-text floor; hairline 1.4:1 / 1.6:1. A logo takes no ratio. The fade is a mask and never covers a focused item, because focus scrolls it clear first. Print draws the wrapped wall, as 8 Marquee does: paper has no viewport, and a rail's overflow would be a page edge cutting a mark in half.
