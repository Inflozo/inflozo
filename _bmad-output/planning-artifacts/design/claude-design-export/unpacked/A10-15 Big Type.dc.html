Big Type

One stat per full-width row, the value at 96 px on the left margin and the label in a 416 px column at the right. Two or three rows, hairlines between them, and nothing else in the section but its head. The design a site picks when the numbers are the page's argument rather than its evidence.

96 is off the category's ladder, which stops at Display 68, and it is the second of the two designs allowed past it — 4 Single at 128 is the other. The count is capped at three because an interior row is 161 px and the whole section is 829: a fourth row takes it to 990, which is past a laptop's viewport before the last label.

Big 96 is the widest type in the category and it still fits the ugly case. Twelve characters plus a prefix at 96 px in Georgia is 633 px; with the 416 label column and the 64 px gutter that is 1,113 px of the 1,296 available. This is the design where the twelve-character ceiling on value earns its place — at fourteen characters the same value would be 738 px and the label column would have to give way. ⚑ Mine: the ceiling's justification and both measurements. Display 68 is on the ladder and is the value to pick when the section has to fit a screen. At 68 an interior row is 133 px, three rows are 334, and the section with its head, source line and Comfortable padding is 745; at Big 96 the same section is 829, so the third label sits below an 800 px viewport. The panel says which, and does not choose: a site whose section is the page's argument wants 96 and the scroll. Label Under moves the label to 12 px below the value on a 620 measure and leaves the right margin empty. It is the arrangement for labels past 40 characters, where the 416 column runs to three lines and the row's height stops being the value's. At Under the rows are shorter and the section is quieter, which is the opposite of what this design is for, and the panel says so rather than hiding the value. ⚑ Mine. A row with no label is the value alone on the left margin, the right margin empty, the hairlines unchanged. Nothing is centred and nothing is substituted — at 96 px an em dash in the label's place would be a 40 px mark on the page.

NO HOVER PLANE · NO ROW LINK · NO ACCENT ON THE VALUE · NO COUNT-UP · NO SCROLL TRIGGER
The rows are not links and take no hover plane, here least of all: a 161 px tall row that fills with a hover surface is a button the size of a paragraph, and there is nothing behind it to open. A10's link treatment is the section's one link, under the source line, and this design draws it unchanged. The value is never the accent, at 96 px above all. One accent numeral in three is a claim about which number matters; three is a section in one colour. At this size the type is already the emphasis, and colour on top of it would be the loudest thing in twelve packs. This is the design the count-up refusal was written for. A 96 px figure ticking up from zero is the effect a site imagines when it picks Big Type, and it is refused here at every motion setting for A10-0's four reasons: it animates the content, it is unreadable while it runs, it re-runs on the way back up the page, and it lies to assistive technology. The panel says so on this design and not just in the proof. Count is Two or Three and there is no One. One stat at 96 px with a label beside it is 4 Single at a smaller size and a worse arrangement, so one authored stat hands off to 4 Single and the panel names it. Four or more draws the first three and says so.

ACCESSIBILITY · ANNOTATED · A 96 PX NUMBER THAT IS NOT A HEADING The value is not an <h3>, at 96 px as much as at 40. It is the largest type in the category and the temptation to mark it up as a heading is strongest here; three headings that are all numerals is a heading rotor full of digits, and the section's one heading is its title. The stat is a <p> inside an <li>, value then label, announced as one string. Label Right and Label Under are the same DOM in the same order — the value is first in both, so the forced Under at ≤ 767 changes nothing a screen reader hears. The 64 px gutter is a flex gap, not a tab or a run of spaces, so nothing is announced between the two. At 200% zoom the row becomes the stacked arrangement — the same rule the 767 breakpoint uses, applied to the available width rather than the device — and at 400% the value is 46 px in a column with the label beneath it. Nothing is clipped and nothing scrolls sideways, because the value's size steps rather than the column's. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, label and source 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, hairline 1.4:1 / 1.6:1 and held to no text ratio. No motion at any setting, and this is the design that names the count-up refusal in its own panel. One tab stop, on the link. Print keeps 96 px values, which on A4 is two rows to a page and is the site's choice to make.
