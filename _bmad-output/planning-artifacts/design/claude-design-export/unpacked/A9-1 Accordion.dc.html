Accordion

One column of hairline rows on a 780 px measure, centred in the 1,296 width. The design a site reaches for when it types “FAQ” into the section picker, the one A9-0's row component was drawn for, and the design six others hand off to when their own precondition is missing.

Two decisions make it the home rather than one of fifteen. It draws every authored question — there is no Rows shown control, so at twenty-four items it is twenty-four rows and the panel reads “24 of 24 drawn”. And the row's padding follows the question's step rather than taking a control of its own, which frees the sixth slot for Open on load and keeps the section from having two spacing controls that argue with each other.

160 ms moves two things and nothing else: the answer's height, and the marker's rotation. The row's hairline does not move, the rows below slide down with the height rather than animating on their own, and the section's own height is what changes — nothing is absolutely positioned and nothing overlays the row below. Under reduced motion both are instant. The row still opens, the marker still turns, the open state is identical; only the interpolation is dropped. A9-0's rule, and the reason no threshold changes: a reader who has asked for less motion has not asked for a different FAQ. Nothing runs while editing. In the editor the rows are drawn in whatever state Open on load names, and pressing a row in the canvas selects it rather than opening it.

Resting: question in text, marker in text-muted. The question is never muted — a closed question is the content, not a label for it. Hover: the hover surface fills the row and extends 16 px past the measure on both sides, so the plane has air around the type instead of clipping it; the marker goes to text. The hairline stays exactly where it was. ⚑ The 16 px overhang is mine. Focus: A6's ring — 2 px accent, 2 px outside, the gap taking the ground — around the whole 780 × 64 row, because the whole row is the button. The ring is drawn without the hover plane here: a keyboard reader gets the ring, a pointer gets the plane, and a reader using both gets both. Open: the marker has turned and the answer is drawn. The open row takes no plane, no accent tint and no border change — the answer being there is the state. That refusal is what keeps a list with three open rows from reading as three selected rows.

1 · The question is an <h3> holding a full-width <button>, so every question is in the heading rotor and the button's accessible name is the question itself. aria-expanded carries the state and aria-controls points at the answer. One tab stop per row — six rows, six stops, in authored order — with Enter and Space opening. No arrow-key navigation: an accordion is not a composite widget, and stealing the arrow keys stops a reader scrolling. 2 · The marker is aria-hidden and drawn in CSS borders, not a glyph or an icon font. It is never the only signal: aria-expanded is the state for assistive technology, the answer's presence is the state for everyone else, and in forced colours the marker takes the system text colour and still turns. 3 · The answer is a <div role="region"> labelled by its button, holding real <p>, <ul>, <a> and <code> elements. Links inside an answer are in the tab order only while the answer is open, which is what hidden gives us for free and a height:0 answer would not. 4 · The closed answers are in the DOM with hidden, so the page's source is complete and nothing is fetched later. They are still not on the page: a reader scanning does not see them and a browser's find does not match them. The panel says so and names 3 Open List. This is the cost A9's ten accordions carry and the reason five designs do not. Contrast · question 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, answer the same, marker resting 5.6:1 / 6.2:1 and 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 while open, sub and note 5.6:1 / 6.8:1, the link's underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1 as a non-text indicator, the ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. Target 44 px at every width and every row-padding value — at Compact 16 the row is 16 + 24 + 16 = 56 px, so the floor is never the binding constraint.
