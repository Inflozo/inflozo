Ticker

Several notices on one continuously moving strip, with a pause control that is not optional. This is the design for a site that genuinely has three things to say at once — and the one where the resting state drawn in the editor is deliberately not the state most readers see, because motion does not run while you are editing.

44 px at Comfortable, drawn 190 px into the loop so the clipping at both ends is visible. Messages are separated by a 4 px dot at 45% of the carried colour and 28 px of space either side. Both edges carry a 56 px fade to the band colour, and the pause and close sit inside the right fade so the strip appears to pass behind them rather than colliding with them. The strip moves right to left at 60 px per second at Medium: a 14 px sentence takes about six seconds to cross a 1440 viewport, which is slow enough to read and quick enough to reach the third message.

Under prefers-reduced-motion the strip does not move at all. It shows message 1, centred, with the three dots from A2·9 Rotator at the right end and no auto-advance: the reader taps a dot. The pause button is gone because there is nothing to pause. This makes Ticker identical to Rotator for those readers — flagged in A2-0 as a convergence I think is correct. The alternative, a strip that steps one message every few seconds, is still motion for someone who asked for none.

1 · Skip link first, ahead of the bar. 2 · <aside aria-label="Announcements"> — plural here — containing a <ul> of the messages in authored order. aria-live="off": the strip is present at load and must not announce itself as it moves. 3 · The visual loop repeats the list a second time so the strip never shows a gap; the duplicate is aria-hidden and carries no links, so nothing is announced or focusable twice. Separator dots are decorative and hidden. 4 · The pause button satisfies WCAG 2.2.2 — motion that runs longer than five seconds needs a mechanism to stop it. It is a real button, labelled “Pause announcements” / “Play announcements”, and it stays in the accessibility tree at every width. At ≤ 767 it is replaced by tap-to-pause and a resume button that appears once paused; hover-pause is never the only mechanism. Motion also stops on focus entering the bar and under prefers-reduced-motion, where the strip does not move at all. 5 · Close is last in focus order: message links in order, pause, close. 6 · Carried text on contrast 13.9:1. The edge fades pass over text — a message is partly faded for about 0.6 s at Medium — so no message is ever only legible mid-fade, which is why the loop repeats rather than jumping.

SPEC · A2·8 TICKER · full text in “A2 Announcement Bars — Spec.md”
Content fields messages[] (list of { message ≤ 120, linkLabel ≤ 24, linkUrl }, req, 2–6 items — one item is not a ticker and the editor says so, offering A2·1) · dismissible · audience · dismissMemory. Uses no label, headline, body, image or clock. Switching to a single-message design keeps all six and shows item 1; switching back restores the order.
Controls · data Messages: the list itself, up to 6, drag to reorder. Speed: Slow 40 px/s · Medium 60 · Fast 90 — constant at every viewport. Separator: Dot · Slash · Wide space. Ground: Contrast · Surface · Accent. Height: Compact 36 · Comfortable 44 · Spacious 56. Placement: In flow · Sticky (not recommended — moving text that follows the reader).
Data: member state only. Fewer than two messages → renders as A2·1 Rule with item 1.
Responsive · motion · a11y · flagged 1440–768: strip with 56 → 40 px edge fades, separator spacing 28 → 24, speed unchanged; all messages stay in the loop at every width because the strip is a queue, not a layout. ≤ 767: bar 48 px for a 44 px close, the pause button leaves the bar and its job passes to tap-to-pause — first tap pauses, second follows the link under the finger — with a 28 px resume button appearing once paused. Motion: pauses on hover, on focus entering the bar, and on the button; stops entirely under prefers-reduced-motion, where the bar shows message 1 with A2·9's dots and no auto-advance, making the two designs identical for those readers. a11y: ul of messages, the visual duplicate aria-hidden and unfocusable, aria-live off, pause button always in the accessibility tree (WCAG 2.2.2).
Flagged as mine: the three speeds in px/s, the 6-message cap, the 56 px fades, dropping the pause button at 390 in favour of tap-to-pause, and session-sticky pausing.
