hero image 4:3 — Maya Okonjo at her desk, Leeds

Seven and five on a 64 px gutter, both halves centred against each other on the cross axis — the picture's optical centre lines up with the block's, not with its headline. 4:3 is the crop for every picture that sits beside text in this library, chosen because it survives being 516 px wide on a desktop and 350 px wide on a phone without the subject leaving the frame. It is fixed here and is not a control. Flagged as mine: the 7/5 division, the 64 px gutter, cross-axis centring, and the 4:3 crop.

↑ FRAMED INSET · 12 px of surface and one hairline around the same 4:3 crop · the picture does not shrink, the column widens by 24

Framed inset is for a photograph with pale edges, which on the Paper ground disappears into the page. The frame is surface with the border token and no shadow — a mount, not a card. Both treatments keep the same radius token, and the crop is identical in both: only the mount is added. On a Contrast ground the frame is the pack's lightest surface at 12% instead, since a white mount on an inverted band is a hole. Flagged as mine: the 12 px mount, its no-shadow rule, and the 12% variant on contrast.

The block does not centre and does not widen: a fall-back that moves the words makes the user think they broke something. In the editor the picture's place shows the striped placeholder and “Drop a hero image here”; on a published page there is no placeholder, ever — A3·14's rule, carried forward.

4:3, same centre

Between 1080 and 768 the split goes six and six on a 40 px gutter, both columns 357 px, and the crop stays 4:3 — the box narrows and the height follows it, which is why the crop can be fixed. The headline steps to 40 rather than the ladder's 48, the one place this design departs from it: at 357 px, 48 px type sets three words a line and the sub outruns the headline. This is also where the actions stack, at 834 rather than 767, because 357 px cannot hold both labels on one row. Flagged as mine: the 40 px headline step and the early stack, both stated in the sidebar.

alt asked for2
1 · Eyebrow and headline as 1 Centred — a <p> above the page's single <h1>, in an unlabelled section. 2 · Alt text is asked for here, not defaulted to empty. This is the category's split: where words sit over a picture the picture is decorative, and where it sits beside them it is usually content — a photograph of the person who writes the letter says something the headline does not. The editor asks, says why in one line, and accepts empty for the case where the picture really is decoration. Explicit width and height, loading="eager", fetchpriority="high", and the 4:3 box reserved before the file arrives so nothing shifts. 3 · Actions are links, 46 px tall, focus order matching reading order. Picture: Left moves the DOM, so a screen reader meets the image before the headline exactly as a sighted reader does; nothing is re-ordered in CSS at any width, and the ≤ 767 change of the picture's position is a real markup change at the breakpoint. Contrast · unchanged from 1 Centred: headline 12.1:1 / 11.8:1, sub 5.4:1 / 6.4:1, primary label on accent 3.2:1 / 8.1:1, the flagged pack finding in A4-0. Nothing sits on the picture, so no contrast in this design depends on the user's photograph — the reason the beside-text arrangement is the safe default for a site that has not thought about its images. Forced colours · the picture keeps its box, the mount's hairline remains, and the buttons take the system's own pair. Nothing in the design is carried by a shadow.
