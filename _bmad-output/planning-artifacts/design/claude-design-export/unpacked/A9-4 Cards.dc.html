A9 FAQ · DESIGN 4 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · ALWAYS OPEN

Cards

One question and its answer per card, three cards of 416 on a 24 px gutter — A8·2's grid and card, unchanged, carrying a question where it carried a quotation. Always open, because a card that has to be pressed to show its contents is a card with nothing in it.

It is the design for a short FAQ with short answers — six questions of two sentences each — and the one place in A9 where answer length has a cost a reader can see: cards in a row are as tall as the tallest answer in that row. A8·4's rule arrives with the grid: rows are equal within themselves and never across the grid, and the slack falls at the foot of the short cards.

CARDS GROUND · THE CARD TAKES THE PAGE'S GROUND AND KEEPS ITS HAIRLINE
Card padding is not a control — it follows the column count, 28 at three and 32 at two, which is A8·4's rule and the reason this design has a Columns control and no padding control of its own. Cards Ground puts the page's ground inside the hairline, for a section sitting on a surface-coloured band above or below it. The hairline is then a 1.3:1 boundary rather than information, which is stated rather than corrected: the card's edge is where the answer stops, and nothing in it needs to be found by contrast. Hairline columns is not offered. A8·4 refused it in a two-row grid for the reason that holds here too — a vertical rule that stops between two rows is a table's rule without a table's header — and 15 Ledger is A9's honest version of that idea.

Nothing is truncated, clamped, faded, given a “read more” or moved to another row; the two short cards carry the difference as white space under their answers. This is the width where the design's cost disappears: nothing is stretched to match a neighbour it no longer has. The second frame is Cards Ground at the same width — a hairline box on the page's own ground, which on a phone reads as a divided list rather than as cards, and the panel says so.

ACCESSIBILITY · A GRID THAT IS STILL ONE LIST One <ul> of six <li>, never two lists of three, with the grid on the list and each card an item. One <h2>, an <h3> per question, its answer beneath. No button, no role="region", no marker — 3 Open List's structure in a grid. The equal-height stretch is invisible to assistive technology, and so is the row it belongs to: a screen reader hears six items in authored order and nothing about which three shared a height. The card is not a link and has no hover state. A9's rule and A8's before it: a card whose only destination is the answer it already shows is a link to nowhere. The only focusable things here are links inside answers and the optional section link. The anchor is on the <h3> with tabindex="-1", as in 3 Open List, so a deep link scrolls the card into view with 96 px above it and puts focus on the question. Nothing opens and nothing is highlighted. Contrast: question and answer 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark on surface, 15.4:1 / 14.7:1 at Cards Ground, card hairline 1.3:1 as a boundary, link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. In forced colours the card keeps its hairline and loses its fill, which makes Surface and Ground the same drawing — stated, not corrected. Print: one column, every card open, hairlines kept, because a printed page is not 1,296 px wide.
