Founder

One person beside one photograph, with 900 characters to say who they are. §8.3's team of one, and the design every grid in A12 hands off to when the list has a single person in it — which makes it the second-most-received design in the category, after 1 Grid.

The arrangement is A8·8's, carried forward verbatim: three named divisions on a 32 px gutter, the halves vertically centred against each other, three named crops, and no focal-point control. What is new here is what the text half holds — a name, a role and a letter, rather than a quotation.

Marguerite Okonjo Editor and founder

Orbit Weekly started as a Thursday email to nine people. It is now read by forty-one thousand, and it is still written the same way: I read everything before it goes out.

I spent eleven years on the science desk at The Ledger and left because the best stories kept arriving on Fridays, after the week's pages had closed. The letter is what I wanted to read on a Thursday morning and could not find.

We have no offices. The letter is put together in Lagos, Lisbon and Malmö, and the Tuesday planning call is the only meeting.

Roles as of March. Freelance contributors are credited on their own posts.

632 · 32 · 632 = 1,296, and the two halves are vertically centred against each other — the only centred pairing in A12, and A8·8's reason holds: a 790 px photograph beside a 420 px column of text would otherwise hang the words off the top of the picture. The text half is a stack of four blocks 24 apart: the name and role, the letter, then the note and the link.
The letter is body, not bio — 900 characters at 17/1.7, up to three paragraphs, on the half's own 632 measure rather than on 620. With no body authored the design falls back to the person's bio, which is 240 characters and reads as a caption at this size; with both authored, body wins and the editor says so. With neither, the design is a portrait, a name and a role — and that is a valid section, not a hand-off. ⚑ Mine: the fallback order, the 24 px stack, and the 17 px letter on a 632 measure.

ACCESSIBILITY · ONE PERSON, NO LIST There is no <ul> in this design — the one design in A12 without a list, because a list of one is a list a reader is told to expect more of. The person is a <div> holding an <img alt="">, a name <p>, a role <p> and the letter's paragraphs. The name is not an <h2> or <h3> at 27 px — size is not level, and at Head None the section simply has no heading. ⚑ A11·15's rule, and A8·10's before it. The photograph is alt="" at 632 × 790. It is the largest decorative image in the library and the reason is unchanged: the name is drawn beside it. Not a <figure>, because the letter is not a caption for the portrait. Reading order is name, role, letter, note, link at both Image side values, with the photograph first in the DOM at Image side Left and second at Right — a source reorder, no order property, and the same sequence at every width once the halves stack. Contrast: name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, letter 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 — text, not muted, as in 4 Story and Team; role and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1; link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. One tab stop when the name is not linked, two when it is — and a linked name here is the ordinary case, since the person almost always has an author page. Nothing moves. No reveal on the portrait, no parallax, no fade. At 400% zoom the halves stack in source order, the crop is 3:2, and the letter reflows on the column.
