Side Rail

The header turns ninety degrees: a fixed 240 px column at the left, logo at the top, nav stacked down it, actions pinned to its foot. The page keeps its full height and there is no band across the top. It changes the page's layout rather than sitting on top of it, and it exists only at 1200 and wider: at 1199 it becomes Rail.

The panel opens to the right of its row, top-aligned to it, overlapping the page by 8 px so the two read as joined. It is the library’s dropdown, opening sideways: same 248 px width, same rows, same two levels. Because it sits over the reading column, the page content beneath it is inset in this frame only; on a real page the text stays where it is and the panel covers it. It closes on scroll, since a fixed panel over scrolling text loses its anchor.

Below 1200 the rail becomes A1·1 Rail. That is a design change rather than a narrowing, which is what the “Becomes a top bar” control sets. ACCESSIBILITY · a header that is not at the top
1Source order is still header first. The rail comes before the main content in the DOM regardless of which side it is drawn on, so “Rail side: Right” is a visual swap only — a right-hand rail that also came second in the source would strand keyboard users at the end of the page.
2Skip link matters more here. Seven nav rows, a search field and two actions sit before the content, so the skip link is mandatory and lands on the reading column, not on the page top.
3The side panel needs a return path. It opens to the right of its row and can sit over the reading column, so Escape closes it and returns focus to the row; it also closes on scroll, because a fixed panel over scrolling text loses its anchor.
4The active row states itself twice. aria-current="page" plus the accent bar, since a 2 px bar on its own is a colour-only signal.
5Zoom. At 200 % a 1440 viewport is effectively 720, so the rail has already become Rail and cannot crowd the text. That is why the threshold is 1200 rather than lower.
SPEC · A1·11 SIDE RAIL · full text in “A1 Headers — Spec.md”
Content fieldsShared A1 list, no additions. Its cap is the category’s highest: 7 nav items shown, items 8+ fold to a “More” row, because a fixed column has vertical room a bar does not.
DataGhost primary nav as rows; children in a 248 px panel opening to the side, two levels. Empty nav → the rail holds logo, search and actions and keeps its width, since the page layout depends on it. An empty nav does not collapse it to Rail.
Responsive≥ 1200 the rail, 240 px at Comfortable (200 Narrow / 280 Wide). 1199 and below the design becomes A1·1 Rail — inline items, underline instead of the leading bar, panel opens downward, actions rejoin the bar, cap folds 7 → 4. ≤ 767 Rail’s drawer. “Becomes a top bar: Early” moves the threshold to 1400, “Late” to 1024.
Empty stateNo CTA → the foot holds the ghost action alone, still pinned. No logo image → wordmark, wrapping to two lines at 240 px if it must; a two-line wordmark is comfortable at this width.
FlaggedThe 1200 threshold, the 7-item cap, the 240/200/280 widths, and the decision that this design becomes another design below its threshold rather than inventing a narrow-rail variant. The last of those matters most: it lets library designs hand off to one another. Confirm before A2.
