Directory

Names and roles in hairline rows, in one, two or three columns. It is the one design in A12 that draws no photograph at any setting — photo is kept, twelve are authored, and none are drawn. The panel says so and names 1 Grid.

This is the design for a masthead where the roles are the content: a contributor list, a “who does what”, a team of twenty-four where photographs exist for six of them. Settlement 1 says a missing photograph is answered by the initials block; this design is the other answer — a section that never asks for the file, and therefore never has a hole where one is missing.

636 · 24 · 636 = 1,296, and twelve people fill the first column before they start the second — six and six, read down and then across, which is the order a directory is read in and the order the DOM is in. A row is 14 px of padding above and below a 17 px name, 53 px including its hairline, and the rows of both columns share a height, so the hairlines line up across the gutter.
The name is the same 17/600 as every other design in A12 and the role is the same 14 px muted, right-aligned to its column's edge. Henry Osgood's row is the name and the rest of the row empty — no dash, no “—”, no “Team member”. Priya Raghunathan's 42-character role fits a 636 column on one line; the width where it stops fitting is drawn two panels down. ⚑ Mine: the column-major fill, the 53 px row, the shared row heights, right-aligning the role, and drawing no photograph at any setting.

A 17 px name and a 14 px role at opposite ends of a 350 px row leave 40 px between them and read as two lists. This is A12's one departure from the category's “two across on a phone” floor, and the reason is that this design has no photograph: two columns of names is a table nobody can scan. ⚑ Mine.
State two is the same twelve people with no head and no rules — the arrangement for a directory under a hero that already said what the section is. With no title there is no <h2> and the section takes no accessible name.

ACCESSIBILITY · A LIST THAT LOOKS LIKE A TABLE AND IS NOT ONE <section aria-labelledby> named by the title, an <h2>, then one <ul> of up to twenty-four <li>, each holding a name and, where authored, a role. It is not a <table>, and it is not marked up as columns: there are no column headers, and nothing in column two relates to the row beside it. The two columns are a CSS grid flowing down and then across, so the reading order and the DOM order are the authored order at every value. ⚑ Mine. The role is a <span> inside the person's <li>, not a definition list: a reader hears “Marguerite Okonjo, Editor and founder” as one item, at both Role positions. Role Beside the name and Role Under the name make no difference to what is announced, which is the point of the shared component's fixed order. Tab stops: one per linked name, and the section link. Four and one on the primary frame; none at all is ordinary. The hairline is a border-bottom on the row and is never a target; focus lands on the name's box, which is 17 px tall and as wide as the name. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; role 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 at 14 px; hairline 1.3:1 against the ground, which is a non-text divider and is why the design offers Rule None rather than a heavier rule. Nothing moves at any setting, and at 400% zoom the ledger is one column with the role under the name. Twelve photographs are authored and none are announced, so a reader who has met this team on another page hears the same twelve names in the same order here. Print is the light mode as drawn, and this is the design that prints closest to what a masthead page has always looked like.
