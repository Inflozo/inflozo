A10 STATS AND NUMBERS · DESIGN 5 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONLY DESIGN WITH TWO RANKS OF VALUE

Lead Stat

The first stat at Display 68, the rest at Row 40 beside it or beneath it. The only design in A10 where two values are set at different sizes, and therefore the only one that makes a claim about which number matters — a claim the site makes by putting that stat first in the repeater.

Everywhere else in the category, rank is refused: the accent is never spent on a value, no stat gets a plane or a border of its own, and 1 Row sets three numbers at one size on one baseline precisely so that none of them wins. Here rank is the whole design, and it is carried by size alone — no colour, no fill, no rule, no icon, no word like “headline”.

A LEAD WITH NO LABEL BESIDE A 60-CHARACTER SUPPORTING LABEL · THE COLUMNS DO NOT REBALANCE
A lead with no label is a 68 px numeral in a 632 column and nothing under it. The stack beside it keeps its own height; neither column grows to match the other and the section's height is the taller one's. This is the design where an empty right-hand column is most visible, and it is still not filled. A 60-character label on a supporting stat is three lines in a 632 column and pushes the stats below it down. The rule that follows it moves with them; nothing is clamped and no stat is dropped. Two hand-offs out. One stat → 4 Single, because a lead with nothing to lead is one number. Two stats → 1 Row, because a lead and one supporting stat is a comparison, and two numbers at different sizes read as a mistake rather than a rank. Five or six → the first four are drawn and the panel names 3 Grid.

A reader hears four stats in authored order and nothing tells them one is bigger. That is the honest report: the rank is a typographic emphasis the site made by ordering the repeater, and order is what a screen reader already conveys. ⚑ Flagged: no <strong>, no “most importantly”, no aria-label announcing rank.
