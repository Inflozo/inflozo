Big Type

The ask set large enough to be the section, flush left across the whole content width, with a rule under it and the actions beneath. It is A4·6's design at banner scale and the only place A6 goes above its own title ladder — which is why it is one design rather than a control on all fifteen.

The sub and the actions share the line under the rule — sub at the left on 620, actions at the right — which is the one thing this design adds to A4·6. A hero can afford to put its actions under its sub because there is nothing below a hero but the page; a banner has a footer 96 px away, and a third stacked row would make the section 480 px tall to say one sentence. Sub and actions on one line makes it 380. The rule is the design's second element and not an ornament: at 76 px the title has no measure to speak of, so the hairline is what gives the section a bottom edge to the type and a top edge to everything else. It runs the full content width at every value.
No eyebrow field is offered — A4·6's refusal, carried without change: a 13 px tracked line above a 76 px one is a label on a poster. The title's line height is 1.02 and its tracking −0.035em, both tighter than A6's ladder, because a display size needs both and 40 px does not. Flagged as mine, along with the shared line and its 64 px gutter.

NOT OFFERED · FILL WIDTH A4·6's third value measures the headline against its column and clamps it between 64 and 160 px. It is not offered here. A hero owns the top of a page and can be as tall as its own type; a banner sits between two sections, and a 160 px line in the middle of a page is louder than the page's own hero — which would make the banner the loudest thing on a page it does not lead. Two named values, both of them numbers a site can reason about.

The ugly test is where this design earns its two values. A 90-character title at Huge 96 is four lines and 420 px of type before the rule; the same title at Display 76 is three lines and 250 px. The editor advises Huge only under 40 characters and says so at the value rather than switching the site's choice, which is the library's rule for every advisory: name the consequence, leave the decision.

RULE HIDE · the gap under the title goes 40 → 28, since the rule was doing the separating

At Sub Hide the actions stay at the right and the left of the line stays empty rather than the actions moving to the left margin — the line's right end is where the actions live in this design, and a site that hides its sub should not find its buttons somewhere new. At Rule Hide the gap under the title tightens 40 → 28, A5·6's logic: with no hairline the space is doing all the separating and less of it reads as more. The two controls are independent and all four combinations are legitimate; the floor is a title and a primary action, which is 220 px of section and the shortest this design gets.

1 · 76 px is a size, not a level. The title is the same <h2> naming the same <section aria-labelledby> as every other design in A6, and a page running this banner and a 40 px one has two h2s of equal standing. Nothing about the scale reaches the document outline. 2 · The rule is a border-top on the block that follows it, never an <hr> — 2 Flush Left's answer, carried. It separates a title from its own sub, which is not a thematic break in any sense a screen reader should announce. 3 · The DOM order is A6's shared order and the visual order is not. Sub then actions in the source; actions to the right of the sub on screen. This is the one design where the two differ, they differ by one element, and both readings say the same thing — the offer's terms, then the offer. Two tab stops, primary first. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · sub 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · button 4.6:1 / 8.1:1 · rule 1.2:1 / 1.3:1, decorative. Zoom: at 200% text the title reflows and the sub and actions stack — the same arrangement the tablet uses, so nothing new is specified. Reflow: at 320 px the title is 40 px and wraps to four lines, which is the design working as intended and the reason Fill width is not offered. Forced colours keep the rule.
