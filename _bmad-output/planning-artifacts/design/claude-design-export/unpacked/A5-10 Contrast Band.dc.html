Contrast Band

The whole set on the inverted ground. Three across, as 1 Three Up, and the ground is the design rather than a control — which is why every other design's Contrast value is a band and this one is a section. It settles the three things a band changes: what an icon container is when there is no surface to tint, what a hairline is worth at 28%, and which action is allowed on it.

1 Three Up's grid, unchanged, on contrast. Three things change and they are the design. Padding is 44 · 64 · 88 rather than 64 · 96 · 132 — one step tighter, A4·9's values carried verbatim, because a dark band reads heavier than the page at the same measurement. The icon container is a 10% lift of the carried colour rather than a surface tint: there is no surface on a band, and the packs do not define one. The action is a solid text-coloured fill carrying the band's own colour — A4·9's inverted primary, also carried verbatim. The page's ground is drawn 40 px above and below so the band's edges are visible; on a real page those are the neighbouring sections. Flagged as mine: the 10% lift, and reusing A4·9's padding and action rather than inventing new ones.

The accent is refused twice on this design and the ratio is shown both times. An accent fill carrying the band's colour is 3.2:1 in Paper — A4·9's measurement, unchanged — so the action is the surface fill and the accent value is disabled with its number, per §7·4. Icon in accent is not offered either, and for a different reason: six accent squares on an inverted band is the loudest object in the library, and §2 allows the accent twice. The outline action is drawn here and not used: this design carries one action, and the outline is A4·9's secondary, kept in the frame so the pair is documented in one place.

The focus ring is the carried colour on this design, not the accent — an accent ring at 3.2:1 against the band would be the one place in the library a focus indicator failed, and the carried colour is 12.8:1. It is the same substitution A1·1's drawer makes on its own dark ground. The surface fill's hover darkens the fill 3% rather than lightening it, since it is already the brightest object on the band. Whole item is not offered: the items have no edge, and a 10% lift behind a whole item on a band is a fourth ground value the packs do not define.

NO PICTURES ON THIS DESIGN
Media offers Icon, Icon in a lift and None. There is no Picture value. A photograph on an inverted band needs a scrim to hold text near it and a border to separate it from the ground, and A5 has neither: the scrims belong to A4's heroes, where words sit over pictures. A site that wants six photographs and a dark ground has 8 Media Top on the page's own ground, which is what the editor names. This is the same boundary 8 states from its side, where its Ground control has no Contrast value. Flagged as mine: refusing Picture here, and the pair of statements that make 8 and 10 agree.

The band is not a landmark, a region or a theme boundary — it is a background colour on a section that already has a name. 2 · The container's 10% lift is 1.2:1 against the band and carries no meaning; the glyph inside it is measured against the band rather than the lift, at 12.8:1. Icon aria-hidden, as everywhere. 3 · The refused accent, in full. An accent fill carrying the band's colour is 3.2:1 in Paper — under 4.5:1 for a 14 px label — so the value is disabled with its ratio shown rather than allowed, per §7·4. The surface fill is 12.8:1 the other way round. A pack whose accent passed on its own contrast would still not get the value: a control whose availability varied by pack would make the same design different in twelve places, which §3·1 refuses. Focus rings are the carried colour, not the accent, on every interactive thing in the band — 12.8:1 against 3.2:1. It is the one design in A5 where the ring's colour changes. Contrast, light band · title 12.8:1 · body 7.4:1 · eyebrow 7.4:1 · glyph 12.8:1 · action label 12.8:1 · hairline 3.0:1 at 28%, decorative. Dark band (which is light) · title 13.9:1 · body 6.9:1 · glyph 13.9:1. Forced colours.
