Grid

Three to twelve people in equal cells, three to five across, each a circle photograph above a name and a role. The design a site gets when it types “team”, the one A12-0's person card was drawn for, and the design ten others hand off to.

All four settlements are on the primary frame at once: eleven photographs and one initials block in identical boxes, a 42-character role wrapping to two lines, a person with neither role nor bio drawn as a name alone, and four names of the twelve carrying links that nothing at rest distinguishes from the other eight. It draws no bio at any setting — a paragraph in a 306 cell is 2 Cards, and a paragraph beside the photograph is 3 Rows.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWELVE PEOPLE, FOUR TAB STOPS, ONE HEADING <section aria-labelledby> named by the title, an <h2>; the team a <ul> of twelve <li>; each person a <p> for the name and a <p> for the role. A name is never an <h3> — twelve headings under one <h2> would put the whole masthead into the page's outline, and a name is not a section of the document. Refused: <dl>, <figure>, a table, and role="list" on a <div>. ⚑ Mine. The photograph is <img alt=""> and the initials block is aria-hidden — A8's rule for the avatar, applied to a photograph, and for the same reason: the name is drawn beside it. So a screen reader hears “Rosa Ferreira, Illustrator” whether the photograph exists or not, and the missing photograph is not announced at all. A stated cost: a reader cannot tell that one person has no picture, and the alternative is announcing “RF” as text. ⚑ Mine. Tab stops: one per linked name, and the section link. Four and one on the primary frame; one, or none, is the ordinary case, and with no links authored the section has no tab stop at all. A linked name is <a> around the name text, taking its accessible name from the name — “Daniel Reith, link” is the whole announcement. No “opens in a new tab”, no visually hidden “read about”, no aria-label repeating the role. Reading order is photograph, name, role, and it is the DOM order in all fifteen designs, so switching designs never changes what a reader hears. At Cells Centred the order does not change — centring is align-items and nothing else, with no order property anywhere in A12. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, role and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, link underline and focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. The initials sit on the hover surface at 5.1:1 light and 5.4:1 dark — checked against that fill rather than the ground, because that is what they are on. A photograph takes no ratio: it is an upload and the theme cannot measure it. Reduced motion changes nothing, because nothing moves. The only transition is the 160 ms underline on a linked name, instant under reduced motion. At 400% zoom the grid is two across, roles wrap further, nothing overlaps and nothing scrolls sideways.
