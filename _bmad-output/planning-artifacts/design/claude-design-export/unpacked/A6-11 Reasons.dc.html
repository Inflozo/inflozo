Reasons

The ask with up to three short lines saying what comes with it. It is the banner for the moment a reader wants one more fact before acting, and it is where A6 borrows A5·11's mark — with the accent moved off the mark and onto the button, because unlike a checklist this section has somewhere to click.

The reasons are A5·11's entry verbatim — 17 px in the body font at 500, still real text, a 16 px mark with a 14 px gap and a 3 px optical lift — with one change: the mark is text-muted at all three values. A5·11 spends its accent once per entry on the argument that the mark is the only thing making eight lines a list; this design has an accent button 40 px away, and three accent ticks beside it is the accent spent four times in one section. The mark is muted, the accent stays where the click is, and the departure is stated on both designs.
Three reasons, and three is a cap rather than a guide. A fourth line makes the right column taller than the text block, at which the section is A5·11 with a button under it and the editor names it. The column is 424 px on a 96 px gutter — wider than the entries need, so a 40-character reason never wraps, which is the reason for the 40-character cap on the field.

Under the actions is a row of three on one line, 24 px under a hairline, at 15 px rather than 17 — the reasons are further from the ask and doing less work, so they take less weight. This is the value that makes the design a centred banner rather than a split one, and it pairs with a centred head; Beside the text is left-aligned at both, since a centred title next to a left-aligned list has two axes. Alignment is therefore not a control on this design: the position decides it. At Under the actions the reasons are shortened by the site, not by the design — the field's 40-character cap holds and the editor advises 20 for this value, because three 40-character lines on one row is 1,100 px.

Drawn at Mark Rule, which in dark is a 13 px text-muted line at 6.1:1 — the quietest mark in the library and still not a hairline: the rule is muted text, not border, so it reads at the same strength as the words it is beside rather than disappearing into the ground. That distinction is why Rule is a mark rather than a divider. The reasons are text at 12.6:1 in both modes and the sub is muted, which is the design's one deliberate inversion of the usual weights: the reasons are more legible than the sentence introducing them, because they are the facts a reader came for.

1 · The reasons are a <ul> of <li> — a count of things, so a screen reader should say how many, which is A5's rule for every set in the library. They are not headings at any position: 17 px at 500 weight is emphasis, and the section already has its one heading. 2 · The mark is aria-hidden at all three values and nothing here is a checkbox — no role, no aria-checked, no input, nothing a reader could think they had to tick. A5·11's rule, carried word for word. 3 · The list comes after the actions in the DOM at both positions, including Beside the text where it is visually to the right of them: the reasons describe the offer the button makes, and a reader who acts on the first thing they hear should hear the offer first. Two tab stops, and no reason is ever a link — A5·11's refusal, carried, because three linked lines beside a button is four asks. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · reasons 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · sub and note 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · mark 5.4:1 / 6.1:1, decorative · button 4.6:1 / 8.1:1. The reasons read stronger than the sub, deliberately. Forced colours keep the marks, since Check, Dot and Rule are all drawn from borders and backgrounds of currentColor rather than as glyphs in a font.
