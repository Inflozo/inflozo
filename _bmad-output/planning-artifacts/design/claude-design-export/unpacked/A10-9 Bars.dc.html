Bars

A proportional bar under each value, on a track the full width of the row. The one design in A10 that draws anything other than type, and the only one with a precondition: every drawn stat must have a share, and the stats must be parts of one whole. Four unrelated numbers with bars under them is a chart of nothing.

It is also where A10 stops. A sparkline, a dial, a donut, a pie, an axis, a legend or a second series are all refused — those are charts, with their own conventions and their own accessibility problem, and a section library is not a charting library. A bar is the one exception because its whole grammar is “this much of that”, which is a thing a label can say in words as well.

The annual letter

The row takes the full 1,296 and the bar takes the full row — the one design in A10 that uses the whole content width for a single element, because a bar's length is a measurement and a longer baseline reads more precisely. Label at the left, share and value right-aligned on one baseline, bar 14 px below them.
The track is border and the fill is text, and the track is not optional. A bar with no track is a line whose 11% cannot be judged against 100%, which is the whole reason to draw it. The fill is not the accent: four accent bars is a section in one colour, and A10 spends the accent on links only. Square ends at every pack radius — the one place in A10 where the radius token is deliberately not applied, because an 11% bar with 8 px of radius on both ends is a lozenge whose length stops being readable. ⚑ Mine: the full-width bar, the track's colour, the fill in text and the square ends.

Bar: Thin 4 · Medium 8 · Thick 16, the same at every width — a bar is a measurement, not type, and scaling it with the viewport would change what 44% looks like on a phone. Thick 16 is capped there because 16 is the label's own cap height, and a bar taller than the text beside it reads as a block rather than a line. ⚑ Mine. Share figure Shown draws the percentage at 15 px muted, to the left of the value on the same baseline — muted because it is the same information the bar already gives, and the value is the figure the site wants read. Hidden removes the number and keeps the bar, for a set where the proportion is the point and the arithmetic is not. The share is a number 0–100 and the bar is that percentage of the track. The theme does not check that the set sums to 100. A set summing to 130 draws four honestly long bars and looks wrong, which is the site's error to see and correct; a theme that normalised the shares to 100 would be publishing figures nobody typed. A share above 100 is clamped to the track and the editor says so. ⚑ Mine.

A SHARE OF 100 FILLS THE TRACK · A SHARE OF 3 IS A 39 PX MARK · A ROW WITH NO LABEL KEEPS ITS BAR
A share of 3 draws a 39 px mark on a 1,296 track, and it is not given a minimum width. A floor of, say, 8 px would make 0.6% and 3% the same length, which is a chart lying about its smallest value. A share of 0 draws no fill at all — an empty track, which is the honest drawing of nothing. ⚑ Mine. A row with no label keeps its bar and its value, right-aligned as usual, and the left of the row is empty. Unlike 8 Ledger, the value does not move: the bar under it already occupies the row's full width, so there is no hole for the value to fall into. The precondition, stated in the panel: every drawn stat needs a share. If one of the four has none, the section draws 1 Row and the panel reads “Elsewhere has no share. Drawing 1 Row.” A bar drawn for three of four rows is a table with a missing cell, and a share invented from the values' own sum would be the theme publishing a figure. Two or fewer stats → 1 Row, because two bars is a comparison and a comparison of two needs no bars. One → 4 Single.

The row holds at ≤ 767 and the bar takes the 350 px column. Label and value stay on one baseline; value 30; row padding 16 · 24 · 32. The bar's height is unchanged on a phone at all three values — a proportion drawn at 4 px on a desktop and 4 px on a phone is the same claim, and a bar that grew on a small screen would read as a heavier emphasis than the site chose. At Thin 4 on a 350 track, 14% is a 49 px mark, which is the smallest readable thing in A10 and the reason Thin is not the default.

ACCESSIBILITY · A GRAPHIC THAT ANNOUNCES NOTHING, AND WHY THAT IS RIGHT The bar is two nested <span>s with aria-hidden="true" — not a <progress>, not role="meter", not role="img" with a label, and not an SVG. A meter announces a value that is already in the text beside it, and <progress> claims something is loading. The share is the drawing's only content and it is in the DOM as text at Share figure Shown — so the bar is decoration for information that is already written. ⚑ Mine. At Share figure Hidden the percentage leaves the page, and a screen-reader user gets the value and the label only. That is a real loss and the panel states it: “At Hidden the proportion is drawn and not written.” A visually hidden percentage was considered and refused — text that exists for one reader and not another is two versions of the section, and the site can turn the figure on. In forced colours the fill takes the system's text colour and the track its background, which keeps the two distinguishable; the fill also takes a 1 px border in forced colours, because a fill and a track that both resolve to the same system colour would erase the bar. That border is drawn nowhere else. ⚑ Mine. Contrast: fill against track 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark, value 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, label and share 5.6:1 / 6.0:1. No animation: a bar that grows from zero on scroll is the count-up in another form and is refused for the same four reasons. One tab stop at most. Print keeps the bars as an outlined track with a solid fill, so a printer that drops backgrounds still shows the proportion.
