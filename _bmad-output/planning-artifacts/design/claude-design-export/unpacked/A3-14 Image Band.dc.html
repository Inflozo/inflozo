One full-bleed photograph closing the page, with the site's name over it and the links and legal line in a plain row beneath. The only footer in A3 with imagery as its subject rather than its content, and therefore the design that has to settle the scrim: how text sits over a picture nobody has chosen yet.

The band is full-bleed at a 6.5:1 crop, the text sits on the bottom-left corner against the page's own 72 px margin, and the scrim is a single bottom-up gradient from transparent at 30% to 55% of the contrast token — warm, never black, and never a flat overlay across the whole picture. The wordmark is 34 px Georgia in the pack's lightest surface: large enough to hold against a busy photograph, small enough not to compete with A3·7. The rows below are ordinary footer furniture on the page's own ground.

With no image the band becomes a flat contrast panel at the same height, no scrim, carried text at the values A3·5 sets — so the composition holds and nothing looks broken. The rows below the band behave exactly as A3·6's link row. Nothing in the band is interactive: the wordmark over a photograph is not a link, for the same reason A3·7's big name is not — a large silent target over an image invites a click that goes nowhere useful.

Dark needs a weaker scrim, not a stronger one: the picture is already darker than the page, so the gradient drops to 45% and starts at 40%, and its colour is the dark pack's background rather than its contrast. Text goes to the pack's text token instead of pure white, which is the same anti-glare step A3·11 makes in its prose. Photographs are never brightened or dimmed by the theme — only the scrim moves.

1 · The photograph is decorative — alt="" — because the words over it already say what it is there for. The editor offers an alt field for the case where the image carries information, and says plainly that a footer photograph usually does not. 2 · The scrim is a sibling element, aria-hidden, never a background-blend on the image, so it survives forced-colours mode by disappearing and leaving the text on a solid ground. 3 · Wordmark and tagline are plain text, not headings and not links; the home link lives in the row below as its first item when the theme adds one. 4 · The link row is a labelled <nav> as A3·6, on the page's own ground where contrast is measurable. Text over an unknown image · the scrim's floor guarantees 4.5:1 for the wordmark and tagline against the darkest reasonable photograph, not the average one; against a bright sky the same scrim gives about 7:1. Where a site's own image defeats it, the editor's contrast check flags the band and offers Overlay: None. Flagged as mine — this is a floor, not a guarantee, and it is the one place in A3 where contrast depends on content nobody has seen yet. Loading · explicit dimensions and loading="lazy"; the band reserves its height before the image arrives, so the page never shifts. No parallax, no motion of any kind.

In dark it is the background token, transparent to 45%, stop at 40%. It is removed entirely when nothing is over the image. Never a flat overlay, never black.
