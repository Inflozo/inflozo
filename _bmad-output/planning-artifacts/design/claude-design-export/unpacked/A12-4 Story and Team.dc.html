Story and Team

The about paragraphs, a photograph of the group, and the team beneath them — the one design in A12 where the two halves of the category's name are one section. It is the only design that reads body, image and imageAlt together, and the only place in the whole category where an image carries real alt text.

The reason that alt text exists here and nowhere else is the whole argument of settlement 1 in reverse: a photograph of a named person is decorative, because the name is already drawn beside it. A photograph of a room is not — nothing else on the frame says what is in it.

Four blocks in one order: head, story, photograph, team. Head to story 48, story to photograph 48, photograph to team 48 — one gap, used three times, because four blocks separated by four different distances is four decisions a reader has to make. The story is on a 620 measure at 17/1.7 in text, not muted: it is the section's reading matter, and A11·14's lede set the same precedent.
The team is drawn beside its photographs rather than under them — a 56 px circle, 16 px, then the name and role — which is the one arrangement of the person card that no other design in A12 uses. It is here because the team is the section's third element rather than its subject: a column of 72 px faces under a 432 px band photograph competes with it. ⚑ Mine: the repeated 48, the Band 3:1 ratio, the side-by-side card, and Small 56 as this design's only default.

The story is text at 17/1.7 on a 620 measure, and the sub is not drawn at all in this design — a 17 px muted line between the title and a 17 px black paragraph is a caption for a paragraph. sub is kept and the panel says so; 5 Split Head is named as the design that draws both. ⚑ Mine. ACCESSIBILITY · THE ONE IMAGE IN A12 WITH ALT TEXT The group photograph takes real alt text from imageAlt, and it is the only image in the category that does. Every person's photograph is alt="" because the name is drawn beside it; nothing on the frame says what is in this one. With no imageAlt authored it falls back to alt="" rather than to a generated string — a filename or “Team photo” is worse than silence. The editor asks for it and does not require it. ⚑ Mine. Four blocks, one <h2>, no subheadings. The story is two or three <p>s in a <div>, the photograph a bare <img> — not a <figure>, because there is no caption to attach — and the team the same <ul> of <li> as 1 Grid. No “About us” or “The team” heading is generated to divide them. Reading order is head, story, photograph, team at both Story values — beside the photograph is a flex row with the story first in the DOM, so the order at 1440 and at 390 is the same sequence. No order property. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, story 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 — text, not muted, the highest-contrast paragraph in A12; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; role and note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1. The photograph takes no ratio, and nothing is set over it, so no scrim ratio exists to check. Nothing moves at any setting. No parallax on the band, no reveal on the story, no lazy fade — the photograph is a normal <img> with loading="lazy" and no transition. At 400% the story is one column and the band keeps its ratio.
