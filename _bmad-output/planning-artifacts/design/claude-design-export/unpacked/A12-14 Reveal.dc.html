1 Grid, where each person's bio opens on a button in their own card. It exists for the team that has twelve bios and no room for twelve bios: a masthead where the roles are the reading and a paragraph is there for the reader who wants one.

The glyph is a plus that becomes a minus, and it does not rotate. A chevron would have to point somewhere, and in a grid there is no shared direction for a card to open in; a plus and a minus say added and removed. 160 ms ease-out on the height, and the glyph swaps at the midpoint. ⚑ Mine. Open takes the pack's hover surface behind the button and the accent on the glyph — #F4F0E8 and #D96C3F in Paper light. That is the whole of this design's accent spend, and it is spent even when nothing else on the frame is authored, which makes 14 Reveal and 13 Rail the two designs in A12 that cannot be drawn without the accent. Cards open independently and any number can be open at once. One-at-a-time and an accordion group are both refused. The row's height follows its tallest open card and the closed cards stay at the top of their cells, which is settlement 2's rule for a short card applied to a card that has just grown. Rows below move down; nothing overlays and nothing is a popover. ⚑ Mine. The bio is the same 240 characters every other design draws, at 15/1.6 in text-muted, and it is not clamped when open: four lines in a 306 cell, five in a 240. Under reduced motion the height transition is dropped and the bio appears at once; the button, the states and the thresholds are unchanged.

A person's panel opens under their own row, not at the foot of the section, so the paragraph and the face are on the same screen. Closing the last open bio in a row removes the panel and its hairline together. Refused: a modal, a popover, a hover-to-open, a card-wide target, a “read more” link to another page, and a bio that scrolls inside a fixed box. Hover-to-open is refused twice over: a bio that appears while a pointer crosses a grid is unreadable, and there is no hover on a touch screen. ⚑ Mine.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWELVE DISCLOSURES, NO GENERATED STRINGS Each disclosure is a <button aria-expanded> with aria-controls on the bio, and it takes its accessible name from the person's name with aria-labelledby. A reader hears “Marguerite Okonjo, button, collapsed”. No label is generated — not “Read more”, not “About Marguerite”, not “Expand” — which is what keeps 13 Rail's “Team, scrollable” the only generated string in the category. ⚑ Mine. The glyph is aria-hidden and carries no text of its own, so the plus and the minus are decoration over aria-expanded. The bio is in the DOM at both states and is hidden with hidden rather than moved off-screen, so a find-in-page does not land on invisible text. Reading order is photograph, name, role, button, bio — the person card's fixed order with the button between the role and the bio, at both Bio opens values. At Under the row the panel follows its row in the DOM, so what a reader hears is what a reader sees. Tab stops: one per bio button, one per linked name, and the section link — seven, four and one on the primary frame, in cell order. A person with no bio has no button and no panel, so the tab order skips them rather than stopping on a control that does nothing. Nothing traps focus, nothing moves focus on open, and closing a card leaves focus on the button that closed it. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; role and bio 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; closed glyph 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; open glyph on the hover surface 3.9:1 in light and 4.7:1 in dark, which is the accent re-checked per mode; focus ring 3.6:1. Print draws every bio open, at both Bio opens values, because a printed page has no button — one of two print exceptions in A12.
