Contrast Band

1 Row inverted onto the contrast token. Switching between this design and 1 Row changes nothing but the colours — the same 416 division, the same baseline, the same 48 px head gap, the same 32 px source gap — which is the tokenisation claim at its hardest and the fourth category to make it.

The reason a stats section wants a band is that numbers at 52 px on an inverted ground are the loudest thing a page can do with type alone. The reason it is one design and not a control on all fifteen is that a band changes every colour in the section, and a section that can be inverted at will is a section whose contrast has to be re-checked at every value of every other control.

ACCESSIBILITY · A BACKGROUND, NOT AN ELEMENT 1 Row's structure unchanged: <section aria-labelledby>, an <h2>, a <ul> of <li>, one <p> per stat. The band is a background colour on the section, not an element, not a region and not a landmark — nothing about it is announced, because a change of colour is not a change of context. Contrast, all five derived values: value and title 15.8:1 light band / 14.7:1 light-in-dark, muted 8.7:1 / 6.0:1, hairline 2.4:1 (decorative), link text 15.8:1 / 14.7:1, focus ring 15.8:1 / 14.7:1 — the strongest ring in A10. The accent is disabled at 2.3:1 with the ratio shown in the sidebar, which is §7.4's rule: a value that would fail is disabled and named, never silently allowed. Forced colours drops the band and keeps every hairline, at which point this design and 1 Row are the same drawing — a user in forced colours cannot tell which of the two the site chose, and that is correct, because the choice was a colour. Print drops the band and prints the values in text on white with the hairlines kept. One tab stop at most — the link. No behaviour, no hover on a stat, no motion, and no count-up, on a band least of all: a number animating in carried colour on an inverted ground is the single loudest thing this library could do and it is still refused.
