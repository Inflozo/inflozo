Split Head

The head in a 416 px column at the left, two or four stats in an 824 px block at the right, on A8·6's 56 px gutter. The design for a section whose sub is doing real work — a 178-character sentence beside the numbers instead of above them, which is where a reader looks for it once the numbers are the first thing on the line.

Inherited unchanged from A8 and A9: the 416 / 56 / 824 division, both columns top-aligned, the head never sticky and never vertically centred, and the split leaving at 1080. What A10 adds is that the stats block is a 2 × 2 rather than a list, which makes this the only design where four stats appear in a column narrower than the content width.

416 · 56 · 824 = 1,296, and inside the 824 block two columns of 384 on the same 56 px gutter. Both outer columns top-aligned; the head is never vertically centred against the stats and never sticky, A8·6's settlement carried forward — and here it matters more, because the stat block's height changes with the count while the head's does not.
Dividers Rules draws one vertical hairline, 28 px into the right-hand cells, running the full height of the block through both rows. A horizontal rule between the two rows is refused: a matrix of hairlines in both directions is 3 Grid, and this design would then be that one in a narrower column. Head to stats is nothing horizontal but the gutter; row to row is 48. ⚑ Mine: the 384 / 56 inner division, the single vertical rule and the refusal of the horizontal one.

At Count Two the stats keep the 384 / 56 / 384 division inside the 824 block rather than widening to two 632 cells. Widening would make the two-stat frame a different design from the four-stat one and would put a 52 px value in the middle of 632 px of nothing. The block's width never changes; only the number of rows in it does. ⚑ Mine. Foot Under the stats takes a hairline above it, spans the 824 block and sits 24 px below the last row — the same hairline the dividers use, which makes the foot read as the last thing in the stat block rather than a caption to it. Under the head is the default, because the source and the link are the publication speaking and the numbers are what it is speaking about. A9·5's control and reasoning, cited. Head column Right is row-reverse, visual only, and the head keeps its own left alignment inside its column. A9·5's rule verbatim: a head column that right-aligns its text when it moves to the right is two different heads.

A 104-CHARACTER TITLE IN A 416 COLUMN, A 60-CHARACTER LABEL AND A MISSING ONE · THE ROWS KEEP THEIR OWN HEIGHTS
A 104-character title at Medium 34 in a 416 column is five lines, which is taller than the head's sub and foot together and is still drawn. The stat block does not move down to meet it and the head does not shrink: both columns start at the same line and end where their content ends. The vertical divider runs the full height of the block, so it passes a labelless cell as well as a three-line one. This is the one place in A10 where a rule is longer than the content beside it, and it is correct: the rule divides the block's two columns, not the cells. Two hand-offs out. No title → 1 Row, because a split head with no head is a row with an empty third of a page beside it — A9·5's hand-off inverted. One stat → 4 Single. Three or five authored at Count Four draws what exists in the 2 × 2, the last cell absent. Six → the panel names 3 Grid.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWO COLUMNS, ONE LANDMARK The two outer columns are a flex row, not two landmarks — no <aside>, no roles, no second heading. A8·6's rule verbatim. The stats are one <ul> of two or four <li> in a CSS grid, never two lists of two, because the 2 × 2 is a wrap and not a grouping. The head is read first at both Head column values, and the Foot control changes reading order as well as position: at Under the head the source and link are read before the numbers, at Under the stats after them. Both are legitimate and the sidebar says which is which. A9·5's disclosure, cited. The vertical divider is a border-left on the right-hand cells, so it is decorative and inherits the cell's box; it is not an <hr> and never a separator role. In forced colours it survives as a system border, which means the two columns stay divided when the fills are gone. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, label 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, hairline 1.3:1 / 2.3:1, underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. One tab stop at most — the link. No behaviour, no sticky, no hover. Print: the split prints as drawn at 1440 and stacks at narrower paper.
