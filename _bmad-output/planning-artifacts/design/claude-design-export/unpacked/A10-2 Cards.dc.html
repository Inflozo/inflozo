Cards

1 Row's division with a card under each stat — A8·2's grid and card, cited whole, holding a number instead of a quotation. For a page whose ground is already carrying an image or a band, where three numbers sitting directly on it read as floating type.

The stat inside is unchanged — same value, same 10 px gap, same 15 px label — so switching between this design and 1 Row adds and removes a container and moves nothing inside it. What the card brings with it is the one thing A10 otherwise refuses: equal heights. Two cards side by side with different fills at different heights is a defect a reader can see, which is not true of two invisible cells.

Cards Ground keeps the hairline and the radius and drops the fill, for a page whose ground is already surface — three white cards on a white band is three floating hairlines. In forced colours the fill is dropped anyway, which makes Surface and Ground the same drawing, and the panel says so rather than pretending the control still does something. Cited from A9·4. Card padding is a control here and nowhere else in A10: Compact 24 · Comfortable 32 · Spacious 40, stepping to 24 · 28 · 32 at 834 and 20 · 24 · 28 at 390. It is the only way to change the density of this design, since the value size is fixed and the count is what it is. The padding is even on all four sides — a card with more space under its label than above its value reads as a card with something missing from the bottom. ⚑ Mine. Cards are equalised to the tallest in the row, and this is A10's second equalisation — A10-0 said 1 Row's label block was the only one, and the drawing overruled it. The slack falls at the foot of the short cards, below their labels. Nothing inside a card is stretched, centred vertically or moved: the value stays at the top, so the values still share one baseline across the row.

A card with no label is a card with one line in it, and it is the same height as its neighbours. This is the one place in A10 where the missing label leaves a visible hole rather than an invisible one, because the card's fill draws the empty space. ACCESSIBILITY · A CONTAINER THAT ANNOUNCES NOTHING 1 Row's structure with a <div> around each stat: one <ul> of <li>, each holding a card that holds one <p>. The card takes no role, no label, no <article> and no heading — it is a plane, and a plane with a role is a region a reader has to leave. The cards are not links and nothing inside them is focusable. The equal-height stretch is invisible to assistive technology, which reads three paragraphs of the same length whatever the boxes do. The empty space at the foot of a short card is not announced and carries no text, not even a hidden one. In forced colours the fill is dropped and the hairline is kept, so Surface and Ground become the same drawing. Stated, not corrected: the control still changes the theme's output, and a user in forced colours sees one of the two. Cited from A9·4. Contrast: value 15.4:1 on surface light / 14.7:1 dark, label 5.4:1 / 5.8:1, hairline 1.3:1 light and 1.5:1 dark against the card. The card-to-ground step is 1.05:1 in light and 1.2:1 in dark, which is why the hairline carries the edge in both modes rather than the fill. One tab stop at most — the link. Print: cards keep their hairlines, the fill is not printed.
