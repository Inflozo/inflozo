Faces

Eight to twenty-four people, four to eight across, a circle and a name and nothing else. §8.3's team of twelve, and the design a site should be sent to when it has more people than roles worth reading. It draws no role and no bio at any setting — the only design in A12 that refuses the role.

It also carries the one exception to settlement 1 in the whole category. Everywhere else a person's photograph is alt="" because the name is drawn beside it. At Names None there is no name beside it — so at that one value, and only there, the photograph takes the person's name as its alt text.

NAMES NONE · THE FACES SIT ON THE SAME CELL GRID WITH NOTHING UNDER THEM · alt IS THE PERSON'S NAME AND THE INITIALS BLOCK IS THE PROBLEM CASE · IT HAS NO alt TO GIVE, SO IT TAKES aria-label WITH THE NAME
Names None is the one value in A12 that turns alt text on. Settlement 1 says a person's photograph is decorative because the name is beside it; here the name is not drawn, so the photograph carries it — alt="Marguerite Okonjo", the plain name, never “photo of”. Without that the section would announce a title and then twelve empty images. ⚑ Mine, and A12-0's settlement 1 is amended to say so. The initials block cannot take alt text — it is not an image — so at Names None it takes aria-label with the name and stops being aria-hidden. Its two letters remain decorative: a reader hears “Rosa Ferreira”, not “R F”. ⚑ Mine. Names None is not the default and the panel argues against it: “Twelve faces with no names is a photograph of a team, not a list of people.” It exists because a masthead under a hero sometimes only needs to say that a team exists — and because A11·10 Slim made the same case for logos. Refused at both Names values: a tooltip, a hover label, a caption on the circle, a role, a number, and a “+4 more” badge. Everyone authored is drawn.

ACCESSIBILITY · WHERE THE ALT TEXT COMES BACK At Names Under the face this design is 1 Grid without the role line: a <ul> of <li>, an <img alt=""> and one <p> a person, no headings, and one tab stop per linked name. At Names None the photograph takes the person's name as its alt text and the initials block takes an aria-label — the one exception to settlement 1 in A12, and the reason it exists is arithmetic: twelve alt="" images with no text near them is a section that announces its title and then nothing. The alt is the plain name, never “photo of” and never the role. ⚑ Mine, and A12-0 records the amendment. At Names None a linked person is an <a> around the image, taking its accessible name from that alt — the one place in A12 where the target is a photograph rather than a run of text, and the target is then 56 × 56 or 44 × 44, which is at or above the touch minimum in both axes. At Names Under the face the target is the name, as everywhere else. Stated in the panel, because it means Names changes what is clickable. One flat list of up to twenty-four items, no grouping and no skip link — A11·13's position: the list-level jump is already in the reader's software. Twenty-four names and no structure between them is a stated cost of choosing density; 15 Groups is named for a team that wants sections. Contrast: title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1; name 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 at 14 px — the smallest text-coloured type in A12, and it is 14 rather than 13 because 13 px is the library's meta size and a name is not meta; note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1; initials on their fill 5.1:1 / 5.4:1. Nothing moves at any setting, and at 400% zoom the wall is three across with names wrapping.
