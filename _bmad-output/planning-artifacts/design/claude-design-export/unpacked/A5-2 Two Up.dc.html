Two Up

Two items across a 632 px column each, for a site whose features need a sentence rather than a phrase. The extra width is the whole design, and it creates the one problem 1 Three Up never has: 632 px of 16 px body copy is about 95 characters a line, well past comfortable. This design settles what to do about that, and the horizontal item that comes out of it.

Two columns of 632 px, 32 px between them, 44 px between rows — four more than 1 Three Up's 40, because a 24 px title needs more room above it than a 21 px one. The body copy is capped at a 520 px measure inside the 632 px item, which is about 78 characters a line and the reason this design is not simply 1 with one column removed. Icon beside the text is the default here for the same reason: a 48 px icon and its 20 px gap take 68 px off the front of the item, leaving 564 px, and the cap is doing almost nothing. Item title 24 px, body 16 px, the head at the left margin. Flagged as mine: the 44 px row gap, the 520 px body cap, the 48 px container against 1's 40, and Left as this design's default head alignment.

WHY THERE IS NO GROUND CONTROL HERE
1 Three Up offers Background · Surface · Contrast. This design offers none of them, and draws on the page's own ground. Two items on a surface ground with no edge between them is a panel with two things on it, which is 16 Panel, drawn there properly with its dividers and its inset. Two items on contrast is 10 Contrast Band, which has the inverted icon container and the band-edge control this design would need and does not have. Both alternatives already exist as designs, and §5 asks for four to seven controls, so the sixth slot goes to Item arrangement instead — the thing only this design can do. The editor names the two destinations when a user looks for a ground here. Flagged as mine: dropping Ground on this design and both named destinations.

1 · A5-0's structure, unchanged: <section aria-labelledby> named by the h2, items a <ul> of six <li>, item titles h3. 2 · The DOM order is title, body, link at both item arrangements. Icon beside the text is a two-column flex; the icon is still the item's first child and still aria-hidden, so nothing about the arrangement changes what is read. 3 · Six links whose labels must differ. This is the first design in the library where a user can write “Read more” six times, and the editor says so: the label is the link's accessible name, and six identical names in one list is the most common real failure in a feature section. It advises and does not enforce, and the item's own title is not appended to the name behind the user's back. Hit area. The link's 44 px target is drawn from the label's centre and overlaps the body's last line without covering it; the label itself stays 15 px so the item's rhythm holds. On a phone the label is 16 px. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · item title 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · body 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · link label 12.1:1 / 12.6:1 · glyph on tint 11.2:1 / 11.9:1. Zoom. At 200% text the two columns become one and Icon beside the text holds, since the container does not scale with the type; at 400% the container is dropped and the arrangement is Stacked, which is the one place a zoom level changes an arrangement in A5.
