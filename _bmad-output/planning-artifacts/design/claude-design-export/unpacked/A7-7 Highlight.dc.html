A7 PRICING AND TIERS · DESIGN 7 OF 15 · PAPER PACK

Highlight

The recommended tier given two thirds of the width and the others stacked beside it in a narrow column. Settlement 2 marks a recommendation with a plane step, a badge and the accent; this design asks how much further a site may go before the section stops being a price list and becomes an advertisement. Its answer is: as far as size, and no further — the small tiers keep every fact the large one has.

This is the one design in A7 that requires a recommendation. Without one there is no tier to enlarge, and enlarging the middle tier by position would be the theme deciding what a site did not: so the design hands off to 1 Cards, the sidebar says which field is empty, and the picker sits directly under the message. WHAT THE CONTROL MAY NOT DO Hide a price · mute a tier's name or price · remove an action · shrink a small card's action below 42 px · draw a small tier's benefits as a count rather than a list. Benefits Hidden hides them on every small tier at once, never on one, and the large tier's list is not affected by the control at all.

Benefits Hidden is the only reduction the design allows, and it is symmetric: every small tier loses its list or none does. A site that wants the small tiers quieter than that is asking the section to argue rather than to price, and the honest version of that page is a CTA banner with one ask on it — A6·1 Centred, which the editor names.

1 · Size is not level. Every tier name is an <h3> — 20 px and 18 px are two sizes of one heading level, and a screen reader hears three tiers of equal standing. The large tier is not aria-current, not role="figure" and not a landmark; its badge is the only thing that says it is recommended, which is why the badge is text. 2 · DOM order is Ghost's price order — Free, Reader, Quarterly — and the visual order at 1440 puts Reader first because it is in the left column. The second design in A7 where DOM and visual order differ, and the one where the difference is largest. Tab order follows the DOM, so a keyboard reader meets the cheapest tier first at every width, which is the order the phone draws. 3 · The small cards are ordinary list items with the same fields in the same order at 14 px. Their actions stay at 42 px rather than following the type down — a smaller card does not get a smaller target — and both are hairline outlines, since the accent has moved to the large tier under settlement 2. The internal hairline is a border-left, never an <hr>, and the benefit list beside it is the same nested <ul> as everywhere else in A7 — moved, not restructured. Contrast · as 1 Cards, with the small tiers' 14 px body at 12.1:1 and their muted description at 5.4:1. Nothing in this design is below 14 px and no text is reduced to make room. At 200% text the columns become the tablet's rows.
