Tiers

Two groups at two box sizes: the first one, two or three logos at Huge 64, the rest at Small 28. The only design in A11 that draws two logo sizes in one section, which means it is the only one that breaks the rule the other fourteen exist to keep — that every mark on a wall carries the same weight.

It breaks it on purpose and only where a site has a real hierarchy to state: a headline partner and its supporters, a founding sponsor and the rest. The size is the tier, and the two optional labels name what the sizes mean. It reads leadLabel and restLabel, which no other design draws.

The lead tier has no cell grid — two marks at natural width on a 96 px gap, starting on the left margin — and the rest tier does, at 1 Row's divisions. That difference is deliberate: two logos in 632 px cells read as a two-column table, and the lead tier is meant to read as a statement. The rest tier is a wall and keeps the wall's geometry.
64 px between the tiers, 16 px from a label to its group, and no rule anywhere. A hairline between the tiers was tried and refused: it makes two sections out of one, and a site that wants two sections can add two. The labels are the eyebrow component at 13 px uppercase tracked, in text-muted — the same component the head uses above the title, which is why the section can carry three of them without a fourth type size. ⚑ Mine: the 64 px tier gap, the 16 px label gap, the lead tier's natural widths, and refusing a rule.

The first N logos in the repeater are the lead tier — A10·5's rule for the same problem, and there is no per-logo tier field. A site reorders the repeater to change the tier, which is one mechanism instead of two. Lead count is One, Two or Three and three is the ceiling: four marks at Huge 64 is 1,300 px of lead tier, which is a wall, and a wall with a wall under it has no tiers. At Lead count One the lead tier is centred and at Two and Three it is flush left. One mark flush left on a 1,296 width reads as an orphan; two or three read as a row. This is the only alignment in A11 that changes with a count, and it is stated at the control. ⚑ Mine. Both labels are optional and each is drawn when authored. One without the other is legal — “Carried in full” above the lead and nothing above the rest is a common and honest thing to write. With neither the section still has tiers, because the sizes are the tiers, and the panel says which labels are missing rather than inventing “Partners” and “Supporters”.

The two sizes are the design and the ratio between them is fixed: Huge 64 or Large 48 for the lead, Small 28 for the rest, always. There is no control over the rest tier's size, because a control that could set both tiers equal would let a site turn this design into 3 Grid by accident and lose the hierarchy it chose the design for. ⚑ Mine, and the same reasoning A10·5 used for its fixed pair. A rest tier of two or three is drawn left-aligned in its cells rather than centred, because the lead tier above it is flush left and a centred remainder under a flush-left statement reads as a mistake. At Lead count One, where the lead is centred, a short rest row is centred too — the two tiers always agree. ⚑ Mine. Under four logos in total the section draws as 1 Row, named: one lead and two others is a row with one mark shouting. Sixteen is the ceiling — three at Huge plus thirteen at Small across three rows — and past it the panel names 13 Dense, which has no tiers and says so.

Lead count One with Head None is the design at its most reduced: a label, one mark at 64, and six at 28. The lead is centred, so the rest tier is centred too — and at Rest count Six the cells fill the content width, so “centred” and “spread” are the same drawing. The 64 px tier gap holds at every setting, and it is the only thing separating the tiers. Without it a 64 px mark 32 px above a 28 px row reads as one group with an outlier. Restlabel is not authored here and nothing is drawn in its place — the rest tier begins with its first logo. ⚑ Mine: the tiers agreeing on alignment, and the label never becoming the heading.

The ratio between the tiers narrows as the width does — 2.3× at 1440, 1.8× at 834, 1.7× at 390 — because the lead has further to fall than the rest, and a 64 px mark on a phone is most of the screen. The hierarchy survives the narrowing and gets quieter, which is stated rather than corrected: forcing the ratio would mean shrinking the rest tier below the 24 px floor. ⚑ Mine, and this design's one real cost.

ACCESSIBILITY · TWO LISTS, TWO LABELS, ONE HEADING Two <ul>s, not one, because they are two groups — and each takes its label as an aria-labelledby pointing at the <p> above it, so a screen reader announces “Carried in full, list, 2 items”. With a label missing that list takes no name and is announced as a plain list; nothing is generated. ⚑ Mine. The tier labels are <p>s, never <h3>s. Promoting them would put “Carried in full” and “Quoted or reprinted” into the page's document outline as subheads of the section, which is a structure a reader would try to navigate by. They label a list, they do not head a subsection — and at Head None the lead label does not become the section's name either. Nothing in the markup says one tier ranks above the other. The hierarchy is carried by size alone, which a screen reader cannot see — so the labels are the only way the ranking is available non-visually, and that is the reason to author them. The editor says so at the field: “Without labels, the tiers are visual only.” A stated loss, not a solved problem. Reading order is authored order: the lead tier is the first N items of one repeater, so the DOM order and the drawn order are the same and no order property is used. A linked logo is an ordinary link at either size; the lead tier's targets are the largest in A11 outside 5 Cards at 64 px tall. Contrast: tier labels 5.6:1 light / 6.0:1 dark, title 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, note 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, hover fill 1.2:1 / 1.4:1 decorative. A logo takes no ratio at either size — and the editor's 3:1 advice matters more for the 28 px tier, which the field says. Reduced motion changes nothing. At 400% the lead is one per row and the rest is two across, with the ratio at its narrowest. Print draws both tiers at their drawn sizes and keeps the labels.
