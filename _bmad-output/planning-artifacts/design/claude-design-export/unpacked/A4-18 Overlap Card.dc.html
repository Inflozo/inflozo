Overlap Card

A wide picture with the text on a surface card that hangs off its bottom edge and sits over the section below. The category's only design that reaches into its neighbour, which makes the page below its specification too: the card's foot lands in space the next section has to give it, and every frame here is drawn with that neighbour visible.

The card is 660 px on surface with the md shadow — the only place in A4 that shadow is used, because it is the only element that is genuinely above the page rather than on it — and it is pulled 96 px up onto the picture, with the rest of its height — the greater part — on the page below. The section's bottom padding is measured from the card, not the picture: 56 px from the card's foot to the first thing below it, which is why the frame draws the posts. Header mode is Overlap because the picture reaches the top of the page and A1·4's transparent header is what belongs over it; the top scrim is 4 Full Bleed's, unchanged. Radius on the card is the pack's 8, matching the picture's, and the card's own corners are all four — a card with square top corners would be a panel attached to the picture rather than a card over it. Flagged as mine: the 660 px card, the 48 px inset, the md shadow, and measuring the section's foot from the card.

OVERLAP · the three named values, and what the next section owes
SMALL 48. The card is barely onto the picture — about a tenth of its own height. For a page whose next section starts close.
MEDIUM 96. The default, about a fifth of the card. Clearly over both the picture and the page, and the composition reads as two planes.
LARGE 144. Around 30% of a full card, for a tall picture. Past this the picture's edge starts crossing into the card's own text, which is why there is no fourth value.
The overlap is never more than 40% of the card's height. Past that the picture's bottom edge crosses into the card's own text rather than passing behind its top margin, and the two planes stop being legible as planes. Where a short card would break the rule — no sub, no actions — the overlap steps down one named value and the editor says which, the same disclosure pattern as A4·16's quote size. What the next section owes. Most of the card sits below the picture, and that is real space: the section's own bottom padding is measured from the card's foot rather than the picture's, so the next section's top padding is untouched and nothing overlaps its content. A page that puts a contrast band immediately below gets the card overlapping a dark ground — legible, because the card is opaque surface, but the band's own top padding then reads as smaller than it is. The editor flags that pairing rather than preventing it.

No image. Both the picture and the card leave: the section becomes 2 Flush Left — the text block on the page's own ground, at the page margin, with the header back to A1·1 Rail. The card is not kept, because a surface card with an md shadow floating on the page with nothing to overlap is a card that has lost its reason. This is the fourth hand-off in the category, and the plainest: the design's whole subject is the relationship between a picture and a card, so without the picture there is no design, only a text block — and 2 already is that text block. No sub, or no actions. The card shortens and the overlap rule may step it down a value; nothing else changes. No headline — the section does not render, as everywhere in A4 except 16 Pull Quote. 1 · Headline is the page's h1 on a surface, not over a photograph — which is this design's real accessibility advantage over 4 Full Bleed and the reason it exists alongside it: the text's contrast is a measured token pair rather than an estimate against an unknown image, so it holds at every pack and every picture. 12.1:1 light, 11.8:1 dark, with no scrim in the calculation. 2 · The picture is decorative — alt="" by default, authorable — with explicit dimensions, loading="eager" and high fetch priority. The top scrim exists only for the overlay header, not for the card. 3 · Reading order matches the visual order and the DOM is flat: picture, then card. The overlap is negative margin on the card, not absolute positioning, so the card is in normal flow and the section's height includes it — which is what lets the next section's padding stay honest and stops the card overlapping content at large text sizes. At 200% zoom the card grows downward and the overlap is unchanged. 4 · The card is not a landmark, a region or a labelled group. It is a visual plane. Marking it up as anything would add a hero-shaped announcement to every page that uses this design, and there is nothing inside it a reader needs to be told they have entered. No motion. The card does not rise, fade or parallax on load or scroll — the composition is two planes at rest, and a card that moves relative to its picture on scroll is a parallax effect the category does not have a budget for. Nothing for reduced motion to remove.
