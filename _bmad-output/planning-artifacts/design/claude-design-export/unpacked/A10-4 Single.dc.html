Single

One number as the whole section, at 128 px. The most-received design in the library so far — nine of A10's fifteen hand off to it when only one stat is authored — and the one place where the value is allowed to be the largest thing on the page by a wide margin rather than a narrow one.

It exists because a row of one is not a row. A single stat in 1 Row's 416 cell is a number in the left third of a section with two thirds of nothing beside it; the same number here is a statement. With two or more stats authored it draws the first and says so, which is the one place in A10 where a design leaves content out on purpose.

ACCESSIBILITY · ONE NUMBER, AND WHY IT IS STILL A LIST A <ul> of one <li>, holding one <p>. A list of one announces “list, one item”, which is one redundant phrase — and it is kept, because the alternative is a different structure at a different count, and a design that is a list at three stats and a paragraph at one is two designs to build and test. Nine other designs hand off to this one, so the structure a reader meets has to be the same structure. ⚑ Mine, and stated as a cost. The value is not an <h2> or an <h3> even at 128 px. This is the design where the temptation is strongest — it is visually the page's biggest thing — and a heading whose text is “41,800” is a heading rotor entry that tells a reader nothing about where they are. The <h2> is the title, as everywhere else in A10; at head None there is no <h2> and the section takes no accessible name. At Label Beside, the DOM order is value then label — the same as Under — so the two arrangements read identically and the control changes nothing but the visual position. The panel at Frame Panel is a plain <div> with no role and no label. Contrast: value 15.8:1 on ground light / 14.7:1 on the dark panel, label 5.6:1 / 5.8:1. Large text at 128 px passes AA at 3:1, so the value has 5× the contrast it needs — which is why it stays text and is never lightened for effect. One tab stop at most. No behaviour: the count-up would be most tempting here and is refused here too. Print and screen are the same drawing.
