Contrast Band

The whole section on the inverted ground, the tiers drawn as panels on it. Ground is not a control here: it is the design. It settles the two things settlement 2 could not settle in advance — what the accent does when an accent fill is 3.2:1, and what the badge does when the badge's own colour is the ground it sits on.

The accent is spent nothing. An accent fill carrying the band's colour is 3.2:1 in Paper, so the recommended tier's action is a solid contrast-carried fill at 12.8:1 and the other two are hairline outlines in the carried colour at 45% with full-strength labels. A4·9, A5·10 and A6·5 reached the same answer; this design carries it rather than re-deciding it.
The badge inverts. Settlement 2 makes it a contrast fill, and on a contrast ground that is a badge the colour of the thing behind it — so here it becomes the carried colour carrying the band's own, the same 12.8:1 the other way round. That is the one substitution the recommendation makes anywhere in A7, and it is why the badge was defined as a token pair rather than a colour.
Panels are hairlines in the carried colour at 22% and the recommended panel is a solid at 7%: the plane step still steps, on a ground where a shadow cannot. Warm shadows are invisible on contrast, so no panel in this design has one at any value. Band padding is A4·9's tighter scale — 44 · 64 · 88 — because an inverted block reads as bigger than it is.

Two steps of the same mix, never a third. Panel style Solid exists for a site whose band sits between two pale sections and reads as a hole; it fills every panel and drops the hairlines, and the recommended panel goes one step further — 7% to 14% — because a step needs something to step from. At Hairline the recommended panel keeps its border, so its edge does not disappear where its neighbours' remain. Both mixes are derived rather than tokens, which is the one place this design invents colour, and it is flagged: a pack defines contrast and the colour it carries, and everything here is those two mixed.

The band is not a landmark, not a region and not a theme boundary — a background colour on a named section, exactly as A6·5 settled it. Structure, headings, list semantics, price string, badge text and Portal links are 1 Cards' unchanged; nothing about the inversion reaches the accessibility tree. Muted on the band is the carried colour at 72% — #B9B1A4, 7.4:1, higher than muted on the page's own ground — and it carries descriptions, the eyebrow, the note and the benefit marks. Benefit text stays at full strength: nine muted lines on an inverted panel is a paragraph nobody reads. The outlined action's border is 2.4:1 against the band, which is why the label carries it at full strength and the border is decoration. Forced colours drop the band, the panels and both mixes — the section becomes 1 Cards with system colours, the badge keeps its text, and nothing in the design depended on the inversion. The switch on the band is the pill at the 7% mix with a 22% hairline, the active label on the 14% mix at full strength and the inactive at 72%. It is the one component whose three colours are all derived, and the sidebar says so. Contrast · title and price 12.8:1 · muted 7.4:1 · benefits 12.8:1 with the mark at 7.4:1 · badge label 12.8:1 · recommended action label 12.8:1 · outlined label 12.8:1 · focus ring 12.8:1. The accent appears nowhere in the section, at any value, in either mode.
