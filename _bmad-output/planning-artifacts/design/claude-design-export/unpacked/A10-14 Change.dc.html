A10 STATS AND NUMBERS · DESIGN 14 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONLY DESIGN THAT DRAWS TWO FIGURES PER STAT

Change

Each value with the figure it replaced, on a line of its own: a glyph, a word and the previous number. The one design that reads prev, and the one where a stat is a comparison rather than a count.

Direction is carried by a triangle and a word, never by a colour. The packs supply one accent and no semantic pair, so green-up and red-down do not exist to be used — and a reader who cannot separate the two would be reading a section whose only signal is hue. The theme compares the two figures and does not subtract them: it will say a number went up, and it will not publish a difference nobody typed.

The cell is value, change line, label, in that order — the change belongs to the figure above it, and the label explains both. 8 px between all three, so the cell is 40 + 8 + 20 + 8 + 22 = 98 px at one label line. The change line is 13 px in text-muted with tabular figures, the same meta treatment as the source line, and the previous figure is set in the body font rather than the heading font: two serif numerals in one cell would read as two stats. ⚑ Mine: the order, the 8 px gaps, the body font on the previous figure.
The glyph is a 10 px triangle, muted, one space before the word. It is a text character and not an icon, so it takes the pack's body font, scales with the line, and needs no icon set across twelve packs. The word is always drawn — a triangle alone is a direction nobody has named, and “up from” is two words.

Direction is computed, and only when both figures parse. Separators, spaces, the prefix and the suffix are stripped and what remains is read as a number; if either side does not parse, the line reads “was …” with no glyph and no direction word. That is what makes “Sold out” against “240 left” safe: the section states the replacement without claiming which way it went. ⚑ Mine, including the four wordings. The four wordings are fixed and not editable: “up from”, “down from”, “unchanged from”, “was”. A site that wants “grew to” or “fell to” is writing a sentence, which is 11 Inline. The period is not named in the line — no “since 2025”, no “YoY”, no “vs. last year” — because the theme does not know the period. The site types it into the sub, once, for all four stats. ⚑ Mine. No difference is calculated. Not 8,400, not +25%, not a ratio: a figure the theme derives is a figure nobody checked, and rounding it is a second decision made on the site's behalf. A site that wants the delta types it as its own stat, with the label saying what it is. This is the same refusal 9 Bars made about normalising shares. ⚑ Mine. A stat with no prev draws no change line and nothing is substituted — no “new”, no dash, no “first year”. The label moves up 28 px, the values stay on one baseline because the value is first in the cell, and the row's height is the tallest cell's. With no stat carrying a prev the section draws 1 Row, named in the panel.

Beside the value puts the change line 10 px after the value on its baseline and takes 28 px off the cell, which is the placement for four short figures. At two long figures it needs 420 px and wraps in a 306 cell, so the panel names Under at Count Four with long values. Nothing shrinks to make it fit. Colour carries nothing, in either mode or any pack. No green, no red, no accent on a rising figure, no tint on the cell. The triangle and the word are both drawn in text-muted, the same as the label beside them, so a reader who sees no colour difference loses nothing. This is the category's stated refusal of colour as meaning, made concrete in the one design that would have used it. The direction is not emphasised by weight either. A 600-weight “up” in a muted line is a section shouting about its own good news; the site's own sub is where a claim about the trend belongs. ⚑ Mine.

ACCESSIBILITY · ANNOTATED · DIRECTION THAT SURVIVES WITHOUT COLOUR OR SHAPE The direction is a word, so it survives every reading condition: greyscale, forced colours, a screen reader, and a printed page. The triangle carries aria-hidden="true" — “black up-pointing triangle, up from 33,400” is the glyph announced twice, and the word is the part that means something. ⚑ Mine. The stat is one <p> holding all three lines, so it announces as “41,800, up from 33,400, readers on the Thursday letter” — one string, in the order it is drawn. The change line is not a separate paragraph and not a <del>/<ins> pair: the previous figure is not deleted text, it is a fact about last year. Under and Beside announce identically, because the placement changes the layout and not the DOM. That is what makes the forced Under at ≤ 767 safe: a reader on a phone hears exactly what a reader on a desktop hears. Contrast: value 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark; change line, glyph and label all 5.6:1 / 6.0:1 — one colour for the whole meta line, so no part of it is quieter than another. Link underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. In forced colours the triangle and the words take the system text colour and nothing is lost, because nothing was carried by hue. One tab stop. No motion.
