Wall

Up to twelve quotations in three or four CSS columns at their natural heights. 4 Grid stops at six because a grid of equal-width cells with equal rows runs out of patience; this design takes the same content past that point by giving up equal heights entirely. Nothing stretches, nothing is padded, and the section ends on an uneven line.

Two things follow and both are settlements for the category. The quote is fixed at Small 17 and has no size control — twelve quotations at Card 20 is a page of prose, not a wall. And because Small is the floor of the ladder, this is the one design where a long quotation does not step down: it takes more of its column and costs nothing else.

Four columns of 306 px on a 24 px gap, twelve items, 24 px apart down the column. The card is the shared one at its smallest scale: padding 20, quote Small 17, avatar 32, name 14/600, role 13 muted, the internal gap 16 rather than 20 — the one place in A8 the card's fixed 20 px separation is reduced, because at 17 px type a 20 px gap makes twelve cards read as twenty-four blocks. Still no shadow, and now it matters most: twelve warm shadows is a texture, and the hairline holds twelve edges as easily as three.
The bottom edge is uneven and that is the design. The browser balances the columns, so the last card in each ends where its content ends — the shortest column here finishes 46 px above the tallest — and the section's own padding starts from the lowest. Nothing is stretched to square it off. ⚑ The 306/24 measure, the reduced internal gap, the 32 px avatar and the twelve-item ceiling are mine.

AUTHORED ORDER RUNS DOWN EACH COLUMN, THEN ACROSS · 1 2 3 · 4 5 6 · 7 8 9
This is the one place in A8 where the drawn order is not the reading order left to right, and it is stated rather than hidden. CSS columns fill down and then across: quote 2 is under quote 1, not beside it. The DOM order is still the authored order, so a screen reader and a keyboard hear 1 to 12 in the order the site typed them, and the visual sequence agrees with the DOM — it is only the eye's habit of reading rows that disagrees. The editor says so at the repeater: “On the wall, quotes read down each column.” A site that wants its strongest quotation first should know it appears top-left and that the second appears beneath it. The column count changes which quote sits where, and nothing else. No quote is moved between columns to balance heights, no quote is duplicated, and the browser's own balancing decides where a column breaks. ⚑ The rule, the editor's line and the refusal to rebalance by hand are mine.

CARDS NONE · no planes and no hairlines · 32 px apart down the column, the avatar dropped

Cards None is the wall as a page of quotations rather than a page of cards, and it is the only value in A8 that drops the avatar: at 17 px with no plane, twelve circles are twelve dots down a column and the names carry the attribution alone. The vertical rhythm opens from 24 to 32 px to keep the items apart without an edge. Cards Ground sits between the two — hairline, no fill — and is the value for a wall inside a page that already has white cards. Columns Four is only available at 1440 and above; below that the control reads “Three at this width”, because a 306 px column at 1,296 is a paragraph and at 1,000 it is a stack of two-word lines. ⚑ Dropping the avatar at Cards None, and the 32 px rhythm, are mine.

The columns are column-count on the list, not three lists — so the DOM is one sequence and the announced order is the authored order, which is exactly what the visual order does too. The column count is presentational and is never announced. 2 · break-inside: avoid on every item, so no card is split across a column boundary. A card that broke in two would give a reader half a quotation with an attribution in the next column, which is the one failure this arrangement can produce and the one line of CSS that prevents it. 3 · Twelve quotations and still one focus stop — the foot link, A6's ring — and no hover state anywhere.
