Contact Block

A footer that answers “where are you and how do I reach you” — a postal address, an email, a phone number and the hours somebody replies, set as a labelled block beside the link columns. It is the only design in A3 that carries the contact fields, and the only one whose content is legally load-bearing: in several countries a publication's address in the footer is a requirement, not a courtesy.

The contact block is a fixed 300 px beside the brand block, so the two fixed columns sit together at the left and the link columns flex on the right. Its labels are 12 px muted — smaller than the category's 13 px meta, and the one place that floor is broken, because these are field labels rather than reading text and the value under each is 15 px. Values are text, not muted: an address you cannot read is worse than no address.

Address and email drops the phone and the hours; Email only leaves one line and the block keeps its 300 px so the grid does not move. Email and phone are links — mailto: and tel: — and carry a permanent 20% underline rather than only showing one on hover, because a link in text colour among non-links in text colour is otherwise invisible. Hover takes the underline to full. Address and hours are not links; the address is not wired to a map, which is A16 Contact's job.

1 · The block is <address> inside a section labelled by its visible <h2>. <address> is for exactly this — the contact details of the page's owner — and its default italics are reset. 2 · Labels and values are a <dl>, so “Post” names the address that follows rather than floating above it. The postal address keeps its line breaks in <br> at desktop; at ≤ 767 the same field renders as one line with commas, which reads correctly either way. 3 · Email is a mailto: link and phone a tel: link, both with the address as their visible text — no “email us” label, which strands anyone reading a link list out of context. The phone number keeps its spaces visually and is unspaced in the href. Labels at 12 px · the only place the category goes below 13 px, allowed because each label is a <dt> naming a 15 px value directly under it, and it measures 5.4:1 light / 6.4:1 dark. Flagged as mine, and it is the one type-floor exception in A3. Legal note · where an imprint or business address is legally required, this design and A3·11 Colophon are the two that carry it. I have not encoded any jurisdiction's rule — flagged as a gap: the field exists, what a given country requires in it is the site owner's to know.

Controls · data Padding: Compact 36 · Comfortable 52 · Spacious 72. Ground: Background · Surface · Contrast. Details: All four · Address and email · Email only. Columns: Auto · Two · Off. Social: Glyphs · Labels · Off. Ghost attribution: Show · Hide.
Data: none of the contact fields exist in Ghost's settings, so all four are section content. Flagged: a site with several footers would have to keep them in step, which is a real cost of this decision; the alternative — a site-wide contact record — is not something Ghost gives us. Email and phone become mailto: and tel: links automatically.
Responsive · empty · a11y · flagged 1440–1081: brand 300 px, contact 300 px, links flexing. 1080–768: two-across; the contact block becomes a two-by-two field grid so it matches the brand block's height. ≤ 767: contact comes second, above the links, and the address renders as one comma-separated line; email and phone get 44 px targets.
Empty: each missing field takes its label with it. All four empty → the block and its heading are removed and the design is A3·2 Columns; the sidebar says so. The heading is kept even for a single field.
a11y: <address> in a section labelled by its <h2>, italics reset; labels and values as a <dl>; link text is the address itself; phone spaced visually, unspaced in the href.
Flagged as mine: the 12 px labels (the category's one type-floor exception), the fixed field order, the permanent 20% underline on the two links, the mobile address collapse, contact coming before links at ≤ 767, and the absence of any jurisdiction's imprint rule.
