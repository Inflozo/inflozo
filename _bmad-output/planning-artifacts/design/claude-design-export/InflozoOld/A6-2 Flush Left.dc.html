The rule above the banner is the design's answer, and it is why the control exists. A centred banner is separated from what came before it by symmetry; a left-aligned one is not — its title starts exactly where the previous section's title started, at the same margin, and with 96 px of space between them a reader cannot tell whether it is a new section or the end of the last one. A single border hairline across the content width says it is new. It runs to the page margin, not the window's edge, at every width. Where the section above already ends in a hairline the value is Hide, and the editor says so rather than drawing two lines 1 px apart.
The right half stays empty. 620 px of text on a 1,296 px content width leaves 676 px of nothing, and that is the arrangement rather than a gap waiting for content — anything put there makes this 3 Split. Everything else is 1 Centred's: the same ladder, the same 46 px accent action, the same 24 px gap, the same note.

A5·6 reached the same answer for its row hairlines and this carries it. 2 · 1 Centred's structure unchanged: <section aria-labelledby>, the title an h2, the eyebrow a paragraph above it. Left alignment is CSS and nothing else — the DOM order of eyebrow, title, sub, actions, note is identical in all fifteen designs, which is what makes design-switching safe for a reader as well as for the content. 3 · Two <a>s, primary first, visible labels as their whole names. MEMBER ASK AND NO JAVASCRIPT · PATCH PASS · 29 AUG 2026 The ask is conditional. Where the primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting rather than shipping a button that fails. An ordinary link is unaffected. Ruled by the owner, 29 August 2026: the button keeps its place and its scale, and the site chooses what it says and where it goes — a fallback label and link (fallbackLabel ≤ 24 · fallbackUrl, in the panel beside the ask), so a reader meets a working button rather than a gap. Left empty, this design’s own rule applies: no primary label or URL, no section. Where the ask opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real href to a Portal address, so the page stays where it is and no window opens. No JavaScript: this design draws no form, so there is nothing to replace — every word and both actions are rendered by the server, and the banner is the banner.
