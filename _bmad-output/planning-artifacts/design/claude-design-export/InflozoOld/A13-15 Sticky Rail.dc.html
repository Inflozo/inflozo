Sticky Rail

A sticky list of the steps at the left, the steps themselves at full length at the right, and the list marking whichever step the reader is level with. The design for a process that is genuinely long — the second of two designs that read the section's own body, and the second that takes steps[].body to 400 characters.

It declares scroll-spy, and it is the only thing in A13 that marks a current step — derived from the reader's position rather than authored, which is exactly why A13-0 refuses a per-step “current” flag everywhere else. With JavaScript off the list renders with the first step marked and stops tracking, which costs the reader an indicator and no content at all.

306 · 40 · 950 = 1,296; the list sticks 96 px from the top of the viewport and unsticks at the foot of the last step, never travelling past the section. A list row is 10 px of padding with a 2 px left border in border, going accent for the current step, whose numeral and title also go text and 600. The current step is never marked by colour alone, so the state survives forced colours and a reader who cannot see the accent. On the right each step is a numeral and a label on one line, a 24/600 title, and a body on the 824 measure; steps are 56 px apart, the largest step gap in A13, because the gap is what tells the reader a step has ended when the next one has no rule above it.
The list is a set of links to the steps, and it is the only interactive thing in A13 that is not a button, an arrow or a tab. Each is an <a href="#step-2"> to that step's own <li>, which is what makes the whole design work without script: the anchors are real, the sticky is CSS, and only the tracking is JavaScript. ⚑ Mine: the 96 px offset, the 56 px step gap, the 10 px list row, and making the list anchors rather than buttons.

Module: scroll-spy. JS off, quoted from the registry: “The sticky list renders with the first item marked current; no active-item tracking.” That is exactly this design's degradation and nothing is lost: position: sticky is CSS and still works, the anchors are real <a href>s and still jump, every step and every word is on the page, and the reader loses only the moving indicator. It is the cleanest module match in A13, and unlike 11 Walkthrough it changes no markup and no heading level. Edit-safe. The tracking does not run while the section is being edited, the current step is never persisted, and the section always opens with the first step marked. Every frame in this file except the primary one is drawn at that resting state; the primary is drawn mid-scroll, and it says so. The tracking rule, stated: a step becomes current when its title crosses the sticky list's top edge, and stays current until the next one does. Exactly one step is current at all times, including at the top and the bottom of the section — no “none”, no two at once, no proportional highlight. Reduced motion changes nothing, because the marker moves instantly at every setting and nothing animates. ⚑ Mine, the whole rule. Refused: a progress bar, a percentage, a “4 of 6” counter, a smooth-scroll on the anchors, and collapsing a step once it is read. The first three are reading-progress's neighbourhood and this is a marketing section rather than an article; the last is accordion, and A13 ships no disclosure design at all.

At Marker Weight only the section has no accent pixel unless a link is authored, and the current step is carried by text at 600 against text-muted at 500. That is a 2.8:1 difference in luminance between the two states rather than a contrast ratio, and it is the quietest current-state indicator in the library — deliberately, because the reader already knows where they are and the list is a map rather than a control panel. List column Right is a real value and it changes what the list is for. At the left it is a contents list read before the steps; at the right it is a position marker read beside them. The DOM order is the same at both — the list first, the steps second — placed by flex-direction and never by order, so a reader always meets the contents before the content. List Plain drops the numerals from the list and keeps them on the steps. It is the value for a six-step list whose titles are long — the 80-character title is four lines in a 306 column with a figure and three without — and the steps' own figures still carry the sequence.
