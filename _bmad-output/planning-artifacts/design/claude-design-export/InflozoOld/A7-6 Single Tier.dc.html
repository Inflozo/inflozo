Single Tier

One card at 480, centred on the page's own ground, with the free tier beside it where the site has one. This is A7-0's fourth settlement built rather than ruled: a site with one paid tier, and a site with none. It is the only design in A7 whose primary frame is not three tiers, and the only one that advises upwards — at three paid tiers it stacks them in its own 480 column and the panel advises 1 Cards.

One paid tier is a choice between free and paid, and the design draws it as one — but not as two equal cards. The paid tier is the card: 480 px, surface, the sm shadow, the price at Display 56 (the ladder's top step, available here because there is one price rather than three), the accent action filling it. The free tier sits beside it at 320 px with no plane at all — name, price, description and a text link with a typed arrow, A6's second allowed secondary form, carried. There is no badge and no recommendation: with one paid tier the site is not choosing between plans, it is asking, so settlement 2's three signals have nothing to mark.
The benefits go to two columns inside the card — 3 Stack's rule at card scale — because a 416 px list on a 480 px card leaves the right half empty for four lines. The free tier draws no benefits at this value: it is the alternative, not the offer, and the editor names 11 Free and Paid for a site that wants both lists.

1 · Still a <ul>, even with one or two items — a list of one is a list, and a reader switching from 1 Cards to this design should hear the same structure. Tier names are h3s, the price is one readable string, the card is a <div> and never a link. 2 · The free tier is a list item like any other, not an aside, not a footnote and not aria-hidden at any value — it has a plane at none of them, which is a visual weight rather than a semantic one. At Free tier Hide it is absent from the DOM, not hidden in it. 3 · The arrow is aria-hidden and typed, never an icon, and it does not move on hover — A6's rule. The link's target is 44 px tall, extending past the label, and it underlines on hover and focus rather than changing colour alone. At Under it the sentence around the link is fixed copy: “Or keep reading free — ” plus the authored label. The card's states are 1 Cards' exactly — the accent fill hovering 6% darker, pressing 10%, the 2 px ring 2 px out with the gap taking the card's own colour. This design adds no state of its own. Contrast · as 1 Cards. EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub and note edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Tier names, prices, descriptions and benefits are Ghost’s: clicking one says “Edit in Ghost”. Every URL opens the Link Picker — a subscribe ask is its Portal · Sign up action — and every action takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label. The cadence words and the computed saving line are catalog strings, not fields. “Or keep reading free —” is now the freeLine field, its drawn text the default, still wrapping the authored freeLabel.
DATA · from Ghost · outside the design's listTiers come from {{#get "tiers"}} filtered type:paid+visibility:public in Ghost's own price order; the free tier is its own type:free object. A Ghost list: no Add, no Remove, no reorder. Every paid action and the cadence switch gate on @site.paid_members_enabled — Ghost seeds an active $5 paid tier at install, so a tier existing never means it can be bought. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render. Every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — no Stripe, or self-signup switched off, and the action is absent rather than dead. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, so the section reads but cannot be subscribed from. No JavaScript, elsewhere in the section: both of a tier's prices render side by side, each labelled in words, with no switch — and no saving line, which is the switch's own arithmetic. No paid tier is the state before Stripe is connected: the free tier alone, the switch gone with the prices it controlled.
