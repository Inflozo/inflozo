Two Column

Two independent lists of 632 px on a 32 px gutter, split by count: the first half at the left, the second at the right, the extra row going left on an odd count. It is the only two-column list of questions in A9 — 4 Cards is a grid of always-open cards, which has no rows to reflow — and it exists because eight rows down one column at 1440 is a tall thin band on a wide page.

The whole difficulty is what happens when a row opens, and A9-0's fourth settlement is drawn here twice over: a CSS multi-column list pushes a question across the break when its neighbour opens, and a row-major grid leaves a hole beside the row that grew. Two independent lists move only the rows below the one a reader pressed, which is the only behaviour a reader can predict.

632 · 32 · 632 = 1,296, A8·3's gutter and column at a list rather than a card, because two planes of type need a wider gap than three to read as two things. The columns are top-aligned and never equal in height — the left column here is taller by one open answer, and that is the visible proof that the columns do not know about each other. Row 2 is open and rows 5 to 8 have not moved: row 3 and row 4 came down with it and nothing else in the section did.
Question Row 17 is the default here, not Large 20, and Feature 24 is not offered at all: a 24 px question in a 632 column wraps to two lines at 44 characters, and sixteen lines of question is what this design exists to avoid. ⚑ The 632/32 division, the top alignment, the default at Row 17 and the refusal of Feature are mine.

The split is by count and never by height. Twelve rows are six and six; eight are four and four; seven are four and three, the extra row going left. Balancing the two columns' heights would move a question between columns whenever an answer's length changed, and a list whose membership depends on how much somebody typed cannot be read down a column. The DOM is the authored order, unbroken: list one holds items 1 to 4 and list two holds 5 to 8, so a screen reader hears 1 to 8 and a tab press moves 1 to 8. Nothing is reordered at any width. Two lists, not one, and the panel says so: “two lists, four questions each”. A single list styled into two columns would report neighbours a reader does not have. It is the one place in A9 where a design draws two <ul>s, and it costs a screen-reader user one extra “list of four” announcement. ⚑ Mine, including the cost.

ACCESSIBILITY · WHAT DIFFERS FROM 1 ACCORDION, AND NOTHING ELSE DOES Two <ul>s inside one <section aria-labelledby>, one <h2>, and every question an <h3> holding a full-width <summary>, exactly as 1 Accordion. The two lists take no roles, labels or headings of their own — they are two columns of one thing, and a label like “More questions” would be the theme writing copy. Tab order is 1 to 8 down the left column and then the right, which is the authored order, the DOM order and the visual reading order at both widths. There is no case in this design where those three disagree. The divider is decorative. A border-left on the second list, never an <hr> and never role="separator": the two lists are not two sections. In forced colours it keeps a system-coloured hairline, like every other rule in A9. Contrast is 1 Accordion's, unchanged: question 15.8:1 / 15.1:1, answer the same, marker resting 5.6:1 / 6.2:1, divider and row hairlines 1.3:1 as boundaries rather than information, focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. 44 px targets at every width, including the 365 px tablet column where a question runs to two lines and the row is 78 px tall. Every row prints open, in one column, because a printed page is not 1,296 px wide and two columns of open answers on paper is a newspaper.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Questions and answers edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The answer raises the marks toolbar on selection with bold · italic · link · inline code and no underline — an underline inside prose is a link that is not one. Recorded as a PRD delta. Nothing in A9 is Ghost-owned, so nothing here says “Edit in Ghost” — every word on the page is the site’s own. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Questions are the P0·3 item list: Add question arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only.
