Split Head

A5·5's division, carried whole: the head held in a 416 px column at the left margin, the quotations as rows in an 824 px column at the right. Every other design in A8 with a head puts it above the quotes, which makes the note and the link easy — they go at the foot. Here there are two feet, and choosing between them is what this design settles.

The default is under the head: the note and the link are the publication speaking, the quotations are readers speaking, and keeping the site's two sentences together in one column stops the section ending in the site's own voice. A control offers the other answer for a site whose link is the point.

416 · 56 · 824 is 1,296, and the 56 px gutter is A5·5's — wider than any card gutter in the category, because the two columns are two kinds of thing rather than two of the same thing. The head is Medium 34 and capped there: a 416 px column will not hold Display 48 in fewer than four lines, and a four-line title beside three quotations is a column of shouting. The two columns are top-aligned and the head does not stick, scroll or centre itself against the rows.
The rows are hairline-separated, 32 px above and below the rule, with no plane — the quotation and its attribution are the only things in the right column, and 824 px at Card 20 is a 60-character measure that needs no card to hold it. The first row has no rule above it and the last none below: the rules are between the quotations, not around them. ⚑ The 34 px cap, the 32 px row padding and the rule-between rule are mine.

UNDER THE QUOTES · a rule above it, 32 px · the section ends in the site's voice

Under the quotes takes the rule above it — the same hairline that separates two quotations, doing the same job between the last quotation and the site's own line — and that is why it is the second value rather than the first: it makes the note look like a fourth item in the list. A site whose “Read more letters” is the reason the section exists will want it anyway, at the end of the reading, and the control says so in one line. The note and the link always travel together; there is no value that splits them across the two columns. ⚑ The default, the rule above the second value, and keeping the pair together are mine.

HEAD COLUMN RIGHT · the mirror · the head still reads first in the DOM

Head column Right is a visual order only — the head stays first in the DOM, so a keyboard and a screen reader meet the title before the quotations at both values, and the mirror is flex-direction: row-reverse rather than a reordered document. It is the value for a page whose previous section is left-weighted. Rows Cards puts the shared 824 px card back, 16 px apart with no rules, which is the value for a page where this section would otherwise be the only unplaned thing on it. ⚑ Both values are mine; the DOM-order rule is A5·5's, carried.

1 · One <h2> in the head column, naming the section. The two columns are a flex row inside the section, not two landmarks — there is no aside, no role and no second heading, because the head is this section's head wherever it sits on screen. At Head column Right the DOM is unchanged and the visual order is reversed with row-reverse. 2 · The quotations are a <ul> of figures as everywhere else in A8. The hairlines are border-top on the list items and not <hr> elements: a rule between two quotations is a boundary a list already implies, and three <hr>s would be three separators announced inside a list of three. 3 · One focusable element, in the head column at the default. At foot Under the quotes it is the last thing in the section, and at Head column Right it is still reached before the quotations because the DOM order does not mirror — which is the reason the mirror is CSS and not markup. Contrast · quote and name 15.8:1 light / 15.6:1 dark; sub, note and role 5.6:1 / 6.8:1; the hairline 1.3:1 light and 1.5:1 dark, a boundary rather than information. Focus ring 3.3:1 / 6.3:1. Forced colours: the hairlines survive as system borders; the split becomes two columns of type with no visible boundary between them, which is what it already is. No motion at any value, and the head never sticks — there is no scroll behaviour in this design to disable.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. Every avatar carries Image focus — P0·9's shared control, both axes, in its Image Picker popover, never as a hidden field; the values are not restated here — P0·9 defines them once for the whole library.

Item counts are a number pickerQuotes shown is now a number picker, 1 to 4, default three, where it drew Two · Three · Four. The cap is this design's own: past four the right column outruns the head beside it.
The Remove button never greys outRemove was never dimmed here and still is not, at any count. Removing the last row leaves the list empty, the section then does not render, and the editor keeps the empty repeater and its Add quote control rather than drawing a placeholder person.
Member buttons are conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up pop-up needs JavaScriptThe section link may target Ghost's Portal, so two notes now sit on the Link Picker: where the target is a member action it does not render on a connected site with self-signup switched off or no payment provider connected, and where it opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up, with JavaScript off nothing happens and the reader stays where they are. Separate from Member visibility, which hides the whole section server-side. A8 draws no labelled button anywhere, so the section link is the only surface this rule touches here.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject, and nothing to withdraw: this design holds no subscribe and no sign-in field, so there is nothing for the notice to replace, and no sentence here ever promised a subscription without JavaScript. With JavaScript off this design is unchanged — every quotation, name, role and organisation is server-rendered.
Ghost’s templates cannot count, add, or rememberThe step-down and the character counters are editor values: the editor counts the authored string as it is typed and writes one value onto the item, and the stylesheet reads it. “N of M drawn” is written by the editor too — a template cannot count its own loop, and no arithmetic happens as the page is served.
Avatars with no photographWith no photograph the attribution closes up — no circle, no initials, no indent held for it — and Avatars Hidden draws the same thing. The two-initial form lands on 11 Faces only, the one design in A8 with a circle to fill; nothing here is pulled from Ghost, so the one-letter rule never applies in this category.
