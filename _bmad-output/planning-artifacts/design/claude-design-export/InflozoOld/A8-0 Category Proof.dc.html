A8 TESTIMONIALS · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

Nothing here comes from Ghost. That is the category.

A7's fifteen drew almost nothing a site typed: names, prices and benefits all arrived from Ghost's tiers. A8 is the mirror. Ghost has no testimonial, review or endorsement resource, so every word in these fifteen designs is authored in the section — which makes the field list, its caps and its empty states the whole engineering problem, and makes the arrangements the whole design problem.

This file carries the four settlements §8 asks A8 to make, the quote ladder, the attribution block, the rules all fifteen share, the roster, the shared field list, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame and the consistency pass. Read it before the designs. Where a design's own panel and this file disagree, the drawn panel is the authority and the disagreement is a defect to be logged.

THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · ALL FOUR ANSWERS ARE INVENTED, AND FLAGGED HERE AND ON EVERY FRAME THAT DRAWS THEM
SETTLEMENT 1 · QUOTE LENGTH · AND WHAT 300 CHARACTERS DOES TO A CARD

40 characters at the floor, 300 at the ceiling, and the ceiling is hard. Under 40 a quote is a slogan and reads as one the site wrote itself; over 300 it is an excerpt from a post, and the editor names A19 Featured, which links to the piece instead of quoting it. A quote is never truncated, clamped, faded or given a “read more” at any width or any value. What happens instead is the step-down, carried verbatim from A4·16 Pull Quote: above 180 characters the quote renders one value below its design's step on the ladder, and above 260 characters two, measured from the authored string and disclosed under the size control rather than silently disagreeing with it. The card grows; the row it sits in grows with it; its neighbours stretch and are honestly unequal in content.

THE THREE THINGS THAT ARE NOT COMPONENTS
The quotation marks are typed characters in the authored string, A4·16's rule — never a ::before, never a font's own glyph substituted in, and never removed from a string that has them. A site writing in a language with other marks types those. There is no decorative quote glyph anywhere in A8. The 120 px grey “ behind a testimonial is the trope this category most obviously reaches for; it takes a display moment the quotation itself has earned. Refused at every value in all fifteen. ⚑ Mine. Image focus is one shared control, and it is P0·9's. Where a design crops a photograph into a frame of its own shape, focus says which part survives the crop. The control and its values are defined once in P0·9 and are deliberately not restated in this category — both axes, their defaults, the one-control-per-slot rule and the rule for narrowing an axis all live there, so the side-to-side axis added by the owner's ruling of 3 September 2026 is in force in A8 by reference rather than by restatement. It sits under two kinds of slot here, in the Image Picker popover and never as a hidden field: 15 Overlap's band and every item's avatar. Ghost never sees it — it is a hint the compiler resolves into the crop the theme ships with, which is why it is a control and not a data binding, why it declares no behaviour module, and why nothing about it is lost with JavaScript off. 8 Portrait withholds it, naming its crop instead; the refusal is kept as that design's own decision and the form it takes is open for the owner. There is no accent by default. A testimonial section asks a reader for nothing, so most of the fifteen spend the accent zero times. It appears in exactly three places: the optional text link at the section's foot, 11 Faces' active tab underline, and a focus ring. This is the first category in the library where the resting state of most designs has no accent in it at all.

THE RULES ALL FIFTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE
Structure. <section aria-labelledby> named by its title, an <h2>; the set a <ul> of <li>, each holding A4·16's figure: <figure> → <blockquote><p> + <figcaption>, so the attribution is programmatically the quotation's source. There is not one <h3> in the whole category — a quotation is not a heading at any size, so unlike A7's fifteen these sections have exactly one heading each. Two designs have no <h2> and take no <aria-labelledby> rather than being labelled by invented text: 8 Portrait, 10 Big Quote, 12 Rows and 14 Slim Line — the first two because they draw no title at any value, the last two permanently. ⚑ Amended after the fifteen were drawn; see the consistency pass. No cite attribute and no schema.org/Review markup anywhere. cite takes a URL for the source document and a reader's letter has none; review markup invites a star rating into a search result that no reader ever gave. ⚑ Both refusals mine. The avatar is aria-hidden and takes no alt text, because the name it sits beside is already text; the initials fallback goes with it. A photograph of a person whose name is printed 12 px to the right is decoration in the technical sense, and describing it twice is the same fact read twice. Nothing in A8 is a link except the optional section link, and 11 Faces' tabs are its only <button>s. Names are not links, avatars are not links, cards are not links, and a whole-card link is refused at every value: a card whose only destination is the quotation it already shows is a link to nowhere. Section padding Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132, 80 at 834, 64 at 390; page margin 72 / 40 / 20; content width 1,296. Two designs carry their own scale: 9 Contrast Band's band at 44 · 64 · 88 (A4·9's) and 14 Slim Line's at 32 · 44 · 56 (A7·8's). Motion: two behaviours in the category — 7 Slider's rail and 11 Faces' tabs. Both are 160 ms ease-out, both are instant under reduced motion, neither runs while editing, and every frame in A8 is a resting state. Hover on a card does nothing: no lift, no shadow, no border change, no scale. The only hovers in the category are on the link, the arrows and the tabs. Focus is A6's ring verbatim: 2 px accent, 2 px outside, the inner gap taking the ground it sits on, the carried colour on a band. Forced colours keep every hairline and drop the band; the striped placeholder becomes a bordered box. Responsive floor. Three or more cards hold above 1081; at 1080 and below the columned designs take 2 Three Up's two-column grid, and at ≤ 767 one column at 16 px apart in authored order, card padding 24, quote at the phone step, the attribution unchanged. The authored order is the drawn order at every width in all fifteen — nothing is sorted by length, nothing is shuffled, and no design promotes a quote with an avatar over one without. Empty. No eyebrow, sub, note or link → absent. No avatar → the block closes up. No role and no organisation → the name alone. No quotes → the section does not render. One quote where a design wants three → the design named in its panel. A quote past 180 or 260 characters → the step-down, disclosed at the size control.
FIVE STATED REFUSALS No rating, score, star, tick or “verified” mark, at any value, in any design. Ghost stores none of it, a site typing five stars into a section is typing its own review of itself, and the mark carries a promise of a review system standing behind it. A4·16 refused this for one quotation; A8 refuses it for fifteen designs. No date, source, post link or “as seen in” on a quote. A dated quotation reads as journalism with a provenance, and there is no provenance here beyond the site's word. No organisation logo. The organisation is a text field on the attribution line; A11 Logo Walls owns marks, and a wall of client logos beside three quotations is two sections in one. The quote field is plain text. No bold, no italic, no links, no line breaks and no lists inside a quotation — a site emphasising a phrase inside someone else's sentence is editing it in the reader's mouth. Ellipses and square brackets are typed by the author, which is how editors have always marked a trim. No “+4 more”, no pagination, no “load more”. A design draws its stated count and the panel says what is not drawn. ⚑ All five are mine.

Four things to notice. The quotation takes the pack's heading font at the pack's own regular weight — Georgia 400 and Bricolage 500 are the two packs' “roman”, and setting the ladder in sizes rather than weights is what lets a variable sans and a static serif both read as a quotation rather than a headline. The radius token reaches the card and nothing else: the avatar is a circle in all three packs because it is a circle, not a rounded rectangle, so Tangerine's 14 and Ink's 6 have nowhere else to land in this category. The striped image placeholder is derived per pack — each pack's own two-step warm or cool pair rather than Paper's, which is why the Ink frames read cool and the Tangerine frames warm at the same 45°. And no frame here contains an accent pixel, in either mode: A8's accent lives on the optional link, 11 Faces' active tab and a focus ring, none of which are in a resting Three Up.
