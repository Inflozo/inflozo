Centred

Tag, title, standfirst and byline on one axis, the picture beneath them at the content width. It is the arrangement most readers have already seen a thousand times, which is the reason it is the default: a post header's job is to be recognised in the half second before anyone decides to read, and recognition is not a place to be original.

It is also the design that settles the category's numbers. The 820 title measure, the 24 px gap under the eyebrow, the 20 under the title, the 40 above the picture and the 64 to the article are all set here and inherited by fifteen designs. When a design switch has nowhere obvious to land, it lands here, and it is the frame the stress test and the tokenisation proof are both drawn on.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES Six controls and none of them is a colour, a size in pixels or a font. Alignment, Title size, Standfirst lines, Image ratio, Rule, Padding — and the whole panel is arrangement. The pack decides what the type looks like and the post decides what it says; the user decides where it sits and how much of it there is, which is the only decision the section can safely offer across twelve packs. The Post block is the category's replacement for the Posts block, and this is the design where it is drawn at rest. Fifteen designs narrow, set, lock, ignore or interpret one of its four fields; this one takes all four as they come, which is what makes it the safe destination for a design switch — no field arrives here with nowhere to live. Cut, and named so nobody re-proposes them: a title-colour control (a coloured h1 is a pack decision and usually a mistake); an eyebrow-style control offering a filled chip (settlement 4 refused it category-wide); a "show updated date" toggle (A26); and a caption-position control — the caption sits under the picture in every design that has one, because a caption above a photograph is a standfirst that lies about which thing it describes. What I would have needed to know: whether P0·9’s per-slot rule locates the control under the Post block’s Feature image row when Ghost owns the photograph and the section has no image slot of its own. Why it is open: P0·9 says the control is drawn under the image slot it belongs to, and A24 has no image field — the picture is the post’s. What I did instead: left the row exactly where the reconciliation pass put it, beside Feature image, drew both axes there, and claimed nothing about a slot.
