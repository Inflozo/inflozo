Marginalia

The article on its measure with figure captions — and optionally pull quotes — placed in a 240 column in one margin, beside the block they belong to rather than under it.

It is the only design in A25 that moves the author's own elements, and it does it with grid placement rather than a card, a script or a duplicated node: nothing moves in the DOM, and an author who writes a caption in Ghost gets a margin note without knowing this design exists. Where Ghost has no primitive to move — footnotes, asides — the design says so instead of inventing one.

WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLESWhat moves out has two values and not four. Footnotes, asides and margin notes were drawn and refused, all for the same reason: Ghost emits none of them ⚑. A footnote card does not exist, an aside is a callout, and a design cannot move an element the content model has no way to produce — which is the finding this design contributes: if the product wants true marginalia it needs a card, and that is the architect's decision, not a layout's.There is no caption-length threshold, and no control for one. ⚑ A stylesheet cannot count characters — and a user who could set a limit would be setting how long a caption may be, which is a content rule wearing a layout control’s clothes; and there is nothing left to state about length: every caption goes to the margin column however long it runs, is clipped to its own row and scrolls inside it, and no words are lost.Cut: a margin column width. 240 is 1 Measured's index column and A24·12's rail, and a third width for the same margin would make the library's side columns three sizes. Cut: both margins at once — captions on one side and quotes on the other reads as two designs interleaved, and the squint test fails on it. Cut: a caption size, which Type scale would then contradict.
