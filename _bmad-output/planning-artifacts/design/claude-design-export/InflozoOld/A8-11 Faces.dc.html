A8 TESTIMONIALS · DESIGN 11 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONLY TABLIST IN THE CATEGORY

Faces

The attributions become the control: a row of avatars and names, one quotation shown at a time. The tabs take A5·12's structure and behaviour — role="tablist", arrow-key roving focus, a 2 px accent underline on the active one from A1·1's nav item — and the cross-fade is 160 ms, instant under reduced motion. Three properties of the tab itself are changed here and flagged: resting and active weights go up one step to 500 and 600, and hover takes the hover surface as a plane where A5·12 has none. It is one of only two behaviours in A8, the other being 7 Slider's rail.

And it has a cost the other fourteen do not: five of six quotations are hidden on load. They are in the DOM and reachable, but they are not on the page, so a reader scanning does not see them and a browser's find does not match them. The design states that plainly, spends its one accent on making the row look like a control, and gives the behaviour up entirely on a phone, where a tab row of six faces would be a scroll inside a scroll.

ARCHITECT · tabs REGISTRY RULING · UNSETTLED · THIS FLAG STAYS ON THE FRAME This design declares tabs, whose degradation the registry states as “All panels render stacked and visible, each preceded by its tab label as a heading.” A8 has no <h3> in fifteen designs and holds that a reader's name is not a heading at any size, so with JavaScript off this one design would put three to six people's names into the document outline. The registry is the authority on degradation, so its sentence stands as quoted and the no-JS state is drawn below either way. Either 11 Faces takes <h3> names in its no-JS branch alone, or tabs takes a stated exception for a category whose panel labels are attributions. No module name is coined here and none is needed — the closest module is the one already declared.

Resting: name at 500 in text-muted, avatar at 70%. Hover: the hover surface behind the tab, the name to full text, the avatar to 100% — a departure from A5·12, whose tab is 400 resting and 500 active with no plane at any state. Focus: A6's 2 px ring, 2 px out, the gap taking the ground. Active: name at 600 in text, avatar at 100%, and the 2 px accent underline sitting over the row's hairline. One transition per state change, 160 ms. The avatar's opacity is the only fade in A8, and it is 70% to 100% on a photograph rather than a colour change, because there is no token for “a quieter photograph”. On the initials fallback the same step is the muted text going to full strength. ⚑ The 70% resting opacity, the 500 / 600 weights and the hover plane are mine: a 44 px row with a 36 px photograph in it answers a pointer poorly with a colour change alone, and a 400-weight name beside a photograph reads faint. Active is never colour alone: the underline, the weight and the avatar all change together, so a reader who cannot see the accent still has two other signals. This is A5·12's rule and the reason the tab is not just an accent-coloured name.

Separate from Member visibility, which hides the whole section server-side. A8 draws no labelled button anywhere, so the section link is the only surface this rule touches here.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject for the notice: this design holds no subscribe and no sign-in field. With JavaScript off every panel renders stacked and visible, each preceded by its tab label as a heading — the registry's sentence. Ruled by the owner on 30 August 2026: this design takes those names as headings in its no-JavaScript branch alone, so a reader without scripts gets every quotation clearly labelled; with scripts on nothing changes and no other design in A8 gains a heading. The alternative — stacking the quotations with the names in ordinary text — was refused as harder to scan and as a change to shared behaviour other categories depend on. No exception to the module and no module name was coined.
Ghost’s templates cannot count, add, or rememberThe step-down and the character counters are editor values: the editor counts the authored string as it is typed and writes one value onto the item, and the stylesheet reads it. “N of M drawn” is written by the editor too — a template cannot count its own loop, and no arithmetic happens as the page is served. The two initials are the same mechanism, derived in the editor rather than by the template.
Avatars with no photographTwo initials, and the editor writes them. This list is typed by the site, so the two-letter form is the correct one — the one-letter rule is for authors pulled from Ghost, and A8 pulls nothing from Ghost at all. Because a template cannot take initials from a name, the editor derives them as the name is typed and the template only prints them. They stay “aria-hidden”: the name beside them is already text.
