10 · Cover

The gate over a photograph: a full-bleed cover 520 tall carrying a warm scrim, with the copy centred on it and every colour on the cover derived from the carried light rather than from the page.

It is the only A32 design with an image, and the only one whose image is required rather than optional — a cover with no photograph is not a quieter version of this design, it is a different one.

PATCH PASS · 29 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED IN THIS DESIGN No image no longer hands off to 4 Contrast Band. The cover draws its scrim colour as a flat ground at the same height ⚑ and the gate sits on it exactly as drawn — same height, same measure, same alignment. The editor still says a photograph is expected, and the panel advises 4 Contrast Band without switching to it. (No design ever turns into another design.) The fade at the cut is a fixed height, and the two-line rule is deleted. It is 96, 160 or 240 px of the page ground anchored to the end of the preview, whatever is above it. The old rule suppressed it “under two lines of visible text”, and a stylesheet cannot count lines ⚑ — so a one-line last paragraph would have faded to nothing. Suppression after a heading, a figure, a code block or an embed stays: that is an element type, which CSS can see. (CSS cannot see content.) The action is conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up window is JavaScript. The primary button does not render at all where the connected site cannot take it — self-signup switched off, or no payment provider connected — and where it does render it opens Ghost’s own sign-up window, so with JavaScript off, nothing happens ⚑. The words, the fade, the measure and every drawn state are unchanged; with no button the gate is its words and the sign-in line ⚑. Both facts are now read-only rows in the panel and mono lines on every gate. (Member buttons are conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up pop-up needs JavaScript.)

WHAT THE FOUR GATES SETTLE FOR THE WHOLE CATEGORY
Four gates, one arrangement. Free-signup · paid · upgrade (a paid post, a signed-in free member) · specific tier ⚑. It must not offer an email field: Ghost already has the address.A reader with access never sees the section ⚑ — no unlocked state, no thank-you, no dismissal. The free gate is the only one whose action a theme can complete itself ⚑.

The upgrade gate over a cover is the category’s loudest frame ⚑ and the panel names 1 Fade as the alternative. The tier gate is where the cover earns itself: a named tier with a photograph reads as a product, which is what a Patron tier is ⚑.

WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE · THE THREE STATES EVERY A32 DESIGN OWES
In the editor the gate draws at its resting state ⚑ — the paid gate, the fade at its control value, no hover and no ring. Nothing in A32 has a behaviour to preview, so the edit-time frame and the published frame are the same picture. With JavaScript off the section is drawn identically and every action in it is dead ⚑ — the gate is server-rendered HTML, the fade is a gradient and the suppression rules are :last-child selectors, but every action opens Ghost’s Portal, which is JavaScript. 1 Fade and 6 Split Pitch replace their email field with the no-JavaScript notice; 11 Sticky Bar hides its close button; 12 Meter keeps its static reading-time line and loses the fill — the label needs no script and the fill is the module’s. And no member button renders at all where the connected site cannot take it ⚑. A reader who has access never sees this section ⚑ — Ghost sends the whole post and A32 does not render. There is no “unlocked” state to design, and no design may draw one.

Focus on the cover is the carried colour at 2 px ⚑ — the accent measures 2.9:1 against a 45 % scrim over a mid-tone photograph and fails, so it is not used ⚑. That is A20·13’s substitution, carried.

The fence sees a fragment the size of a fist at eight hundred kilometres, roughly. The word doing the work in that sentence is roughly: a fragment is not a sphere, it tumbles, and its radar cross-section changes while it does. The same piece of debris can appear to grow and shrink across one pass.

PASS FIVE · 3 SEPTEMBER 2026 · IMAGE FOCUS IS ONE SHARED CONTROL, DEFINED IN P0·9
One control name means one set of values. Image focus was never a shared control — two dozen category specs each wrote out their own copy of its values. It is defined once now, in P0·9, and this panel cites it instead of listing anything: the control row above carries the label and the two rows, and the values live in P0·9 alone. Both axes are drawn. The side-to-side axis is the owner's ruling of 3 September 2026 and this category did not have it. It earns itself here: the cover is the only frame in A32 that crops a photograph, and a wide picture in a cover 520 tall loses its sides as readily as its top. Neither axis is narrowed, so nothing greys and no constraint is claimed. Ghost never sees this. Image focus is a hint the compiler resolves into the crop the theme ships — the position the photograph is anchored at inside the cover. Nothing about the choice reaches Ghost and Ghost sends nothing back, which is why it is a control and not a data binding. It follows that it declares no behaviour module and nothing about it fails with JavaScript off: this design's no-JavaScript line is unchanged — the section is drawn identically and the button opens nothing. The count did not move. One label with two segmented rows is one control, so 10 Cover still declares nine of its own, with the universals, the Data group and the Access source group outside it.
