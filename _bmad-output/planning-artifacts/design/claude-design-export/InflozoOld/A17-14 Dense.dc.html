A17 POST GRIDS · DESIGN 14 OF 18 · PAPER PACK · FIVE CONTROLS

Dense

Six 196 px cells across, image and title only. The narrowest cell in A17 and the only design that uses the Six division — twelve or eighteen posts without a scroll, at the point where a feature image is still a picture and not yet a thumbnail.

It sits between two designs that solve the same problem differently. 10 Ledger drops the image to fit an archive; 13 Thumb Side shrinks it to a fixed square. This one keeps the image proportional and shrinks the cell instead — so it is the densest design in the category that still reads as a grid of photographs.

What it gives up is everything else. No excerpt at any setting, no author, no reading time, and the tag is optional — a 196 px cell holds a picture, a title of about four words a line, and a date. The panel is explicit that this is a design about how many, not about any single post.

Twelve posts in two rows of six 196 px cells, on the 24 px gutter, with 36 px between rows. The row gap comes down from the category's 48 because a 131 px image needs less air beneath it than a 277 px one — the proportion is what matters, and 36 against a 131 px image is the same relation as 48 against 175. ⚑ Mine.
Titles run to three lines here and are still not clamped. A 196 px cell at 17 px Georgia holds about four words a line. Measured on this frame, post 2's 69-character title takes three lines and post 3's takes three; every other title takes two. A17-0's rule holds at the category's narrowest measure, which is the real test of it: the row's height comes from its tallest cell, every card sits at the top of its own, and the ragged bottom edge is the honest cost of never cutting a title.
The date is abbreviated to “12 Mar” without the year — the second design in A17 to abbreviate, after 10 Ledger, and for the same reason: at 196 px “12 March 2026” wraps to two lines and a date on two lines reads as a second title. The datetime attribute carries the full date.
Post 3's plate is a 196 × 131 box with its tag at 11 px, the same one-step-down treatment 13 Thumb Side uses and for the same reason. At this size a plate is nearly invisible in the grid, which is the compensation for everything else this design gives up.

SIX · 196 · THE DEFAULT AND THE FLOOR · AN IMAGE 131 PX TALL IS STILL A PICTURE
Six is the floor and the reason is the image, not the title. A seventh column puts the cell at 164 px and the image at 109 px tall — below about 120 px a landscape photograph stops having a subject you can identify and becomes a texture. A17's ladder stops at Six for the same reason, which is why this design uses the last division rather than inventing one. ⚑ The 120 px floor is mine. Per row Four exists and the panel does not recommend it: at 306 px this design is 3 Four Up with its excerpt and meta removed, which is a worse 3 Four Up rather than a denser Dense. Five at 240 is the only place in A17 the Five division appears — it is a genuine middle setting, and it exists because twelve posts in five columns is two rows and a two-cell orphan, which some archives prefer to two clean rows of six. Cut: a Seven and an Eight. Cut: a ratio control offering Portrait — a 196 px portrait cell is 245 px tall and the grid becomes a wall of postage stamps. Landscape 3:2 and Square 1:1 are the two ratios offered, and square is the denser of the two because 196 is shorter than 131 × 1.5.

TWELVE AT SIX ACROSS · TWO CLEAN ROWS · ⚑ POSTS 10–12 ARE ADDED FOR THIS DESIGN, BEYOND A17-0's NINE
Twelve and eighteen are the counts that divide by six, and the editor's counter names them: “12 posts fills two rows exactly.” At Count 9 the second row is three cells of six, which is the category's short-last-row rule doing exactly what it always does — and at six across an orphan row is more conspicuous than anywhere else in A17, because half a row of empty space is 636 px wide. ⚑ Disclosed, not solved. It passes the squint test on the strength of the image grid alone. At 25% the twelve images are a regular field of twelve rectangles and the titles are grey texture beneath them — there is no focal point, and for this design that is correct rather than a failure. The brief's squint test asks for one composition with an obvious focal point; a design whose subject is “how much has been published” has the grid itself as its focal point, and I am recording that as a considered exception rather than pretending otherwise. ⚑ Mine, and arguable. This is the design where A17's refusal of load-more and infinite-scroll costs the most, because eighteen cells is where a reader most plausibly wants more. 16 Load More is the design that answers it, and a site that wants density plus growth should use that one at six across — which the panel says: “For more than eighteen, try Load More.”

WHAT IS MISSING FROM THIS LIST, AND WHY EACH ABSENCE IS STRUCTURALThis design already governed Tag and Date, so the category-wide Tag row changes nothing. Padding retired into Vertical spacing; “View all” link added.No Meta enum and no With photograph ⚑ — there is no author or reading time at any setting to enumerate, and a 24 px avatar in a 196 px cell would be a quarter of its width. There is no Excerpt control, and this is the only design in A17 without one in any form. Every other design offers Off plus at least one line count; a 196 px cell cannot hold a legible excerpt at all — 15 px body text on a 196 px measure is about five words a line, and three lines of that under a four-line title is a paragraph competing with its own heading. The absence is structural rather than a judgement about density. There is no Meta control either — Tag and Date are two separate toggles instead. Everywhere else in A17 meta is one enumerated control (None · Date only · Name and date…); here there is no author and no reading time to enumerate, so an enum of two values would be two toggles wearing a dropdown. ⚑ Mine, and it makes this design's control list incomparable with the rest of the category — which the brief expects and permits. Tag defaults to Off, the only design in A17 where it does. At six across a tag above a title puts two lines of small type above the picture's title, and the eyebrow at 11 px beside a 17 px title is the wrong ratio. It is offered because a tag-heavy publication may want it, and the default says which way the design leans. Cut: a hover that reveals the excerpt. It is the obvious answer to a cell with no room, and A17-0 refuses it — a hover-only excerpt is invisible on a touch screen and to a keyboard user it appears on focus, which moves the layout. The registry has no module for it either. Stated as a finding: if the build wants excerpt-on-hover it needs a new module, and I am not naming one.

Six becomes four at 834, not three. The category's ladder goes five → four → three; this design stops at four because three cells at 235 px is 3 Four Up's territory and a Dense that looks like Four Up has stopped being dense. Twelve images at 196 × 131 on a deep ground is the case where dark mode helps most in A17. In light, twelve pale rectangles on a pale page rely on the image's own edges to separate them; on a dark ground each photograph is a lit object and the grid reads as a contact sheet, which is what this design is. No token changes to make that happen — ground, text, muted, plate, and the photographs untouched. The plate is the least noticeable it is anywhere in the category, which follows the size rule 13 Thumb Side's frame states: a plate's cost in dark is proportional to its area.
THE WRITTEN SPEC · ALL TEN FIELDS
1 · Descriptor. Six 196 px cells across carrying an image, a title and an abbreviated date and nothing else — the category's narrowest cell and its only use of the Six division. 2 · Structural descriptor. grid-of-N · none · page · many · top · smallest cell
One of the group sharing the five closed slots; distinguished by the emphasis phrase, and here it is a measurable claim rather than a mood — no other design in A17 uses a cell below 240 px. 3 · Archetype. grid-of-N. Two departures. At 834 it stops at four rather than the ladder's three, because three at 235 px is 3 Four Up. At ≤ 767 it keeps two columns rather than collapsing to one — one of only two designs in A17 that do, and the only one of the two with a photograph. 4 · Responsive rule. 1440 six 196 cells, 24 gutter, 36 row gap, image 131 at 3:2, title 17. 1080 five at 240. 834 four at 170 (4 × 170 + 3 × 24 = 752 in a 754 measure), image 114, title 16 — ⚑ 114 is 6 px under the design's own 120 px picture floor, accepted and flagged. ≤ 767 two at 167, image 111, title 16, date kept, tag dropped if on. Never one column at any width. 5 · Content fields. Section: the six, all optional, link as a pair. Query: all four. Post: title, url, published_at, feature_image + feature_image_alt, primary_tag at Tag On. Five fields — the fewest of any image-bearing design in A17. Never reads custom_excerpt, primary_author or reading_time at any setting. Reads featured, never draws it; visibility read, never drawn. 6 · Controls. Per row Four · Five · Six. Image ratio Landscape · Square (no Portrait, no Wide). Tag On · Off (defaults Off — the only design in A17 where it does). Date On · Off. “View all” link Matches the query · Custom. Five, plus the universal trio (Background role · Vertical spacing · Top divider) and the Data group, both outside this list. No Excerpt control in any form — the only design in A17 without one — and no Meta enum, because there is no author or reading time to enumerate.
7 · Data. Ghost's #get "posts" helper per the shared Data group. Designed for 12 and 18; correct at 3 to 18. 0: section does not render; in the editor, six outlined cells and the query line. 1 or 2: drawn at 196 px at the left — this design's weakest case by a distance, because one 196 px cell on a 1,296 px page is a stamp in a field of white, and the panel suggests a wider design rather than switching design: “One or two posts read better in a wider design.” Counts not divisible by six: short last row at the left, and the orphan is more conspicuous here than anywhere else in A17 — half a row is 636 px of space. The counter names the divisors: “12 posts fills two rows exactly.” Above 18: not drawn; the panel points at 16 Load More rather than at A34. 8 · Empty state. No image → a 196 × 131 plate with the tag at 11 px, and at this size it is the least conspicuous plate in the category. No tag either → plain plate. No image on any post → no design switch, and the result is twelve plates, which is poor but legible; at this size a wall of plates is a texture rather than a series of holes, which is why this design is not in the group that draws its cells without their image boxes, and the panel may advise 9 Big Type. Section fields absent → not drawn. 9 · Behaviour module. None; core assumed. No-JS degradation: pixel-identical with JavaScript off. Two modules were considered and neither is declared. load-more belongs to 16 Load More and a site wanting both should use that design at six across. And there is no module for an excerpt on hover, which is the thing a 196 px cell most wants — a finding for the architect, and I am not naming a module for it. Edit-safe. 10 · Accessibility. <section aria-labelledby> → <h2>; no accessible name at Head None. One <ul> of <li>s, title an <h3> in each card's <a>, A17-0's DOM order honoured. The 17 px title is above the category's 15 px body floor, and the 11 px tag inside the plate is the smallest type anywhere in A17 — it is aria-hidden decoration on a box whose post's tag is already announced when Tag is On, and at Tag Off it is the only place the tag appears, which is a real gap I am flagging rather than solving. Date a <time datetime> carrying the full ISO date behind the abbreviated “12 Mar”. A6's ring on the whole cell; the cell is 196 × ~200 px, far above the 44 px touch minimum. Contrast: 15.46:1 and 5.56:1 light, 15.64:1 and 6.89:1 dark.
