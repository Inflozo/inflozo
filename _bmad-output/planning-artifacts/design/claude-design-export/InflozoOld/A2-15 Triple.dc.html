Triple

Three short notices side by side in one band, divided by hairlines — the only design that shows more than one message at once without moving. It closes the category by answering the question Ticker and Rotator both dodge: what if the reader should see all three at a glance? Below 768 the band becomes one slot and the notices cycle through it — five seconds each, crossfading in, looping — so the design shows in sequence what will not fit at once, and stays itself at every width (Part A·A8).

Below 768 the band is one slot and the notices cycle through it — five seconds each, crossfading in, looping. Owner’s ruling, patch pass two, superseding the single static slot. The label stays, the sentence wraps in full, one close serves the whole cycle, and the band stays 77 px — the indicator is A2·9’s inline “1/3” at the end of the sentence, the owner’s pick over a row of dots, so the cycle costs the band nothing. Height is fixed at the tallest notice in the cycle, measured once, so the page never moves under the reader mid-turn. Every drawn notice now reaches a phone reader, which the old single slot did not; authored order decides where the cycle starts and in what sequence it runs. Still this design, not A2·9 (Part A·A8): its own label, its own ground, its own close, and no dot rail at the right end.

Reduced motion draws the no-JavaScript band with the “1/3” back on it — no auto-advance and no crossfade, but notices two and three still have to be reachable, so the indicator stays and swaps instantly on press. The cycle pauses on focus entering the band and on hover where there is a pointer, and stops for the session when the “1/3” is pressed, which also advances one; swipe does the same job with no target at all. A2·9’s answer to WCAG 2.2.2, reused rather than reinvented, because content that advances itself and loops has to be stoppable. One divergence from A2·9, flagged as mine ⚑: there, only Dots can be pressed and Numbers are inert. Here the “1/3” is pressable — it is the only indicator on a band with no hover, so an inert one would leave 2.2.2 unanswered and notices two and three unreachable under reduced motion. Its 44 px target is made by extending the hit area into the sentence’s trailing space, not by growing the numerals.

Labels are text in the same paragraph as their sentence. 3 · A clipped sentence is a real accessibility problem, not a visual one: the visible text ends in an ellipsis but the element's text is complete, so screen readers get the whole sentence and sighted readers do not. Where a slot clips, its link's accessible name is the full message — and the editor marks clipping per slot rather than hiding it. 4 · With Link style: Whole slot each cell is one link wrapping label, sentence and arrow; with Arrow link only the arrow and sentence are the target. Either way one link per slot, never two. 5 · The close sits outside all three links and last in focus order, on its own ground patch inside the third slot's reserved 24 px. It dismisses all three notices at once — there is no per-slot dismissal, and the sidebar says so, because a band that can lose a column mid-session would re-lay-out under the reader. MEMBER ASK · PART A · A9 · EACH SLOT’S LINK, SLOT BY SLOT It does not render when the connected site cannot honour it — signup closed, members disabled, or no payment provider for a paid ask. The band closes up: no reserved gap, no empty slot. It opens Ghost’s Portal, so with JavaScript off nothing happens. Nothing in this bar is subscribable without script. It renders when members are on and signup is open; a paid ask needs a payment provider connected and a live paid tier as well; a sign-in link needs members on and nothing more. The ask is per slot, so the notes attach to each item’s link in the repeater. A slot whose ask cannot render is not a link under Link style: Whole slot and draws no arrow under Arrow link; the grid keeps its columns and the slot keeps its sentence — an empty cell is the one thing this design cannot show. Below 768 every drawn ask comes round on the cycle — the old “invisible outside slot one” consequence withdraws. It survives for a reader without script or under reduced motion, who gets the first notice only.
