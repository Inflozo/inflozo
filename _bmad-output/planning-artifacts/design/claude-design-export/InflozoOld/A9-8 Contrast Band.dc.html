Contrast Band

1 Accordion inverted onto the contrast token. Same 780 list, same row, same marker, same 620 answer, same behaviour: switching between this design and 1 Accordion changes nothing but the colours. The band, its 44 · 64 · 88 padding scale and every derived mix in it are A8·9's, reused without a new number.

One thing changes and it is not colour. The accent is unavailable on the band — the pack's dark accent is 2.3:1 against the light band and a 2 px indicator needs 3:1 — so the answer's links and the focus ring take the band's carried colour in both modes. A section on a band has no accent pixel in it at all, and the failing ratio is shown at the disabled value rather than being quietly allowed.

The focus ring is the carried colour, 2 px out, with the band itself as the inner gap — A6's ring with one substitution. On the light band that is #FBF9F5 at 15.8:1 against the band, which is a stronger ring than the accent gives on a page, and the only place in A9 where the ring is not accent-coloured. The hover plane is the carried colour at 6%, a 1.15:1 step. It is deliberately at the edge of visible: on a dark band a stronger plane reads as a selected row, and the marker going to full strength is the state a pointer actually needs. The answer's links take the carried colour for both the text and the underline, which makes them the one thing in A9 whose underline matches its text. On a band there is no third colour to underline with, and an accent underline at 2.3:1 would be a decoration a reader cannot see. Inline code on the band takes the carried colour at 6% as its chip — the same mix as the hover plane, which means a hovered row's code chip disappears into the plane. Stated rather than corrected: two 6% fields on one row is a boundary nobody needs to see. ⚑ Mine.

ACCESSIBILITY · 1 ACCORDION'S STRUCTURE, UNCHANGED The band is a background, not an element. One <h2>, a <ul>, <h3> + full-width <summary> per row, the answer the <details>’s own content — no role="region" and no hidden attribute. A8·9's rule: the inversion is a colour decision and carries no structure with it. Contrast on the band: question and answer 15.8:1 light / 14.7:1 dark, muted 8.7:1 / 6.0:1, marker resting 8.7:1 / 6.0:1 and full-strength while open, hairline 2.4:1 as a boundary, focus ring 15.8:1 / 14.7:1 — the strongest ring in A9, because the carried colour is the maximum contrast the band has. The accent is refused at 2.3:1 and the value is shown disabled with its ratio, in both modes, rather than being silently dropped. §7.4's rule, and the one place in A9 a control has a struck-through value. Forced colours drop the band entirely and keep every hairline: the section becomes 1 Accordion in the system's own two colours, which is the correct outcome — a reader who has asked the operating system to pick the colours has refused the inversion. Print drops the band too, and prints every row open in text on white. A page of reversed type is a page of ink, and no reader asked for that. ⚑ Mine.
