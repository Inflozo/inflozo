Tabs

The group labels become a tab row and one group's questions are shown at a time. The tablist is A5·12's structure and behaviour and the tab's own styling is A8·11's — 15 px at 500 resting and 600 active, a hover surface plane, a 2 px accent underline — which is the second use of that departure rather than a new one.

The rows inside a panel are always open. A disclosure inside a tab is two doors to one sentence, and A9-0 refuses it outright — which makes this the only design in A9 where a tab's whole panel of questions and answers is on the page at once. It is also the design that hides the most: three of four groups are not on the page until a reader picks them, and the panel says so and advises 6 Grouped.

The tab is the group label, so no label is drawn inside the panel — which is why the questions here are <h3> and not the <h4> that 6 Grouped and 7 Index use. A label above the rows and a tab saying the same word is the word twice. The tab row is 4 px apart, centred under the head, sitting on a hairline with the active tab's 2 px accent underline over it, and the panel starts 48 px below the row.
The rows are 3 Open List's rows exactly: question above answer, hairline each, none under the last, row padding 16 at Row 17, answer capped 620. The panel is as tall as its own group and the section's height changes when a reader switches tabs — the opposite of A8·11, whose panel was fixed to the longest quotation. Here a group of five and a group of one are honestly different lengths, and forcing the shorter one to the taller one's height would put 400 px of white space under two questions. ⚑ Mine, and stated against A8·11.

Resting 15 px at 500 in text-muted; hover the hover surface behind the tab and the label to full text; focus A6's 2 px ring, 2 px out; active 15 px at 600 in text with the 2 px accent underline over the row's hairline. A8·11 established these weights and the plane against A5·12's 400 / 500 and no plane; this design cites A8·11 and changes nothing. Active is never colour alone — the underline and the weight change together — which is A5·12's rule and the reason the tab is not just an accent-coloured word. There is no avatar here to fade, so this design has two signals where A8·11 had three. 44 px targets at every width, the label centred in the box. 160 ms cross-fade on the panel, instant under reduced motion, nothing on a timer, nothing while editing, the first tab active on load at every value and never remembered. The tab row never scrolls horizontally. A8·11 refused that and so does this: a scroll that hides the choice a section exists to offer is worse than a wrap, and four labels wrap to a second centred row at 834 rather than sliding out of view.

Seven of ten questions are not on the page on load, and neither are their answers. They are in the DOM with the hidden attribute — correct for a tablist — so a reader scanning does not see them and a browser's find does not match them. That is worse than an accordion's cost, which hides answers but shows every question. The panel states it in one line and advises 6 Grouped, which draws every label and every question with nothing hidden. This design is for a FAQ whose groups are genuinely separate audiences — a membership question and a shipping question — and against one whose reader does not know which group their question is in. There is no “All” tab: an All tab is 6 Grouped with a tab row above it, and a tablist whose first tab contains every other tab is not a choice. A deep link selects the tab holding the question, then scrolls the question into view with 96 px above it and puts focus on its heading. The other tabs stay unselected and the URL is not rewritten as a reader switches tabs.

ACCESSIBILITY · A PROPER TABLIST, AND THE HEADING LEVEL IT KEEPS role="tablist" on the row, role="tab" with aria-selected and aria-controls on each, one tab stop with roving tabindex, left and right arrow keys, role="tabpanel" labelled by its tab, inactive panels hidden. A5·12's structure, unchanged. The tab's accessible name is the group label and nothing else — no count, no “tab 2 of 4”. The questions are <h3>, not <h4>, because there is no group heading inside the panel to be their parent — the tab is the label. It is the one design that reads groups[] and keeps A9-0's original heading level, and the difference from 6 Grouped and 7 Index is noted in all three files. The rows inside a panel have no buttons and no regions. Nothing to expand, so the panel is prose under headings: the only focusable things inside it are links in answers. A reader who tabs past the tab row lands on the panel's first link, not on a row. The cost, in a11y terms: seven of ten questions carry hidden, so they are absent from the heading rotor as well as from find-in-page. A reader using headings to scan sees three questions and four tabs. 6 Grouped shows them fourteen headings instead, and the panel names it. Contrast: active tab 15.8:1 light / 15.6:1 dark, resting 5.6:1 / 6.8:1, underline 3.3:1 / 6.3:1, question and answer 15.8:1 / 15.1:1. Forced colours: the selected state comes from aria-selected and a system-coloured underline, so the row still reads as tabs with no accent at all. Print: every group printed in full with its label, as 6 Grouped — a printed page with three tabs and one panel is a page missing three quarters of its content. ⚑ Mine.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesEyebrow, title, sub, note and the link label edit inline with the P0·1 toolbar — bold · italic · underline · link, the popover carrying open-in-new-tab and rel nofollow · noreferrer · sponsored. Questions and answers edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The answer raises the marks toolbar on selection with bold · italic · link · inline code and no underline — an underline inside prose is a link that is not one. Recorded as a PRD delta. Group labels edit inline too — they live in a second repeater, so renaming one on canvas renames the group for every question in it, and the confirm says so. Nothing in A9 is Ghost-owned, so nothing here says “Edit in Ghost” — every word on the page is the site’s own. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Questions are the P0·3 item list: Add question arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. A tab label is a group label, so renaming a tab on canvas renames the group. Open rows is not offered: the rows inside a panel are always open.
