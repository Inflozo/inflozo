Big Type

The site's name set large enough to be the last thing on the page, with the links and the legal line kept small above it. This is the category's one display moment, and the only design where the wordmark is type rather than a lockup — the mark is dropped, because a 26 px square beside 128 px letters is a mistake at every size.

128 px Georgia at −0.045em, line-height 0.84, with the descender allowance pulled back so the baseline sits 26 px above the page's bottom edge rather than the font's own metrics deciding it. The links stay 15 px muted and the legal line 13 px — they are furniture and the name is the picture. Nothing here spends accent: at this size the letterforms are the emphasis, and an accent word would fight them.

Fill width sizes the name to span its measure on as few lines as the 44 px floor allows. A name too long for one line shrinks until it fits two; a name too long for two at the floor takes a third rather than going below it — owner's ruling this pass: smaller type, but the whole name, never a cut and never a switch to another design (Part A·A8). Measured on this design's own drawn names, a character costs 0.44 em including the tracking, consistent at 76, 96, 128 and 158 px. That puts two lines at the floor near 134 characters on the 1296 px measure and near 36 on a phone's 350 — so the floor is a stated bound at both widths rather than one a publication name meets, and one floor serves every width. Those are ceilings from the advance alone; word boundaries land a real name below them, as the 460 px card shows. Line-height opens from 0.84 to 0.9 once there is more than one line, because tight leading on stacked serif capitals closes the counters.

Light type on a deep ground looks about 2% wider at this size, so dark tightens the tracking one notch — −0.045em to −0.043em — and takes no other change. That is the only optical correction in the category, and it exists because 128 px is the one place the difference is visible.

At ≤ 767 the scale is always Fill width whatever the control says, because a fixed 128 px cannot fit 350 px of measure — the control's value is remembered and returns above the breakpoint. A twelve-character name fills the 350 px measure on one line at 64 px — the measured advance is 0.442 em, so 64 px sets 339 px of ink and 66 would sit exactly on the measure and break at the space. Well clear of the floor either way, so the rule takes the single line and the larger type rather than the word break. Leading stays at 0.84 with one line, and the baseline sits 20 px off the bottom. A name long enough to need the floor wraps here as it does anywhere. The 44 px floor serves this measure too, on the measurement above: 350 px at 0.44 em holds about 18 characters a line, so two lines reach about 36 — “Columbia Journalism Review” (26) and “The Los Angeles Review of Books” (31) both set at or above the floor. One floor at every width; a name past 36 characters here takes a third line rather than going below it, the same rule the desktop measure follows. ⚑ An earlier draft of this pass invented a second, lower floor for phones on a mis-measured advance; it is withdrawn.
