Masthead

The publication's nameplate at the top of its own front page: the site's name set large and centred between rules, an issue line on the rule above it, and the headline beneath as a standfirst. The only hero that draws the site title from Ghost, the only one where the largest words are not the h1, and the only one with no action by default.

Two rules, 1100 px wide — narrower than the page's 1296 measure, so the nameplate reads as a plate rather than as a band. The name is 72 px with 1.0 leading; the issue line sits on the upper rule at the eyebrow's own size and tracking, and the lower rule closes the plate. Below it the headline is 26 px in the heading font and the sub is 17 px: the standfirst arrangement, where the largest words on the page are the publication's name and the h1 is the sentence under it. That inversion is the design, and it is why this is not 6 Big Type with a different field. Flagged as mine: the 1100 px plate, the 36 px gap to the standfirst, and the 26/17 px pair.

Site title comes from Ghost's own settings and is not authored here — renaming the publication in Ghost renames the nameplate, and the field cannot drift from the title in the header's lockup. No site title → no design, and the editor says the site needs a name, exactly as A3·7 does. Issue line is section content and flagged as invented: Ghost has no issue number and no publication date for a home page. A site that forgets to update it shows a stale issue line, which is why it is optional and why the editor's helper text says plainly that nothing updates it automatically.

1 · The issue line is a <p> in the eyebrow's own style, authored in sentence case and uppercased in CSS. The date inside it is the user's typed text and is not a <time> element: nothing machine-readable is claimed for a string nobody validates. Flagged as mine. 2 · The nameplate is a <p>, not a heading, and not a link. The header's lockup two rows above has already announced the site title and links home; a 72 px repeat of it as an h1 would give the page an outline entry that says nothing new, and as a link it would be a second home link in the first 200 px of the document. This repeats A3·7's decision in the same words, and it is flagged there and here: a real heading is defensible and I chose against it. 3 · The standfirst is the page's single <h1> even though it is 26 px and the name above it is 72. Visual weight and document structure are allowed to disagree; what a page is about is the sentence, not the masthead. Rules · CSS borders, never <hr>: they are the plate's frame, not thematic breaks, and under forced colours they may vanish without the composition losing anything it needs. Contrast · nameplate and standfirst 12.1:1 / 11.8:1 · issue line and sub 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · rules 1.4:1 and decorative. With one action, the accent button carries its label at 3.2:1 light and 8.1:1 dark — the flagged pack finding in A4-0. Zoom · every size here is type, so at 200% the whole plate grows and the rules grow with it; the 1100 px plate becomes the measure long before the type runs out of room.
