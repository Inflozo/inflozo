Author Bio

The four footer blocks stacked on the article's own measure in the category's fixed order — tags, author, share, next and prev — each opening with a hairline, and the author block leading: a 56 px avatar, the name in the heading font, two lines of bio and a link to the rest of their work.

The footer is on the measure, not the content width — 720 centred in A17's 1,296 box ⚑, so the first tag sits under the first character of the article and nothing in the footer is wider than the thing it closes.
Order is fixed and is not a control: tags, author, share, next and prev ⚑. A design may drop a block; no design may reorder one. The reason is that a footer read twice on two posts of the same site must be the same shape, and an order control makes that the user's problem.
One accent, and it is not here — this design has no subscribe button, so the accent appears only in A6's focus ring ⚑. The "More from" link is text at 600, not accent: a link at the end of every post is not an action.

POST FOOTERS PATCH · 30 AUGUST 2026 · WHAT CHANGED ON THIS FRAME, AND THE RULE THAT REQUIRED IT
Bylines carry no number“Rosa Menendez and three others” is now “Rosa Menendez and others”. A template cannot subtract the drawn names from the author count, so the number could only ever have been hard-coded or wrong. The row, the avatars and the type are unchanged.
“More from” uses the full nameThe archive link reads “More from Rosa Menendez”, not “More from Rosa”. Ghost gives a theme one display name and no separate first name on the versions we support, so a first name was never available to interpolate. The default of authorLinkLabel changes with it.
Avatars with no photographThe avatar fallback draws one letter — “Rosa Menendez” shows R. Every person here comes from Ghost, which cannot produce two initials from a display name, so two initials were never available. The circle, its sizes and its colours are unchanged.
Item counts are a number pickerShare links is a stepper, 2 to 4, default 4 — new in this pass, where the row drew Two · Three · Four — drawing the first N of the site’s enabled destinations in the site’s own order. The + is greyed at four with the reason visible: a fifth link takes the row onto a second line. Superseded 1 September 2026: the stepper is cut and sharing opens Ghost’s own share panel.
The two free designs are the owner’s choice — ask himShortlisted the five plainest designs in the category — 1 Author Bio, 6 Slim, 2 Rows, 9 Share Row and 8 Tag Row — none of which needs the customer to have good photography. Recommended 1 Author Bio · 6 Slim, and the question is asked in plain words at the end of the written spec. Nothing is renumbered either way.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject in this design — it contains no subscribe and no sign-in form, so there is nothing for a designed notice to replace. The share links are real <a href> URLs and work unscripted; only the copy-link button is hidden.
Member buttons are conditionalNo subject at rest — this design draws no member action. At Blocks Everything the inherited subscribe bar carries the note, and the note is 12 Subscribe’s.
POST FOOTERS PATCH · PASS TWO · 1 SEPTEMBER 2026 · INCLUDING THE NATIVE-SHARE RULING · WHAT CHANGED ON THIS FRAME, AND THE RULE THAT REQUIRED IT
Sharing is Ghost’s own share panel, everywhereOne trigger, and Ghost’s panel owns the destinations. Where the app reports an older Ghost, that panel does not exist — the same link opens the sign-in box — so the trigger is replaced by Copy link and Email, two links in the row the destinations used to fill, and the editor panel greys its Share destinations row with the reason beside it. The version comes from a capability flag the app sets when the site is connected ⚑, because a theme cannot ask Ghost its version. The site setting is reduced to two switches, Copy link and Email. This supersedes the earlier ruling of the same day that we would not use Ghost’s panel at all.
A control switched off by another is greyed, with the reason beside itThe Share destinations row is greyed with the reason beside it — Ghost’s panel owns the destinations — and the old 2-to-4 stepper is cut outright rather than greyed: a control with nothing to do is not a disabled control. On an older Ghost the row stays greyed and says which. Nothing else here switches another control off, and Top divider’s Line resolves into the design’s own top hairline.

PASS FIVE PATCH · 3 SEPTEMBER 2026 · IMAGE FOCUS IS ONE SHARED CONTROL, DEFINED IN P0·9
What changed on this frameNothing changed on this frame. Image focus has never been named in this design’s panel, control list, data fields or spec, so there was no private copy of its values here to withdraw. No control was added or removed, no default moved, and no picture, measure, type step, colour or spacing step changed.
The rule that required itOne control name means one set of values. Image focus was never a shared control: two dozen category specs each wrote out their own copy of its values, which is that rule’s worst case in the library. It is now defined once, in P0·9, carrying both axes — the side-to-side half by the owner’s ruling of 3 September 2026, which this category did not have — and a category supplies only the slot it applies to.
Where the control lands hereNowhere. This design authors no image — the only picture it draws is Ghost’s profile_image in the avatar, with A1’s one-letter fallback where there is none — so the control has no authored slot to sit under and none is drawn. The avatar is a crop all the same, which is the second open question below.
Ghost never sees thisFocus is a hint the compiler resolves into the crop the theme ships with. The project stores the choice, the compiler resolves it, and nothing about it reaches Ghost or comes back — which is why it is a control and not a data binding. It declares no behaviour module, so this design’s no-JavaScript line and its declared behaviour are both unchanged.
OPEN FOR THE OWNERIs an avatar’s circular crop a subject for Image focus? What I would have needed to know: whether a small circle counts as “a photograph cropped into a frame of its own shape”, or whether there is a size below which focus is not worth a control. Why it is open: this design crops Ghost’s profile_image to a circle, so on the letter of P0·9 it would draw a focus control; at avatar size the choice is close to invisible, and ten new rows across the category is not the naming change the work list asked for.
