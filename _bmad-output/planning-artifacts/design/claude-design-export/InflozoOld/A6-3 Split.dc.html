A6 CTA BANNERS · DESIGN 3 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · THE ONE-ROW ASK

Split

The words on the left of one row and the actions on the right of it, so the whole ask is a single line of the page. It is the shortest banner in A6 that still carries a title and a sub, which makes it the one a long page can afford twice, and it settles where the note and the secondary go when the actions are a column rather than a row.

The actions are a right-aligned column, not a row, and that is the design's own settlement. A row of two actions inside the right half either crowds the page margin or floats in the middle of it; a column puts the primary's right edge on the page margin, the secondary beneath it and the note beneath that, so the whole block has one edge and reads bottom-up as offer, alternative, terms. 12 px between the primary and the secondary, 12 again to the note. The note lives in the actions column here, where 1 Centred and 2 Flush Left put it under the actions in the text's own alignment.
Title Medium 34 is the default and Display 48 is not offered: a 48 px line beside a 46 px button makes the button look like a caption. Ground Surface is the default too, and the reason is the frame — a one-row banner on the page's own ground has nothing to say it is a section, where 2 solves the same problem with a hairline. Drawn on surface, full bleed, no radius, the footer's ground returning beneath it.

TOP OF THE ROW · the primary's top aligns to the title's cap height, a 3 px optical lift

Actions alignment matters more the taller the text gets. Centred on the text is right for a two-line sub and wrong for a four-line one, where the button ends up level with the middle of a paragraph and looks unattached; Top of the row puts it level with the title, which is the thing it answers. The lift is 3 px — A5·2's cap-height rule, carried, so a button beside a heading and an icon beside a heading are aligned the same way. The 76 px gutter is fixed at both divisions and is not a control: it is the smallest gap at which a right-aligned column stops looking like the text's third paragraph.

1 · The DOM order is A6's shared order — eyebrow, title, sub, primary, secondary, note — and the two columns are one flex row over it. Nothing is reordered to put the actions on the right, so the reading order and the tab order are the same at every width and at every division. 2 · Actions are <a>s with their visible labels as their whole names. The right-aligned column is align-items: flex-end and not text-align: right, so the primary's box ends at the margin rather than its text ending there — a 24-character label and a 12-character one line up on the same edge. 3 · The note is the last thing in the DOM and reads bottom-up as offer, alternative, terms — which is the argument for putting it in this column rather than under the text. It is a paragraph, not aria-describedby the primary; right-aligned at ≥ 1081 and left-aligned below it, following its column. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 12.6:1 dark · sub and note 5.4:1 / 6.1:1 · button 4.6:1 / 8.1:1 · the focus ring's inner gap takes the ground it is on. Zoom: at 200% text the row becomes the tablet's stack, which is the same hand-off the width triggers, so nothing new has to be specified for it. Forced colours keep the plane's edge only where the theme supplies one; the design does not depend on the surface step, which is what the sub and the button are for.

MEMBER ASK AND NO JAVASCRIPT · PATCH PASS · 29 AUG 2026 The ask is conditional. Where the primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting rather than shipping a button that fails. An ordinary link is unaffected. Ruled by the owner, 29 August 2026: the button keeps its place and its scale, and the site chooses what it says and where it goes — a fallback label and link (fallbackLabel ≤ 24 · fallbackUrl, in the panel beside the ask), so a reader meets a working button rather than a gap. Left empty, this design’s own rule applies: no primary label or URL, no section. Where the ask opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real href to a Portal address, so the page stays where it is and no window opens. No JavaScript: this design draws no form, so there is nothing to replace — every word and both actions are rendered by the server, and the banner is the banner.
