Track

Three to five steps in one line, each behind a 32 px marker, with a single hairline running through the markers from the first to the last. The design A13-0's settlement 3 was written for, and the one that has to answer what a horizontal connector does when the steps stack.

The answer is that it turns rather than disappearing: at ≤ 767 the same hairline runs vertically down the markers' centre line and the text sits beside it. Nothing is added and nothing is dropped — the track rotates. This is also the design that sets the marker for the category, a box at the pack radius rather than a circle, so that a 6 px pack and a 14 px pack both get a marker rather than one of each.

The marker is a 32 px box at the pack radius with a surface fill, a 1 px border hairline and one figure at 15/600 in text — the one place in A13 a numeral is text rather than text-muted, because it sits on a fill of its own rather than on the ground. It is a box and never a circle, so a 6 px pack and a 14 px pack each get a marker in their own shape instead of one of them getting a circle that ignores the radius token. ⚑ Mine, and A13-0's tokenisation proof is drawn on this design for that reason.
The markers sit at the left edge of their columns, not at the centre. A marker centred over a left-set paragraph reads as a bullet floating above the text; at the left it reads as the start of the line. Marker to text 20; columns 306 on a 24 px gutter across 1,296; the hairline runs from marker 1's centre to marker 4's centre and passes behind each of them, so it never overshoots the first or trails past the last. Step 03's 80-character title is four lines in a 306 column and pushes nothing, because the marker row is a separate row from the text row and the text columns are top-aligned. ⚑ Mine: the 20 px marker gap, the marker at the column's left, and the two-row construction.

CONNECTOR UNDER THE ROW · ONE HAIRLINE AT THE CONTENT WIDTH, UNDER EVERYTHING · NO MARKERS DRAWN
Three to five steps, and the design is designed for exactly those. Five markers on 240 columns is the tightest it goes and the body is two lines there; six or more → 1 Three Up, named in the panel before the add, because a track that wraps is not a track — the connector would have to run off one line and start again on the next, and there is no honest way to draw that. ⚑ Mine, and the clearest count ceiling in A13. Marker Dot is this design's Numerals None, and it keeps the connector. A 10 px dot in border is a non-text mark on a line, so it takes the divider's 1.3:1 rather than a text ratio; the sequence is carried by the line and by the <ol>. There is no third marker value — no tick, no chevron, no icon in the marker, no filled “done” state. Connector Under the row drops the markers entirely and draws one hairline at the content width under the whole text row, 24 px below it. It is the value for a site whose steps are all one line long, and it is the only value where this design has no marker at all. Connector None drops the line and keeps the markers, at which point the design is 1 Three Up with a marker instead of a numeral — the panel says so rather than hiding it. Refused: an arrowhead on the line, a chevron between markers, a dashed or dotted connector, a curve, a connector in accent, a thickness control, a progress fill, and a “current step” marker. The last is the one a site will ask for and the answer is 15 Sticky Rail, where a current step is derived from the reader's scroll rather than authored. ⚑ A13-0's settlement 3.

Track: Content width · Full bleed. At Content width the track is 1,296 and the first marker lines up with the title's first letter — the ordinary case and the primary frame's. At Full bleed the track runs to the page margin at both ends, 72 / 40 / 20, and the head stays on the content width, so the line is wider than the type above it. Hidden leaves a marker, a title and the line — the shortest this design gets, about 180 px tall at Compact — and it is what a five-step track wants. A step's body is never clamped here; a long one simply makes the text row taller, and the panel states the section's height rather than capping the field. The one measurement: the design refuses to draw below three steps. Two markers with a line between them is a comparison rather than a process, so at two steps this design draws 1 Three Up's two cells and the panel names it. At ≤ 767 the track turns and the connector turns with it. The markers stack down a 28 px column at the left, the hairline runs vertically between them behind each marker, and the text sits 16 px to their right. The line still starts at the first marker and stops at the last — there is no tail under step 4. Marker 32 → 28, title 20 → 18, step gap 28. Track Full bleed has no effect at ≤ 767, because the turned track has no horizontal run to bleed, and the control is disabled with that reason shown. Connector Under the row also has no effect and resolves to Through the markers. ⚑ Mine, both resolutions, and they are the two places this design's controls stop applying.

ACCESSIBILITY · FOUR STEPS, ONE TAB STOP, TWO ROWS THAT ARE ONE LIST The marker row and the text row are one <ol>, not two rows of markup. Each <li> holds its marker and its text and the two-row appearance is a grid with two implicit tracks, so reading order is marker, title, body, step by step and never all four markers followed by all four bodies. This is the single most important build note in the design and it is the thing a two-row implementation gets wrong. ⚑ Mine. The marker's figure is aria-hidden, like every numeral in A13, and at Marker Dot the dot is aria-hidden too. The <ol> carries the order at both values, so Numeral and Dot sound identical — the same test A13·1 puts on Numerals None. The connector is a border on a decorative <span aria-hidden>, never an <hr> — an <hr> between list items announces a thematic break three times in a four-step process. Forced colours keep the marker's border and the connector and lose the marker's fill, which is the one mode where Marker Numeral and Marker Dot look most alike. Tab stops: one, the section link, or none where no link is authored. No marker is focusable, nothing is a target, and there is no keyboard interaction of any kind. Contrast: marker figure 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 on its surface fill, title the same on the ground, body 5.6:1 / 6.0:1, connector and dot 1.3:1 as non-text marks, disclosed. At 400% zoom the track is turned and vertical, which is the ≤ 767 drawing.
