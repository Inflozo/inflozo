Slim

A page title, not an argument: one line, one row, 160 px of section, for the top of an archive index, a tag page or an About. It is the hero for pages where the hero is not the point, and the design most likely to be used on every page of a site — which makes its restraint the specification rather than a shortcoming.

Drawn with the next section visible, because a slim hero cannot be judged alone — its whole job is the relationship between the title and what follows, and the 40 px between the rule and the first card is the measurement that does it. The band is 56 px of padding around a 40 px title: 160 px total, against the category's 96 px padding alone, and it is the only design in A4 whose default padding is not Comfortable's 96 because Comfortable here means comfortable for a page title. The title and the meta sit on a baseline-ish row — the meta is bottom-aligned with 6 px of optical lift rather than true baseline alignment, since the two fonts' metrics differ. Flagged as mine: the 56 px padding, the 40 px title, the 6 px optical lift, and drawing the following section.

Authored
The one design in A4 whose title can come from Ghost. On a tag or author page the title, eyebrow and count are filled from the archive itself and the authored fields are shown as placeholders the user may override — the editor says “From your tag” beside each, so nobody wonders why typing changes nothing until they do. A tag with no description renders Title and meta even where Title and description is chosen, and the editor names the fall-back rather than showing an empty line. A count of one reads “1 post”, and zero reads “No posts yet” rather than “0 posts”. Offered on every route — the only design in the category that is. The boundary with A29 Archive Headers: this design is a hero a user places on any page, including a tag or author page; A29 owns the archive header a theme renders on those templates by default, with the filtering, sorting and pagination context that belongs to them. A site uses one or the other, and the editor names the collision rather than drawing both. It is also the only one that composes on a page whose content follows immediately, which is why it is the default hero for pages rather than home.

NONE · nothing between the title and the content · the padding is the separation

Below is the default because a page title's job is to name what is under it, and a hairline says that in one pixel. The rule is never above — the header already ends in one, and two hairlines 160 px apart with nothing between them but a title reads as a mistake. On the Band value the section's own hover-surface ground is the separation and no hairline is drawn; the hairline and the band are alternatives rather than a stack. Flagged as mine: all three values and the refusal of “above”.

THE LONG TITLE · two lines, and where the meta goes

The title wraps at 60% of the measure and the meta stays where it is, bottom-aligned to the title's last line. It does not move to a second row and it does not shrink: the meta is 14 px at every width in the library. Where the title is long enough to reach the meta — past about 75% of the row — the meta drops to its own line above the rule, right-aligned, at the same 14 px. Flagged as mine: the 60% wrap, and the 75% drop threshold.

the content this title names — h2s from here down4
1 · The title is the page's <h1>, and on a tag or author page it is the archive's own name rather than an authored line — which is the correct h1 for those templates and the reason this design is the default hero for them. 2 · The eyebrow is a <p> above the h1, as everywhere in A4. On a tag page it reads “Tag” and on an author page “Writer” — words rather than a glyph, so the template is named in the page's text and not only in its URL. 3 · The count is plain text in the same <header>, not a live region and not aria-describedby on the title — it is a fact about the page, read in reading order. “1 post”, not “1 posts”; “No posts yet”, not “0 posts”. 4 · The heading contract with the section below. This design is nearly always followed by a list of posts, and its h1 makes every card title an h2 — so the page's outline is one h1 and a flat run of h2s. That is the whole reason the title's level matters more here than in any other hero: get it wrong and the archive's outline collapses. Nothing is interactive except the action, where the arrangement includes one — the standard primary button, A1·1's, with the library's focus ring. No motion of any kind. Contrast · title 12.1:1 light / 11.8:1 dark · eyebrow 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · count 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · description 5.4:1 / 6.4:1 · rule 1.4:1, decorative. On the Band value the surface step is 1.1:1 against the page and carries no meaning of its own; the hairline is removed there, so nothing depends on seeing the step.
