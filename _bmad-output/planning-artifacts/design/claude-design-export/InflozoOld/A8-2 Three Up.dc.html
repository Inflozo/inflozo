Three Up

Three quote cards of 416 px on a 24 px gutter under a centred head. It is the design most sites will pick, and it carries two loads for the whole category: it is the quote card's home — the component A8-0 specifies and ten designs reuse — and its two-column arrangement is the collapse target every columned design in A8 names at 1080.

What it settles is the row. Three cards side by side are equal in height and unequal in content, and the honest answer to that is stretch, never truncate: the tallest quote sets the row and the short ones keep their attribution at the foot of a card with space above it. The alternative — clamping the long one — edits a reader's sentence to make a grid tidy.

HAIRLINE COLUMNS · no plane at all · 32 px inset either side of each rule, no gutter

Three ways to hold three quotes, in descending weight. At Ground the card keeps its hairline and loses its fill, which is the value for a page that already has cards above and below this section. At Hairline columns the gutter is replaced by a 1 px rule with a 32 px inset either side and the card padding control becomes the column inset — the one place in A8 a control changes what it measures, and the sidebar renames it rather than leaving the user to work it out. The rules run the full height of the tallest column and are the only vertical lines in the category. 12 Rows is still the design for a site that wants no boxes at all: this value keeps a grid, that one is a list. ⚑ All three values, the 32 px inset and the control's rename are mine.

TWO QUOTES AUTHORED · two cards of 416 centred, the row not filled and not restretched

Two quotes do not become 3 Two Up. They stay 416 px and sit centred with the gutter between them, because a card that changes width when a site deletes a quote is a card that reflows its own text for a reason the site cannot see. One quote does hand off — to 1 Single, at Feature 27 with no card — and the sidebar says which design is drawing before the frame changes under the site. Four or more authored quotes stay at three drawn, and the panel reads “3 of 7 quotes drawn” with 4 Grid named for six and 5 Wall for twelve. ⚑ Holding the card width at two quotes, and the one-quote hand-off, are mine.

1 · A <ul> of three <li>, each a <figure> with <blockquote><p> and <figcaption>. The list order is the authored order, so the DOM and the screen agree at every width — the two-column and one-column arrangements are CSS, not a re-sort. One <h2> for the section and no <h3> on the cards: a quotation is not a heading at any size. 2 · Avatar aria-hidden, no alt, no initials fallback — this design has no fixed avatar column, so a missing photograph closes the block up. The card is not a link, not focusable and not hoverable: no lift, no shadow, no border change, no scale, at any value. There is nothing in a quote card to click. 3 · The foot link is the section's only focusable element and its only accent — A6's 2 px ring, 2 px out, the gap taking the ground, 3.3:1 in light and 6.3:1 in dark; permanently underlined, the underline in accent and the label in text. The quote, name, role and organisation edit inline on the card too, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater — and the quote edits with the marks toolbar suppressed: A8 puts no bold, italic or link inside somebody else’s sentence. Nothing here is Ghost-owned, so nothing says “Edit in Ghost”. The link opens the Ghost-aware Link Picker and takes an optional P0·2 icon before or after its label, off by default. Quotes are the P0·3 item list: Add quote arrives with content, Remove is never disabled, drag to reorder, per-item content only. DESIGN PATCH PASS · 30 AUGUST 2026 · EVERY CHANGE WITH THE NAME OF THE RULE THAT REQUIRED IT
No design ever turns into another designAt one quotation this design draws one 416 card centred and keeps its head, its gutter and its card. The sentence that handed the frame to 1 Single is deleted; the panel advises “at one quotation, 1 Single reads better” and the site decides. The 1080 two-column step is a shared measurement, not a change of design.
Item counts are a number pickerNo count row: three is the design. The surplus is kept and counted — “3 of 4 drawn” — and Add stops at twelve with its reason on the control.
The Remove button never greys outRemove was never dimmed here and still is not, at any count. Removing the last row leaves the list empty, the section then does not render, and the editor keeps the empty repeater and its Add quote control rather than drawing a placeholder person.
Member buttons are conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up pop-up needs JavaScriptThe section link may target Ghost's Portal, so two notes now sit on the Link Picker: where the target is a member action it does not render on a connected site with self-signup switched off or no payment provider connected, and where it opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up, with JavaScript off nothing happens and the reader stays where they are. Separate from Member visibility, which hides the whole section server-side. A8 draws no labelled button anywhere, so the section link is the only surface this rule touches here.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject, and nothing to withdraw: this design holds no subscribe and no sign-in field, so there is nothing for the notice to replace, and no sentence here ever promised a subscription without JavaScript. With JavaScript off this design is unchanged — every quotation, name, role and organisation is server-rendered.
Ghost’s templates cannot count, add, or rememberThe step-down and the character counters are editor values: the editor counts the authored string as it is typed and writes one value onto the item, and the stylesheet reads it. “N of M drawn” is written by the editor too — a template cannot count its own loop, and no arithmetic happens as the page is served.
Avatars with no photographWith no photograph the attribution closes up — no circle, no initials, no indent held for it — and Avatars Hidden draws the same thing. The two-initial form lands on 11 Faces only, the one design in A8 with a circle to fill; nothing here is pulled from Ghost, so the one-letter rule never applies in this category.
