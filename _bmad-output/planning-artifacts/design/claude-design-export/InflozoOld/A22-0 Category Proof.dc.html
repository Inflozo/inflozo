A22 · Newsletter / Subscribe

Sixteen designs, one field. What separates them is where the ask sits, what stands beside it and what ground it rests on — never the field itself, which is A3·4’s form row carried verbatim into all sixteen.

This frame carries what belongs to the category rather than to any one design: the four settlements §8 asked for, the tokenisation proof, the stress frame, the roster, the shared field list, the component inventory and the findings.

Controls-reconciliation patch — 25 August 2026. The sixteen designs were audited against the PRD’s control vocabulary and Ghost’s verified data surface, reusing the P0 editor primitives by name. What changed across the whole category is listed below; what each design gained is in the ⚑ RECONCILED card at the foot of its own control panel, and the frame-by-frame list is in Reconciliation notes at the end of the written spec.

Newsletter patch — 28 August 2026. Four claims in this category were wrong about what Ghost can do, and all four are corrected below and on every design frame: a form cannot work without JavaScript, a theme cannot know which letters a member takes, a newsletter has no cadence, and “not public” is not the same as paid. Nothing was renumbered — the designs run 1 to 16 as drawn.

THE RULETHE CHANGE
The no-JavaScript noticeThe “works without JavaScript” promise is withdrawn in all sixteen. Ghost’s signup endpoint refuses a plain form submission, so the form is replaced by P0·4’s notice at the form row’s own height — “Signing up needs JavaScript — turn it on to subscribe.”, the note kept beneath, no field and no button. Member buttons are conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up pop-up needs JavaScriptEvery subscribe ask carries two lines on its panel: it does not render where the connected site has self-signup switched off, members disabled, or — for a paid ask — no payment provider connected; and any button that opens Ghost’s own sign-up pop-up does nothing with JavaScript off. That covers 10 Choice’s and 15 Two Up’s members links and every button an editor points at Portal.
The cadence line goesGhost’s newsletters carry no cadence field, so “weekly” and “twice a month” could never be generated. The generated newsletter meta is now one word read from visibility — “Members”, and only where that value is paid ⚑ — in 15 Two Up’s card meta and 10 Choice’s badge. A letter any member receives carries no word at all (the owner’s ruling, 28 August 2026): a mark on every card tells a reader nothing, and the card closes up rather than reserving the line. A newsletter’s own description is still Ghost’s and is untouched.

Nothing swaps when a paid letter is ticked10 Choice swapped the field for a Portal link when a reader ticked a paid letter. THE FOUR QUESTIONS §8 ASKED, ANSWERED
1 · The form’s four states, in every arrangement. There are seven, not four ⚑: A3·4’s empty, focus, invalid, submitting and done, plus the signed-in subscriber and members off in Ghost, which are states of the same slot and are drawn on every design’s states frame. One geometry holds all of them ⚑ — the form’s block is the same height in every state, so nothing below it moves when a reader submits. The two stated exceptions are 9 Slim Bar, where invalid grows the strip by 22 px because there is no note to put the message in, and 14 Slide-in Card, where done shrinks the card because nothing sits under it.2 · The Ghost members handoff. Every design posts to /members/api/send-magic-link/ and the section cannot repoint it ⚑ — the row is read-only in all sixteen panels. Ghost sends its own confirmation and answers an already-subscribed address in the same slot. With members disabled the field is replaced at the server, before the page is sent: the button becomes a link to the subscribe page, the placeholder and note are kept, and the substitution holds with or without JavaScript ⚑. Fourteen designs keep their head and substitute; two hide entirely — 10 Choice and 15 Two Up, whose whole arrangement is a chooser with nothing to write to ⚑ — and 14 Slide-in Card, which never interrupts a reader to show them a link they did not ask for. The setting is a site setting: the sidebar states the substitution and links to it, and no section can switch members on.
3 · Sites with more than one newsletter. Two designs let the reader choose and fourteen do not ⚑. 10 Choice draws one checkbox row per newsletter above a single field — the answer for three or more. 15 Two Up draws a card and a field per newsletter — the answer for exactly two, where the letters have different audiences. The other fourteen post to the newsletter named in the shared source group, defaulting to the site’s own default, and the sidebar names which one ⚑. Ghost’s newsletter order is the order in both designs; there is no picker, no reorder and no per-newsletter styling ⚑. Zero newsletters is not reachable — Ghost ships every site with one and it cannot be deleted.4 · The already-subscribed signed-in member. The form is never drawn to them ⚑ at the shared control’s default. In its place, at identical height: “Signed in” ⚑ corrected in the newsletter patch — Ghost does not tell a theme which newsletters a member has, so the line names the signed-in address and claims nothing about a subscription — and a link to #/portal/account/newsletters ⚑ — Ghost’s own preferences panel, not a section-built one. The alternative value exists and is not the default: The form anyway, for a publication that wants a second address captured. 14 Slide-in Card ignores the control ⚑ and never renders for a subscriber at either value — a corner card that interrupts a paying reader to tell them they already pay is the worst version of this section in the library.

FINDINGS FOR THE ARCHITECT · SEVEN, FIVE OF THEM NEW
1 · Ghost has no newsletter-issue object. ⚑ 12 Issue Preview draws posts, and a post that went out as a letter is indistinguishable in the API from one that did not — there is no sent_at, no issue number and no way to filter to “things that were emailed”. Every “recent issues” section in the library will have this problem. A platform decision.2 · There is no newsletter archive route. ⚑ Ghost has / and tag and author routes, and nothing that means “the letters”. 12’s archive link points at the site’s post index, which is the closest true thing. A20 found the same gap for tags and A21 for authors; this is the third category to hit it.3 · Dismissal memory is not in the registry. ⚑ 14 Slide-in Card offers Not again this session and Not again for thirty days, and slide-in-card’s registry entry does not say whether it remembers a dismissal or where. dismiss is the closest module and belongs to A2’s bar. Named, not resolved — the control is drawn because the behaviour is needed, and the module that implements it is the architect’s call.4 · One design declares two modules. ⚑ 14 Slide-in Card declares slide-in-card and member-form. Nothing in the registry forbids it and nothing in the brief anticipates it. Both degrade independently and the combined no-JavaScript result is an ordinary card at the page foot carrying the no-JavaScript notice where the form would be ⚑ corrected in the newsletter patch, so the acceptance criterion is met twice over — but the build should know that a design can carry two.
5 · The seven roles contain no error colour. ⚑ Invalid is drawn in text at 500 weight with a 1.5 px text border and no red anywhere — A3·4’s call, carried into sixteen designs. It works, and it is worth saying out loud that a form category built on a seven-role palette cannot signal an error by colour and must do it by weight, border and position instead. If a role is ever added, this is the category that changes.6 · The focus ring is suppressed on the field. ⚑ A6’s 4 px accent ring would collide with a button 10 px away, so the field takes a 1.5 px accent border instead — A2·5’s documented departure, now in sixteen more places. It is a real reduction in focus visibility and is flagged as one: 1.5 px at the field’s own edge is less findable than a 4 px offset ring, and the alternative was a ring overlapping the submit button.7 · A section cannot know what precedes it on the route. ⚑ Repeated from A21 and A26–A29 and still open. It matters more here: a page can carry a newsletter section, a footer newsletter band (A3·4) and a slide-in card (A22·14) at once, and nothing in the system stops a reader being asked three times on one page. No section can detect the others, so this is a route-level warning the editor would have to raise, not something any design here can fix.Repeated and still open: count.posts and pagination.total can disagree (A29 finding 4, A21 finding 6); nothing in the registry sorts or filters in place.
