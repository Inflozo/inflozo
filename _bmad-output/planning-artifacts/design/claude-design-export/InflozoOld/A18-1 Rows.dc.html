A18 POST LISTS · DESIGN 1 OF 15 · PAPER PACK · SEVEN CONTROLS · THE CATEGORY DEFAULT

Rows

Full-width rows separated by a hairline: tag, title and excerpt at the left on an 820 px measure, author and date hung at the right in a 200 px column. This is what a site gets when it asks for a list, and every other design in A18 is a departure from it.

A17 answered “how do posts divide a page”. A18 answers a different question: how does one post use a whole page width. A row at 1,296 px is too wide to read a title across, so this design does not let it — the text stops at 820 and the meta hangs at the far right, which spends the extra 476 px on a rhythm of aligned dates rather than on a longer line.

It draws no image at any setting. That is the design's real distinction from A17·13 Thumb Side, which is the nearest thing to it in the library: a list with no picture cannot have a missing picture, so the tag plate — A17's largest single piece of machinery — is not reachable from here.

The row is 1,296 wide and its text is 820. Between them sits a 276 px gap and then a 200 px meta column, right-aligned. The gap is not a gutter and is never drawn — it is what is left when a readable measure and a fixed meta column are placed at opposite edges of the content width, and it grows and shrinks with the page. ⚑ 820 is mine: it is A17's 780 head measure plus one 40 px step, because a row's title carries no sub beneath it.
The rule sits above each row, and the last row carries one below it. That is the only place in A18 where a rule is doubled up in the markup, and it is what closes the list — without it the final row bleeds into the note. Post 4 draws no tag and no excerpt and is 46 px shorter than its neighbours; the rule keeps it a row rather than a gap. Post 6 has no excerpt authored either — two ragged rows in six is the ordinary case, not the stress case.
The meta column right-aligns, and that is the whole reason it is a column. Six dates at a shared right edge read as a date column; six dates trailing six titles of different lengths read as nothing. Names sit on their own line above the date so that the second line is uniform in shape — “26 February 2026 · 6 min read” is the longest string the column has to hold at 13 px, and it fits 200 px with 14 px to spare.

Row density is a second padding control and A18 needs it because a list has two paddings. The section's Padding sets the space above the first row and below the last; Row density sets the space inside the list, and a Comfortable section around a Compact list is a legitimate and common setting. A17 needed only one because a grid's internal space is a gutter, and a gutter is not adjustable in this library. ⚑ A18 sets the floor for a readable row at 44 px, and this design never reaches it. Compact here is 60 px: 16 above, 16 below, and a 22 px title with a 6 px descender allowance. 3 Slim is the design that goes to 44, by dropping to a 19 px title on a single line. 44 is A1's touch-target minimum, and a row that is a link should not be smaller than a button. Mine, and it is the answer to §8's “the smallest row that still reads”. The excerpt is clamped by line and the title never is — A17's rule, carried over without change. At One line the excerpt is one line at any width, so a row's height is knowable from its settings; at Two lines a 90-character title plus two excerpt lines is the tallest ordinary row in A18 at 202 px.

Post content is Ghost’s and is never inline-editable ⚑ — clicking a post title, tag, author name or excerpt says “Edit in Ghost”. WHAT THIS PANEL SETTLES FOR THE WHOLE CATEGORY Rule's three values are A18's separator vocabulary and every design in the category draws from them. Off · Between rows · Boxed. Between rows is the default in eleven of the fifteen; Off belongs to the designs that mark their rows some other way — 8 Row Cards has a plane, 13 Timeline has a rail, 2 Thumb Rows has a picture. Boxed is never the default anywhere, because a list that closes itself top and bottom is a table, and A17·10 Ledger is where a site should go for that. ⚑ Mine, and it is §8's first settlement. ⚑ The Show ladder changes for A18: 5 · 10 · 15 · 25, against A17's 3 · 6 · 9 · 12. A grid's count is chosen so rows come out even; a list has no rows to even out, so the count is chosen by how much page the site wants to spend. This is a real departure from a block A17 called identical in all eighteen, and it is a finding for the architect: either count becomes a per-category enum, or the union of both ladders is offered everywhere and half the values are wrong in half the sections. Cut from this design: an image control. Rows draws no picture at any setting and 2 Thumb Rows is one click away in the Design picker with the site's content preserved. A toggle here would make the two designs the same design, which is the trap rule 2 names — arrangement is what distinguishes a design, and “with a picture” is an arrangement. Cut: an alignment control. A centred list has no meta column to hang, and 9 Big Type is where centred type lives in this category. Cut: a hover-fill control — A17 refused card fills on hover and A18 inherits the refusal; the accent underline on the title is the whole hover state.

A ruled list is the hardest thing in A18 to get right in dark, because the rule is the design. A17 measured its hairlines once — #EBE5DB sits 9.1 lightness points below the light ground and #332E27 9.8 above the dark one — and that symmetric step is inherited here without adjustment. Six rules on a page is six times as much hairline as a grid draws, so if the step were wrong this is the design that would show it.
Nothing else changes: ground, text, muted. No plate, no photograph, no scrim, no shadow. This is the cheapest dark mode in the library, and the reason is the same as the reason the design is immune to a missing image — there is nothing in it but type and lines.
