Ledger

The question in a 300 px column at the left, the answer on 620 at the right, rows between hairlines — A8·12's geometry holding formatted prose instead of a quotation. Always open, no head, no marker, no button. The plainest thing A9 can put on a page, and the design a site picks when every answer should sit beside its question.

It exists because a FAQ's two halves are not the same kind of writing. A question is a label and an answer is prose, and setting them side by side lets a reader run down the left column looking for theirs without reading a word of the right. That is the same scanning an accordion offers, with every answer already on the page.

300 · 56 · 620 = 976, centred, with 320 px of the content width left unused. A8·12 left 160 for the same reason at a narrower answer: the question column is a label column, not a second column of prose, and widening it to fill the page would make a 90-character question look like the row's main text. Both halves are top-aligned, so the question is level with the answer's first line rather than centred against three paragraphs of it.
The answer's 620 is the category's cap drawn exactly — this is the one design where the row's width and the answer's measure are the same number, because the row was built around the cap rather than capped afterwards. Row padding is a control here, 24 · 32 · 44, and not the question's step: with no marker and no plane, the space above and below a row is the only thing separating two answers. ⚑ Mine: the 300/56/620 division, the 320 px left unused and giving row padding its own control.

NONE · 44 PX APART
At Rules None the rows sit 44 px apart at every row-padding value — the Spacious value, whatever the control says, which is A8·12's rule in this category's numbers: with no rule between two rows, 24 px of nothing does not end an answer. A9's three Rules None values are now 28, 40, 44 and 48 across 13 Slim, 3 Open List, this design and 9 Numbered, each set against its own row's density and each stated on its own frame. Rules are border-top on the items, never <hr>, and at Above and below the outer two are borders on the <ul>. The rule runs the full 976, not the length of either column: a rule that stopped at the question column would be an underline on the question. Alignment Centred or Flush left moves the 976 block; it does not change either column's width. At Flush left the block starts at the page margin and the 320 px of unused width is all at the right — which is A8·12's own arrangement, and the reason the control exists rather than the design picking one. This design has no head at any value and no marker at any value. eyebrow, title and sub are kept and never drawn; the note and the link are drawn at the foot, 32 px under the last row, on the block's own left edge.

A 96-CHARACTER QUESTION IS SIX LINES IN 300 PX · THE ROW GROWS AND THE ANSWER STAYS PUT
A long question is the one thing this arrangement handles worse than every other design in A9. 300 px at Row 17 is about 34 characters a line, so the field's 120-character ceiling is six lines and a row can be taller than its own answer. Nothing is truncated and the columns do not rebalance: the row grows, the answer stays at the top of it, and the editor's counter turns muted past 70 characters with “Shorter questions read better in a ledger”. ⚑ Mine, advice and threshold. Two ways a site arrives at this design, both of them a choice rather than a substitution. 5 Split Head with no title — a 416 px head column holding an eyebrow beside five rows is a caption pretending to be a head, and this design is the one that never had a head to lose. And any design a site leaves when it wants every answer visible beside its question, which is the pairing 3 Open List makes vertically and this one makes horizontally. Nothing here is focusable unless the content puts something there — links inside answers, and the optional foot link. With none of those, this design and 3 Open List are the only two sections in A9 with no tab stop at all. No hover state anywhere, no motion at any value, and print and screen are the same drawing — the second of the two designs in the category where that is true.

ACCESSIBILITY · A TABLE THAT IS NOT A TABLE A <ul> of <li>, each an <h3> and its prose in a flex row. Not a <table>: two columns of unrelated pairs are not tabular data, there is no header row, and a table would promise a reader that the columns can be compared down their length. Not a <dl> either — A9-0's refusal, and the layout is the strongest temptation in the category to reach for one. No <h2> and no accessible name, with 13 Slim the only two in A9: the design draws no title at any value, and naming it “Questions” would be the theme writing copy. The questions are <h3>, each with tabindex="-1" and the anchor as its id, so a deep link lands and focuses exactly as in 3 Open List. Reading order is question then answer at every width, because the flex row never reverses: there is no Alignment value that puts the answer first, and Flush left moves the block rather than the columns. No button, no region, no marker, no hover, no motion, and no interactive element unless the content has one. With no links authored this section has no tab stop at all. Contrast: question and answer both 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, list markers with them, inline code 15.4:1 / 14.6:1 on its chip, note 5.6:1 / 6.8:1, link underlines 3.3:1 / 6.3:1, hairlines 1.3:1 as boundaries. In forced colours this design is unchanged apart from the system's colours — no plane, no band, no marker, no ring to re-check. Print and screen are the same drawing.

EDITING · the P0 primitivesQuestions and answers edit inline on canvas, selected where they are drawn rather than only in the repeater. The answer raises the marks toolbar on selection with bold · italic · link · inline code and no underline — an underline inside prose is a link that is not one. Recorded as a PRD delta. Nothing in A9 is Ghost-owned, so nothing here says “Edit in Ghost” — every word on the page is the site’s own.
