A7's fifteen draw Ghost's tiers — name, description, price, currency, benefits — and the site authors the head above them, the note under them, which tier is recommended, and nothing else. 1 · THE FOUR SETTLEMENTS · §8 · ALL FOUR ANSWERS ARE INVENTED AND ALL FOUR ARE FLAGGED

The brief asks four questions. Here are the answers, and the arguments.

SETTLEMENT 1 · ONE, TWO OR THREE TIERS · AND THE MONTHLY / YEARLY SWITCH

The tier count is data, not a control. A site has the tiers it has, and no sidebar value can add one. So every design states three arrangements — three cards on a 24 px gutter, two on 32, one at 480 centred — and the theme picks by counting. The order is Ghost's own, price ascending, at every width, and the recommended tier is never moved to the front: a reader scanning three prices needs them in order more than the site needs its favourite first.

THREE · 416 on a 24 gutter
TWO · 632 on a 32 gutter
ONE · 480, centred, never stretched
The switch is a control, and its third value is the answer to the other half of the question. Billing period: Monthly · Yearly · Both. At Both a hairline pill of two labels sits under the head and the prices change beneath it — the category's one behaviour. At Monthly or Yearly there is no pill and no toggle, and the price carries its own cadence in words. And the cadence is content once, on 15 Both Prices, which draws both numbers on every card — yearly at the price size, monthly at 15 px muted beneath — and offers no switch at all. That is the design for a site that would rather answer the question than ask it, and it is why the switch is a control everywhere else rather than a category-wide rule. The saving line is the switch's own arithmetic, and it belongs to the switch alone: where the yearly price is lower than twelve monthly ones the module states the difference in whole months — “Two months free on yearly” — rounded down, and says nothing where the saving is under a month. A template cannot add, so the line exists only on a design carrying the billing switch and it does not render with JavaScript off; 15 Both Prices, which has no switch, draws no saving at all. A site cannot type a discount that its Stripe prices do not support. Where any tier has no yearly price the switch is not drawn and the section shows monthly, with the sidebar naming the tier.
SETTLEMENT 2 · MARKING THE RECOMMENDED TIER WITHOUT A SECOND ACCENT

Three signals, none of them a new colour: the accent moves, the plane steps, and a contrast badge sits at the end of the tier name's line. The accent is not added to the recommended card — it is taken away from the others. With a recommendation, that card's action is the only accent fill in the section and every other action is a hairline outline. With none, every action is filled and the section says the tiers are equal.

SETTLEMENT 3 · THE PORTAL HAND-OFF · AND WHAT THE BUTTON SAYS BEFORE IT

Every action is a link into Ghost Portal and the section draws none of it. Portal is JavaScript: with JavaScript off these links do nothing, and every paid action renders only where a payment provider is connected — so a section can be complete, readable and still not subscribable, which every panel now says. #/portal/signup/{tier}/{monthly|yearly} for a paid tier at the cadence on screen, #/portal/signup/free for the free one, #/portal/account/plans for a signed-in member changing plan. No fields, no card entry, no confirmation, no modal of our own, and no focus management into Portal's dialog — A6·14's boundary, carried.

SETTLEMENT 4 · ONE PAID TIER, AND NONE

One paid tier is two cards, not one — Ghost's free tier is a tier, and a site with one paid plan is choosing between free and paid. No paid tier is one card: the free tier alone, its benefits, and a signup action. Both render, both are drawn on 6 Single Tier's frames, and the head does not change: an authored title written for three tiers still reads over one.

THE RULES ALL FIFTEEN SHARE · STATED ONCE, HERE The head. A6's title ladder unchanged — Medium 34 · Large 40 · Display 48 at 1440, 30 / 34 / 38 at 834, 26 / 28 / 30 at 390 — on a 780 px measure, sub 17 px muted on 620, eyebrow 13 px uppercase tracked .08em muted. Section padding Compact 64 · Comfortable 96 · Spacious 132, 80 at 834, 64 at 390; page margin 72 / 40 / 20. The head sits 48 px above the tiers, 32 px above the switch where there is one. Structure. <section aria-labelledby> named by its title, an <h2>. The tiers are a <ul> of <li>, each tier name an <h3>, so a reader hears “list of 3 items” before the first price. The benefits are a nested <ul>. 2 Table is the exception and the only real <table> in the library so far. The price is one string a screen reader can read: “€7 a month”, in a paragraph, with no aria-label and no visually-hidden alternative. Nothing is marked as an offer, a product or a price in structured data — no microdata, no JSON-LD; Ghost owns what it publishes about its tiers. Actions are <a> elements, never buttons: they change the URL and Portal opens on the fragment, so a reader can middle-click them. The visible label is the whole accessible name — three cards each saying “Subscribe” is three links with the same name inside three named list items, and that is what a list is for. The only <button>s in A7 are the cadence switch's two and 10 Tabs' three. DOM order is eyebrow, title, sub, switch, tiers, note; inside a tier it is badge, name, price, description, action, benefits. Two stated exceptions: 2 Table's column heads carry name, price and action with the benefits in the rows below, and 15 Both Prices puts the second price after the first inside one paragraph. Behaviours: two, and they are the category's only ones. The cadence switch at Billing period Both, and 10 Tabs' tier switch. Both cross-fade at 160 ms and nothing slides — a moving pill under changing numbers is two motions saying one thing. Neither runs while editing: the resting state is the control's own value, and every frame in A7 is a resting state. Focus. A6's ring verbatim: 2 px accent, 2 px outside, the inner gap taking the ground it sits on; the carried colour on a band. Hover darkens a fill 6% and pressed 10%; an outlined action fills to the hover surface instead. Nothing scales, lifts or gains a shadow. Data. Name, description, monthly price, yearly price, currency and benefits are Ghost's, per tier, and none of them is authorable in a section — the editor links to the tier's own settings. Authored: eyebrow, title, sub, note, the recommended tier, its badge label, the two action labels, and the design's controls. 13 Members is the only design that reads a reader's state. Empty. No description → the card is name, price, action, benefits. No benefits on a tier → that card ends at its action, and where no tier has benefits the hairline goes too. No eyebrow, sub or note → absent. Members off in Ghost → the section does not render, and that is the only such state in A7. Frames. A7's sections are drawn alone on the page ground, not above a footer as A6's were: a pricing section has ordinary sections above and below it, and A5's convention is the right one here. Where a design's rule concerns its neighbour the frame draws the neighbour and the caption says so.

7 · THE CONSISTENCY PASS · WRITTEN AFTER ALL FIFTEEN WERE DRAWN

Eight things the rules did not cover, and where they cluster

WHAT WAS CHECKED Every control list against its drawn panel. Every ladder value against every frame. Every piece of advice in a panel against the design it names. Every “flagged as mine” line against the settlement it departs from. The word for each fixed number — 416, 632, 480, 24, 32, 96 — in all sixteen files. Every price string, tier name and benefit line against the shared content in §4, so that no frame quietly carries a shorter list than its siblings. THE EIGHT THAT WERE WRONG, OR UNSTATED 1 · The Contrast ground on 8 Slim Row. §2's roster says 5 Contrast Band owns the inverted ground; 8 offers it as a value. The value stays — one line of prices is small enough to invert without a design of its own — and §2 now says so where it did not. 2 · Two accents on 10 Tabs. The selected tab's 2 px underline and the recommended tier's button are both accent. THE PATTERN IN THEM Six of the eight are designs with no card, or designs that know something about the reader. A7's shared rules were written for a tier in a plane on a page — settlement 2's three signals assume a plane to step, the accent rule assumes an author's choice, the order rule assumes three equal columns. Where a design has no plane (3 at None, 8, 12) the recommendation loses a signal; where it knows the reader (10's default tab, 13's computed accent) the section starts making decisions the sidebar did not. Both are worth having and both had to be said out loud rather than left to be discovered in a build.
TWO THINGS THIS CATEGORY OWES THE NEXT ONES The tier card is now a component — name, price, description, action, benefits, with fixed separations of 16 / 12 / 20 / 20 / 20 and rows 10 apart — and A30 Members Pages, A32 Paywall and A22 Newsletter will each want a version of it. It should carry forward verbatim, as A1's button did. The Portal boundary is now settled for the whole library: a section links into #/portal/…, draws none of Portal, manages no focus into its dialog, and never states a price, a proration or a saving that Stripe did not compute. A6·14 began it; A7 finished it.
WHAT IS NOT PROVEN HERE No frame in A7 has been built. Every contrast ratio quoted in this project is computed from hex values rather than measured in a browser, every collapse is drawn rather than resized into, and the two behaviours — the cadence switch and 10 Tabs — are drawn as resting states rather than run. The first build will find things. Every control list in the written spec was rewritten from its drawn panel as part of this pass. Where the two ever disagree again, the drawn panel is the authority. 2 Table's matrix is now literal, and that was the riskiest thing in the category. One row per tier-and-benefit pair, ticked in that tier's column and nowhere else: no de-duplication of shared benefits and no reading of “Everything in Free” as inheritance, because a template can neither compare two tiers' lists nor count them. A benefit two tiers both claim is drawn twice, the panel says so, and a first build should look at real tiers before trusting it.
