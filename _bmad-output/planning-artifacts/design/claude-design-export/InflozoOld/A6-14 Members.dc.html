Three states, two of them authored and one of them empty. Ghost tells the theme whether a reader is signed in and whether their subscription is paid; those are the only two facts the design uses, and it uses nothing else — not the reader's name, not their tier's name, not when they joined. A paid member sees no section at all by default, which is the honest answer to a banner whose whole purpose has already been served. The alternative value, When signed in → Hide the section, hides it from free members too, for a site that only wants to talk to visitors. Members off in Ghost → only the visitor state exists, the sign-in line is dropped and the primary links to the site's own subscribe page; A3·4's substitution, carried, with the sidebar naming the setting.

1 · Both Portal actions are <a href="#/portal/signup"> — real links, not buttons, because they change the URL and Portal opens on the fragment; a reader can middle-click them and land somewhere sensible. The banner does not manage focus into Portal's modal, does not trap it and does not return it: Portal owns its own dialog, and a theme reaching into it is how two focus traps end up fighting. That boundary is stated rather than implied. 2 · The sign-in line is one sentence with one link inside it, so “Sign in” is the whole accessible name and “Already a member?” is read as the text around it. The link carries a permanent underline rather than colour alone, and its target is 44 px tall. 3 · The member state is rendered on the server and the markup for the other states is not in the page — no hidden copies, no aria-live, nothing swapped after load, and therefore no announcement to make. A reader who signs in gets a new page, and the new page has the member state on it. Nothing about a reader's membership is exposed as a state, badge or role anywhere in the section. MEMBER ASK AND NO JAVASCRIPT · PATCH PASS · 29 AUG 2026 The ask is conditional. Where the primary is a sign-up ask or a paid-tier ask it does not render when the connected site cannot support it — self-signup switched off in Ghost, or no payment provider connected — and the sidebar names the setting rather than shipping a button that fails. An ordinary link is unaffected. Ruled by the owner, 29 August 2026: the button keeps its place and its scale, and the site chooses what it says and where it goes — a fallback label and link (fallbackLabel ≤ 24 · fallbackUrl, in the panel beside the ask), so a reader meets a working button rather than a gap. Left empty, this design’s own rule applies: no primary label or URL, no section. Where the ask opens Ghost's own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. The link is a real href to a Portal address, so the page stays where it is and no window opens. No JavaScript: Ghost's sign-up address will not accept a plain form submission — tested against two live Ghost servers — so the form needs JavaScript. Without it the form is replaced by the designed notice drawn above: one line telling the reader what happened and a link that works. The empty, focus, invalid, submitting and done states are unchanged — Ghost's own script applies those.
