Slim Line

One short quotation on a single line between rules, the attribution inline after it. It has no head, no card, its own 32 · 44 · 56 padding scale — A7·8's — and a 120-character ceiling that no other design in A8 imposes. Past that the quotation is not a line any more, and the design says so instead of wrapping to three.

It sits close to two things that are not it. A6·10 Slim is a banner: it asks the reader for something and ends in a link. A2's bars are page furniture: they sit at the top of every page, and they can be dismissed. This is a section in the flow of a page that says one reader's sentence and asks for nothing — which is why it draws no button, no dismiss, and no link at any value.

PRIMARY · desktop 1440 · light · padding Comfortable 44, rules Above and below, alignment Centred, quote size Card 20, attribution Inline, ground Background · 1 of 7 authored quotes drawn

One line, two rules, 44 px of air above and below. The quotation and the attribution sit on the same baseline with a 16 px gap, and the attribution is one string rather than two lines — name at 600 in text, then a comma, then role and organisation in text-muted, all at 14 px. It is the only place in A8 where the attribution is inline, and it is what keeps the section to one line.
The rules run the full content width, not the length of the type — a rule that stops where a 41-character quotation stops is a underline, and the point of the rules is to make the section a band of the page. There is no avatar at any value, no eyebrow, no title, no note and no link: this design draws four of the category's fourteen fields and keeps the other ten. ⚑ The inline attribution string, the 16 px gap and the full-width rules are mine.

155 CHARS · PAST THE CEILING · THE DESIGN HANDS OFF TO 1 SINGLE, WHICH IS WHAT THIS IS
120 characters is where the quotation stops being a line. At 1440 the content width holds about 118 characters at Card 20 with the attribution after it; past that the line wraps, and a two-line quotation between two rules is not this design — it is 1 Single with rules. So the ceiling is a hand-off, not a truncation: over 120 characters the section draws 1 Single and the sidebar reads “121 characters — drawing 1 Single. Under 120 for one line.” The editor's counter is hard at 120 for this design, unlike every other design in A8 where it is hard only at 300, and it is the one place a design's own limit is stricter than the category's field limit. The field still stores up to 300 — a site that types 200 characters here and switches to 2 Three Up keeps every word. ⚑ The 120 ceiling, the hand-off and the counter's wording are mine. The step-down does not apply in this design. Its two sizes are Small 17 and Card 20, and a quotation short enough to be here is under 180 characters by definition, so the ladder's thresholds never fire.

THE BOUNDARY · WHAT THIS IS NOT A6·10 Slim. Same height, same rules, same one line — and it ends in an action, because it is a CTA banner. If a site wants a quotation with “Subscribe” after it, that is A6·11 Reasons, which is built for exactly that and has the accent button to do it with. This design has no button and no link at any value. A2's announcement bars. They live at the top of the page above the header, they are dismissible, they can be sticky, and A2·9 rotates between them. This is none of those things: it sits where the site put it in the flow, it cannot be dismissed, it never sticks, and it draws one quotation that does not change. A4·16 Pull Quote. That is a quotation inside an article, from the article. This is a reader's words about the publication. The two look alike at Small 17 and they are different sections with different fields. ⚑ All three boundaries stated by me.

1 · <figure> → <blockquote><p> + <figcaption>, no <ul>, and no <h2> — so no aria-labelledby: with 12 Rows, one of the two designs in A8 that is a region with no accessible name. Inventing “Testimonial” for a one-line section would put a heading in a design whose whole argument is that it does not have one. 2 · The inline attribution is one <figcaption> with the name in a <span>, not two elements laid out in a row: it reads as one sentence — “Peter Lindqvist, Reader since 2020” — because that is what it is. The comma is typed, and the muted colour is styling on the second half rather than a separate field. The rules are borders on the figure, never <hr>: two <hr>s around one sentence is two separators announced for a section with one line in it. Nothing is focusable, nothing is hoverable, nothing can be dismissed and nothing moves. This design has no interactive element at all, which no other design in A8 can say — the other fourteen at least have the optional link. There is no accent pixel in it in either mode. No design ever turns into another designPast about 120 characters the line wraps to two and this design draws itself wrapped — rules full width, attribution in whichever value is set, nothing truncated, clamped or faded. “Over 120 the section draws 1 Single” is deleted; the counter advises 1 Single instead.
Item counts are a number pickerNo subject: one quotation is drawn and there is no count row; the other eleven rows are kept and never drawn.
The Remove button never greys outRemove was never dimmed here and still is not, at any count. Removing the last row leaves the list empty, the section then does not render, and the editor keeps the empty repeater and its Add quote control rather than drawing a placeholder person.
Member buttons are conditional, and Ghost’s sign-up pop-up needs JavaScriptNo subject: this design draws no link at any value, so it bears no ask to gate and carries neither note. It is also the one design in A8 a site cannot hide from a member state — no Member visibility row — which stays on record as a cost rather than a tidy exemption.
The no-JavaScript noticeNo subject, and nothing to withdraw: this design holds no subscribe and no sign-in field, so there is nothing for the notice to replace, and no sentence here ever promised a subscription without JavaScript. With JavaScript off this design is unchanged — every quotation, name, role and organisation is server-rendered.
Ghost’s templates cannot count, add, or rememberThe 120-character advisory is the editor’s count of the authored string, written as the words are typed — a template cannot count characters. CSS cannot see content either, which is why nothing here measures the line on the page: past the advisory the line simply wraps and the design draws itself wrapped.
Avatars with no photographNo subject: no avatar and no initials block is drawn in this design at any value, which is why it carries no Avatars row either.
