Utility + Nav

Two tiers with two different jobs: a 38 px utility strip for the things that are not navigation — secondary links, social, an editorial note — and the main bar beneath it for the nav that matters. It is the first design to render Ghost's secondary navigation in the header, and the one A2 must be checked against: an announcement bar above a utility strip is three stacked bands before the reader reaches a headline.

The strip is 38 px, on surface, 13 px throughout, and carries no accent at all — it must read as a rail the eye can skip. The one editorial line in it (“Issue 47 · out today”) is optional content, not a control, and it is the only place in A1 where the header states something about the publication’s state rather than its structure. The mode toggle leaves the strip (owner ruling): when its control is On it renders as the standard 38 px icon button in the main bar’s cluster and as the drawer’s switch row, the two in sync.

The strip does not survive at 390 — two bands plus a headline is the whole screen — so its entire contents move into the drawer under a labelled group, with the mode toggle becoming a proper switch row. This is the clearest case of the category’s “unused fields go to the drawer foot” rule, and the one that justifies it: the editorial note is the only thing dropped, because it is decoration, and the sidebar says so.

ACCESSIBILITY · two tiers, one header
1Two navs, two names. The strip is nav aria-label="Secondary", the bar is nav aria-label="Primary", both inside one header. Unlabelled duplicate navs are a common failure in two-tier headers.
2Focus order is top tier first. Which means a keyboard user passes five utility links before the primary nav — so the skip link is mandatory here, not optional, and it is the first stop on the page.
313 px is the floor, and it is checked. Strip text is 13 px in text-muted on surface — the lowest-contrast pair in the whole design. Every pack must clear AA at this pair or the strip falls back to text colour; the mode toggle and social links are icon-plus-name, never icon alone.
4The mode toggle announces state. A button with aria-pressed, name “Dark mode”, not a sun/moon glyph alone; in the drawer it is a switch row with the same name, and the two stay in sync.
5Scrolling away is not removal. When the strip leaves on scroll it is not taken out of the tree or the tab order; the sticky bar simply covers the page above it. Everything in it also exists in the drawer and the footer.

Data Strip links follow Strip source: Ghost’s secondary navigation (read-only rows — “Edit navigation in Ghost”, never an Add button) or Authored (P0·3 item list; labels inline, links via the Link Picker; reorder moves items across sides). Caps 5/3 in both modes; social binds to Ghost’s accounts, version-gated (§0·8). Empty secondary nav and no social and no note → the strip is not rendered at all and the bar stands alone — still this design, never another (Part A·A8) — stated in the sidebar. Mode toggle appears only if the site’s pack ships both modes.
Responsive ≥ 1024 both tiers full. 768–1023 strip sheds social then the note; bar loses search and the ghost action. ≤ 767 strip removed entirely, contents move to a labelled group in the drawer, note dropped. Strip height 38 and bar height hold at every width above the drawer.
Empty state · flagged A strip with one link renders as one link — it does not stretch or centre. Flagged: the shed order (social, then note, then nothing) and the 5/3 caps are mine. So is the claim that this design is the A2 stress case; whether an announcement bar is permitted above a utility strip at all is A2’s decision, and I have assumed yes with the bar taking contrast so the three bands read as three.
