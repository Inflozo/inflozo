A13 PROCESS / HOW IT WORKS · CATEGORY ARTEFACTS · READ FIRST · PAPER PACK

Fifteen arrangements of an ordered list

A13's content is a sequence: a short title, usually a sentence or two of body, sometimes a stage label, sometimes a picture. What makes it different from every category before it is that the order is the content. A team read out of order is still the team; a process read out of order is wrong. Everything below follows from that — the markup is an <ol> in all fifteen designs, the numeral is a rendering of position rather than a field anybody types, and reorder is consequential in every single design rather than in six of them.

It is also the first category whose repeating item has no required media at all. A11's item was a logo and A12's was a face; a step is a line of type that may or may not have picked up a picture along the way. So the missing-image answer here is not a second upload and not an initials block — it is the numeral plate, a mark the theme generates from something it already has.

This file carries the four settlements §8 asks A13 to make, the step card, the numeral ladder and the marker, the rules all fifteen share, the fifteen-field shared list, the roster with its structural descriptors, the eight steps every frame draws, the tokenisation proof across three packs and the stress frame. The prose companion is A13 Process — Spec.md. Where the two disagree, the drawn panel is the authority.

THE ITEM LIST · steps[] IS A13'S ONE REPEATER Where it lives. Below the design's own controls: one row per step showing the rendered numeral at 20 px in muted as the row's leading mark — not a thumbnail, because nine designs have no picture — with title as the row's label, label in muted beside it where authored, a drag handle, a remove action, and Add step at the end. Add step is disabled at eight with “Eight steps is the most a section holds.” ⚑ Mine: the seeded string and the eight-step message. Remove. Never disabled. Every step after the removed one re-numbers, which is A13's one destructive-looking edit that is not destructive — nothing was authored, so nothing is lost. Below a design's designed floor it hands off to the design its panel names rather than blocking the edit; at one step the section does not render, at zero the repeater shows Add step and a line saying so. Reorder. Meaningful and consequential in all fifteen, and it is the only category where that sentence has no exceptions. It changes the numbering, the reading order and the meaning. There is no sort control of any kind, and a “reverse” value is refused — a process backwards is a different process, and the author can drag. Inside an item the user edits content only: title, body, label, and icon or image where the design reads one. Nothing about a step's size, crop, emphasis, ground or numeral is exposed anywhere in A13. So “make step 1 bigger”, “highlight the step we are on” and “start this one open” are not expressible by construction. Four per-item controls are refused in writing: a per-step accent, a per-step crop, a “current step” flag, and a per-step media family. The current-step flag is the one a site will ask for, and the answer is that a marketing section describes a process rather than tracking a reader through it — scroll-spy in 15 Sticky Rail is the only thing in A13 that marks a current step, and it is derived from the scroll position rather than authored. What empty looks like, per field. title is required and a step without one cannot be saved. body empty draws a numeral and a title alone — nothing reserved, nothing generated, no “Coming soon” — and the cell simply stays shorter, except in 4 Cards where the card keeps its row's height and the space falls at its foot. label empty closes the gap above the title. image empty draws the numeral plate; icon empty draws the numeral in the icon's box. imageAlt empty gives alt="".

Six steps and about three weeks, though a news piece can go from pitch to send inside four days if two editors are free and the reporting is already done.
