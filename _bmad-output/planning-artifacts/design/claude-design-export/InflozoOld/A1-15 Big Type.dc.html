64 px cannot stay on screen while someone reads, so the stuck state is this design’s own 56 px bar: wordmark 17 px, nav inline after it, one action at the right.

The tagline, the search button and the ghost action leave with the large type and return at the top of the page. This is the largest change any A1 design makes on scroll, and the reason the transition is a 160 ms cross-fade rather than a size animation — interpolating 64 px down to 17 px draws the eye to the movement instead of the page.

At 390 the wordmark is 28 px — the floor — and wraps to two lines if the name is long, with the bar growing to fit. ACCESSIBILITY · large type is not a heading
1Size does not confer heading level. The wordmark is a link to / at every size, and an h1 only on the home page. At 64 px it is tempting to mark it up as a heading on every template; that would give each page two competing h1s.
2The hover underline is scaled, not invented. 3 px at 8 px offset is the 2 px body-size underline proportioned to 64 px type. Its colour is accent, which at this size and weight clears contrast comfortably against the page.
3Reflow at 320 px and 200 % zoom. The 28 px floor and the two-line wrap are what keep the wordmark inside the viewport at 320 px; nothing in the header scrolls horizontally, and the collapsed bar is what a zoomed reader sees for most of a session.
4The collapse is a cross-fade, and it respects reduced-motion. Under reduced-motion the two states swap instantly. Neither state reorders the DOM, so focus survives the change.
5Tagline is a paragraph. It sits after the wordmark in the source, is never a heading, and is hidden by its control rather than emptied.
