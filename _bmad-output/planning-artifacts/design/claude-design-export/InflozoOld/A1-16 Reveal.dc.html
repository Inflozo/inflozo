The slow return of the personal homepage

Why writers are leaving the feed — and coming home to a page of their own.

At the top of the page the header is ordinary: in flow, on the page ground, hairline under. It only becomes a floating object once it has been away and come back, which is what makes the returned state read as a response to the reader rather than a permanent fixture.

BEHAVIOUR · hidden · scrolled 900px, going down

…the personal page never disappeared; it went quiet, and quiet is not the same as gone. What changed was where the default lived: for fifteen years the answer to “where do you write?” was the name of a company.

The writers coming back are not nostalgic. They want the archive to outlive the platform, and they have noticed that a page of their own is the only version of that which has ever worked.

NO HEADER The bar is off-screen and the viewport is entirely the post. Annotation only — nothing renders here.

Hiding starts after 200 px of downward scroll and takes 160 ms. The header stays in the DOM and in the tab order while hidden, so it is reachable by keyboard even when it cannot be seen — tabbing to it brings it back.

Returned, the bar is a floating panel: 58 px, on surface, inset 12 px from the top and 24 from the sides, with the md shadow. Search and the ghost action stay behind — four nav items and one action are what a reader coming back up the page needs.

Returning takes one upward gesture of any distance, so the header is never more than a flick away. It leaves again only on a downward scroll of 200 px, which stops it flickering on the small corrections people make while reading.

At 390 the panel is 56 px inset 10/12 and carries the wordmark, the short CTA and the drawer toggle; the nav is in the drawer as everywhere else. The drawer itself never hides — once it is open the reveal behaviour is suspended, so scrolling a long nav list cannot dismiss the header underneath it.

ACCESSIBILITY · a header that is sometimes not there
1Hidden is not removed. The header stays in the DOM, in the accessibility tree and in the tab order while off-screen; tabbing into it returns it. Nothing is display:none and nothing is aria-hidden — a keyboard user must never lose the navigation because a mouse user scrolled.
2Reduced motion keeps the behaviour. The slide is dropped, the thresholds stay. Suppressing the reveal entirely would leave a reader mid-post with no header at all, which is a worse outcome than an instant change.
3Anchors and the panel. The returned panel is 58 px plus a 12 px inset, so in-page anchor targets get 70 px of scroll padding. A heading reached from a table of contents is never left underneath it.
4The drawer suspends it. While the drawer is open the reveal rules stop, the page behind is inert, and Escape closes the drawer and returns focus to the toggle. Otherwise scrolling inside the drawer could dismiss the bar that owns it.
5Skip link is unaffected. It stays the first focus stop on the page whichever state the header is in, and focusing it does not trigger a return — it goes straight to the content.
