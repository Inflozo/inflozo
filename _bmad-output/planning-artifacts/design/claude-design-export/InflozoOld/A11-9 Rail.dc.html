Rail

One line of logos wider than the window, scrolled by the reader. The distinction from 8 Marquee is the whole design: a marquee moves whether anyone is watching, and a rail moves only when a hand causes it. Nothing on this frame is in motion at rest, and nothing ever starts on its own.

It is the one design in A11 whose furniture depends on a measurement — where the track already fits its container there is nothing to scroll, so no arrows and no fade are drawn, and it is still a rail — and, because its two arrows are real controls rather than artwork, the one design in the category that always spends the accent, on their hover and focus, whatever the site authored.

The rail is full bleed, its content starts on the left page margin, and the logos sit at natural width on the gap the site chose — 8 Marquee's geometry exactly, with no cells and no counts. The arrows sit on the page margins, on the track's centre line, over the fade: 38 px boxes, surface, hairline, pack radius, A1·14's outlined icon button. At the start the left arrow is disabled at 40% and still focusable, so a keyboard reader can tell it exists and that it does nothing yet.
One press moves the rail by the viewport minus 96 px — 1,248 at 1440 — so the logo that was at the leading edge is still on screen after the press and nothing is stepped over. Scroll snapping is refused: a snap point has to be a logo's edge, and a 216 px wordmark and a 26 px square mark cannot share one rule without one of them sitting off-centre. What is measured is the furniture, not the design. Six logos is the floor for choosing this design, and the test at render is whether the track is wider than its container — so the same six logos scroll on a phone and sit still on a desktop, and it is a rail at both widths. Where the track fits, the arrows and the fade are not drawn; the panel may advise “at this count, 1 Row reads better”. Advice, never a switch. ⚑ Mine. The arrows are the reason this design always spends the accent. They are controls, so their hover and focus take accent — A6's treatment for an icon button — while every logo stays untinted. Fourteen of the fifteen designs in A11 can contain no accent pixel; this one cannot. A disabled arrow stays in the DOM, stays focusable and stays 38 px. aria-disabled="true" rather than the disabled attribute, drawn at 40% — so the pair never changes width and the rail never shifts sideways when a reader reaches an end. ⚑ Mine.

ACCESSIBILITY · A SCROLL CONTAINER A KEYBOARD CAN REACH The track is a role="group" with tabindex="0" and an accessible name taken from the section's title — “The letter, elsewhere, scrollable”. That is what makes the arrow keys work, and it is required rather than decorative: a scroll container with no tab stop is content a keyboard cannot reach, which is 2.1.1 whatever the arrows do. With no title authored the name is “Logo wall, scrollable”, the one generated string in A11. ⚑ Mine. The arrows are <button>s named “Scroll left” and “Scroll right”, with aria-disabled rather than disabled at the ends so focus is never dropped mid-interaction. They are after the track in the DOM at Arrows At the ends and before it at Above the rail — the drawn order in both cases. Under reduced motion the rail still scrolls; it just stops animating. scroll-behavior: smooth becomes auto and a press jumps. Reduced motion changes nothing here, and it stops 8 Marquee's track, because a rail's motion is the reader's own — the preference is about motion nobody asked for. Neither design becomes another one. A linked logo inside the rail is an ordinary link and tabbing to one scrolls it into view — the browser's own behaviour, not a script — so unlike 8 Marquee there is nothing to chase. This is the design to choose when the logos are links and there are too many for 5 Cards. Contrast: title 15.8:1 light / 15.1:1 dark, arrow glyph 15.8:1 / 15.1:1 resting and 3.3:1 / 6.3:1 on hover and focus, where it takes the accent; a disabled glyph at 40% is 4.1:1 / 3.9:1, above the 3:1 non-text floor; hairline 1.4:1 / 1.6:1. A logo takes no ratio. The fade is a mask and never covers a focused item, because focus scrolls it clear first. Print draws the wrapped wall, as 8 Marquee does: paper has no viewport, and a rail's overflow would be a page edge cutting a mark in half.
