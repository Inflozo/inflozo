Flush Left

The same set as 1 Centred, set flush to the left margin on a half measure, with the right half left empty. The empty half has to read as a composition rather than a hole — that is what this design settles. It also carries the proof row, three authored pairs on a hairline, as one of its control values.

The text block is 620 px at the left margin and the remaining 604 px is empty on purpose. It is not centred, not stretched and nothing is invented to fill it: the emptiness is what makes the headline's ragged right edge read as deliberate, and it is the reason the three image designs can fall back here without the words moving a pixel. The sub is held to 540 px inside the block — 80 px short of the headline's measure — so the two ragged edges do not line up and pretend to be a rectangle. Flagged as mine: the empty right half as a rule, and the 80 px offset.

1 · Eyebrow and headline as 1 Centred: a <p> above the page's single <h1>, unlabelled section, no landmark. 2 · Flush left with no justification and no hyphenation, so the rag is even and no line is stretched. text-wrap: pretty handles the widow; nothing is manually broken, because a manual break in a user's headline is wrong at the next width. 3 · The proof row is a <dl> — each value a <dd> named by its <dt> label, which is why the label may be longer than the value and still be the thing that explains it. Visual order puts the value first and the DOM keeps the term first, so the reading order is “issues published, 48”. Flagged as mine — the alternative, a term after its definition, reads worse aloud than it looks on screen.
