Settlement 3's hover with its third clause finally visible: the picture darkens 4% — a brightness(0.96) filter, not an overlay, so a photograph's own colour survives it. The step is deliberately small; the title's underline is what says “link”, and the picture only has to acknowledge the pointer. In dark the same hover lightens 4% instead, since darkening a near-black photograph does nothing. 160 ms ease-out, one transition.

WHY 16:9 AND NOT 3:2 · the arithmetic
At 410 px wide, 16:9 is 231 px of picture and 3:2 is 273. The card's text is about 110 px at a 21 px title and two lines of body, so the picture is two thirds of the card at 16:9 and three quarters at 3:2. Six cards at 3:2 make a section where the words are a caption band under a grid of photographs. 7 Alternating Media can afford 3:2 because its picture has a whole half-page beside it, and this one cannot, which is why the two designs disagree on a crop the category otherwise keeps consistent. The disagreement is stated on both frames. Flagged as mine: the arithmetic and the decision to let two designs in one category carry different crops.

Some fields we drew do not existGhost has no “posts per page” setting a theme can read, so How many belongs to this section and is written here. At Content source: From posts the list is Ghost-sourced — no Add, no Remove, no drag — and post content is plain-text-locked, which is unchanged.
Member buttons are conditionalThe section action does not always render. Below the set: Action is where it is drawn. It is withheld when the connected site has self-signup switched off or no payment provider connected — a “Sign up free” or paid-tier button on a site that cannot take the sign-up is a dead end. And where the action opens Ghost’s own sign-up pop-up: with JavaScript off, nothing happens. Both notes are drawn at the action’s own editor, beside Member visibility, which is a different thing — that hides the whole section server-side.
The no-JavaScript noticeThis design holds no subscribe or sign-in field, so it draws no no-JavaScript form notice. A5 holds no form anywhere: every sentence a reader sees is authored text, and the category makes no claim that anything can be subscribed to without JavaScript.
