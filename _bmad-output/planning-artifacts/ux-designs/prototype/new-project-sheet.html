The greyed-with-a-reason pattern it uses

A control that could act but cannot now is greyed with its reason in the helper slot; one that could never act here is absent and the panel says why. The two mean different things (rulings R-33, R-68).

WHERE THE SENTENCE SITS: DIRECTLY UNDER THE CONTROL IT EXPLAINS, INSIDE THAT CONTROL'S OWN ROW GROUP, 11.5PX #6E6A64 · NOT A TOOLTIP, NOT AN ICON, NOT A FOOTNOTE AT THE BOTTOM OF THE PANEL · ONE SENTENCE, NAMING THE CONTROL THAT DID IT THE ROW NEVER MOVES AND NEVER DISAPPEARS · THE PANEL GROWS BY THE HEIGHT OF THE SENTENCE, WHICH IS WHY THE SENTENCE IS ONE LINE OR TWO AND NEVER A PARAGRAPH A GREYED CONTROL DOES NOT ACCEPT A VALUE IT WILL NOT HONOUR · IT IS NOT FOCUSABLE FOR EDITING, BUT IT IS READ ALOUD WITH ITS REASON: "OVERLAY TINT, SOFT DARK, UNAVAILABLE — NOT AVAILABLE WHILE THE MEDIA USES THE ACCENT COLOUR"

Greying is the visible half; the sentence is the whole point. A control that greys without saying why has only moved the confusion — the user now knows something is wrong and still cannot tell whether it is a rule or a bug. The sentence names the control that switched it off, so the user can reach the cause in one move.
