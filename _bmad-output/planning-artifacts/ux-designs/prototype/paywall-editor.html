C.3 · THE PAYWALL EDITOR

A canvas that is not a page

Ghost renders the paywall where the author put the members-only marker, mid-article. That makes it a template surface rather than a placeable section: there is no page to drop it on, and no design picker in the sidebar can reach it. It gets its own canvas, and the canvas has to say so at a glance — the chrome above it is dark, the breadcrumb reads Paywall, and what it shows is a cut, not a page.

Notes. Four things mark this out as a different canvas: the chrome is ink rather than paper, the breadcrumb sits under Template surfaces with a mono chip saying so, the canvas mat is a colder grey than the page canvas, and the article above the cut is dimmed to 55% because it is context the user cannot edit here. The dashed coral line is the author's Public preview marker, with the honest warning beside it — everything below it is stripped server-side, which is why the Fade treatment is masked onto the last visible paragraph and nowhere else. View as stays in the chrome because switching to Paid member is the only way to check that the cut disappears entirely for someone who has paid.
