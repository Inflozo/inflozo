# PRD Quality Review — Inflozo v3.0

*Reviewed 2026-08-18 against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Inputs: `prd.md` (971 lines), `addendum.md`, `sections-inventory.md`, `appendix-b1-template-contexts.md`, `appendix-h1-string-catalog.md`, `.memlog.md` (for decision provenance). Third validation round.*

## Overall verdict

This is an unusually well-built PRD and it survives a hostile read: the FR set is complete and contiguous, every requirement is owned by exactly one epic, the soft adjectives that normally rot a spec have all been given pass/fail bounds (FR-D14 bounds "lockup" at a 5-second main-thread block; FR-H4 bounds "gracefully" with "a section bound to zero items renders nothing"), and the hardest delegation in the document — §7.5's schema sketch — is handled better than most PRDs handle things they *do* specify, because it names twelve tables the sketch omits and assigns the whole-model story to E1. What is at risk is not the specification; it is the **hand-off scaffolding around it**. §7.6's fourteen verify-at-build items — one of which is the load-bearing premise under thirteen A30 variants, FR-I1, FR-D6 and FR-O1, and whose *previous* version was already discovered false mid-encode — carry the instruction "re-confirm each before the dependent epic starts" and no epic in §8 owns doing it. The same pattern repeats three more times: G1 and G2 are the PRD's top two goals and no epic schedules the moderated session that measures them; §7.3 names a focus model as "a UX-pass deliverable" that the UX mandate in the header does not include; and E1 points the Dev agent at a design directory that a sibling file in this same folder documents as ~31 items stale against PRD v1.0, with no warning attached. Every one of these is a gap in *who acts on what the PRD honestly disclosed*, not a gap in the disclosure.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, and — rarer — the PRD repeatedly names the argument it is *refusing* to make. §7.3's same-origin iframe section is the clearest case: "not because cross-origin is incapable. A cross-origin frame can measure its own rects and host its own `contenteditable`… any claim otherwise is false and must not be used to defend this boundary." That is a decision-maker being handed the real trade (cost, not capability) instead of a rationalization. NFR-2 does the same for Lighthouse — "a host-determined number has no truth value" — and then replaces the dropped metric with five CI-assertable properties rather than leaving a hole. FR-I4 settles routes upload as impossible-by-permission and promotes the manual card to a first-class flow rather than a fallback. AD3 keeps the rejected alternatives on the record with reasons.

Trade-offs name what was given up. FR-Q2's three reserved custom-setting slots: "a Light-only project consequently holds three slots it never compiles, which is a deliberate trade for a uniform cap and not an oversight to be optimized away." FR-J14's always-latest library: "chosen for implementation simplicity" with the consequence — "any redeploy includes all library changes since the project's last deploy — there is no way to redeploy without them" — stated in the same sentence, then mitigated with a mandatory confirm. FR-K2's destructive optimization states the trade in the UI copy.

Where this dimension is thinner is the *business* case's presentation. G7 quotes "infra break-even at **9–10 Pro subscribers**… **before** the permanent Test-environment cost" — the smaller of the two numbers Appendix F computes, while F.3 has just argued at length that Test is permanent and F.5 gives ≈ 11 on the total. The caveat is present and honest, but the goal table leads with the flattering figure.

### Findings

- **medium** Goals table quotes the pre-Test break-even (§1.3 G7, Appendix F.5) — G7's headline is "9–10 Pro subscribers… **before** the permanent Test-environment cost", while F.3 establishes Test as "a standing cost from the first customer onward" and F.5 computes ≈ 11 against the total. The goal a reader acts on should be the one against the cost base the PRD says is real. *Fix:* state G7 as "≈ 11 Pro subscribers on the total permanent fixed cost (9–10 on the baseline before Test)".
- **low** "The most beautiful UI/UX in the Ghost ecosystem" (§1.2) is the one unfalsifiable clause in an otherwise gated document, and it is load-bearing enough to appear in the Vision. Nothing in §1.3, §4's DoD or §8 tests it, and §1.4 does not claim it as a differentiator. *Fix:* either scope it to what the moderated session can observe, or move it to Appendix H as a voice directive rather than a product promise.

## Substance over theater — adequate

Almost nothing here is furniture. The NFRs carry product-specific thresholds and refuse the generic form outright (NFR-2 names 50 KB gzipped CSS per rendered template and 40 KB JS for a defined "maximal design"; NFR-5 names the tool, ruleset and zero-violation threshold; NFR-1 names the reference laptop, the throttle and the 40-section fixture, and then explicitly rules CI out as a venue). §1.4's differentiators are each gated somewhere: #1 by NFR-6(c3) and E5's play-loop gate, #2 by the same, #5 by FR-J13/J16, #7 by FR-J14's cadence commitment. Appendix F.7 pre-empts its own misreading — "It is **not** a forecast — 200 of 1,200 is a **16.7% paid share against G9's ≥ 3% target**, more than five times the goal" — which is the opposite of theater.

The exception is §3. Four personas, one line each, and the PRD says outright they drive nothing: "for tone and for the UX workflow, not permissions — no requirement is prioritized by persona." Under the rubric that is textbook persona theater, and normally it would be harmless. It is not harmless here, because the header then hands journey authorship to bmad-ux "using the §3 personas" with a mandated floor of four journeys. Three of those four (connect → first deploy, blank-canvas build, Free-plan ship) map plausibly onto "the solo publisher." The fourth — **downgrade recovery** — maps onto no named persona at all, and it is the journey with the most product surface behind it (FR-L3's block-until-resolved sheet, the plan-locked read-only state, the safety-operations carve-out, FR-J12's stays-exportable rule). A one-line persona carrying no goal, no context and no failure mode is not enough input to author that journey.

### Findings

- **high** Personas are too thin to carry the journey delegation they are assigned (§3, header note) — each persona is a single clause with no goals, context, or failure states, and the PRD concedes they drive no requirement. The four mandated journeys are then to be authored "using the §3 personas". Downgrade recovery in particular has no persona: nothing in §3 describes a user who lapses, and FR-L3's flow is the most emotionally loaded surface in the product. *Fix:* give each persona a goal, a pre-Inflozo status quo and one failure mode, and name which persona owns each of the four mandated journeys — a two-paragraph addition that makes the delegation executable.
- **low** "The multi-site operator" persona is defined entirely by a plan limit ("Pro; up to 10 connections, 25 projects"), which is Appendix F restated as a person (§3). It drives no distinct surface — there is no cross-site view, no bulk operation, no shared asset scoping beyond FR-K1's global library. *Fix:* either cut it to three personas or name what it makes true that Pro's caps do not.

## Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and the document is built on it: Ghost has a primitive theming story (§1.1), the fix is a builder whose canvas *is* the page rather than a preview of it (§1.2), and everything expensive in the PRD is downstream of that one bet — P4's one-source-per-section, §7.3's two-renderers-over-one-source, FR-G3's annotated-HTML registry, the rejection of a React preview layer ("Any proposal to re-implement a section as a React preview component is rejected by this PRD"), and NFR-6(c)'s three fidelity layers with the golden-diff option explicitly refused because "it could only pass by normalizing away the image URLs FR-J5 depends on." The second bet — safe installs — produces its own coherent cluster (FR-C1's Staff token, FR-J13, FR-J16, FR-J9), and the PRD accepts a real onboarding cost for it rather than softening the promise.

Prioritization follows the thesis rather than difficulty. E4 is the runtime, E5 the editor, E7 the compiler, and the five pilot sections are fixed normatively "so they cannot be chosen at implementation time from the easy end of the library" — each chosen for the hard axis it exercises. The E4/E7 joint gate exists because neither epic can verify the central claim alone. That is thesis-driven sequencing.

Counter-metrics exist on all three business goals (G8/G9/G10), and they are real counters, not decoration: G10's is "cancellation always completes in ≤ 3 clicks (no retention dark patterns)", which constrains the product rather than measuring it. Scope kind is coherent — platform-shaped, with §4's "No de-scope contingency" logic matching ("The sections are the heart of the tool — every one must work perfectly").

The gap is that the thesis's *experiential* half has no gate. §1.2 promises "extremely easy, genuinely fun"; G1, G2 and G6 are the only measurements anywhere near it, and the finding below shows two of the three are unowned.

### Findings

- **high** G1 and G2 are unowned and outside the Definition of Done (§1.3, §4, §8 E15) — both are specified as "p75 across a moderated usability test of **8 first-time users**… timed by the observer on the NFR-1 reference environment, pre-launch", and both appear exactly once in the document. §4's DoD list does not include them. E15 "owns the gates" and lists the E2E suite, the perceptual diff, the render matrix, the axe scan, "the manual NFR-1 performance gate on the reference laptop", quota alarms, the PITR drill, legal pages and the cutover — but not the moderated session. Nobody recruits the eight users, nobody schedules it, and no consequence is named for failing it. The PRD's top two goals and the only measurement of its "extremely easy" promise are, operationally, decoration. *Fix:* add the moderated session to E15's owned gates with recruitment and timing, and add G1/G2 to §4's DoD alongside the render matrix and accessibility scan.
- **medium** No gate on the "fun" half of the thesis (§1.2, §1.3) — G6 (60fps, <100 ms) and E5's play-loop gate cover *responsiveness* and *never lose content*; nothing covers whether variant shuffling is enjoyable, which §1.4 #2 claims as a differentiator ("Choosing a design becomes play"). The moderated session is the natural venue and does not ask. *Fix:* add one observed question to the G1/G2 session rather than inventing a metric.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This is the dimension the PRD is best at, and it holds under an unforgiving read. I searched for the standard tells and the PRD has pre-bounded every one:

- "degrades gracefully" (FR-D14) is immediately bounded — "slower is acceptable, lockups and state corruption are not. **'Lockup' is bounded as any main-thread block over 5 seconds**, so the clause has a pass/fail condition on the stress fixture."
- "no-op gracefully" (FR-D9) is bounded by "never corrupt state" plus the AD1 journal-clearing table, and the testable claim is scoped honestly: "**the journal is present and replayable after a reload within the same session**; loss to browser eviction is a stated limitation, not a requirement."
- "render gracefully with fewer items" (FR-H4) is bounded by "a section bound to zero items renders nothing on the live site."
- "60fps" (NFR-1) is decomposed into "p95 frame time ≤ 16.7 ms with **no long task > 50 ms** across a 3-second trace of drag, reorder, Variant Shuffle and control changes."
- FR-G5's uniqueness bar — the requirement most likely to be untestable — is given a mechanical proxy: "no two variants in a category may share a structural descriptor tuple — a CI assertion over the inventory", with the honest caveat that "the proxy makes the failure case findable, not the judgment unnecessary."
- FR-D17's "never-lose-content" is converted into "zero prop loss across N shuffles and a full re-roll on the 40-section stress fixture", and E5 fixes N at 20 and adds "Asserted programmatically over the project doc: no telemetry, no human judgment."
- FR-J16's "zero false positives" binds the compiler as well as the check: "a compiled filename must never change when the user changed nothing."

Epic exit criteria are testable rather than narrative, and the E4/E7 joint gate correctly refuses to let either epic self-certify. The two gaps below are places where the *procedure* rather than the *criterion* is missing.

### Findings

- **high** The three membership canvases have no specified filenames, and the filename is a frozen public API (§FR-D6, FR-I1, §7.4, Appendix A A30) — FR-D6 lists three fixed built-in canvases, "Membership (Signup / Signin / Member Home)". FR-I1 says each "compiles to `custom-{name}.hbs` at the theme root". §7.4 then makes that string permanent and user-visible: "A `custom-{name}.hbs` filename may **never** change once deployed… The filename is also the label: Ghost derives the Template dropdown's entry from it by a fixed transform." The inventory gives an *example* (`custom-membership.hbs` → "Membership") but never states what `{name}` is for the three built-in canvases, or whether the user names them. A dev cannot implement E7's emission or E5's switcher without inventing a string that can never be changed afterwards. *Fix:* fix the three names normatively (e.g. `custom-signup.hbs`, `custom-signin.hbs`, `custom-member-home.hbs`) in FR-I1 and §7.4's tree, and state that they are not user-editable.
- **high** Binding a designed membership page to a Ghost Page has no in-product flow and no docs page (FR-I1, FR-I5, FR-N4, FR-O1) — the PRD is emphatic that this is *easier* than a route ("removes a manual Ghost Admin step rather than adding one", "no Ghost Admin step beyond that one choice"), but the user must still go to Ghost Admin, create a Page, and select the template from a dropdown. The exactly analogous manual step for `routes.yaml` gets a designed first-class surface (FR-I4's guided card, with byte-comparison verification and a place in the UX mandate) *and* a docs page ("routes.yaml upload step" in FR-N4). The membership step gets neither: FR-N4's docs list has no entry for it, and no FR specifies a card. A user designs a signup page, deploys, and nothing appears on their site. *Fix:* add a guided card mirroring FR-I4's (deep-linked to the Ghost page editor, naming the exact template label to pick) and a docs page to FR-N4's list. Note that verification is not possible here the way it is for routes — the Content API exposes pages but the selected template is an Admin-side field — so state whether the card ever clears.
- **medium** NFR-6(a)'s cadence is unstated (§6 NFR-6) — (b) is "nightly", (c1)/(c2) "per-commit", (c3) "nightly", (d) implied per-release. (a) — 8,730 Playwright renders with diff gates, the heaviest job in the suite and the substrate the NFR-5 axe scan rides on — has no cadence at all. That is a real Architect decision (per-commit is likely infeasible; per-release loses the regression value) and the PRD is silent. *Fix:* state it, or state that the Architect owns it and name the constraint (must complete inside the library-wave gate).

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and the explicitness is structural rather than performative. Appendix G is a genuine Non-Goals section and closes with the clause that makes it enforceable: "This list is binding in both directions: nothing elsewhere in the PRD calls 'deferred' anything absent from it, and no FR delivers anything on it." It also chases consequences — the in-app code viewer entry does not merely defer, it states what the deferral does to a live requirement: "FR-L3's exit gate names 'any surface exposing compiled theme code', and **no such surface ships in v1**… so that clause gates nothing until the viewer ships." RTL is the one deferral given a gate rather than a silence, and the gate is specified (blocking acknowledgement, recorded, repeated pre-deploy).

§7.6's two tables are a working assumptions index in everything but name. The "Assumed, unvalidated" table is admirably blunt — every business figure carries "Founder estimate" and the paragraph that follows the whole appendix says so again. §7.6 item 13 discloses that the `@even`/`@odd` docs are backwards in a way that "would invert zebra striping across the entire library", and item 14(b) discloses a **direct contradiction between two of the PRD's own normative companions** on tier visibility filtering, then resolves the practical consequence ("until it is settled FR-H6's explicit `visibility:public` filter is correct either way"). Disclosing that your own source-of-truth files disagree is the opposite of smoothing.

Open-items density is appropriate to the stakes on its face — 14 verify items, 9 unvalidated assumptions, for a full-product PRD. The problem is not the count. It is that a document with `status: final` and "No de-scope contingency" has no mechanism attached to any of them.

### Findings

- **critical** §7.6's fourteen verify-at-build items have no owner anywhere in §8 (§7.6, §8) — the instruction is "Re-confirm each before the dependent epic starts", and no epic's entry or exit criteria reference §7.6. E15 "owns the gates" and does not list it. Item 14(a) is described in the PRD's own words as "the load-bearing claim under FR-I1, FR-D6 and the whole A30 re-point" — thirteen A30 variants, the membership canvases in FR-D6, `custom-{name}.hbs` emission in FR-I1 and FR-O1's starter rule all rest on it, and it "was not executed at runtime". The memlog shows the *previous* version of this exact premise (a `members/*.hbs` family) was discovered false mid-encode and forced a rebuild of A30, FR-D6, FR-I1, §7.4 and the Synthesis Defaults. Repeating that failure is the single largest schedule risk in the document, and the PRD's own mitigation is a bullet list nobody is assigned. Items 7 (gscan's v6 warning set, which the PRD says can make "E9/E10/E11's exit criteria unmeetable") and 13 (`@even`/`@odd`, "the entire library") carry the same profile. *Fix:* add a verification story to §8 — either an E0 spike ahead of E4, or per-item assignment into the entry criteria of the epic that depends on it — and make each item's owning epic explicit in the §7.6 table with a column.
- **medium** No BMAD-convention scope markers anywhere (`[ASSUMPTION: …]`, `[NOTE FOR PM]`, `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` — zero occurrences across `prd.md`) — the *function* is discharged well by Appendix G and §7.6, so this is not a substance gap. But downstream BMAD agents and any tooling that greps for those markers find nothing, and inline inferences are not individually tagged: §7.6's assumption table is index-only, with no inline counterparts to round-trip against. *Fix:* if the markers are deliberately retired for this PRD (the memlog does not record such a decision), say so in the header note beside the UJ delegation, so a downstream agent does not read their absence as an absence of assumptions.
- **low** T3's drop leaves NFR-7's 5.x claim untested with no stated fallback (§4, NFR-7) — T3 is "the **first candidate to drop under schedule pressure**", and it is the only 5.x deploy target. If it drops, "generated themes support Ghost 5.x **and** 6.x" becomes an unverified claim that §1.5's re-verification rule does not cover (that rule scopes to competitor claims). *Fix:* state what NFR-7 degrades to if T3 is dropped — most likely "5.x connections accepted, 5.x deploys unverified" — so the decision does not silently propagate into marketing.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The mechanical substrate is close to flawless. Every FR ID from FR-A1 to FR-Q6 is defined exactly once, contiguous within its domain, and **every FR reference in the document resolves to a defined FR** — no dangling cross-references. Epic FR-ownership is complete and checkable: I verified all 116 FRs against §8's ownership lines and every one is claimed by exactly one epic (or jointly by the three library waves, as §8 states). The arithmetic reconciles throughout — per-category counts sum to 485; group sums (47 + 199 + 109 + 102 + 28) reconcile; the wave splits (156 / 199 / 130) sum to 485 and their story counts (10 / 13 / 11) match their category ranges; 485 × 3 packs × 2 modes × 3 viewports = the stated 8,730 renders; 68 + 2 re-tiers = 70 [Free]. Appendix I's glossary is real work — it separates "pre-Inflozo snapshot" from "cloud snapshot", "locked elsewhere" from "plan-locked", and "Live environment" from the "Live!" of the deploy toast, each of which is a collision that would otherwise have cost a downstream agent a wrong implementation.

§7.5 is the model for how to delegate. It declares itself a sketch, sets precedence ("Where this sketch and an FR disagree, the FR wins"), enumerates twelve tables the sketch omits *with the FR that requires each and the failure mode if it is missed*, and assigns the whole-model story to E1 with the reason ("four epics write to it before E13 builds its UI"). The Architect has more than enough. Appendix B.1 does the same for FR-H7, with a decision procedure an implementer can execute without opening the research.

The weakness is the **UX** hand-off, and it is three separate holes.

### Findings

- **high** E1 points the Dev agent at a design directory that a sibling file documents as stale, with no warning (§8 E1) — E1 says build "design tokens and components per the design references at `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/design/`". That directory holds 24 `.dc.html` mockups. `reconcile-mockups.md`, in this same PRD folder, catalogues them against **PRD v1.0** and lists ~31 stale-design items plus 14 prd-gaps, including cross-cutting numbers that are wrong in every screen: "480 / 480+ sections", "Free = 120 sections (count-gated library)", "Pro sites = 3", "Pro projects = Unlimited", "Free assets = 500 MB", "Ghost 5.x". The PRD has since gone through v2.x and v3.0, adding the Staff Access Token to connect, the edit lock, notifications, the Paywall-as-template-surface reversal and the entire membership rebuild — none of which the mockups reflect, and none of which `reconcile-mockups.md` covers either, since it reconciles against v1.0. `prd.md` never mentions the mockups, the reconciliation, or their staleness. *Fix:* either add both files to the normative-companions list with an explicit staleness scope, or change E1's reference to name what in that directory is still authoritative (tokens and the Calibration Set, presumably) and what is not.
- **high** §7.3's one named UX deliverable is absent from the UX mandate (§7.3, header note) — §7.3 states "**The focus order is currently unspecified and is a UX-pass deliverable**" and gives the Architect three constraints to keep the seam addressable. The header's mandate to bmad-ux is four journeys plus five named flows, and the iframe focus/keyboard model is none of the nine. NFR-5 requires the editor to be "keyboard-complete… **across the canvas iframe boundary**", so this is a shipped accessibility requirement with a specified owner who has not been told. *Fix:* add the canvas focus and keyboard model to the UX mandate as a sixth flow.
- **medium** §7.5's beyond-sketch list omits the two fields AD4 flags as load-bearing (§7.5, `addendum.md` §AD1/AD4) — the list exists "so nothing is lost between the PRD and the schema" and enumerates twelve items in detail, but `project_templates` gets no `base_revision`, and the `edit_locks` row names "holder, heartbeat, and the unsynced-edit count" without the **lock generation**. AD4's closing paragraph names both explicitly as untunable: "the `base_revision` comparison and the lock-generation test that decide journal clearing (§AD1)". AD1's three-outcome hydrate table and AD2's revived-holder detection are unimplementable without them. *Fix:* add `base_revision` to the `project_templates` bullet and `generation` to the `edit_locks` bullet.
- **medium** The two research companions contradict each other and precedence is undefined (§7.6 item 14(b), Appendix B.1 header) — Appendix B.1 states "where they disagree the research is right and this file is a bug", but §7.6 discloses that `research-ghost-binding-contexts.md` and `research-ghost-membership-pages.md` disagree with each other on whether the tiers endpoint filters by visibility. The header lists both as "source of truth" for different areas and names no tie-breaker for their overlap. FR-H6 makes the practical question moot, but a downstream agent hitting a second overlap has no rule. *Fix:* state a precedence order between the two research files in the header's companion list.
- **low** FR-D5's "CTA-bearing sections" is not self-resolving (FR-D5, Appendix C, `sections-inventory.md`) — FR-D5 introduces member visibility on "CTA-bearing sections" without enumerating them; the normative list (A2, A6, A22, A26) lives in Appendix C and the inventory. A6 (CTA Banners) and A22 (Newsletter) are obvious; A7 (Pricing), A16 (Contact) and A3 (Footers, which carries a newsletter toggle) are plausibly CTA-bearing and are excluded. *Fix:* inline the four category ids in FR-D5.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape is right for what this is: a chain-top, greenfield, consumer-facing SaaS PRD written for BMAD agents rather than humans, and it is formalized to match — capability-spec density for the FRs, a normative appendix set the compiler can be tested against, and a §8 whose ownership is checkable rather than asserted. The library epics correctly refuse variant-per-story ("a category is a story, not a variant — 34 stories, not 485") with the reason stated, which is exactly the granularity call an SM agent would otherwise get wrong at a cost of 485 near-identical acceptance-criteria sets.

The deliberate absence of UJs is defensible on its face — journeys genuinely are UX's craft, the mandated floor is specific, and five of the nine mandated artifacts are named by FR — but this is a "meaningful UX" product by the rubric's own test, and the rubric flags under-formalization when a consumer product ships with no UJs. The delegation is what has to carry that, and the three findings above (thin personas, unflagged stale mockups, the focus model outside the mandate) mean it currently carries it at about two-thirds strength. That is a fixable delegation, not a wrong shape — hence adequate rather than thin.

One genuine shape question: the PRD is written throughout in a defensive register that anticipates objections and pre-refutes them inline ("must not be modeled as one", "is not an oversight to be optimized away", "any claim otherwise is false"). That register is *correct* for an agent audience — it prevents a downstream agent from re-litigating a settled decision — and it is why this PRD holds up under adversarial reading. It also makes it a poor document to hand a human stakeholder cold. Worth knowing, not worth fixing.

### Findings

- **medium** No path exists for a connecting user who is not the Ghost site Owner (FR-C1, FR-C3, FR-C8, FR-J13) — FR-C1 requires the user to "mint a Staff Access Token on the site Owner's account" and states "The Staff Access Token is required, not optional". Ghost Staff Access Tokens are per-user; a site Administrator (or any non-Owner staff) cannot produce the Owner's. The words "administrator", "non-owner" and "staff user" appear nowhere in the PRD. FR-J13 describes a degraded path for a token that is "absent or revoked", but FR-C1 makes it a validation input at connect, so the two do not obviously compose: it is unclear whether a connection can even be *created* without one. For the "indie founder" and "multi-site operator" personas — precisely the ones on team-run sites — this is the first screen. *Fix:* state whether connect proceeds without a Staff token (in a snapshot-less mode carrying FR-J13's degraded path from the start) or blocks, and say so in FR-C1's guided copy.
