Three things to hold alongside the finding list. Strategic coherence

strong

There is a thesis and the document is built on it: Ghost has a primitive theming story (§1.1), the fix is a builder whose canvas is the page rather than a preview of it (§1.2), and everything expensive in the PRD is downstream of that one bet — P4’s one-source-per-section, §7.3’s two-renderers-over-one-source, FR-G3’s annotated-HTML registry, the outright rejection of a React preview layer, and NFR-6(c)’s three fidelity layers with the golden-diff option explicitly refused because “it could only pass by normalizing away the image URLs FR-J5 depends on.” The second bet — safe installs — produces its own coherent cluster, and the PRD accepts a real onboarding cost for it rather than softening the promise.

The gap is that the thesis’s experiential half has no gate.

Shape fit

adequate

The shape is right for what this is: a chain-top, greenfield, consumer-facing SaaS PRD written for BMAD agents rather than humans, and it is formalized to match — capability-spec density for the FRs, a normative appendix set the compiler can be tested against, and a §8 whose ownership is checkable rather than asserted. The deliberate absence of UJs is defensible on its face, but this is a “meaningful UX” product by the rubric’s own test, and the delegation is what has to carry that — which the three findings above (thin personas, unflagged stale mockups, the focus model outside the mandate) mean it currently does at about two-thirds strength. A fixable delegation, not a wrong shape. One genuine shape question: the PRD is written throughout in a defensive register that anticipates objections and pre-refutes them inline. That register is correct for an agent audience and is why the document holds up under adversarial reading; it also makes it a poor document to hand a human stakeholder cold. Worth knowing, not worth fixing.
