# PRD Quality Review — Inflozo (v2.3, status:final)

## Overall verdict

This is a decided document, not a deliberating one: §5's 116 FRs carry bounded consequences rather than adjectives, §7.6's "Verify at build time" list is a better instrument than an Open Questions section for a `status:final` PRD, and the hard trade-offs (destructive image optimization, un-pinnable library versions, takeover data loss, export-and-cancel churn) are named with what was given up. Two things are at risk. First, Appendix A's normative inventory degrades after category A16: **219 of 487 variants carry no declared control set**, which is exactly the deliverable §4 declares non-de-scopeable. Second, §8's epic breakdown has silent coverage holes — FR-B (dashboard), FR-P (email), and FR-D18 (edit lock) are named in no epic — so an SM agent driven by epic order will under-produce against a PRD whose own DoD says "every FR implemented."

---

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and attributed. §4 carries "**No de-scope contingency (owner decision)**" and "**Live-infrastructure testing (owner mandate — must-have)**"; FR-J8 carries "(owner requirement: 'deploy only' vs 'deploy and activate' are distinct buttons)"; FR-D4 carries "Every text prop is *also* editable in the Content group of the sidebar (owner requirement)". A reader can tell which constraints are the founder's taste and which are engineering consequence — that distinction is what a decision-maker needs and most PRDs blur it.

Trade-offs name the cost, not just the choice. FR-J14: "no per-project pinning — chosen for implementation simplicity); consequently **any redeploy includes all library changes since the project's last deploy** — there is no way to redeploy without them." FR-K2: "Optimization is destructive by design — originals are not retained." FR-J13 refuses to over-promise: "Inflozo does not guarantee restoring it beyond the snapshot or the Settings → Design guidance." `addendum.md` AD1 states "**Risk accepted:** work that exists only locally is lost if the browser profile is wiped before a flush," and AD3 is a standing rejected-alternatives register with reasons. Appendix F volunteers the worst news in the document — "themes work forever and export stays open, so an export-and-cancel cohort is expected — model a month-1 churn spike honestly in blended LTV" — which is the opposite of a PRD smoothing everything to neutral.

Zero Open Questions, zero `[NOTE FOR PM]`, zero `[ASSUMPTION]` tags is the correct end state for `status:final` at launch stakes, and here it is earned rather than achieved by erasure: §7.6's six-item "Verify at build time" list carries the residual uncertainty with a named re-verification trigger ("re-confirm each before the dependent epic starts"). Where it falls short is the numbers that decide whether the business works.

### Findings

- **medium** G1 and G2 state p75 targets with no way to compute them (§1.3 Goals table; §4 DoD) — G1 "< 10 minutes (p75) end-to-end for a first-time user" and G2 "< 15 minutes (p75)" are the only two goals with a percentile and no measurement binding. §1.3 forecloses the mechanism — "No in-app analytics or telemetry requirement exists in v1" — and §4's Definition of Done omits G1/G2 entirely, while G6 and NFR-1 are carefully bound ("pass/fail on the NFR-1 reference environment and stress fixture"). The two goals that describe the product's core promise are the two nobody can grade. *Fix:* bind them the way G6 is bound — e.g. "median of N moderated first-run sessions on the reference environment, measured once before GA" — or demote them to design targets and say so.
- **medium** Appendix F's modeled mix contradicts G9 by ~5× (Appendix F "Economics basis"; §1.3 G9) — "Modeled 1,000 Free + 200 Pro: ≈ $110–160/mo infra vs ≈ **$2,700/mo net**" implies a 16.7% paid share, against G9's target of "≥ 3% free→Pro within 90 days" with G10's "Pro monthly churn < 5%." At the PRD's own targets the steady-state paid share lands nowhere near 200/1200. The infra-cost conclusion survives (it is dominated by fixed cost), but the "$2,700/mo net" figure is the sentence a founder re-reads when deciding to build this, and it is inconsistent with the goals two pages earlier. *Fix:* re-model at G9's rate, or relabel the mix explicitly as a capacity-headroom scenario rather than a revenue projection.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here reads as furniture. The Vision (§1.2) is unswappable — "compiles the design into a clean, valid, marketplace-quality Ghost theme and deploys it to the user's site in one click" describes this product and no other. §1.4's seven differentiators each carry a mechanism and, where relevant, FR pointers (#5 "Safe installs" cites FR-J6, FR-J9, FR-J13; #1 cites §7.3, which then *mandates* the architecture that makes the claim true). §1.5 names the incumbent with checkable specifics — "Fantasma (fantasma.io, Pro $99/yr) … form-based editing (no inline WYSIWYG), one Source-derived aesthetic … no deploy versioning or rollback" — rather than gesturing at a market.

NFR theater is absent, which is rare. Every NFR carries a threshold or a named mechanism: NFR-1 supplies a reference environment ("a mid-tier laptop with 4× CPU throttle") *and* a stress fixture ("a 40-section template"); NFR-2 gives "total theme JS < 30 KB gzipped for a maximal design"; NFR-3 enumerates the sanitization surface rather than saying "secure"; NFR-6 names four distinct gate types with their inputs. FR-F4 goes further and pre-empts the classic PRD failure of competing budgets: "one budget per interaction — no competing numbers."

Even the delight claim is partly made testable. Appendix H is normative and specific — canonical strings quoted verbatim, "exactly **one confetti moment** in the product," "destructive and billing flows stay serious, never cutesy" — which converts P6 from a mood into a rule someone can violate.

### Findings

- **low** §3's personas are declared to drive prioritization but no requirement cites one (§3; §5; §8) — "Personas (for tone and prioritization, not permissions)" lists four, each one line. Their mapping to product decisions is inferable (the multi-site operator ↔ Pro's 10 connections; the creator with taste ↔ FR-E3/FR-D7/FR-I2) but never stated, and no FR, epic, or scope call in §5 or §8 invokes a persona as its reason. Their real job is being handed to bmad-ux by the header sentence, which is legitimate — the claim about prioritization is what's unearned. *Fix:* either attach one persona name to each of the four or five prioritization calls that actually turned on them, or narrow the parenthetical to "for tone and for the UX workflow."

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD has a thesis and bets the architecture on it. The thesis is that fidelity plus library scale plus Ghost-native depth make a $15/mo subscription defensible against a $99/yr incumbent and $89–149 one-time themes (§1.5), and §7.3 turns that into law: "One section = one `.hbs` + one CSS module … **Any proposal to re-implement a section as a React preview component is rejected by this PRD.**" That is a thesis with a standing veto attached, not a positioning paragraph.

Prioritization follows the thesis rather than ease. The epic order runs E4 Section Runtime Platform → E5 Editor Shell → E6 Style Packs → E7 Compiler → E9–E11 Library Waves: the moat is built first and the 487 variants — the largest and most mechanical block of work — come last, gated by the CI that E4 established. §4's "the full FR scope and the 487-variant inventory are not de-scopeable; if library waves slip, launch slips" is the thesis restated as scope policy, and it is the correct policy given §1.5's argument rests on "library scale (487 variants vs ~155 presets)."

Counter-metrics are present where the rubric asks for them: G8 pairs activation with "7-day rollback rate < 10%", G9 pairs conversion with "refund/dispute rate < 2%", G10 pairs churn with a dark-pattern guardrail. MVP scope kind is coherent — an experience-plus-platform MVP with P7 "Everything ships before launch" and a single GA release, no drip.

### Findings

- **medium** Nothing validates the thesis's headline claim (§1.2; §1.3 Goals; §4 DoD; FR-D17) — §1.2 stakes the product on being "**extremely easy, genuinely fun, and the most beautiful UI/UX in the Ghost ecosystem** — closer to playing with a well-made toy than operating software," and §1.4 #2 says "Choosing a design becomes play." G3–G6 measure output correctness and editor responsiveness; G8–G10 measure business outcomes; none measures the play loop. FR-D17 Site Remix — the purest expression of the claim, with "never-lose-content" as a hard requirement — appears in no goal, no NFR, no §4 DoD line, and reaches §8 only inside E5's list, whose exit criterion is "full editing loop on pilot sections at 60 fps." The most differentiated feature in the product is the least gated. *Fix:* give the play loop one gate — e.g. a Site Remix / Variant Shuffle acceptance in E5's exit (content preservation across N shuffles and a full re-roll on the 40-section fixture, zero prop loss), which is testable and does not require telemetry.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Section 5 is strong enough to be unforgiving with and still come up nearly clean. The usual weasel words are absent — no "reasonable," no "user-friendly," no "robust," no "seamless" — and every instance of "gracefully" is immediately bounded rather than left as a mood: FR-D14 "behavior degrades gracefully — **slower is acceptable, lockups and state corruption are not**"; FR-D9 "no-op gracefully with a notice (**never corrupt state**)"; FR-H4 "renders what exists plus an editor-only indicator ('this site has 2 posts; section shows up to 6')." FR-J13's "best-effort" is scoped by naming what *is* guaranteed alongside it ("guaranteed only for Inflozo-signed themes"). FR-K2 converts a browser-capability risk into a pass/fail line: "no browser silently uploads unoptimized originals (acceptance criterion)." FR-Q2's cap arithmetic is closed and consistent with FR-Q5 (20 − 3 reserved = 17). FR-H2's main-feed designation has a full lifecycle — auto-designate, transfer on delete/hide, reassign, and a defined zero-feed branch with an SEO guard.

The hole is not in §5; it is in the normative appendix §5 depends on. `prd.md` Appendix A promises "The full normative inventory — **per-category content models, controls**, and all 487 variant descriptors." `sections-inventory.md` delivers that for A1–A16 — every one of those sixteen carries an explicit `Content:` line and a `Controls:` line — and then the pattern stops. From A17 onward the entries are a one-sentence context line plus numbered structural descriptors. FR-G3 requires a registry entry of `{ … contentSchema, controlSchema, quickControls[] … }` per variant and FR-F3 requires "3–5 **Quick Controls** pinned on top (chosen per section as the highest-impact decisions)" plus a "Hard cap ≈ 15 visible controls per section." For 219 variants there is no normative source for any of that, and the universal-controls line supplies only three (background role, vertical spacing, top divider).

### Findings

- **high** 219 of 487 variants (16 categories) declare no control set (`sections-inventory.md` A17–A24, A26–A33; against `prd.md` Appendix A, FR-G3, FR-F3) — A1–A16 each declare `Content:` and `Controls:`. A17–A24 and A26–A33 declare neither (A22 declares content only; A25 and A34 declare controls in prose). Some of these have a legitimate external source — A17's "Data group per FR-H2" covers Source/Count/Order/meta toggles for post feeds — but that reference appears on A17 alone, does not extend to A20 Tag Collections or A21 Author Showcases (FR-H2's Source list is post-oriented), and does nothing for A24 Post Headers, A26 Post Footers, A28 Comments, A29 Archive Headers, A30 Members Pages, A31 Error & Utility, A32 Paywall, or A33 Koenig. The schemas demonstrably exist somewhere: `sections-inventory.md` § Synthesis Defaults specifies "A24 #1 Classic Center … Meta row (date/author/reading time/tag) **hidden**" and A29 #1's "tag accent color when set (built-in)" — controls A24 and A29 never declare. This lands on the deliverable §4 declares non-de-scopeable ("every one must work perfectly") and on the category the inventory itself calls "A flagship differentiator" (A32). E9–E11's Dev agent will invent product surface with no PRD authority, on 45% of the library. *Fix:* extend the A1–A16 pattern through A17–A34 — one `Content:` line (or an explicit "post-bound per Appendix B / FR-H2" pointer) and one `Controls:` line per category. This is mechanical, not creative: the Synthesis Defaults and the variant descriptors already imply most of it.
- **low** FR-I4's routes verification has two undefined terms — "Inflozo verifies **where possible** by fetching a **representative route URL** on the live site." Neither "possible" nor "representative" is defined, and the resulting state ("Routes unverified — did you upload routes.yaml?") is user-visible and persistent. Everything else in FR-I4 is tight — the "last routes.yaml **offered**" tracking and the resurfacing triggers are exemplary. *Fix:* name the rule — e.g. verify when the compiled routes define at least one collection or custom route with a static prefix; fetch the first such prefix and treat a 200 with the expected template marker as verified.
- **low** Two different measurement bases for the same two Lighthouse numbers (§1.3 G4; NFR-2) — G4 says "≥ 90 (mobile), Accessibility ≥ 95 — measured on **library defaults and the 12 preset packs**"; NFR-2 says the same thresholds "on the **fixture content set**." "Fixture content set" appears once, is not in the Glossary, and is not obviously Orbit Weekly (FR-H3) or the "fixture theme" of FR-H5/NFR-6(c). E15's "perf/a11y audits" inherits the ambiguity. *Fix:* pick one basis and reference it from both, or add "fixture content set" to Appendix I.

## Scope honesty — strong

Appendix G is a real Non-Goals section doing real work — eighteen entries, several carrying the reason inline: "in-app compiled-code viewer (export covers v1)", "live-site theme preview UX (deploy-without-activate exists, but no preview surfacing — previews would require a working Ghost install)", "promotion of non-accent color tokens to Ghost Admin (v1 is accent-only — FR-Q5; other tokens have no dark built-in counterpart)". The RTL entry is the tell that this section was written rather than filled in: it deferred the thing and then carefully fenced what *does* ship ("Translation itself ships in v1 … chrome strings are user-overridable via the Translations module").

De-scoping is proposed openly, never done quietly. FR-L3's "**Downgrade rules (explicit)**" spells out every over-limit resource and commits to "Inflozo never auto-deletes," down to the edge case that a reconnection performed to run a restore still counts against the cap. AD3 records five rejected alternatives with reasons. Open-items density is zero, which at `status:final` and launch stakes is the target rather than a gap — and §7.6 keeps the residual risk visible under a name that carries an action ("Verify at build time … re-confirm each before the dependent epic starts").

The gap is that §7.6's discipline covers only one class of assumption.

### Findings

- **medium** The market/behavioral assumption class carries no source and no verification trigger (§1.3 G8–G10; §1.5; Appendix F) — §7.6 handles engineering-world facts impeccably: six named items, each tied to the FR that depends on it. Nothing does the same for the numbers that decide whether the business is worth building. "≥ 30% of signups reach first deploy within 7 days," "≥ 3% free→Pro within 90 days," "Pro monthly churn < 5%", §1.5's "~155 presets" and "one Source-derived aesthetic (a **known user complaint**)", and Appendix F's modeled mix are all presented with the same typographic confidence as the verified facts, with no attribution and no re-check. For a real-money launch these are the load-bearing inferences. *Fix:* add a short second block under §7.6 — "Assumed, unvalidated" — listing the five or six behavioral/market figures with whatever basis exists (comparable-product benchmark, competitor page snapshot date, or "founder estimate"), so a later reader can tell evidence from estimate.
- **low** NFR-8 leaves a door §1.3 closes — NFR-8 permits "no third-party trackers **beyond privacy-respecting analytics**"; §1.3 states "No in-app analytics or telemetry requirement exists in v1 (NFR-8's privacy stance stands)" and cites NFR-8 as its authority. A Dev agent reading NFR-8 alone may ship Plausible into the app; a Dev agent reading §1.3 will not. The likely intent is marketing-site-only, but NFR-8 does not scope it. *Fix:* scope the clause — "privacy-respecting analytics on the marketing site only; the authenticated app ships no analytics in v1."
- **low** Whether an over-limit read-only project can still export is unstated (FR-L3; FR-J12) — FR-L3 puts "all but one project" into read-only on a downgraded Free account and says they "stay viewable"; FR-J12 grants ZIP export to all plans and gates it only on Pro-section content ("Export gating mirrors deploy gating exactly"). FR-L3 explicitly preserves rollback and snapshot restore through downgrade but does not mention export. Given the PRD's no-lock-in stance (Appendix F: "export stays open"), the answer is probably yes — but a Dev agent has to guess, on a billing boundary. *Fix:* one clause in FR-L3 stating whether read-only projects retain export.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The traceability fundamentals hold. All 116 FR IDs are contiguous within their domain, unique, and every FR reference in the document resolves to a defined FR; NFR-1…9 likewise; every `§` cross-reference resolves to a real heading; all nine Appendix references resolve, including the three `Appendix A §nn` pointers that must land in the companion file. Appendix I is a genuine glossary — seventeen terms, each with a one-line meaning *and* a "Defined in" pointer, which is what makes source-extraction work. Sections read standalone: FR-L3 restates the exit-gate rule rather than saying "see above," and FR-Q5 restates the cap arithmetic rather than pointing at FR-Q2. Appendix A's summary table and `sections-inventory.md`'s per-category counts agree exactly, and both sum to 487 with 68 `[Free]` — verified line by line, and the tie-break rule is stated ("on any conflict the inventory wins").

Two structural problems sit between the PRD and the agents meant to consume it, and both are at seams rather than inside sections.

### Findings

- **high** §8's epics leave FR-B, FR-P, and FR-D18 unowned (§8 E1–E15) — across fifteen epics, §8 cites exactly five FR IDs (FR-B7, FR-C2, FR-C7, FR-J12, FR-Q). Mapping the prose, three blocks land nowhere. **FR-B1–B6** (project cards with auto-captured thumbnails and deploy-status chips, four creation paths, delete-warning semantics, plan caps at creation, the connected-sites strip / quota meter / changelog popover) reduce to E1's exit phrase "dashboard skeleton" — a skeleton is not FR-B. **FR-P1–P3** (five transactional emails plus the two owner-relay flows) appear in no epic at all; Resend is in §7.1's stack and nowhere in the sequencing. **FR-D18**, the edit lock, is absent from E5's list ("…undo/autosave, device + member-state + dark toggles, keyboard map") even though E7's deploy and export require it (FR-D18: "**Deploy and ZIP export require the edit lock**") — a cross-device data-loss mechanism with a bespoke protocol in AD2, owned by nobody. §8's preamble says "scope is bounded by §5 and appendices," and §4's DoD says "every FR implemented," so the backstop exists — but an SM agent expanding epics in order produces no dashboard stories, no email stories, and no lock stories. *Fix:* add the FR ranges to each epic line (E1 … FR-A1, FR-B1–B6; E5 … FR-D1–D18; and either a small E5b or an explicit line in E12/E15 owning FR-P1–P3), so epic membership is checkable rather than inferred.
- **high** The UX delegation's mandated floor omits the flows the PRD itself calls designed (header sentence; FR-J13, FR-D18, FR-I4, FR-J14, FR-C2) — the header delegates journeys cleanly and names a floor: bmad-ux "must cover at minimum connect → first deploy, blank-canvas build, Free-plan ship, and downgrade recovery, using the §3 personas." Those four are the happy paths. Meanwhile FR-J13 instructs a UX workflow directly — "where it is unavailable the degraded path is a **designed flow, not a warning toast**" — and that flow is on none of the four. Nor are FR-D18's three-party lock choreography (nudge prompt, takeover prompt with "That session had 14 unsaved edits; they were not included", revived-holder message), FR-I4's "one more step" card and persistent "Routes unverified" state, FR-J14's mandatory library-update confirm, or FR-C2's Preview-only explanation and its clearing conditions. These are the five highest-friction moments in the product and each already has named UI states in the FR text. A UX agent reading the header literally delivers four journeys and leaves them unclaimed; because the delegation is the *only* journey authority, nothing downstream notices. *Fix:* extend the header's minimum list to name these as required flows (they are flows, not journeys — the distinction is worth making in the sentence), or add a one-line "Flows delegated to bmad-ux" list next to §4 enumerating them with their FR anchors.
- **medium** "Read-only" names two different editor states with different remedies (FR-D18; FR-L3; Appendix I) — FR-D18's read-only means *another session holds the edit lock*, and the remedy is "Request editing" or take over. FR-L3's read-only means *the account is over its Free project cap*, and the remedy is upgrade or pick a different project to keep editable. Both render the same editor in a non-editing state; the Glossary defines "Edit lock" but not "read-only," and no passage distinguishes them. A UX agent designing the read-only banner, or a Dev agent implementing the guard, will conflate them and ship one message for two situations. *Fix:* name them distinctly (e.g. "locked elsewhere" vs "plan-locked") and add both to Appendix I.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a chain-top PRD for a consumer-facing product with heavy UX weight, feeding Analyst → PM → Architect → SM → Dev → QA, and it invests where that shape demands: a normative glossary, a registry entry format (FR-G3) that is a literal downstream contract, a generated-theme file tree (§7.4), a data-model sketch (§7.5), and epic sequencing. It resists the two failure modes the rubric names. It is not over-formalized — 116 FRs against 487 library deliverables, a Handlebars compiler, a helper-emulation layer, a routing builder, a custom-settings builder, a translations module, and a billing state machine is FR density tracking real surface area, not ceremony; four personas is at the limit, not over it; there are no ceremonial sections. And it is not under-formalized despite having no user journeys, because the FRs carry behavioral detail at the level journeys usually supply (FR-C2's connect-and-degrade path, FR-L3's downgrade table, FR-D18's takeover choreography).

On whether the journey delegation is *safe*: for build it is. Architect, SM, and Dev never need a journey document here — §5 plus the appendices plus `addendum.md` are sufficient to design and story-map the system, and the delegation is explicit, names its consumer, names its inputs (§3 personas), and names a floor. That is a real handoff contract rather than an omission dressed as one. The residual risk is entirely on the UX side and is captured in the Downstream finding above: the floor is set at the four happy paths while the PRD elsewhere mandates designed flows for its five hardest moments. Narrow that gap and the delegation is unambiguously safe.

One shape note worth stating: this is greenfield code against a brownfield *platform*. Ghost's undocumented endpoints, plan lineup, and version behavior are the highest-variance dependencies in the build, and §7.6's "Verify at build time" list plus §4's four live targets (T1–T4, with T4 existing solely to exercise the Starter block path) are exactly the right instrument. That is a shape-aware choice, not a template artifact.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** clean. 116 FRs across domains A–Q with no gaps, no duplicates; every `FR-*` and `NFR-*` reference in the document resolves to a definition. Domain letters run A–I, then Q, then J–P — deliberate and explained in §5's preamble ("Sections are ordered by dependency, not alphabetically — FR-Q … sits between FR-I and FR-J"). Fine for machine consumption; worth knowing that a naive alphabetical sort will misorder it.
- **Cross-references:** all resolve. Every `§n.n` maps to a real heading; `Appendix A §31/§33/§34` land correctly in `sections-inventory.md`; `addendum.md §AD1/§AD2` resolve. E1's `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/design/` exists and is populated. One notation inconsistency: §7.6's risk table says "Epics 9–11" where §8 uses "E9 · … E11 ·".
- **Inventory arithmetic:** verified programmatically. All 34 declared per-category counts equal the actual numbered entries; they sum to 487; exactly two `[Free]` per category, 68 total; `prd.md`'s Appendix A summary table matches `sections-inventory.md` category by category. Every variant line carries a structural descriptor after an em dash (FR-G5's bar) — 0 bare entries.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** N/A — the PRD uses no `[ASSUMPTION]` tags. §7.6's "Verify at build time" list is the functional substitute for the engineering-fact class and roundtrips correctly (all six items cite the FR or appendix that depends on them).
